Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the given rules alone.

A standard way is to use the recursive path ordering (RPO) with precedence `F > G` and lexicographic status for `F`.

- `F(x, y, Z) -> x` is oriented because `x` is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
- `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, y, F(x, y, n))` is oriented because:
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) > y` by the subterm property;
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) > F(x, y, n)` since the first two arguments coincide and the third argument decreases: `S(n) > n` by the subterm property;
  - hence, with `F > G`, the left side is greater than the whole right side.

Since RPO is a well-founded reduction order, every rewrite step decreases in this order, so there are no infinite rewrite sequences. Thus the system is strongly normalizing, with no extra structural or object-level axioms needed.
