Yes. Strong normalization can be established from these rewrite rules alone.

A standard recursive path ordering / lexicographic path ordering suffices. Choose a precedence such as

\[
F \;>\; G
\]

and give \(F\) lexicographic status on its arguments. Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) decreases because the right-hand side is a variable occurring as a subterm/argument of the left-hand side.
- For
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,y,F(x,y,n)),
  \]
  the left side is greater than \(y\), and it is greater than \(F(x,y,n)\), since with the same first two arguments the third argument decreases from \(S(n)\) to its proper subterm \(n\). Since \(F > G\), the entire left side is greater than the \(G\)-headed right side.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded syntactic reduction ordering. No associativity, commutativity, induction principle, semantic interpretation, or other object-level axioms are required.
