Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone, under the ordinary first-order TRS setting of finite terms.

A standard lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules. For example, give `F` higher precedence than `G`, and use lexicographic comparison for `F`:

- `F(x, y, Z) -> x` decreases because `x` is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
- For  
  `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, y, F(x, y, n))`,  
  the recursive `F` call is smaller than the source because its third argument changes from `S(n)` to the proper subterm `n`. The entire right-hand side is then smaller because its root symbol is `G`, below `F` in the precedence, and all of its arguments are smaller than the left-hand side.

Thus every rewrite step decreases a well-founded ordering. Duplication of `y` does not defeat termination: it may enlarge a term, but it cannot create an infinite rewrite sequence in this finite first-order setting.

So no extra structural axioms or object-level assumptions are needed beyond the ordinary meaning of first-order terms and rewriting.
