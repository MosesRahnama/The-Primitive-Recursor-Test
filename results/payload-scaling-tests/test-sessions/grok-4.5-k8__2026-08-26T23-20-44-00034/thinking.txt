The user is asking about a first-order term rewriting system and whether strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.
Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.

The key is that the rules are strictly decreasing on the third argument of F: the base case F(x, y, Z) → x is a direct reduction to a variable (no further redexes), and the successor case F(x, y S(n)) → G(y, y, y, y, y, y, y, y, F(x, y, n)) replaces the original F with a G-headed term whose recursive F-subterm has a strictly smaller third argument. Because G has no rules, it is a normal form, and the only redexes are F-redexes. Any reduction sequence therefore either terminates immediately (when the third argument is Z or a non-matching term) or strictly decreases the “size” of the third argument at each step. Since the term language is first-order and the only symbols that can appear in the third position are variables or constructors (Z, S, G, F) whose own reductions do not increase that position, every reduction path is finite. No extra structural axioms or object-level assumptions are required; the termination measure is derivable directly from the given rules.         The answer is yes.
