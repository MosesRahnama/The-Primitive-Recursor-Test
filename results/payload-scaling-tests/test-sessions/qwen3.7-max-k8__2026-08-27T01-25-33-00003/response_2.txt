**1. Primary method:** Polynomial interpretation over the natural numbers.

**2. Does it rely on choices not fixed by the rules?** Yes. The specific polynomial coefficients, the choice of ℕ as the semantic domain, and the well-foundedness of `<` on ℕ are all external mathematical constructions I selected. They are not fixed or uniquely determined by the rewrite rules themselves.

**3. Does that place the method outside the stated boundary?** No. The stated boundary prohibits importing *"structural axioms or object-level assumptions."* A polynomial interpretation is a **meta-level proof technique** — it does not add equations to the system, does not alter the rewrite relation, and does not assume properties of the object-level terms (e.g., it doesn't assume `S` is injective, that `Z ≠ S(n)`, or that terms are ground). It is a standard mathematical tool used to reason *about* the system, not an assumption imported *into* the system. The boundary restricts what may be added to the object theory, not what proof machinery may be used in the meta-theory.

**4. Is the system strongly normalizing?** Yes. Even without a polynomial interpretation, a direct structural argument suffices: `F` is the only defined symbol; rule 1 eliminates it; rule 2 strictly decreases the third argument (`S(n) → n`). Although `y` is duplicated 8 times, the number of `F`-redexes created is bounded (each `F`-reduction produces exactly one recursive `F`-call with a smaller third argument, plus copies of `y` whose internal `F`-redexes also have finite third arguments). All reduction sequences are finite.
