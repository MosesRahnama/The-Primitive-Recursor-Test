### Explanation of Reasoning

To prove strong normalization, we can show that the rewrite system is compatible with a reduction order (a well-founded partial order on terms closed under context and substitution). Specifically, we can use the **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)**.

Let's define a precedence on the function symbols:
$$\text{Precedence: } F \succ G \quad \text{and} \quad F \succ S$$

We check both rewrite rules against this LPO:

1. **Rule 1: $F(x, y, Z) \rightarrow x$**
   * Under LPO, any term is strictly greater than its proper subterms. 
   * Since $x$ is a subterm of $F(x, y, Z)$, we have $F(x, y, Z) \succ_{\text{lpo}} x$. This rule is compatible.

2. **Rule 2: $F(x, y, S(n)) \rightarrow G(y, y, y, y, y, y, y, y, F(x, y, n))$**
   * Since the root symbol of the left-hand side is $F$ and the root symbol of the right-hand side is $G$, and we have the precedence $F \succ G$, LPO requires us to show that:
     1. $F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{\text{lpo}} t_i$ for all arguments $t_i$ of the right-hand side.
   * The arguments of $G$ are $y$ (multiple times) and $F(x, y, n)$.
     * For the variable $y$: Since $y$ is a subterm of the left-hand side, $F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{\text{lpo}} y$ holds trivially.
     * For the term $F(x, y, n)$: We must compare $F(x, y, S(n))$ with $F(x, y, n)$. Since they share the same root symbol $F$, we compare their arguments lexicographically. The first two arguments are identical ($x$ and $y$), and for the third argument, $S(n) \succ_{\text{lpo}} n$ holds by the subterm property. Thus, $F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{\text{lpo}} F(x, y, n)$.
   * Therefore, Rule 2 is compatible with LPO.

Since both rules are compatible with a lexicographic path ordering, the term rewriting system is terminating (strongly normalizing).
