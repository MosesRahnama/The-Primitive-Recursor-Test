Yes. Strong normalization follows from the rewrite system’s syntactic structure alone.

For example, a lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules by choosing \(F \succ G\) and giving \(F\) lexicographic argument status:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\) by the subterm property.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n)\), since the first two arguments agree and \(S(n) \succ n\).
- Consequently, \(F(x,y,S(n))\) is greater than every argument of the outer \(G\), and with \(F \succ G\), it is greater than the entire right-hand side.

The duplication of \(y\) can increase term size and derivation length, but it does not defeat this well-founded ordering. Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded term order, establishing strong normalization without extra object-level axioms about \(S\), \(Z\), or the represented data.
