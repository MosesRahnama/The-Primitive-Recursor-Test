1. **Method used:** Recursive Path Ordering (RPO), a meta-level termination proof technique.

2. **External dependencies:** Yes. The proof required choosing a symbol precedence (**F > G**) and a comparison status (lexicographic, left-to-right). Neither of these is fixed or derivable from the rewrite rules themselves — they are prover-supplied parameters. One could equally well choose G > F (which would fail), so the proof's success depends on an imported ordering choice, not on anything intrinsic to the rules.

3. **Boundary compliance:** Yes, this places the method outside the stated boundary. The original instruction asked whether strong normalization can be established *"from the presented rewrite system alone, without importing…assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given rules."* An RPO precedence and status are exactly such imported assumptions: they are external proof machinery brought in by the analyst, not consequences derivable from the two rules. Any termination proof technique (polynomial interpretations, semantic labelling, etc.) similarly imports meta-level structure not contained in the rules.

4. **Is the system actually strongly normalizing?** Yes. Independently of proof-methodology concerns, the system is strongly normalizing: every application of Rule 2 strips one `S` constructor from the third argument of `F`, and Rule 1 handles the `Z` base case. Since all terms are finite, the third argument can only be peeled finitely many times, and duplication of `y` into `G` produces independent sub-terms that are each themselves strongly normalizing. No infinite reduction sequence is possible.
