**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone.

A lexicographic path ordering (LPO) proves termination by choosing precedence \(F > G\). Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z) > x\) by the subterm property.
- For the second rule,
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)
  \]
  because the first two arguments agree and \(S(n) > n\) by the subterm property.
- The left-hand side also dominates both occurrences of \(y\), and since \(F>G\), it therefore dominates
  \[
  G(y,y,F(x,y,n)).
  \]

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded simplification ordering, including under substitutions and contexts. No additional structural or object-level axioms are needed.
