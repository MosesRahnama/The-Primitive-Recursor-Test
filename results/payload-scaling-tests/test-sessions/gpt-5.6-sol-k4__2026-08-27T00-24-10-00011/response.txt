Yes. Under the standard interpretation of first-order rewriting over finite terms, the system is strongly normalizing.

A lexicographic path order can orient both rules by choosing \(F \succ G\) and giving \(F\) lexicographic status:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\), since \(x\) is a proper subterm.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n)\), because \(S(n) \succ n\) by the subterm property.
- The left side also dominates every occurrence of \(y\), so with \(F \succ G\),
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \succ G(y,y,y,y,F(x,y,n)).
  \]

Although the second rule duplicates \(y\), this does not threaten termination because the path order is well-founded and closed under substitutions and contexts. Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases the order, using only the displayed syntactic rules.
