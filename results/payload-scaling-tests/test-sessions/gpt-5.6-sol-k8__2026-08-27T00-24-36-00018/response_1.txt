**Yes.** Under standard first-order term-rewriting semantics, strong normalization follows from the rules alone.

A lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules. Choose \(F \succ G\) and give \(F\) lexicographic status:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\) by the subterm property.
- For the second rule, \(S(n) \succ n\), so
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n).
  \]
  The left side also dominates every duplicated occurrence of \(y\). With \(F \succ G\), it therefore dominates the entire \(G(\ldots,F(x,y,n))\) right-hand side.

Because this ordering is well-founded and closed under substitutions and contexts, every rewrite step strictly decreases it. Duplication of \(y\) may increase term size, but cannot create an infinite reduction. No object-level assumptions about \(Z\), \(S\), or the terms substituted for variables are needed.
