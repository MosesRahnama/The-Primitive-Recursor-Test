## Reasoning

**Key observation: `G` is uninterpreted.** There are no rewrite rules for `G` at all. This means `G` behaves as a free constructor: a term rooted at `G` can only be reduced by reducing *inside* its arguments, never at the root. This is crucial, because it means we are free to choose *any* monotone interpretation for `G` — we are not constrained by any rule that forces a relationship between `G`'s interpretation and its arguments' interpretations.

**The only genuine recursion is in `F`.** The two rules are:

- `F(x,y,Z) -> x`
- `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y,y,y,y,F(x,y,n))`

This is structurally a primitive-recursive-style definition: the second rule strictly decreases the third argument (from `S(n)` to `n`), and the "step function" wraps the recursive call in `G` together with copies of `y`.

**A polynomial interpretation suffices.** Choose:
- `|Z| = 1`
- `|S(n)| = n + 2`
- `|F(x,y,z)| = x + y + z + 1`
- `|G(a,b,c,d,e)| = e + 1` (ignore `a,b,c,d`, i.e. coefficient 0 — this is a legitimate *weakly monotone* choice, valid precisely because `G` has no rules constraining it)

Check the rules:

- Rule 1: `|F(x,y,Z)| = x+y+2 > x = |x|`. ✔ (strict decrease)
- Rule 2: `|F(x,y,S(n))| = x+y+n+3`, and `|G(y,y,y,y,F(x,y,n))| = |F(x,y,n)|+1 = (x+y+n+1)+1 = x+y+n+2`. Since `x+y+n+3 > x+y+n+2`, this holds. ✔ (strict decrease)

Both rules yield strict decrease under a weakly monotone algebra over the naturals, which is a standard sufficient condition for termination of the rewrite relation (this is exactly the classical "polynomial interpretation method" for proving SN, applicable to any TRS without further assumptions).

**Why this matters generally:** The potential difficulty one might worry about is that `G` appears with *four* copies of `y`, which could blow up under an interpretation that treats all arguments uniformly (e.g., summing all arguments with unit coefficients gives `4y` on the right, breaking a naive decrease argument). But since `G` is free (rule-less), the interpretation of `G` is *unconstrained* — we may legitimately assign it a monotone algebra that discards the first four arguments. This resolves the apparent difficulty without needing any extra axiom; it's purely a modeling choice permitted because there is no rule for `G` to respect.
