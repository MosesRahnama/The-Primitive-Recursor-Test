Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the given rewrite rules alone.

Reason: the only recursive rule is

`F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, y, y, y, F(x, y, n))`

and the recursive call `F(x, y, n)` has third argument `n`, which is a proper subterm of `S(n)`. There are no rewrite rules for `G`, so `G` terms do not generate further reductions except inside their `F` subterm.

A standard recursive path ordering with precedence `F > G` proves termination:

- `F(x, y, Z) -> x` is decreasing by the subterm property.
- `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, y, y, y, F(x, y, n))` is decreasing because `F > G`, and the argument `F(x, y, n)` is smaller than the left-hand side since `S(n) > n`.

Thus no extra structural axioms or external assumptions are needed; the system is strongly normalizing.
