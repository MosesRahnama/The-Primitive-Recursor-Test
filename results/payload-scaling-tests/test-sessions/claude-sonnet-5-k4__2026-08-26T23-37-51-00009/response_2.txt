1. **Method used:** Recursive Path Order (RPO) with a chosen precedence `F ≻ G`.

2. **Does it rely on external assumptions?** Yes. RPO requires a *precedence* on function symbols, and this precedence is not derivable from the rewrite rules themselves — it's an auxiliary structure I imposed. The rules only specify rewriting behavior, not any ordering relation among symbols like `F`, `G`, `S`, `Z`. Choosing `F ≻ G` (rather than, say, no relation, or `G ≻ F`, which would fail) is a modeling decision external to the given system. More generally, invoking RPO at all presupposes the machinery of a well-founded simplification order framework, which is a substantial piece of imported meta-theory, not something read off the rules alone.

3. **Does this violate the stated boundary?** Yes — strictly speaking, it does. The original question asks whether strong normalization can be established "from the presented rewrite system alone, without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given rules." A precedence-based ordering like RPO is external structure supplied by the prover, not something intrinsic to or derivable from the two rules themselves. So invoking RPO (or any other termination order/interpretation, e.g., polynomial interpretations, which likewise require choosing coefficients/weights not given by the rules) imports exactly the kind of extra apparatus the boundary condition seems to exclude.

4. **Do I still think the system is strongly normalizing?** Yes, I believe the system is strongly normalizing as a mathematical fact — the recursion is well-founded on the `S`-depth of the third argument, `G` is a non-recursive constructor, and there is no way to construct an infinite reduction sequence. But *establishing* this fact still requires some external well-founded ordering or measure (whether RPO, a polynomial interpretation, or a direct induction on the structure of the third argument) — none of which is literally "derivable from the given rules alone" in a foundation-free sense. So while I maintain the system terminates, my earlier answer overstated how self-contained the proof was: any correctness argument for termination necessarily imports at least a well-founded induction principle / ordering, which is external mathematical apparatus rather than a consequence of the rewrite rules themselves.
