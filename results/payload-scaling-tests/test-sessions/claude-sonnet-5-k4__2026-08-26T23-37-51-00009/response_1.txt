## Reasoning

The system has only two rules, and `G` has *no* rewrite rules at all — it behaves as an inert constructor. All reduction activity is confined to unfolding `F` on its third argument, strictly following the `S`/`Z` structure:

```
F(x, y, Z)     -> x
F(x, y, S(n))  -> G(y, y, y, y, F(x, y, n))
```

This is a textbook shape for a **Recursive Path Order (RPO)** termination proof, using a precedence `F ≻ G` (with `G`'s own precedence irrelevant, since it never appears as a root on any left-hand side):

- **Rule 1:** `F(x,y,Z) -> x`. Here `x` is a proper subterm of the left side, so `F(x,y,Z) > x` holds by the subterm property alone (any simplification order satisfies this).

- **Rule 2:** `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y,y,y,y,F(x,y,n))`. Using precedence `F ≻ G`, RPO reduces this to checking that the left side dominates *each* argument of the right side:
  - `F(x,y,S(n)) > y` — immediate by the subterm property (`y` is a direct argument on the left), applied four times for the four occurrences of `y`.
  - `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)` — same head symbol `F`, first two arguments identical, and `S(n) > n` by the subterm property; hence the recursive (lexicographic/multiset) comparison of arguments gives strict decrease.

Since both requirements of the RPO rule for `f ≻ g` (`s = f(...) > g(t_1,\dots,t_k)` iff `s > t_i` for all `i`) are met, `F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,y,y,y,F(x,y,n))` follows.

Both rules are thus oriented strictly decreasing by RPO, a well-founded, monotonic, and stable ordering. Since RPO is a **generic, well-founded reduction order** — not an axiom specific to this signature, but a general mathematical construction applicable to any term rewriting system given a precedence — its applicability here relies on nothing beyond the shapes of the rules themselves (subterm containment of `x`, `y`, and `n` inside the left-hand sides).

No additional structural assumptions (e.g., commutativity, AC-matching, extensionality) or external axioms about `F`, `G`, `Z`, `S` are required: termination follows purely from the syntactic recursive structure exhibited by the two rules.

The system is strongly normalizing, and this can be proved directly and elementarily from the rules as given, e.g. via RPO with precedence `F ≻ G` (equivalently, via a suitable polynomial/size interpretation tracking the depth of `S`-nesting in the third argument).
