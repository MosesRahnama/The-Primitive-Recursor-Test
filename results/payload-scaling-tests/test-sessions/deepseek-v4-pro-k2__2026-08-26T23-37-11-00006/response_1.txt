Raw assessment: **Yes**, the system is strongly normalizing, and this can be established from the given rules alone.

Reasoning: The only recursive rule is

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,y,F(x,y,n)),
\]

which decreases the third argument from \(S(n)\) to \(n\). A simple termination proof is obtained by the polynomial interpretation

\[
[Z]=0,\quad [S(t)]=[t]+1,\quad [F(x,y,n)]=[x]+[n]+1,\quad [G(x,y,z)]=[z].
\]

Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z)\to x\) decreases: \([F(x,y,Z)] = [x]+1 > [x]\).
- \(F(x,y,S(n))\to G(y,y,F(x,y,n))\) decreases:
  \[
  [F(x,y,S(n))] = [x]+[n]+2 > [x]+[n]+1 = [G(y,y,F(x,y,n))].
  \]

Every rewrite step strictly decreases a natural-number measure, so no infinite reduction is possible. Thus strong normalization follows without importing extra structural axioms or assumptions.
