Yes. The presented system alone is strongly normalizing.

Reason: the only active symbol is `F`, and its second rule decreases the third argument structurally from `S(n)` to `n`:

`F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, y, F(x, y, n))`

Since `G` has no rewrite rules, `G`-terms are inert at the root; reductions can only occur inside their arguments. No rule creates a larger `S`-argument, so every `F`-reduction chain eventually reaches an argument that is either `Z`, a variable, or some non-`S` term.

This can be formalized, for example, by RPO with precedence `F > G`: the first rule is oriented by subterm, and the second by lexicographic decrease on the third argument `S(n) > n`. Thus all reduction sequences terminate.
