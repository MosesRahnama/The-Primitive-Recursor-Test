## Assessment: **Yes**, strong normalization can be established from the given rules alone.

### Reasoning

The system has two rules:

1. `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, y, y, y, F(x, y, n))`

**Key observations:**

- **Rule 1** eliminates an `F`-symbol entirely, replacing it with a subterm (`x`). This trivially reduces complexity.
- **Rule 2** replaces `F(x, y, S(n))` with a `G`-term containing a recursive call `F(x, y, n)`. Crucially, the third argument strictly decreases structurally: `S(n)` becomes `n` (a proper subterm). The first two arguments (`x`, `y`) are passed through unchanged.
- **`G` has no rewrite rules**, so `G`-rooted terms cannot reduce at the root; only their arguments might be reducible (and those subterms were already present).

**Termination proof via Recursive Path Ordering (RPO):**

Choose precedence `F > G` and lexicographic status for `F` (left-to-right):

- **Rule 1:** `F(x, y, Z) > x` holds because `x` is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
- **Rule 2:** `F(x, y, S(n)) > G(y, y, y, y, F(x, y, n))` holds because:
  - `F > G` in precedence, and
  - The LHS is greater than every argument of `G`:
    - `F(x, y, S(n)) > y` — subterm property.
    - `F(x, y, S(n)) > F(x, y, n)` — same head symbol `F`, arguments compared lexicographically: `x = x`, `y = y`, `S(n) > n` (subterm). ✓
  - The LHS is also greater than all sub-arguments of the inner `F(x,y,n)`: `x`, `y`, `n` are all subterms of the LHS. ✓

Since RPO is a well-founded reduction ordering and both rules are strictly oriented left-to-right, **the system is strongly normalizing**. No external axioms or structural assumptions are needed.
