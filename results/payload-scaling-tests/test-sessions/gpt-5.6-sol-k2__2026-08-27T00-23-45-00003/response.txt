**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the rewrite system alone.

A well-founded recursive/lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules, for example with precedence \(F \succ G\) and lexicographic comparison of \(F\)’s arguments:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\) by the subterm property.
- In the recursive rule, \(S(n) \succ n\), so
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n).
  \]
  The left side also dominates \(y\), and with \(F \succ G\), this yields
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \succ G(y,y,F(x,y,n)).
  \]

Although the rule duplicates \(y\), that can increase term size but does not invalidate the well-founded ordering. Hence every rewrite step strictly decreases a syntactic termination ordering, establishing strong normalization without additional axioms or assumptions.
