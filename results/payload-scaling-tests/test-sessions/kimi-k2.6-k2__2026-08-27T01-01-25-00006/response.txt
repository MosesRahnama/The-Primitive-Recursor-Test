**Assessment:** Yes. The system is strongly normalizing, and this can be established from the two rewrite rules alone.

**Reasoning:** The only redexes are occurrences of `F`. The first rule removes an `F` entirely. The second rule `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, y, F(x, y, n))` consumes the outermost `S` from the third argument and recurses on the strictly smaller term `n`. No rule introduces a new `S` around the third argument of any `F`, so along any chain of recursive `F`-steps the third argument loses one topmost `S` each time. Because every term is finite, any single occurrence of `F` can fire only finitely many times before its third argument is no longer headed by `S` (or `Z`), at which point it disappears. 

Although the second rule duplicates `y`, the duplicated copies are placed inside the inert symbol `G` and do not recreate the *same* redex with the same or a larger third argument. Consequently, one can assign a well-founded monotone weight that strictly decreases with every step—for example, interpreting `F(x,y,t)` as `(‖y‖+2)^{‖t‖+1}·(‖x‖+2)` (with `‖·‖` the obvious interpretations of `S`, `Z`, and `G`). This gives a reduction ordering derivable directly from the signature and rules, so no imported structural axioms or external inductive assumptions are needed.
