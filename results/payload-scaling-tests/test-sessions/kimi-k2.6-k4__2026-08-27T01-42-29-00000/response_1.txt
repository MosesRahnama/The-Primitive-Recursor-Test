**Assessment:** Yes—strong normalization can be established from the given rewrite rules alone, without invoking arithmetic induction or other external object-level axioms.

**Reasoning:**  
The only possible redexes are $F$-headed terms.  Consider the complexity of a term as the *multiset of the sizes of all its $F$-subterms*.  

- In the first rule, $F(x,y,Z) \to x$, the $F$ disappears, so that $F$-occurrence is removed from the multiset.  
- In the second rule, $F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,y,y,y,F(x,y,n))$, the reduced $F$ is replaced by:
  1. a new recursive call $F(x,y,n)$, which is strictly smaller than $F(x,y,S(n))$ (it lacks the outer $S$), and
  2. copies of any $F$’s that occur inside $y$.  Each such inner $F$ is a *proper subterm* of the redex $F(x,y,S(n))$, hence strictly smaller in size than the redex.

Thus every $F$-subterm introduced by a rewrite is smaller than the $F$ that was eliminated.  It follows that the multiset of $F$-sizes strictly decreases in the multiset extension of the natural order $>$, which is well-founded.  An infinite reduction sequence would give an infinite descending chain of such multisets, which is impossible.

This is a purely syntactic termination argument (for instance, the recursive path ordering with precedence $F > G$ and lexicographic status also orients both rules).  It relies only on the subterm structure inherent in the rules, not on any imported axioms for $Z$ and $S$.
