Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

A purely syntactic recursive path ordering / lexicographic path ordering proves termination. Choose a precedence with:

\[
F \;>\; G
\]

and use a status for \(F\) that compares its arguments so that:

\[
S(n) > n.
\]

Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) decreases because the right-hand side is a proper subterm/variable occurring as an argument of the left-hand side.
- For

  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,y,y,y,F(x,y,n)),
  \]

  the left side is greater than each \(y\), and it is greater than \(F(x,y,n)\) because its third argument \(S(n)\) is greater than \(n\). Since \(F > G\), the full left side is greater than the \(G\)-rooted right side.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded syntactic term ordering. No arithmetic interpretation of \(S\), induction principle for naturals, structural axiom, or external object-level assumption is required. The duplication of \(y\) in the second rule does not prevent this ordering argument.
