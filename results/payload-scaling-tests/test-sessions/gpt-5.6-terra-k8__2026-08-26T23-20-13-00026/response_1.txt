Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

The key point is that the only recursive-looking rule is

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,\ldots,y,F(x,y,n)).
\]

Although it duplicates \(y\), the recursive \(F\)-occurrence has a strictly smaller third argument: \(S(n)\) becomes \(n\). No rule rebuilds an \(S\) around that recursive argument or otherwise increases this descent.

A standard recursive path ordering / lexicographic path ordering can orient the rules, for example by giving \(F\) precedence over \(G\) and comparing the third argument of \(F\) so that \(S(n) > n\). Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) decreases by the subterm property.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,\ldots,y,F(x,y,n))\) decreases because the left side dominates each duplicated \(y\), and the remaining \(F\)-term is smaller due to \(S(n) > n\).

The duplication of \(y\) may increase term size, but it does not create an infinite reduction sequence. No additional structural or object-level axioms are needed.
