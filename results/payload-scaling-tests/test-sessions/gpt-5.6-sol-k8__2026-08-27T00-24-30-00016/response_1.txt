Yes. Strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone, assuming ordinary finite first-order terms.

The only recursive call is

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to \cdots F(x,y,n),
\]

where the third argument strictly decreases from \(S(n)\) to its proper subterm \(n\). This can be oriented by a standard simplification ordering such as lexicographic path ordering, choosing \(F \succ G\) and comparing the third argument of recursive \(F\)-calls. The first rule directly reduces to a subterm.

Although the second rule duplicates \(y\), this may increase derivation length but cannot create an infinite reduction: no new recursive call has a non-decreased third argument. No interpretation of \(Z\) and \(S\), induction axiom, or other object-level assumption is needed.
