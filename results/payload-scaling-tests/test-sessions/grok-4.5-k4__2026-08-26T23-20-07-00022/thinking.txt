The user is asking about a first-order term rewriting system and whether strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.
Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.

The key is that the second rule strictly decreases the "depth" of the third argument: every application of F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, y, y, y, F(x, y, n)) replaces a term whose 3rd argument is S(n) with a term whose 3rd argument is the proper subterm n. The first rule replaces F(x, y, Z) with x, which is a variable (hence a proper subterm). Because every reduction step either removes an F-redex entirely or replaces it with a strictly 3rd-argument-smaller F-redex, and because every term is finite, there cannot be an infinite reduction sequence. No other rules exist, so G and the constructors Z, S never introduce new redexes that could loop. This measure (lexicographic on the multiset of F-redexes ordered by 3rd-argument size) is derivable directly from the given rules and the standard well-foundedness of finite terms; no external structural axioms or object-level assumptions are required.
