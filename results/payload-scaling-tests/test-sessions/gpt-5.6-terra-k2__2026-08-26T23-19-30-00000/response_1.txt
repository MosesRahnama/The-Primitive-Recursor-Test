Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

A simple termination ordering suffices. For example, use a lexicographic path ordering with precedence

\[
F > G
\]

and lexicographic status for \(F\), comparing its third argument after the first two.

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) strictly decreases because the left-hand side contains \(x\) as a proper subterm.
- For

  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,y,F(x,y,n)),
  \]

  the recursive \(F\)-call is strictly smaller because \(S(n) > n\), while the first two arguments are unchanged. The outer \(G\) is also below \(F\) by the chosen precedence.

Duplication of \(y\) on the right does not prevent termination: no rule creates a larger recursive \(F\)-argument, and the sole recursive call replaces \(S(n)\) by \(n\).

Thus there is no infinite rewrite sequence derivable from these rules, without needing additional structural or object-level axioms.
