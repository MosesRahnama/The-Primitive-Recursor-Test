The user is asking about a first-order term rewriting system and whether strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.
Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.

The only redexes are the two F-rules. The first matches F(x,y,Z) and contracts directly to x (a variable). The second matches F(x,y,S(n)) and contracts to a G-term whose argument is F(x,y,n )—a strictly smaller instance of the third argument. Because Z and S are constructors (they do not appear on the left-hand side of any rule), every reduction path on F simply peels off one S-layer at a time until it reaches the base case F(…,Z) → x. No rule creates new F redexes outside this pattern, and G has no rules at all, so there are no other redexes to generate. The lexicographic measure (size of the third argument, measured by its S-nesting depth) therefore strictly decreases on every step, proving termination directly from the given rules without any additional structural or object-level axioms.
