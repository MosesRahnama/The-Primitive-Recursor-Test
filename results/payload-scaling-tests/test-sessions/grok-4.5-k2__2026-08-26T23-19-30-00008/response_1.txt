**Yes — SN holds and follows from the rules + the ambient definition of finite first-order terms alone.**

The signature is free and first-order (no binders). The only rules that fire are the two given for \(F\); \(G\) is inert.  

- The \(Z\)-rule erases an \(F\) redex outright.  
- The \(S\)-rule replaces a redex \(F(t,u,S(v))\) by a term whose only possible new \(F\)-redex is \(F(t,u,v)\), whose third argument is a proper subterm of the previous third argument.  

Every finite term has finite constructor depth in its third argument, so the measure  
\[
\bigl(\text{multiset of depths of third arguments of all }F\text{-occurrences},\;
\text{total number of }F\text{-symbols}\bigr)
\]  
(lexicographic, multiset extension of \(>\mathbb{N}\)) decreases at every step. Hence every reduction sequence is finite.  

No object-level equations, no induction principles beyond the inductive construction of the term algebra itself, and no extra axioms about \(Z\)/\(S\) are required; the argument uses only pattern matching against the two rules and the fact that terms are finite trees.
