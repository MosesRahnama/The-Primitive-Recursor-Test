### Reasoning

The system has two rules:

1. `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, y, F(x, y, n))`

A valid **polynomial interpretation** over the positive integers (≥1) proves termination:

| Symbol | Interpretation |
|--------|---------------|
| `Z` | `1` |
| `S(n)` | `n + 1` |
| `G(x,y,z)` | `x + y + z` |
| `F(x,y,n)` | `x + n² · (y + 1)` |

**Rule 1 check:**
- LHS: `x + 1² · (y + 1) = x + y + 1`
- RHS: `x`
- Difference: `y + 1 ≥ 2 > 0` ✓

**Rule 2 check:**
- LHS: `x + (n+1)² · (y + 1)`
- RHS: `2y + x + n² · (y + 1)`
- Difference: `(2n + 1)(y + 1) − 2y = 2ny + 2n − y + 1`
- Since `n ≥ 1, y ≥ 1`: `2ny ≥ 2y > y`, so the difference is strictly positive ✓

**Monotonicity:** All interpretations are strictly monotone in every argument (all partial derivatives are positive over the domain), ensuring compatibility with contexts and substitutions.

Since every rewrite step strictly decreases the polynomial interpretation and the positive integers are well-founded, **all rewrite sequences must terminate**. No external axioms or assumptions are needed—only the two given rules and standard termination proof techniques.
