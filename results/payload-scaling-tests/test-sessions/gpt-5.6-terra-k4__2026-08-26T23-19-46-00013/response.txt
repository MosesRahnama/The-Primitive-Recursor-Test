Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

A lexicographic path ordering, for example, suffices. Give `F` higher precedence than `G`, and use lexicographic comparison for `F`’s arguments. Then:

- `F(x, y, Z) -> x` decreases because the right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
- For  
  `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, y, y, y, F(x, y, n))`,
  the left-hand side is greater than each `y`, and it is greater than `F(x, y, n)` because the first two arguments agree while `S(n) > n`. Since `F > G`, the whole left-hand side is greater than the `G(...)` right-hand side.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded simplification ordering. Duplication of `y` does not prevent termination here, because the ordering still orients the second rule. No extra structural or object-level axioms are needed.
