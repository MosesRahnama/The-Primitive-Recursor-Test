Yes. Strong normalization can be established from these rewrite rules alone.

A standard recursive path ordering proves termination. Choose a precedence such as

\[
F \;>\; G,\quad F \;>\; S
\]

and give \(F\) lexicographic status. Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) decreases by the subterm property: the left-hand side strictly contains \(x\).
- For
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,y,y,y,y,y,y,y,F(x,y,n)),
  \]
  the left-hand side is greater than every argument of the right-hand-side \(G\):
  - it is greater than each occurrence of \(y\) by the subterm property;
  - it is greater than \(F(x,y,n)\), since the third argument \(S(n)\) is greater than \(n\);
  - and \(F\) has higher precedence than \(G\).

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded ordering. Duplication of \(y\) in the second rule does not create a loop, and the recursive \(F\)-call is on the strictly smaller third argument \(n\) rather than \(S(n)\).

Therefore the presented TRS is strongly normalizing without needing additional structural or object-level assumptions.
