**Yes.** Strong normalization can be established from the given rules alone.

**Reasoning:**  
The two left-hand sides, `F(x, y, Z)` and `F(x, y, S(n))`, do not overlap, so every redex is an instance of exactly one rule. Rule 2 replaces an `F` with third argument `S(n)` by a `G` containing a single new `F`-call whose third argument is the proper subterm `n`. Because there are no rules for `G`, the four copies of `y` placed inside the `G`-context are inert with respect to `F`-reductions—they cannot feed back into new outer `F`-redexes. Thus every sequence of reductions on an `F`-symbol strictly descends along the well-founded `S`-height of its third argument until it either reaches `Z` (and vanishes by rule 1) or becomes irreducible. This well-founded descent can be captured directly by a reduction ordering such as the recursive path ordering with precedence `F > G > S > Z` (lexicographic status on `F`), proving termination without any imported axioms. Hence the system is strongly normalizing.
