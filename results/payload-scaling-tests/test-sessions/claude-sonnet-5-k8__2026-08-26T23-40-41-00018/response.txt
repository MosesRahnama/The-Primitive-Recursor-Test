**Assessment: Yes — strong normalization is established straightforwardly from the rules alone.**

**Reasoning:**

The system has only two rules, both defining `F` by structural recursion on its third argument:

- `F(x,y,Z) -> x`
- `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y,y,y,y,y,y,y,y,F(x,y,n))`

Key observations:

1. **No rules for `G`.** The symbol `G` is a "dead" constructor as far as rewriting is concerned — once introduced, the subterms `y,...,y` and `F(x,y,n)` sitting inside it can still be rewritten (if `y` or the inner `F`-redex reduce), but `G` itself never triggers a rule. So `G` cannot cause any looping; it just packages results.

2. **Each application of the second rule strictly decreases the "S-depth" of the third argument of the head `F`-redex.** The only recursive occurrence of `F` on the right-hand side is `F(x,y,n)`, where `n` is a proper subterm of `S(n)` (one `S` fewer). All other occurrences of `x,y` introduced by the rule are *not* wrapped by fresh `F`-redexes on `n`'s original value — they're just copies of `y` (already present) passed into `G`, not fed into new `F` applications with a "larger" numeral.

3. Hence a simple well-founded measure works: take μ(t) = the number of `S` symbols along the third-argument position of every `F`-subterm occurring in `t` (or more simply, a recursive path order / polynomial interpretation with precedence `F > G` and `F` counted recursively only on its third argument). This is the standard schema for primitive recursion over a free constructor `Z/S`, which is well known to be captured by simplification orderings (RPO, or a polynomial/interpretation-based order assigning e.g. `[F](x,y,n) = n+1`, `[G](\ldots) = $ sum of arguments, `[Z]=0`, `[S](n)=n+1`), and both rules are compatible with such an order:
   - `F(x,y,Z) -> x`: LHS interpretation `1 > x`'s trivially, fine.
   - `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(...)`: the interpretation strictly decreases since the recursive call is on `n`, a proper subterm of `S(n)`.

4. Since the rewrite relation is compatible with such a well-founded monotone order (a *simplification order*/polynomial interpretation), and no other rules exist that could create infinite chains (no rule expands `Z`/`S` themselves, no rule for `G` that could resurrect a larger `n`), the system is terminating on all terms, including open terms with variables, by the same reasoning (variables are treated as immutable constants under the order).

Thus, strong normalization here does **not** require importing any external structural principle (like well-founded induction on a separately assumed numeral type, or meta-level assumptions about `S`/`Z` beyond what's given): the termination proof is derivable purely combinatorially from the two rules, via a standard reduction-order argument (RPO/polynomial interpretation), which is exactly the kind of argument these systems are designed to be checked against.

**Conclusion:** This TRS is trivially and self-containedly strongly normalizing — it is a direct instance of primitive recursion over the `Z/S` structure with an inert extra symbol `G`, and standard simplification-order techniques (derivable from the rules themselves) suffice to prove SN.
