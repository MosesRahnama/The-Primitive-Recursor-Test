Yes. The system is strongly normalizing.

Reason: the only recursive F-rule

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,y,F(x,y,n))
\]

makes the third argument decrease structurally from \(S(n)\) to \(n\). Since there are no rules for \(G\), \(G\) is effectively a constructor and does not create any further F-redexes beyond those already in its arguments.

A concrete termination proof can be given by the monotone polynomial interpretation:

- \(\lceil Z\rceil = 0\)
- \(\lceil S(n)\rceil = n+1\)
- \(\lceil G(a,b,c)\rceil = c\)
- \(\lceil F(x,y,n)\rceil = x+n+1\)

Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z)\): \(x+0+1 > x\)
- \(F(x,y,S(n))\): \(x+(n+1)+1 = x+n+2 > x+n+1 = \lceil G(y,y,F(x,y,n))\rceil\)

So both rules strictly decrease this interpretation. Hence all rewrite sequences terminate. No extra structural axioms or object-level assumptions are needed.
