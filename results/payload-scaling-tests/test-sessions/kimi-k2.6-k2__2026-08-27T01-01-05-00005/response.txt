The only defined symbol is `F`; `G`, `S`, and `Z` are inert (no rules apply to them).  
- Rule 1 removes an `F` entirely.  
- Rule 2 consumes an `F` whose third argument is `S(n)` and produces a new `F` whose third argument is the proper subterm `n`. Thus each application of rule 2 strictly decreases the size of the third argument of the `F` being reduced.

Although rule 2 duplicates the second argument `y`, it does **not** create any new `F`-redex with a third argument as large as the one just eliminated; the only new `F` introduced is `F(x,y,n)`, which carries a structurally smaller third argument. The duplicated subterms in `y` may themselves contain `F`s, but those `F`s were already present in the original term and each of them independently discharges its own (finite) recursive potential.

Consequently, there is no way to generate an infinite chain of `F`-reductions. In fact, one can give a direct monotone polynomial interpretation that decreases with every rewrite, e.g. (interpreting over the naturals):
- `[Z] = 0`, `[S](m) = m+1`,

- `[F](a,b,c) = (c+1)·(a + 2b + 1)`.

Under this interpretation both rules strictly decrease the value, which is a standard, purely syntactic/meta‑level argument that requires no imported structural axioms, induction principles, or other object‑level assumptions beyond the rewrite rules themselves. Hence strong normalization follows directly.
