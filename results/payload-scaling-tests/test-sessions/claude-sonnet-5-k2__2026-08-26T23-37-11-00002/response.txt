## Assessment: **Yes — strong normalization can be established from the rules alone.**

### Reasoning

The system consists of exactly two rules:

```
F(x, y, Z)    -> x
F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, y, F(x, y, n))
```

with `G` having **no** defining rules at all — it behaves purely as an inert/constructor symbol from the point of view of reduction.

**Key structural facts:**

1. **Strict decrease of the recursion argument.** In the second rule, the third argument shrinks from `S(n)` to `n`, i.e., a proper subterm. No rule ever reintroduces an `S` on a right-hand side, so the "S-depth" available for firing rule 2 can only decrease along a derivation (it is never regenerated).

2. **`G` is unproductive.** Since `G` has no rules, `G(y, y, F(x,y,n))` can only ever reduce *inside* its arguments — never at the root. This means the duplication of `y` in the right-hand side is harmless for termination: the duplicated copies of `y` are inert with respect to `F`'s recursion scheme (they don't introduce fresh redexes tied to the decreasing argument, and they are exactly matched by an occurrence of `y` already present on the left-hand side).

3. **This is precisely the shape handled by simplification orders (RPO/LPO).** Choose a precedence `F ≻ G` (with `S`, `Z` unrelated or minimal). Then:
   - Rule 1: `F(x,y,Z) ≻ x` holds immediately by the **subterm property**.
   - Rule 2: `F(x,y,S(n)) ≻ G(y,y,F(x,y,n))` holds because `F ≻ G` in precedence, and every argument of the right-hand side is bounded by the left-hand side:
     - `y ⊑ F(x,y,S(n))` (subterm),
     - `F(x,y,n) ≺ F(x,y,S(n))` (same head `F`, and the argument tuples compare via `n ⊏ S(n)`, all other arguments equal).
   
   This is the standard recursive-path-order pattern for primitive-recursive-style schemas with duplicated "context" arguments (`x`, `y`) and a genuinely decreasing recursion argument. Duplication of `y` does not defeat RPO, since RPO's well-foundedness argument does not depend on term size but on the well-founded precedence/subterm induction — duplicating an already dominated subterm changes nothing.

Since RPO/LPO is a **simplification order**: it is monotonic (closed under contexts) and stable (closed under substitutions), and well-founded on the given finite signature. Establishing `lhs ≻ rhs` for every rule under such an order is a *sufficient and self-contained* certificate of strong normalization — it requires no semantic assumptions about `F` or `G`, no imported axioms about naturals or induction principles beyond the ordinary well-foundedness of the path order itself (a purely combinatorial fact about the signature and precedence).

### Conclusion

The system is strongly normalizing, and this can be verified **directly from the two rules**, using a standard, self-contained termination technique (recursive path order with precedence `F ≻ G`). No external/object-level axioms beyond the syntactic rule set and standard well-founded-order machinery are needed.
