The user is asking about a first-order term rewriting system and whether strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.
Yes, strong normalization can be established from the given rules alone.

The key is that every application of the second rule strictly decreases the "depth" of the F-term in a way that is independent of any object-level structure. Specifically, the second rule replaces an F-term whose third argument is S(n) with a G 3-tuple whose third component is an F-term whose third argument is n. Because the only rules that can ever fire on an F-term are the two shown, and the first rule eliminates F entirely when the third argument is exactly Z, every reduction path must eventually reach a term whose outermost symbol is not F (either 0, G, or a variable). No rule can introduce new F-symbols whose third arguments are larger than those already present, and G itself has no rules, so it cannot create further F-terms. Consequently, the multiset of third-arguments of all F-symbols, ordered by the subterm relation, decreases at each step. This is a purely syntactic measure that can be read off the rewrite rules without invoking any external axioms about Z or S. Hence the system is strongly normalizing.
