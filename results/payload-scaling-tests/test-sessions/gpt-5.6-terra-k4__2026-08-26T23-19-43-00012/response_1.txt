Yes. Strong normalization can be established from these rewrite rules alone, under the ordinary first-order TRS interpretation over finite terms.

A termination ordering such as a lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules. Give `F` higher precedence than `G` (and treat `S(n)` as larger than `n`):

- `F(x, y, Z) -> x` decreases because `x` is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
- `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, y, y, y, F(x, y, n))` decreases because the recursive `F` call replaces the third argument `S(n)` with the smaller term `n`; the outer symbol also changes from the higher-precedence `F` to `G`. The copied occurrences of `y` are already subterms of the left-hand side.

The duplication of `y` does not itself create an infinite reduction: it can enlarge terms and duplicate reducible subterms, but the ordering still strictly decreases at every rewrite step. No additional structural or object-level axioms are needed.
