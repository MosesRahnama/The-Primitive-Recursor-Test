Yes. Strong normalization can be established from these two rewrite rules alone, for ordinary finite first-order terms.

A standard recursive path ordering / lexicographic path ordering suffices. Choose a precedence such as:

\[
F \succ G
\]

and give \(F\) lexicographic status. Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) decreases immediately by the subterm property.
- For
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,y,y,y,F(x,y,n)),
  \]
  the left side is greater than each occurrence of \(y\), since \(y\) is a subterm of it. It is also greater than \(F(x,y,n)\), because with lexicographic comparison the first two arguments agree and \(S(n)\) is greater than its proper subterm \(n\). Since \(F \succ G\), the left-hand side is therefore greater than the entire \(G(\ldots)\) right-hand side.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded term ordering. Duplication of \(y\) does not invalidate this: the duplicated terms are each already smaller than the redex, and \(G\) has no rewrite rule that can create a new increasing cycle.

So no structural or object-level axioms are needed; termination follows syntactically from the presented TRS.
