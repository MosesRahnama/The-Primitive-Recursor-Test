Yes, strong normalization can be established from the given rules alone.

The system is first-order with no variable binding, and the only redexes are the two F-rules. Every reduction sequence is driven exclusively by the second rule, which replaces F(x, y, S(n)) by G(y, y, y, 4, F(x, y, n)). The inner F has its third argument replaced by the strictly smaller term n (in the sense that any constructor term built from S and Z will eventually reach Z after finitely many steps). The first rule simply contracts F(x, y, Z) to x, which removes the red ex entirely.

Because G has no rules, the only way to continue reducing is to reduce the inner F. Each application of the second rule therefore strictly decreases the “depth” of the F-chain measured by the number of S-constructors in the third argument position. Once the third argument becomes Z, the first rule fires and the F disappears. No rule can increase the size of any subterm in a way that would allow an infinite loop, and there are no other redexes that could create cycles.

Standard termination techniques (e.g., recursive path order with precedence F > G > S > Z and lexicographic status) confirm that F(x, y, S(n)) > G(y, y, y, y, F(x, y, n)) and F(x, y, Z) > x, so the rewrite relation is strongly normalizing. All of this follows directly from the presented rules; no external structural axioms or object-level assumptions are required beyond the signature and the two given equations.
