**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone.

A recursive path ordering can orient both rules. Give \(F\) higher precedence than \(G\), and use a lexicographic status for \(F\) in which the third argument is compared after the identical first two arguments:

- \(F(x,y,Z) > x\) by the subterm property.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\), since \(S(n) > n\) by the subterm property.
- Therefore \(F(x,y,S(n))\) is greater than every argument of  
  \(G(y,y,y,y,F(x,y,n))\), and with \(F>G\), it is greater than the whole right-hand side.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded, substitution-stable simplification ordering. Duplication of \(y\) in the second rule does not invalidate this argument. Hence the system is strongly normalizing without additional axioms or assumptions.
