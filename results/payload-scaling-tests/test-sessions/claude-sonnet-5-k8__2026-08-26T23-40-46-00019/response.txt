## Assessment: **Yes — strong normalization is established purely from the rules given.**

### Reasoning

The system consists of two rules:

```
F(x, y, Z)     -> x
F(x, y, S(n))  -> G(y,y,y,y,y,y,y,y, F(x, y, n))
```

Note that **G has no defining rules at all** — it behaves as an uninterpreted (free) constructor. Hence the *only* way a reduction sequence can be infinite is through unbounded application of the two `F`-rules, and the only recursive occurrence of `F` on the right-hand side has its third argument strictly smaller (`n` instead of `S(n)`). The duplicated occurrences of `y` do not introduce new redexes involving `F`, since `G` cannot rewrite them into anything containing further `F`-applications; any reduction happening inside the copies of `y` is inherited from whatever reductions `y` itself admits, which is independent of and does not interact with the `F`/`G` rules.

This can be made fully rigorous with a standard **simplification ordering**, e.g. the Lexicographic/Recursive Path Order (LPO), using only the precedence `F ≻ G` (with `S`, `Z` placed anywhere):

- **Rule 1** (`F(x,y,Z) -> x`): trivial — the right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left, so it holds by the subterm property of any simplification order.

- **Rule 2** (`F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y,...,y,F(x,y,n))`):
  - Each `y` on the right is a direct subterm of the left, so `F(x,y,S(n)) ≻ y` by the subterm property.
  - `F(x,y,S(n)) ≻ F(x,y,n)` holds by the lexicographic comparison of arguments: the first two arguments are equal, and `S(n) ≻ n` (subterm property), while `F(x,y,S(n))` dominates `x`, `y`, `n` individually (again by the subterm property, transitively through `S(n)`).
  - Since `F ≻ G` in the precedence and the left-hand side dominates every argument of the right-hand side, LPO orients the rule: `F(x,y,S(n)) ≻ G(y,...,y,F(x,y,n))`.

Both rules are thus compatible with a simplification order (LPO), which is well-founded and monotonic/stable by construction. Compatibility with such an order is a standard sufficient condition for strong normalization of a finite first-order TRS, and this argument uses nothing beyond the syntactic shape of the rules themselves — no extraneous structural or semantic axioms are needed.

### Conclusion

The system is strongly normalizing, and this fact is derivable directly from the two rewrite rules via a simplification-order (LPO) argument with precedence `F > G`. No additional imported axioms are required.
