**Yes.** Under the standard semantics of finite first-order terms, the system is strongly normalizing.

A lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules. Choose \(F \succ G\) and give \(F\) lexicographic status:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\), since \(x\) is a proper subterm.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n)\), because \(S(n) \succ n\).
- Consequently,
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \succ G(y,y,F(x,y,n)),
  \]
  since the left side dominates both \(y\) and the recursive \(F\)-term.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded ordering stable under substitutions and contexts. Although the second rule duplicates \(y\), that does not invalidate the ordering argument. No additional object-level axioms are required.
