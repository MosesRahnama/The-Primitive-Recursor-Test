**Yes.** Under standard first-order rewriting on finite terms, strong normalization follows from the rules alone.

A lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules by choosing \(F \succ G\) and giving \(F\) lexicographic status:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\) by the subterm property.
- In the second rule,
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n)
  \]
  because the first two arguments agree and \(S(n) \succ n\) by the subterm property. The whole left side then exceeds the \(G\)-rooted right side since \(F \succ G\), while also exceeding every argument of that \(G\).

Although \(y\) is duplicated, the path ordering remains stable under substitutions and contexts, so this does not threaten termination. Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded simplification ordering, establishing strong normalization without additional object-level axioms.
