Yes. Under ordinary first-order rewriting over finite, acyclic terms, the displayed rules alone suffice to establish strong normalization.

A direct termination ordering works. For example, use a lexicographic path ordering with:

- \(F \succ G\),
- lexicographic argument comparison for \(F\),
- and the usual subterm property, so \(S(n) \succ n\).

Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\), since \(x\) is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n)\), because the first two arguments are unchanged and \(S(n) \succ n\).
- Consequently,
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \succ G(y,\ldots,y,F(x,y,n)),
  \]
  since \(F \succ G\), the left-hand side is greater than each copied \(y\) by the subterm property, and it is greater than the recursive \(F(x,y,n)\) as above.

The duplication of \(y\) by the second rule does not create an infinite reduction by itself: it may enlarge a term, but the recursive \(F\)-call strictly loses one outer \(S\), and the ordering accounts for the duplicated arguments.

Thus every rewrite step decreases a well-founded ordering, so the TRS is strongly normalizing. No additional object-level or structural axioms are needed.
