Let me think about whether this system is strongly normalizing (SN), meaning every reduction sequence starting from any term eventually terminates.

First, let me understand the structure:
- F takes three arguments
- G takes five arguments
- Z is a constant (zero-like)
- S is a unary function symbol (successor-like)

The first rule says: when the third argument of F is Z, it reduces to the first argument. This is a base case that eliminates F.

The second rule says: when the third argument of F is S(n), it reduces to G applied to four copies of y and a recursive call F(x, y, n).

Key observation: In rule 2, the third argument of F goes from S(n) to n. This means the third argument is structurally decreasing - we're peeling off one S at a time. Eventually, the third argument will be Z (if it's a ground term built from S and Z), and rule 1 will apply.
