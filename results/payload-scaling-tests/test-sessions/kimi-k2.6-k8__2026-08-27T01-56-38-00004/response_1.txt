**Reasoning:**  
The system is a structural recursion on the third argument of `F`. The second rule peels off one `S` constructor (`S(n) → n`) before recursing, and the first rule removes the `F` entirely when that argument reaches `Z`. No rule introduces a new `S` around the recursion variable, so any `F`-reduction chain along a given occurrence is bounded by the size of its original third argument.  

Although the second rule duplicates `y` eight times, those copies are *identical* to the original `y`; they do not create larger third arguments for any `F`. Consequently, every reduction sequence must be finite. This can be formalized directly by a syntactic reduction ordering—e.g., the lexicographic path ordering with precedence `F ≻ G` orients both rules strictly—without appealing to any imported type axioms, induction principles, or other object-level assumptions. Hence the TRS is strongly normalizing on its own.
