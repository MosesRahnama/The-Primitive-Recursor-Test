Yes. Strong normalization can be established for the ordinary finite first-order terms generated by this signature.

A simple proof uses a lexicographic path ordering with, for example, precedence

\[
F \;>\; G
\]

and lexicographic status for \(F\). Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z) > x\) by the subterm property.
- For the second rule,
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,y,F(x,y,n)).
  \]
  Since \(F>G\), it suffices that the left side exceed each argument of \(G\). It exceeds each \(y\) by the subterm property, and it exceeds \(F(x,y,n)\) because the first two arguments agree while
  \[
  S(n) > n.
  \]

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded ordering, so no infinite rewrite sequence exists.

The duplication of \(y\) in the second rule does not itself create nontermination: the recursive \(F\)-occurrence has a strictly smaller third argument, and the ordering accounts for the duplicated occurrences. No extra object-level axioms are needed.
