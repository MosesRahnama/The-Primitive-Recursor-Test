**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone for ordinary finite first-order terms.

A recursive path ordering can orient both rules. Choose a precedence with \(F \succ G\), and give \(F\) lexicographic status:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\) by the subterm property.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n)\), since the first two arguments agree and \(S(n) \succ n\) by the subterm property.
- Therefore \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ G(y,y,F(x,y,n))\): \(F \succ G\), and the left-hand side dominates each argument of the \(G\)-term.

Although \(y\) is duplicated, each copy remains smaller than the original redex under this ordering. Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded ordering, proving strong normalization without any additional axioms about \(Z\), \(S\), \(F\), or \(G\).
