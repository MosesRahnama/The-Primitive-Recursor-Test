The given TRS is strongly normalizing, and this can be read off from the two rules by themselves.

The signature is free and the rewrite relation is the standard one on finite first-order terms.  Both rules are strictly decreasing under the recursive path order generated by the precedence \(F>G\) (lexicographic status on the arguments of \(F\)).  Consequently every rewrite step decreases a well-founded measure that depends only on the shape of the terms and on the two given rules; no constructors need be assumed free, no induction principle on an object-level sort of numbers is required, and no equations outside the rules are used.

(The same conclusion is reached by a simple monotone interpretation into the naturals:
\[
[Z]=0,\quad[S](n)=n+1,\quad
[G](y_1,\dots,y_8,z)=\sum y_i+z,\quad
[F](x,y,n)=x+(y+1)\cdot10^n.
\]
Each rule again yields a strict decrease, confirming SN directly from the presented system.)

Thus strong normalisation follows from the rewrite system alone.
