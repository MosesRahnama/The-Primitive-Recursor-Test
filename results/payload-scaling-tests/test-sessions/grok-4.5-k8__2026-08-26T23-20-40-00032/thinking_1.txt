The user is asking about a first-order term rewriting system and whether strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.
Yes, strong normalization can be established from the given rules alone.

The second rule is a recursive definition of F on its third argument. Each application of that rule replaces an F(x, y, S(n)) redex with a term whose only F-redex is F(x, y, n). Because the third argument S(n) is strictly smaller than S(n) in the usual term order (Z is a constant, S is a unary constructor), every reduction path strictly decreases the size of the third argument of every F that appears. The first rule simply erases an F(x, y, Z) redex to x, which removes that redex entirely. No rule ever creates a new F whose third argument is larger than the one it replaced, and G has no rules, so once an F is replaced by G it disappears from the term. Consequently the multiset of the sizes of all third arguments of F-redexes is strictly decreasing, which is a well-founded measure. Hence every reduction sequence is finite. No additional structural axioms or object-level assumptions are required; the termination argument follows directly from the shape of the two rules and the fact that the third argument is a subterm.         The duplication of y does not affect the argument: any F-subterms inside y are copied, but they are already present in the original term and their own third arguments are independent of the outer counter; the outer counter still decreases, so the overall measure remains well-founded.    This is a standard first-order TRS that is strongly normalizing by the recursive path order (with F > G and the usual subterm property), again derived solely from the given rules.
