### Reasoning

Strong normalization can be proved using a **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)** with:
- **Precedence:** `F > G > S > Z`
- **Status:** left-to-right lexicographic comparison for `F`

**Rule 1:** `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
Trivially oriented: `x` is a proper subterm (variable) of the LHS, so `F(x, y, Z) >_{lpo} x` holds by the subterm property of LPO.

**Rule 2:** `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, y, F(x, y, n))`
Since `F > G` in the precedence, we need the LHS to be strictly greater than each immediate subterm of the RHS:
1. `F(x, y, S(n)) >_{lpo} y` — holds (y is a variable/subterm of LHS).
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) >_{lpo} y` — same.
3. `F(x, y, S(n)) >_{lpo} F(x, y, n)` — same root symbol `F`, so we compare arguments lexicographically:
   - `x = x`, `y = y`, and `S(n) >_{lpo} n` (since `n` is a proper subterm of `S(n)`).
   - The subterm conditions (`F(x,y,S(n)) >_{lpo} x, y, n`) all hold trivially.

Since LPO is a well-founded reduction ordering (closed under contexts and substitutions), every rewrite sequence must terminate. The duplication of `y` in Rule 2 poses no obstacle — LPO is a simplification ordering and handles variable duplication safely.

No structural axioms about `S`/`Z` (e.g., induction) are needed; they are treated as plain constructors, and the ordering argument works purely on term structure.
