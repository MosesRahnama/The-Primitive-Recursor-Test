### Reasoning

The system has two rules:
1. `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, y, y, y, y, y, y, y, F(x, y, n))`

We can prove termination (= strong normalization for TRSs) using the **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)** with the simple precedence **F > G**.

**Rule 1:** `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
- The LHS is strictly greater than `x` by the subterm property of LPO. ✓

**Rule 2:** `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, y, y, y, y, y, y, y, F(x, y, n))`
- Since `F > G` in the precedence, we must show the LHS is strictly greater than every argument of `G`:
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) > y` — holds by the subterm property. ✓
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) > F(x, y, n)` — the head symbols are equal (`F = F`), so LPO recurses lexicographically on the argument tuples `(x, y, S(n))` vs. `(x, y, n)`. The first two components are identical, and `S(n) > n` by the subterm property. Thus the lexicographic comparison succeeds. ✓
- Since the LHS dominates all arguments of `G` and `F > G`, the LHS is strictly greater than the entire RHS. ✓

Because LPO is a **reduction ordering** (well-founded, monotone under contexts, and stable under substitutions), orienting both rules strictly with LPO is sufficient to conclude that no infinite rewrite sequence exists from any term. No external axioms or structural assumptions are needed—only the rules themselves and the standard definition of LPO.
