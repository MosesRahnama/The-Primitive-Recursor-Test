**Yes.** Strong normalization follows syntactically from the rewrite rules alone.

A lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules by taking \(F \succ G\) and giving \(F\) lexicographic status:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\) by the subterm property.
- For the second rule, \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ G(\ldots,F(x,y,n))\), since:
  - \(F \succ G\);
  - the left side is greater than every repeated \(y\) by the subterm property; and
  - \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n)\), because the first two arguments agree and \(S(n)\succ n\).

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded simplification ordering. Duplication of \(y\) does not undermine this argument. Therefore the TRS is strongly normalizing without additional structural or object-level assumptions.
