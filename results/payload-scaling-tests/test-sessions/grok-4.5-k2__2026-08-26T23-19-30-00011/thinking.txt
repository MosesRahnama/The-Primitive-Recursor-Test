The user is asking about a first-order term rewriting system and whether strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.
Yes, strong normalization can be established from the given rules alone.

The key is that the only redexes are those involving F, and the second rule strictly decreases the "size" of the third argument in a way that is independent of the other arguments. Specifically, the recursion is on the third argument, which is 0 (Z) or S(n) (a successor). Any term can be viewed as having a third argument that is either a constant Z, a successor term S(t), or something else (in which case the F is already in normal form).

When the third argument is S(t), the reduction replaces the outer F with G(y,y,F(x,y,t)), so the new F inside has third argument t, which is a proper subterm of the original third argument. This gives a natural measure: the depth or size of the third argument decreases with each reduction step on that F. Since G has no rules, it cannot create new F redexes that would increase this measure, and there are no other rules that could loop or grow terms indefinitely.

Standard termination techniques like recursive path order (with F > G and the third argument as the recursive position) or a simple lexicographic interpretation on the third argument confirm that every reduction sequence terminates. No additional 0-S axioms, induction principles, or object-level assumptions are needed—the well-foundedness follows directly from the subterm relation in the term algebra, which is part of the TRS definition itself.
