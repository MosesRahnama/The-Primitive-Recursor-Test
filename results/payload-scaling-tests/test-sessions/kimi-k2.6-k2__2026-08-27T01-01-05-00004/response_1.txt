**Yes.** Strong normalization can be established from the given rules alone.

**Reasoning:**  
The only redexes are `F`‐terms. Rule 1 deletes an `F`; rule 2 replaces `F(x,y,S(n))` with `G(y,y,F(x,y,n))`, stripping one `S` from the third argument. Because no right‐hand side contains an `S`, the number of `S` constructors in the third argument of any given `F` strictly decreases each time rule 2 fires, and rule 1 fires only when that argument is `Z`. Thus any chain of head reductions on a given `F` is finite.

The duplication of `y` into the inert `G` context does not endanger termination: `G` has no rules, and the recursive call `F(x,y,n)` has the same `x` and `y` but a strictly smaller third argument. A global decreasing measure exists (for example, the polynomial interpretation `[Z]=1`, `[S(t)]=[t]+1`, `[G(a,b,c)]=[a]+[b]+[c]`, and `[F(x,y,n)]=(2[y]+1)[n]+[x]+1`), which strictly orients both rules by elementary arithmetic. Since a well‐ founded decrease is derivable syntactically from the signature and rules, no imported induction axioms or external structural assumptions are needed. Every reduction sequence is therefore finite.
