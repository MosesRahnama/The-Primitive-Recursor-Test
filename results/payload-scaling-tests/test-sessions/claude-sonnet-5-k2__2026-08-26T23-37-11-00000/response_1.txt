**Yes** — strong normalization of this system follows directly from the rules alone, using a standard, elementary termination technique (e.g. a recursive path ordering, RPO). No external axioms or imported assumptions are needed.

### Reasoning

The signature consists of `F` (defined, arity 3), `G` (arity 3, **no rules at all**), and the constructors `Z`, `S`. Key observations:

1. **`G` is not defined.** There is no rewrite rule with `G` as the root symbol. Hence `G`-headed terms can only be reduced by reducing their arguments — `G` itself never creates or "hides" new top-level redexes beyond what is already present in its arguments.

2. **Rule 1** (`F(x,y,Z) -> x`) is trivially decreasing (right side is a proper subterm of the left side).

3. **Rule 2** (`F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y,y,F(x,y,n))`) replaces `F` applied to `S(n)` by a term whose only "new" `F`-redex is `F(x,y,n)`, i.e. the same function applied to the **immediate subterm** `n` of `S(n)`. No rule ever increases the `S`-depth of the third argument; it can only stay the same (via copying `y`, which contains no `F`-redex acting on this position) or strictly decrease along any `F`-chain.

4. Even though `y` is **duplicated** in `G(y,y,F(x,y,n))`, duplication alone does not endanger termination here: nothing in the rules allows a duplicated subterm to grow, or to feed back into producing a *larger* `S`-nested argument for a later `F`-redex. Termination arguments (unlike confluence/complexity bounds) are not defeated by duplication as long as no measure increases — only branching in the reduction tree occurs, not growth of any single measure.

5. Concretely, this is captured by a straightforward **RPO argument**: take precedence `F > G` (with `S`, `Z` incomparable/minimal), and lexicographic status on `F`'s arguments. Then
 - `F(x,y,Z) > x` by the subterm property, and
 - `F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,y,F(x,y,n))` because `F > G` and each argument of the right side is dominated by the left: `y`, `y` are proper subterms of the left side, and `F(x,y,n) < F(x,y,S(n))` since they share head `F` and differ only in the last argument where `n < S(n)` by the subterm property.

This is a completely syntactic, structural argument based solely on the shapes of the two rules — it requires no semantic interpretation, no imported axioms about naturals, and no assumptions beyond the term structure given.

### Conclusion

The system is strongly normalizing, and this can be established **entirely from the presented rules**, e.g. via a simple RPO with precedence `F > G`. No additional structural or object-level axioms are needed.
