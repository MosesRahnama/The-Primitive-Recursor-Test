Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the given rules alone.

A direct polynomial interpretation over the natural numbers suffices. For example, interpret:

- \(Z = 0\)
- \(S(n) = n+1\)
- \(G(a_1,\ldots,a_9) = a_1+\cdots+a_9\)
- \(F(x,y,n) = (n+1)(x+8y+2)\)

Then:

1. \(F(x,y,Z) = x+8y+2 > x\).

2. Writing \(A=x+8y+2\),
   \[
   F(x,y,S(n))=(n+2)A,
   \]
   while the right-hand side has value
   \[
   8y + F(x,y,n)=8y+(n+1)A.
   \]
   Their difference is
   \[
   A-8y=x+2>0.
   \]

Thus every rewrite strictly decreases a natural-number-valued, context-monotone interpretation. Since the natural numbers are well-founded under \(>\), there can be no infinite rewrite sequence.

The duplication of \(y\) in the second rule does not prevent termination: it is explicitly accounted for by the \(8y\) term in the interpretation.
