**Assessment:** **Yes** — strong normalization can be established directly from the two rewrite rules, without importing any external object‑level axioms.

**Reasoning:**  
The only symbol with rewrite rules is `F`, and the rules describe a structural recursion on the third argument:

- `F(x, y, Z) → x` is the base case.  
- `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y,…,y, F(x, y, n))` peels off one `S` constructor and recurses on the strictly smaller subterm `n`.

Because terms are finite trees, any starting term contains only finitely many nested `S` constructors. Each application of the second rule consumes one `S` from the third argument of the `F` being reduced; the new recursive call `F(x, y, n)` therefore has a shorter `S`‑prefix. The symbol `G` is a free constructor (it has no rules), so it merely wraps the recursive call and does not create new reducible `F`‑redexes. Although the second rule duplicates the second argument `y`, duplication only produces finitely many copies of subterms already present in the initial term, and each copied `F` can be reduced only as many times as the finite `S`‑depth of its own third argument.

Consequently every reduction sequence is finite. In fact, the system is easily oriented by a standard lexicographic path order (with precedence `F ≻ G`), which is a well‑founded simplification order and requires no additional structural or object‑level assumptions beyond the term algebra itself. Hence the system is strongly normalizing.
