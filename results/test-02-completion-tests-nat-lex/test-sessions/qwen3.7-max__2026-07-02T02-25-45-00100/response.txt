### Explanation of the strategies used:
1. **`R_int_delta` & `R_eq_refl`**: The target term is `void`, which has `kappa = 0` and `mu = 0`. Since the source term's `kappa` could also evaluate to `0`, we split the proof using `by_cases` on whether the source's `kappa` is `0`. If it is, `kappa` ties and we use `drop_right` (showing strict decrease in `mu`). If it isn't, `kappa` strictly decreases to `0`, and we use `drop_left`.
2. **`R_rec_zero`**: The target is `b`. Its `kappa` is bounded above by the source's `kappa` (`max (kappa b) (kappa s)`). We again use `by_cases` to check if they are equal. If they are not, the inequality must be strict, allowing `drop_left`. Otherwise, `mu` strictly decreases, allowing `drop_right`.
3. **`R_rec_succ`**: The recursion unwraps a `delta`, which strictly decreases the `kappa` measure by exactly `1` (due to the `+ 1` in the `kappa` definition for `delta`-wrapped third arguments). The `max` simplifies cleanly because `kappa s` is bounded by the larger max expression. Thus, `kappa` strictly decreases, and we just use `drop_left`.
4. **`R_eq_diff`**: Both sides have identical `kappa` measures (`max (kappa a) (kappa b)`), but the `mu` measure strictly drops from `4 + mu a + mu b` to `3 + mu a + mu b`. We simply use `drop_right`.
