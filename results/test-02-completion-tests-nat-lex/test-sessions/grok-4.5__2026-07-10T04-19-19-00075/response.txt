### Proof skeleton for the five remaining cases

The three already-proved cases all follow the same pattern: establish a strict drop in `mu` together with a tie in `kappa`, then finish by `drop_right`. The remaining rules need a mixture of `drop_left` (when `kappa` falls) and `drop_right` (when `kappa` ties and `mu` falls). Four of the five rules admit a complete proof of that form; the fifth does not, because the supplied measure is not strictly decreasing on every instance of `R_rec_succ`.

```lean
-- integrate (delta t) --> void
-- kappa may fall (0 < κ t) or tie (both 0); μ always falls (0 < 1+μ t)
| _, _, Step.R_int_delta t => by
  by_cases h : kappa t = 0
  · -- kappa ties at 0
    apply drop_right
    · simp [mu]          -- 0 < 1 + mu t
    · simp [kappa, h]
  · -- kappa drops
    apply drop_left
    simp [kappa]
    exact Nat.pos_of_ne_zero h

-- recDelta b s void --> b
-- κ b ≤ max(κ b, κ s) always; μ always falls by at least 3
| _, _, Step.R_rec_zero b s => by
  have hmu : mu b < mu (recDelta b s void) := by
    simp [mu]; omega
  by_cases h : kappa b = kappa (recDelta b s void)
  · exact drop_right hmu h
  · apply drop_left
    -- κ b ≤ κ(source) is immediate from the definition of kappa;
    -- the disequality supplied by h therefore yields a strict inequality
    simp [kappa] at h ⊢
    exact Nat.lt_of_le_of_ne (le_trans (Nat.le_max_left _ _) (Nat.le_max_left _ _)) h

-- recDelta b s (delta n) --> app s (recDelta b s n)
-- The given measure does *not* decrease on every instance.
-- When n itself begins with delta the two sides have identical kappa
-- while mu strictly *increases*, so neither drop_left nor drop_right applies.
| _, _, Step.R_rec_succ b s n => by
  cases n with
  | delta m =>
    -- κ ties, μ increases  (counter-example to measure_decreases)
    -- concrete witness: b = s = void, m = void
    sorry
  | void | integrate _ | merge _ _ | app _ _ | recDelta _ _ _ | eqW _ _ =>
    -- residual recDelta does not start with delta, therefore
    -- κ(source) = max(max κb κs, κn)+1  >  max(κs, max(max κb κs, κn)) = κ(target)
    apply drop_left
    simp [kappa]
    -- the inequality is pure arithmetic on max; omega or
    -- Nat.max_le_iff / Nat.lt_succ_self closes it
    omega

-- eqW a a --> void
-- identical shape to R_int_delta
| _, _, Step.R_eq_refl a => by
  by_cases h : kappa a = 0
  · apply drop_right
    · simp [mu]; omega   -- 0 < 4 + 2·μ a
    · simp [kappa, h]
  · apply drop_left
    simp [kappa]
    exact Nat.pos_of_ne_zero h

-- eqW a b --> integrate (merge a b)
-- kappa ties for every a,b; mu falls by exactly 1
| _, _, Step.R_eq_diff a b => by
  have hmu : mu (integrate (merge a b)) < mu (eqW a b) := by
    simp [mu]; omega
  have hk : kappa (integrate (merge a b)) = kappa (eqW a b) := by
    simp [kappa]
  exact drop_right hmu hk
```

### Remark on the failing case

The pair `(kappa, mu)` is therefore **not** a valid termination measure for the whole relation `Step`. A correct measure must either
* treat the third argument of `recDelta` with a multiset / recursive-path order, or
* give `recDelta` a weight that decreases even after the residual redex has been buried under an `app` constructor.

(The absence of congruence rules does not rescue the measure: already the single root step `R_rec_succ` can increase it.)
