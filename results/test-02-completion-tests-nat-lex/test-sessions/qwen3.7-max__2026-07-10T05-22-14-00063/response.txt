### Brief Explanation of the Cases:
1. **`R_int_delta`**: `kappa` ignores `integrate` and `delta`, so it evaluates to `kappa t`. If `kappa t = 0`, `kappa` ties and `mu` drops by `1 + mu t`. If `kappa t > 0`, `kappa` strictly drops to `0`.
2. **`R_rec_zero`**: The third argument is `void` (kappa 0), so `kappa` evaluates to `max (kappa b) (kappa s)`. This is `≥ kappa b`. If it's strictly greater, `kappa` drops. If it ties, `mu` strictly drops because the `recDelta` wrapper and `s` add structural weight.
3. **`R_rec_succ`**: Unwrapping the `delta` in the third argument of `recDelta` strictly drops the `+ 1` recursion depth penalty in `kappa`. The new `app` and inner `recDelta` have a combined `kappa` that absorbs `kappa s` without exceeding the original max, guaranteeing a strict `kappa` drop.
4. **`R_eq_refl`**: Similar to `R_int_delta`, `kappa` evaluates to `kappa a`. It either ties at `0` (where `mu` drops by `4 + 2 * mu a`) or strictly drops to `0`.
5. **`R_eq_diff`**: `kappa` evaluates to `max (kappa a) (kappa b)` on both sides, resulting in a perfect tie. However, `mu` drops strictly because the `eqW` constructor (weight 4) is replaced by `integrate` (weight 1) and `merge` (weight 2), totaling a weight of 3.
