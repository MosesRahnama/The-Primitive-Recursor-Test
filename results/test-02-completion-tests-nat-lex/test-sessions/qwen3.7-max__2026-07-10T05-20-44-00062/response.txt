The proofs rely on case splits for the `kappa` measure (using `by_cases` and `omega`) to determine whether the recursion depth strictly decreases or ties, and then apply the appropriate lexicographic drop lemma (`drop_left` or `drop_right`).

-- integrate (delta t) --> void
| _, _, Step.R_int_delta t => by

    · have hmu : mu void < mu (integrate (delta t)) := by simp [mu]; omega
      have hk  : kappa void = kappa (integrate (delta t)) := by simp [kappa]; omega
      exact drop_right hmu hk
    · have hk : kappa void < kappa (integrate (delta t)) := by simp [kappa]; omega

    have hk : kappa (app s (recDelta b s n)) < kappa (recDelta b s (delta n)) := by

| _, _, Step.R_eq_refl a => by
    by_cases h : kappa a = 0

### Explanation of the strategies:
1. **`R_int_delta` & `R_eq_refl`**: The target is `void`, which has a `kappa` of `0`. If the source term's `kappa` is also `0`, the `kappa` measure ties and we show a strict decrease in `mu`. If the source `kappa` is `> 0`, it strictly decreases, so we use `drop_left`.
2. **`R_rec_zero`**: The target is `b`. The `kappa` of the source is `max (kappa b) (kappa s)`. If `kappa s > kappa b`, the `kappa` strictly decreases. Otherwise, it ties, and we fall back to the strict decrease in `mu` (since `mu` drops by `3 + mu s`).
3. **`R_rec_succ`**: Unfolding the definitions reveals that the `+ 1` from the `delta`-wrapped third argument in the source's `kappa` strictly dominates the `kappa` of the target (where the `+ 1` is absent and the outer `app` just takes a `max`). Thus, `kappa` always strictly decreases.
4. **`R_eq_diff`**: The `kappa` measure is completely preserved because both `eqW` and `merge` take the `max` of their arguments, and `integrate`/`delta` are transparent to `kappa`. Since `kappa` ties, we easily prove a strict drop in `mu` (from `4 + ...` down to `3 + ...`).
