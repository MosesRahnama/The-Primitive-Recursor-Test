### Reasoning

**Structure of the rules:**

1. `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))`

**Key observations:**

1. **The third argument strictly decreases.** Rule 2 rewrites `F(x, y, S(n))` to `G(F(x, y, n))`. The third argument goes from `S(n)` to `n`, which is a strict decrease under the standard subterm ordering on the natural number constructor terms built from `Z` and `S`.

2. **Termination argument via a simple polynomial/subterm interpretation.** Assign to any term a measure based on the size (or depth) of the third argument of any `F` subterm. Each application of rule 2 strictly reduces this measure. Rule 1 eliminates the `F` entirely, replacing it with `x` (a variable/subterm of smaller or equal size). Rule 2 introduces a `G` wrapper but reduces the `F`-redex count / third-argument size.

3. **No circularity or looping.** `G` appears only on the right-hand side and has no rewrite rule of its own, so it is a constructor/irreducible symbol. There is no way to create new `F`-redexes from the right-hand sides — `x`, `n` are variables (subterms of the left-hand side), and `G(F(x,y,n))` contains exactly one `F` with a strictly smaller third argument.

4. **Lexicographic path order (LPO) or recursive path order (RPO)** with precedence `F > G` and the standard ordering on `S`/`Z` immediately orients both rules, confirming strong normalization.

**Conclusion:** The system is **strongly normalizing** purely on the basis of the given rules, with no additional structural axioms needed. A simple subterm/size measure on the third argument of `F` constitutes a termination proof entirely internal to the system.
