My previous answer used a recursive path order (RPO/LPO) with an assigned precedence `F > G`, combined with a size measure on the third argument of `F`.

Yes. The precedence `F > G`, the choice of lexicographic path order, and the polynomial/size interpretation are all *external impositions* — none of them are derivable from or fixed by the rewrite rules themselves. They are analyst choices brought in from outside the system.

**3. Does that place the method outside the stated boundary?**
Yes. The question asks whether strong normalization can be established *from the presented rewrite system alone*. The RPO/interpretation approach imports a proof framework and specific ordering choices not determined by the rules. It falls outside the stated boundary.

**4. Is the system still strongly normalizing?**
Yes, I still believe it is — the third argument of `F` is syntactically a strict subterm on each application of rule 2, and `G` has no rules, so no term can grow unboundedly or cycle. The normalization property is real.

**But the direct answer to the original question is: No** — strong normalization *cannot be established from the rewrite rules alone* without importing external proof machinery (orderings, interpretations, induction principles).
