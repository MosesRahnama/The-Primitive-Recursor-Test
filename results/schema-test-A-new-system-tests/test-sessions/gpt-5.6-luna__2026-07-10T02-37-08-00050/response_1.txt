Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

A simple polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb{N}\) is:

\[
\llbracket Z\rrbracket=0,\qquad
\llbracket S(t)\rrbracket=\llbracket t\rrbracket+1,
\]

\[
\llbracket F(a,b,c)\rrbracket=a+b+2c+1,\qquad
\llbracket G(t)\rrbracket=t+1.
\]

These interpretations are monotone, and each rule strictly decreases the measure:

- \(F(x,y,Z)\) has measure \(x+y+1>x\).
- \(F(x,y,S(n))\) has measure \(x+y+2n+3\), while \(G(F(x,y,n))\) has measure \(x+y+2n+2\).

Therefore every rewrite step decreases a natural-number measure, including rewriting within arbitrary contexts. Hence there are no infinite rewrite sequences, so the system is strongly normalizing. No additional structural axioms or object-level assumptions are needed.
