**Assessment:** Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the two rewrite rules alone.

**Reasoning:**  
No rule creates a new occurrence of the symbol `F`. Rule 1 removes an `F`; rule 2 preserves the existing `F` but replaces its third argument `S(n)` with the proper subterm `n`. Because a term is finite, each occurrence of `F` can be rewritten by rule 2 only as many times as the length of the initial `S`-prefix of its third argument, after which it either matches `Z` and is removed by rule 1 or becomes a non-redex. Hence every `F` occurrence can participate in only finitely many steps, and since the total number of `F`’s in a term never increases, no infinite reduction sequence is possible. This argument uses only the syntactic structure of the given rules and the finiteness of terms; it does not require imported object-level axioms or external induction principles.
