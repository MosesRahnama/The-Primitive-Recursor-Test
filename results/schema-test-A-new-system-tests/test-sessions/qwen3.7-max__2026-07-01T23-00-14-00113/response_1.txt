**Rule 1:** `F(x, y, Z) > x`
- Holds immediately by the subterm property: `x` is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.

**Rule 2:** `F(x, y, S(n)) > G(F(x, y, n))`
- Since `F > G` in the precedence, RPO requires the LHS to be strictly greater than every argument of `G`. The sole argument of `G` is `F(x, y, n)`.
- We must show `F(x, y, S(n)) > F(x, y, n)`. Since the head symbols are equal (`F = F`), RPO compares arguments lexicographically: `(x, y, S(n))` vs `(x, y, n)`. The first two components are identical, and `S(n) > n` by the subterm property. So the lexicographic comparison succeeds.
- We must also confirm `F(x, y, S(n))` is greater than each argument of the inner `F` (namely `x`, `y`, `n`). Each is a subterm of the LHS, so the subterm property applies.
