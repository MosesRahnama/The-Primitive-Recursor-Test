1. **Method used:** Recursive Path Ordering (RPO) with a chosen precedence (F > G) and lexicographic argument comparison.

2. **Does it rely on choices not fixed by the rules?** Yes. The precedence F > G and the lexicographic status are external proof-engineering choices, not determined by the rewrite rules themselves.

3. **Does that place the method outside the stated boundary?** Arguably yes, depending on how strictly one reads "from the presented rewrite system alone." The original boundary prohibits "structural axioms or object-level assumptions," which typically refers to semantic assumptions about the domain (e.g., induction on naturals, S-injectivity, Z ≠ S(n)). RPO's precedence is a meta-level proof artifact, not an object-level assumption about the system's semantics. However, if "from the rules alone" is read to forbid *any* externally chosen ordering or interpretation, then RPO falls outside that stricter boundary. Under that strict reading, no general termination technique would qualify, since all require constructing some external well-founded measure.

4. **Is the system strongly normalizing?** Yes. Rule 2 strictly decreases the S-depth of F's third argument while adding only a G wrapper; Rule 1 eliminates F when that argument is Z. Since all terms are finite, the S-depth is bounded, so every rewrite sequence terminates.
