Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

A simple termination ordering suffices. For example, use a recursive path ordering with:

- `F` higher precedence than `G`, and
- lexicographic comparison for the arguments of `F`.

Then:

- `F(x, y, Z) -> x` decreases because the right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
- `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))` decreases because:
  - `S(n)` is greater than its subterm `n`, so `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)`;
  - and since `F > G`, the left-hand side is also greater than `G(F(x,y,n))`.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded syntactic ordering. No structural axioms, intended interpretation of `Z` or `S`, or external object-level assumptions are needed.
