1. **Primary method**: Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with manually chosen precedence (`F > G`, `F > S`) and lexicographic status for `F`.  

2. **Reliance on external assumptions**: **Yes**. The method requires:  
   - An *arbitrary choice* of symbol precedence (rules do not specify ordering).  
   - An *arbitrary choice* of argument status (lexicographic vs. multiset).  
   These choices are proof-theoretic artifacts not derivable from the rewrite rules themselves.  

3. **Boundary violation**: **Yes**. The boundary explicitly forbids importing assumptions "beyond what is derivable from the given rules." Precedence/status choices are external proof scaffolding—not syntactic consequences of the rules—and thus fall outside the permitted scope.  

4. **Strong normalization status (independent of boundary)**: **Yes**. The system *is* strongly normalizing. Termination follows *intrinsically* from:  
   - Finiteness of terms (meta-theoretic foundation of TRS).  
   - Structural decomposition of the third argument in `F`: each Rule 2 step replaces `S(n)` with subterm `n` (strict subterm descent), and Rule 1 eliminates `F`.  
   - No rule reintroduces reducible `F`-forms with deeper numeral structure.  
   This requires *only* the well-foundedness of the subterm relation on finite terms—a core property of the term algebra itself, not an imported object-level axiom. The rules syntactically expose the descent (`S(n) → n`), making termination evident without external ordering choices.
