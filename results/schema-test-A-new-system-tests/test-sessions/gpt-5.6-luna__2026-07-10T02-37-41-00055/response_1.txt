Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

A suitable recursive path ordering can orient both rules. For example, take `F > G` and use a lexicographic argument status for `F`:

- `F(x,y,Z) -> x` decreases by the subterm property.
- `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(F(x,y,n))` decreases because the outer `F` is replaced by `G`, and the recursive occurrence `F(x,y,n)` has a strictly smaller third argument (`n` is a subterm of `S(n)`).

Since the ordering is well-founded, stable under substitutions, and compatible with rewriting, it proves termination of rewriting at every position in every term. Thus the system is strongly normalizing without assuming any structural or object-level axioms about `Z`, `S`, or the terms involved.
