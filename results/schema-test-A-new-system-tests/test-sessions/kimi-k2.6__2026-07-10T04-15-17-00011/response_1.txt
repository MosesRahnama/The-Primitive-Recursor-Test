**Assessment:** Yes. Strong normalization can be established directly from the two rewrite rules alone, without importing any object-level structural axioms (such as Peano induction or constructor axioms).

**Reasoning:** Every redex is an occurrence of `F`.  
- Rule 1 (`F(x, y, Z) → x`) removes that `F` entirely.  
- Rule 2 (`F(x, y, S(n)) → G(F(x, y, n))`) replaces an `F` whose third argument is `S(n)` by a new `F` whose third argument is the proper subterm `n` (wrapped in `G`).  

Consequently, for any `F`-subterm the number of leading `S`-symbols in its third argument strictly decreases whenever that subterm is rewritten, and no rule introduces new `F`-subterms or increases that count elsewhere. By taking the well-founded multiset ordering over the natural numbers that count these leading `S`’s for all `F`-subterms in a term, each rewrite step produces a strictly smaller multiset. Since the ordering is well-founded, infinite reduction sequences are impossible. This argument uses only the syntactic form of the given rules together with standard meta-level well-foundedness of the multiset ordering; no additional object-level assumptions about `Z`, `S`, or `G` are required.
