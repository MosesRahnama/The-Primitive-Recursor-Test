### Assessment
**Yes**, strong normalization (SN) of the given TRS can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

### Reasoning
The signature consists of the function symbols appearing in the rules (\(F/3\), \(G/1\), \(S/1\), \(Z/0\)) together with any number of free (uninterpreted) symbols; the rewrite relation is the usual compatible closure of the two rules under contexts and substitutions. No properties of the symbols beyond the way the two rules rewrite them are required.

Consider an arbitrary finite term \(t\) and any reduction sequence starting from \(t\). Define two elementary measures on terms:
- \(\#S(t)\): the total number of occurrences of the symbol \(S\) in \(t\),
- \(\operatorname{size}(t)\): the total number of function-symbol occurrences in \(t\).

Both measures are natural numbers. Direct inspection of the rules shows:
- The second rule (the “\(S\)-case”) replaces a subterm \(F(u,v,S(n))\) by \(G(F(u,v,n))\). Consequently \(\#S\) of the whole term decreases by exactly 1, while size stays constant.
- The first rule (the “\(Z\)-case”) replaces a subterm \(F(u,v,Z)\) by \(u\). Consequently \(\#S\) does not increase (it decreases by the number of \(S\)-symbols that happened to occur in the discarded argument \(v\)), while size strictly decreases (at least the two symbols \(F\) and \(Z\) disappear).

Therefore every reduction sequence contains only finitely many applications of the second rule (each drops the global \(\#S\)-measure). After the last such application only the first rule remains; each of its applications strictly decreases size, so only finitely many of them are possible as well. Hence the sequence is finite.

The argument uses nothing beyond the concrete syntactic effect of the two given rules on symbol counts, the fact that every term is finite, and the well-foundedness of the ordinary order on \(\mathbb{N}\) (a purely meta-level fact about measures, not an object-level axiom about the TRS). In particular it does not rely on:
- freeness/injectivity axioms for \(S\) and \(Z\),
- an induction principle or well-founded order on a sort of “natural numbers”,
- the assumption that every third argument is a numeral,
- or any other structural or equational theory imported from outside the rules.

(The same conclusion can be reached by observing that the rules are compatible with the lexicographic path order generated by the precedence \(F>G\), which is again a purely syntactic criterion.)

Thus SN follows from the presented rewrite system alone.
