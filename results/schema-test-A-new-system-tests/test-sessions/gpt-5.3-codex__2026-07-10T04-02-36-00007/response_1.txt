Yes — from these rules alone, strong normalization can be established.

Reason (briefly): the only recursive call is in

- `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))`

and it strictly decreases the third argument of `F` from `S(n)` to `n`.  
The base case

- `F(x, y, Z) -> x`

stops recursion.  
There are no rewrite rules for `G`, `S`, or elsewhere that could re-expand terms or create new redexes leading to cycles. So every rewrite sequence on a finite term must terminate after finitely many decreases of that third argument, with at most one extra step for the base case.

So the TRS is strongly normalizing (terminating) on all terms generated from the given signature.
