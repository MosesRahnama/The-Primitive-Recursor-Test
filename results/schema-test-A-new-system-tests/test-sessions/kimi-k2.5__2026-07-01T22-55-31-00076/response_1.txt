**Assessment: Yes – strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.**

**Reasoning**

The system is terminating because every rewrite step strictly decreases a well‑founded syntactic measure that is definable directly from the signature:

1. **Rule 1** (`F(x,y,Z) → x`) removes one occurrence of the symbol `F` (and discards the subterm `y`).  
2. **Rule 2** (`F(x,y,S(n)) → G(F(x,y,n))`) preserves the number of `F`‑symbols (the outer `F` is replaced by one `F` inside `G`) but removes exactly one occurrence of the symbol `S` from the term.

Consequently, the lexicographic pair  
\[
\mu(t)=\bigl(\#_F(t),\; \#_S(t)\bigr)
\]  
(where `#_F` counts `F`‑symbols and `#_S` counts `S`‑symbols) is strictly decreased by both rules:
- Rule 1 lowers `#_F`.
- Rule 2 leaves `#_F` unchanged and lowers `#_S`.

Since the order on ℕ × ℕ is well‑founded, no infinite reduction sequence exists. This argument uses only the structure of the given rules and the standard fact that term size is finite; it does **not** require importing Peano axioms, induction principles for the natural numbers, or any other object‑level assumptions about `Z` and `S` beyond their appearance in the left‑hand sides. The same conclusion can be reached with a polynomial interpretation (e.g. `[F](x,y,n)=x+n+1`, `[G](t)=t`, `[S](n)=n+1`, `[Z]=0`) or with a lexicographic path ordering, both of which are purely syntactic techniques.

Hence, strong normalization follows directly from the rewrite system itself.
