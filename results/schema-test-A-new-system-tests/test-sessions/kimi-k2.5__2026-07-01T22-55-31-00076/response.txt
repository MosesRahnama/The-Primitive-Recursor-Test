1. **Primary method:** A measure-based argument using a lexicographic ordering of symbol-counts (specifically, counting occurrences of `F` and `S`).

2. **Does it rely on external assumptions?** Yes. The method imports the natural numbers ℕ, their ordering, and the well-foundedness of that ordering to assign a decreasing “size” to terms. It also assumes that mapping terms to these numbers is a valid homomorphism.

3. **Is it outside the stated boundary?** Yes. The boundary forbids importing object‑level assumptions not fixed by the rules themselves; ℕ and its well‑foundedness are external mathematical structures not derivable from the two rewrite rules alone.

4. **Is the system still strongly normalizing?** Yes. Every reduction strictly decreases the number of `S` symbols in the third argument of the active `F`, and finite terms contain only finitely many such symbols, so no infinite sequence exists. (A proof compliant with the boundary would instead use the intrinsic subterm relation— noting that `n` is a proper subterm of `S(n)`—which is structural and does not import arithmetic.)
