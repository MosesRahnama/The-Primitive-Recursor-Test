Yes — under the standard reading of a first-order TRS on finite terms, strong normalization follows from these rules alone.

Reason: there is a simple well-founded measure that strictly decreases on every rewrite. For a term \(t\), let

- \(s(t)\) = number of occurrences of \(S\) in \(t\)
- \(f(t)\) = number of occurrences of \(F\) in \(t\)

Order terms lexicographically by \((s(t), f(t))\) over \(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\).

Then:

1. **\(F(x,y,Z) \to x\)**  
   - \(s\) does not increase: all \(S\)'s in \(x\) were already present, and \(y\) is discarded.
   - If \(s\) stays the same, \(f\) strictly drops because the outer \(F\) disappears.

2. **\(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(F(x,y,n))\)**  
   - \(s\) strictly drops by 1 (the outer \(S\) on the third argument is removed).
   - \(f\) stays the same.

So every rewrite step strictly decreases \((s,f)\), which is well-founded. Hence there cannot be an infinite reduction sequence.

So my assessment is: **yes, strong normalization is provable from the presented rewrite system alone, with no extra axioms needed.**
