**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone for ordinary finite first-order terms.

A simple polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\) is:

\[
Z=0,\qquad S(n)=n+1,\qquad G(t)=t,\qquad F(x,y,n)=x+y+n+1.
\]

Then both rules strictly decrease:

- \(F(x,y,Z)=x+y+1>x\).
- \(F(x,y,S(n))=x+y+n+2> x+y+n+1=G(F(x,y,n))\).

These interpretations are monotone in every argument, so every rewrite step, including one inside a context, strictly decreases a natural-number measure. Hence no infinite rewrite sequence exists; the system is strongly normalizing without additional structural or object-level axioms.
