Yes — **strong normalization can be established from these rules alone**.

Reason (brief): every rewrite step on `F(x,y,S(n))` strictly decreases the third argument of `F` from `S(n)` to `n` (inside the recursive call `F(x,y,n)`), and the other rule `F(x,y,Z) -> x` is a base case that stops recursion on that branch.  
`G(...)` is just a wrapper with no rewrite rules, so it cannot create new redexes or re-expand terms.

So any reduction sequence can only apply the `F`-rules finitely many times (bounded by the number of `S` constructors in that third argument), hence no infinite rewrite sequence exists.
