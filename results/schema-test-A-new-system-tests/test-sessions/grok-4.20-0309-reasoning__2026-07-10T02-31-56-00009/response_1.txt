The TRS is strongly normalizing. The only redexes are outermost F-applications, which match purely syntactically on the third argument:

* If it is exactly Z, the rule eliminates the F entirely (producing x, whose size is strictly smaller).
* If it is exactly S(n), the rule produces G(F(…, n)) where n is a proper subterm of the original third argument.

Because every term is a finite tree, strict descent on the subterm relation for this argument is well-founded; no infinite chain of successive applications of the second rule is possible. Reductions inside proper subterms (x, y, or the third argument itself) are handled by a standard outer induction on term size: any inner redex is smaller, so by the induction hypothesis it cannot generate an infinite sequence. No other rules exist, constructors do not overlap, and the system introduces no cycles or size-increasing rewrites. These facts are direct consequences of the given rules plus the universal syntactic properties of first-order terms; no equations, freeness axioms, or well-foundedness assumptions on Z/S beyond syntactic matching are required.
