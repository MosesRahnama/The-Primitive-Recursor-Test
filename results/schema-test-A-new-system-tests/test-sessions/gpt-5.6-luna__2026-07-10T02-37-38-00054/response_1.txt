Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

A simple polynomial interpretation over the natural numbers is:

- \(Z = 0\)
- \(S(n) = n+1\)
- \(G(t) = t\)
- \(F(x,y,n) = x+n+1\)

Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z)\) interprets as \(x+1 > x\)
- \(F(x,y,S(n))\) interprets as \(x+n+2 > x+n+1\), which is the interpretation of \(G(F(x,y,n))\)

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded natural-number measure. The system is therefore terminating, hence strongly normalizing, without requiring injectivity, disjointness, or other object-level axioms about \(Z\), \(S\), \(F\), or \(G\).
