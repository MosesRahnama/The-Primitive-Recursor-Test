Yes, strong normalization can be established from the given rewrite system alone.

**Reasoning:**  
A lexicographic termination measure suffices. Define the measure of a term as the pair  
\(( \#F, \#S_{\text{third}} )\), where \(\#F\) is the total number of occurrences of the symbol \(F\), and \(\#S_{\text{third}}\) is the total number of \(S\) symbols that appear in the third argument of any \(F\) in the term.

- For the rule \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\):  
  The left side contains exactly one more \(F\) than the right side (since the right side is a subterm of the left side). Thus the first component strictly decreases; the second component may stay the same or change but the pair decreases lexicographically.

- For the rule \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(F(x,y,n))\):  
  The number of \(F\) symbols remains unchanged (one on each side). However, the third argument of the outer \(F\) changes from \(S(n)\) to \(n\), reducing the number of \(S\)'s in that position by exactly one. All other \(F\)'s inside \(x,y,n\) are unchanged, so the second component strictly decreases while the first stays constant. Hence the pair decreases lexicographically.

Every reduction therefore strictly lowers the measure, and since the measure is a finite pair of natural numbers, infinite reductions are impossible. No external axioms or structural properties beyond the rules are needed.
