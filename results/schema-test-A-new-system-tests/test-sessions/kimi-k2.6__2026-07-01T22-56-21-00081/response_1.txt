**Assessment:** Yes. Strong normalization (SN) can be established for this system directly from the given rewrite rules, without importing any external object-level axioms (such as Peano induction) or structural assumptions beyond the standard finiteness of terms.

**Reasoning:**  
The only rewrite rules are the two `F`-rules; `G`, `S`, and `Z` are inert constructors with no associated reductions. Consider any `F`-redex `F(t₁, t₂, t₃)`:

1. If `t₃ = Z`, rule 1 applies and the redex is replaced by `t₁`, eliminating that `F` entirely.
2. If `t₃ = S(u)`, rule 2 applies and the redex is replaced by `G(F(t₁, t₂, u))`. The new `F`-redex has third argument `u`, which is a *proper subterm* of `t₃`.

Thus every `F`-reduction either removes an `F`-occurrence or replaces the third argument of an `F` with a proper subterm. Because terms are finite trees, the number of nested `S` symbols along any branch is finite. A standard well-founded measure—e.g., the multiset of the sizes (or `S`-depths) of the third arguments of all `F`-subterms—strictly decreases with every rewrite step. Since `G` creates no new redexes and no other rules exist, no infinite reduction sequence is possible. SN follows by this purely syntactic, rule-internal argument.
