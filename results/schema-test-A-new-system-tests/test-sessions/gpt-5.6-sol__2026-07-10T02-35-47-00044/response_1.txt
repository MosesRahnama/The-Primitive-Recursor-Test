**Yes.** Assuming the standard setting of finite first-order terms, strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone.

A lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules by choosing \(F \succ G\) and giving \(F\) lexicographic status:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\) by the subterm property.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ G(F(x,y,n))\), because \(F \succ G\), and
  \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n)\) since \(S(n) \succ n\) by the subterm property.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded simplification ordering. No structural or object-level axioms beyond the ordinary metatheory of finite first-order rewriting are needed.
