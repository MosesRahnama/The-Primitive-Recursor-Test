The given system is a pure first-order TRS over a free algebra (function symbols \(F/3\), \(G/1\), \(S/1\), \(Z/0\)). The only redexes are occurrences of \(F\). Define the measure of a term \(t\) to be the sum, over every occurrence of an \(F\)-rooted subterm \(F(u,v,w)\) of \(t\), of the size of its third argument \(w\).

- Root reduction by the first rule removes a summand of size \(1\) (and possibly further positive summands coming from the unused second argument), so the measure strictly decreases.
- Root reduction by the second rule replaces a summand of size \(1+\operatorname{size}(n)\) by a summand of size \(\operatorname{size}(n)\), so the measure decreases by exactly \(1\).
- A non-root reduction occurs inside some argument of a surrounding context. In every case the measure of the contracted redex itself decreases; when the redex lies inside a third argument the size of that argument either stays the same (second rule) or decreases (first rule), therefore the contribution of every outer \(F\) is non-increasing. Consequently the global measure still decreases.

The measure is a natural number. Every rewrite step therefore produces a strictly smaller natural number, so every reduction sequence is finite. The argument uses only the two given rules and the fact that terms of a free first-order algebra are finite; no constructors axioms, no induction principles on an external sort of numerals, and no other object-level hypotheses are required.
