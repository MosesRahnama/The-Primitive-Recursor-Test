Yes. Strong normalization can be established from these rewrite rules alone.

A standard recursive path ordering suffices. Give `F` higher precedence than `G` and use a lexicographic status for `F`’s arguments (in particular, its third argument). Then:

- `F(x, y, Z) -> x` decreases by the subterm property: the left-hand side contains `x` as a proper subterm.
- `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))` decreases because `S(n) > n`, hence  
  `F(x, y, S(n)) > F(x, y, n)`, and since `F > G`, the whole left-hand side is greater than `G(F(x, y, n))`.

Thus every rewrite step decreases a well-founded term ordering. No induction principle for naturals, equations for `S`, or other object-level assumptions are needed: `S`, `G`, and (assuming the undeclared `Z` is a constant symbol) `Z` are just function/constant symbols in the rewrite syntax.
