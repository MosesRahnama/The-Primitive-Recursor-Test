Yes.

This rewrite system is strongly normalizing from the rules alone.

Reasoning, briefly:
- There are only two rules:
  - `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))`
- The only recursive call is in the second rule, where `F(x, y, S(n))` rewrites to `G(F(x, y, n))`.
- In that step, the third argument of `F` strictly decreases from `S(n)` to `n`.
- No rule rewrites `G(...)`, `Z`, `S(...)`, variables, or arbitrary terms to something larger or back into `F(..., S(...))`.
- So every reduction sequence starting from an `F` term can only peel off finitely many `S` constructors until it reaches `Z`, then the first rule applies and recursion stops.

A simple well-founded measure is: the number of `S` constructors in the third argument of each reducible `F` occurrence. Each rewrite strictly decreases that measure, and nothing can recreate a larger one.

So: strong normalization is provable directly from the given rewrite rules, with no extra axioms needed.
