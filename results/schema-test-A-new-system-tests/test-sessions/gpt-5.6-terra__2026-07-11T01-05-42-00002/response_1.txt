Yes. Strong normalization can be established from these rewrite rules alone, for all finite first-order terms over the displayed signature.

A standard syntactic termination ordering suffices. For example, use a lexicographic path ordering with precedence

\[
F \succ G
\]

and lexicographic argument comparison for \(F\). Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) decreases because the right-hand side is a proper subterm/variable occurrence of the left-hand side.
- For
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(F(x,y,n)),
  \]
  the left-hand side dominates the inner \(F(x,y,n)\), since its third argument \(S(n)\) is strictly larger than \(n\). It also dominates the surrounding \(G(\cdot)\) because \(F \succ G\).

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases in a well-founded ordering. Intuitively, the second rule can only strip one outer \(S\) from the third argument of the relevant \(F\); it may add a \(G\), but cannot recreate the removed \(S\) or produce an infinite rewrite cycle. The first rule simply erases the \(F\)-term.

No structural or object-level axioms are needed beyond the ordinary syntactic interpretation of these as first-order rewrite rules.
