**Assessment:** Yes. Strong normalization can be established directly from the rewrite rules, without importing any external structural axioms.

**Reasoning:** Define a simple numeric measure on terms:  
\(M(t) = (\text{number of }F\text{-symbols in }t) + (\text{number of }S\text{-symbols in }t)\).

- **Rule 1:** \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) removes the outer \(F\) and discards \(y\) and \(Z\). Thus the count of \(F\) drops by at least one, and the count of \(S\) cannot increase (any \(S\)’s in \(y\) are deleted). Hence \(M\) strictly decreases.
- **Rule 2:** \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(F(x,y,n))\) replaces the outer \(F\) by a single \(F\) inside \(G\) (so the total number of \(F\)’s is unchanged) but removes one \(S\) (the root \(S\) of the third argument). Hence \(M\) strictly decreases by exactly one.

Since every rewrite step lowers a non-negative integer measure, no infinite reduction sequence is possible. This is a purely syntactic, rule-derived argument; it does not rely on any object-level assumptions about \(Z\) and \(S\) beyond their occurrences in the given rules.
