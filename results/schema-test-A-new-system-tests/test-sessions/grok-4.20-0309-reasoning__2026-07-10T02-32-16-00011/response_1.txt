The TRS has only two rules, both acting on F at the root when its third argument matches Z or S(n). Every rule application removes the top-level constructor (Z or S) of that third argument:
- The Z-rule eliminates the enclosing F entirely, substituting its first argument (strictly decreasing the number of F symbols and the overall term size).
- The S-rule replaces the third argument S(n) by n (strictly decreasing the size of that particular third argument while preserving overall term size and the total number of F symbols).

No rule ever increases term size, the number of F symbols, or the size of any third argument of an F. Reductions inside arguments (including inside a third argument or inside the unused second argument y) are therefore reductions on strictly smaller subterms under the same measure. Because every term is finite, the multiset of third-argument sizes over all F subterms (ordered by the usual > on natural numbers) strictly decreases under multiset extension at every rewrite step. This ordering is well-founded, so every reduction sequence terminates. The argument uses only the form of the given rules, the inductive structure of finite first-order terms, and the induced rewrite relation; no congruences, no induction principles for Z/S, and no assumptions about canonical forms are required.
