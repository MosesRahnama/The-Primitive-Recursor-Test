Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

A suitable recursive path ordering can orient both rules. Give `F` higher precedence than `G`, and use a lexicographic status for `F` that compares its third argument. Then:

- `F(x,y,Z) -> x` decreases by the subterm property.
- `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(F(x,y,n))` decreases because the recursive call has the structurally smaller third argument `n`, and the outer `F` is also above `G` in the precedence.

Thus every rewrite step decreases a well-founded ordering, including reductions inside arbitrary contexts and substitutions. No structural axioms or object-level assumptions about `S` or `Z` are required.
