**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone.

A lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules, taking \(F > G\):

- \(F(x,y,Z) > x\), since \(x\) is a proper subterm.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\), because the first two arguments are unchanged and \(S(n) > n\) by the subterm property.
- Hence \(F(x,y,S(n)) > G(F(x,y,n))\) when \(F > G\).

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded syntactic ordering. No assumption that \(n\) denotes a natural number, nor any additional structural or object-level axioms, is needed.
