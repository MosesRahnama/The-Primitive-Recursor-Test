**Yes.** Strong normalization follows directly from the rewrite rules.

Use a natural-number interpretation:

- \(\llbracket Z\rrbracket=0\)
- \(\llbracket S(t)\rrbracket=\llbracket t\rrbracket+1\)
- \(\llbracket G(t)\rrbracket=\llbracket t\rrbracket\)
- \(\llbracket F(a,b,c)\rrbracket=\llbracket a\rrbracket+\llbracket b\rrbracket+\llbracket c\rrbracket+1\)

Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z)\) has value \(x+y+1>x\).
- \(F(x,y,S(n))\) has value \(x+y+n+2\), while \(G(F(x,y,n))\) has value \(x+y+n+1\).

All interpretations are strictly monotone in their arguments, so every rewrite step, including one inside a context, strictly decreases the natural-number measure. Therefore no infinite rewrite sequence exists. No additional structural or object-level axioms are required.
