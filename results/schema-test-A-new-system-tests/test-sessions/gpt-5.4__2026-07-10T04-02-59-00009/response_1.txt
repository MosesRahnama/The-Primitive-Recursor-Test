Yes.

This TRS is strongly normalizing from the rules alone.

Reason, briefly:
- The only recursive step is
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))`
- This strictly decreases the third argument of `F` from `S(n)` to `n`.
- The other rule
  - `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
  is the base case and ends the recursion.
- There are no rewrite rules for `G`, `S`, or `Z`, so once recursion on the third argument reaches `Z`, no new redexes are created except whatever may already be inside the substituted variables.
- Since terms are first-order and finite, any chain of nested `S` constructors in an actual term is finite, so only finitely many such decreases can occur.

A simple measure is: count the total number of `S` constructors appearing as the third argument of every `F`-redex in the term. Each rewrite step decreases this measure, so infinite rewriting is impossible.

So: strong normalization is derivable directly from the given rewrite system, with no extra axioms needed.
