Yes — assuming ordinary first-order rewriting over finite terms, strong normalization is provable from these two rules alone.

Brief reason: use a simple decreasing measure. Let

\[
\mu(t)=\sum_{\text{subterms }F(a,b,c)\text{ of }t} (1+|c|)
\]

where \(|c|\) is the syntactic size of the third argument.

Then each rewrite step strictly lowers \(\mu\):

- `F(x, y, Z) -> x`  
  removes one outer `F` entirely. Any `F`-subterms inside `x` were already present before, so the measure drops by at least \(1+|Z|\).

- `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))`  
  keeps the same recursive call but with third argument `n` instead of `S(n)`, so one contribution changes from \(1+|S(n)|\) to \(1+|n|\), decreasing by 1.

No other symbol rewrites. So every rewrite strictly decreases a natural-number measure, hence there cannot be an infinite reduction sequence.

So the system is strongly normalizing from the rewrite rules themselves; no extra structural/object-level assumptions are needed.
