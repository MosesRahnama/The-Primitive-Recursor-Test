Assessment: Yes – strong normalization can be proved from the given two rules alone.

Reasoning (sketch)
1. Define a weight |t| on terms as the number of occurrences of the unary symbol S inside the third argument of every F-subterm:
   • |F(u,v,Z)| = 0  
   • |F(u,v,S(n))| = 1 + |F(u,v,n)| (extend homomorphically to other contexts).

2. Rule 1 does not increase this weight (it removes the whole F-term and therefore all S-occurrences that were in its third argument, giving strictly smaller weight).

3. Rule 2 replaces F(u,v,S(n)) by G(F(u,v,n)); the outermost S is deleted, so the weight drops by 1.

4. No rule ever introduces a new S, so |t| is a natural‐number–valued measure that strictly decreases with every rewrite step.

5. Hence no infinite rewrite sequence exists; all terms terminate, i.e. the system is strongly normalizing.

No extra structural axioms or external assumptions are required—termination follows directly from the rules given.
