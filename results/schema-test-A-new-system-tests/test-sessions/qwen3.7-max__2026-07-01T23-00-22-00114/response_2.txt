1. **Method used:** A semantic weight interpretation (a form of polynomial interpretation) mapping terms to positive integers, with specifically chosen weight assignments for each constructor.

2. **Does it rely on external choices?** Yes. The specific weight function (e.g., `Z → 1`, `S(t) → w(t) + 2`), the choice to use additive composition, and the reliance on well-foundedness of `>` on natural numbers are all proof apparatus chosen by me, not fixed or derivable from the two rewrite rules.

3. **Outside the stated boundary?** In a strict reading, yes. The interpretation is an externally imposed structure. The rules themselves say nothing about weights, numeric assignments, or any ordering. However, this is inherent to *all* termination proofs—some well-founded measure must be brought in from outside the rules. If the boundary forbids any external proof apparatus whatsoever, then no termination proof for any TRS could ever satisfy it. If "structural axioms or object-level assumptions" is read more narrowly (e.g., no assumed equations, no assumed properties of constructors like injectivity of `S`), then a proof technique is a meta-level tool, not an object-level assumption, and remains within bounds.

4. **Is the system strongly normalizing?** Yes. Regardless of boundary compliance, the system terminates. Every application of Rule 2 strips one `S` from the third argument of `F`, and Rule 1 eliminates `F` entirely when that argument is `Z`. Since terms are finite, this process must end.
