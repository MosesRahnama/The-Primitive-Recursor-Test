Yes. Strong normalization can be established directly from the rewrite rules, without assuming that `Z` and `S` form an inductive datatype or importing any object-level axioms.

For example, use a lexicographic recursive path ordering in which:

- `F` has lexicographic status on its arguments,
- `F > G` in the symbol precedence.

Then:

- `F(x,y,Z) -> x` decreases because `x` is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
- `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(F(x,y,n))` decreases because `S(n) > n`, so  
  `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)`, and the outer `F` term is also greater than the `G`-term under the precedence.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded ordering. Consequently, the system is strongly normalizing purely as a first-order rewrite system.
