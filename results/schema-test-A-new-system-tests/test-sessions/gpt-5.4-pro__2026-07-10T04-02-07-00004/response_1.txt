Yes — under the standard first-order TRS reading on finite terms, strong normalization can be established from these two rules alone.

Reasoning, briefly:

- The only defined symbol is `F`; `G`, `S`, and `Z` have no rewrite rules.
- The recursive rule
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))`
  strictly decreases the “amount of `S`” in the third argument of `F`.
- The base rule
  - `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
  removes that `F` completely.

A direct termination measure can be given by a natural-number interpretation such as:

- `|Z| = 0`
- `|S(t)| = |t| + 1`
- `|G(t)| = |t|`
- `|F(x,y,n)| = |x| + |y| + |n| + 1`

Then each rule strictly decreases:

- `|F(x,y,Z)| = |x| + |y| + 1 > |x|`
- `|F(x,y,S(n))| = |x| + |y| + |n| + 2 > |G(F(x,y,n))| = |x| + |y| + |n| + 1`

Since every rewrite step decreases a natural-number measure, infinite reduction sequences are impossible. So strong normalization follows from the presented rewrite system itself, with no extra structural axioms or object-level assumptions needed.
