**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone.

A polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\) suffices:

\[
\llbracket Z\rrbracket=0,\quad
\llbracket S(n)\rrbracket=n+1,\quad
\llbracket G(t)\rrbracket=t,\quad
\llbracket F(x,y,n)\rrbracket=x+y+n+1.
\]

Both rules strictly decrease this measure:

- \(\llbracket F(x,y,Z)\rrbracket=x+y+1>x\).
- \(\llbracket F(x,y,S(n))\rrbracket=x+y+n+2>x+y+n+1
  =\llbracket G(F(x,y,n))\rrbracket\).

All interpretations are monotone in their arguments, so rewriting inside any context also strictly decreases the overall natural-number measure. Hence no infinite rewrite sequence exists; no additional structural or object-level assumptions are needed.
