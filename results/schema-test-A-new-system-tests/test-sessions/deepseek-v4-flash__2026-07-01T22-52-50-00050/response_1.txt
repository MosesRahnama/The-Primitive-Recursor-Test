Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone. The only reduction rules are:

- `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
- `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))`

Here `Z` and `S` are constructors, and `G` is an undefined symbol (no rules for it). A redex occurs only when the third argument of `F` is either `Z` or `S(n)`. In the second rule, the third argument decreases from `S(n)` to `n`, which is a proper subterm. Hence the number of `S` symbols in the third argument strictly decreases with each reduction step. Since this measure is well-founded (natural numbers), every reduction sequence must terminate. No additional axioms or structural assumptions are needed; the argument uses only the syntactic form of the rules.
