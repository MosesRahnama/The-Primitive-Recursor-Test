From the rewrite rules alone, strong normalization cannot be established.

Reason:
- The system is
  - `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))`
- The only recursive call is `F(..., n)` inside `F(..., S(n))`, so for terms where the third argument is built from finitely many `S` over `Z`, rewriting does terminate after peeling off the `S` constructors until reaching `Z`.
- But in first-order rewriting, variables range over arbitrary terms, not just numerals unless that is separately imposed.
- There is no rule reducing `G(...)`, `S(...)`, or arbitrary third arguments, and no assumption that every term in the third argument position is generated from `Z` and `S`.

So from the TRS alone, terms like `F(a, b, t)` with `t` not matching `Z` or `S(n)` are simply normal forms, and the recursive rule itself does not create an infinite reduction chain. This strongly suggests termination, but to conclude strong normalization over all terms in the intended sense often relies on an interpretation of the third argument as an inductive datatype (natural numbers). That datatype discipline is not encoded as a rewrite rule here.

Raw assessment: **not provable purely from the presented rules if you mean strong normalization in the intended inductive/numeric sense; only a syntactic termination intuition is visible.** If treated as an ordinary first-order TRS over all terms, however, it does appear terminating because the second rule strictly decreases the `S`-depth of the third argument.
