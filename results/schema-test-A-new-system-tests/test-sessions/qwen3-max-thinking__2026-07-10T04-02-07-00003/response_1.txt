**Assessment:** Yes, strong normalization (termination) of this system can be rigorously established from the rewrite rules alone, without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond syntactic term structure.

**Reasoning:**  
A well-founded lexicographic measure suffices:  
  *μ(t) = ( #S(t),  size(t) )*  
where *#S(t)* counts syntactic occurrences of the symbol `S` in term *t*, and *size(t)* is the total node count (function symbols, constants, variables). Both components are purely syntactic and require no semantic assumptions (e.g., no need to assume `Z` ≠ `S(t)`, injectivity of `S`, or that terms represent natural numbers).

- **Rule 1:** `F(x, y, Z) → x`  
  - *#S*: Cannot increase (discards `y`; `x`’s `S`-count was already present). May stay equal or decrease.  
  - *size*: Strictly decreases by at least 3 (removes `F`, `Z`, and all nodes of `y`; `y` contributes ≥1 node).  
  → *μ* decreases lexicographically (if *#S* unchanged, *size* drops).

- **Rule 2:** `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(F(x, y, n))`  
  - *#S*: Decreases by exactly 1 (the outer `S` in `S(n)` is eliminated; `n`’s `S`-count preserved inside the new `F`).  
  - *size*: Preserved (both sides have identical node count: `F`+`x`+`y`+`S`+`n` vs. `G`+`F`+`x`+`y`+`n`).  
  → *μ* decreases lexicographically (first component drops).

Since every rewrite step strictly decreases *μ* in the well-founded order ℕ × ℕ (lex), **all rewrite sequences—on ground terms, open terms, or terms with arbitrary substructures—are finite**. Matching analysis (e.g., `F(x,y,x)` is a normal form because a variable cannot syntactically match `Z` or `S(·)`) is inherent to first-order TRS semantics and requires no external axioms. The proof relies solely on:  
1. Syntactic structure of terms/rules,  
2. Standard TRS matching (unification/matching is purely structural),  
3. Elementary arithmetic on symbol counts.  

No imported assumptions about constructors, data types, or equational theories are needed. The termination argument is self-contained within the presented system.
