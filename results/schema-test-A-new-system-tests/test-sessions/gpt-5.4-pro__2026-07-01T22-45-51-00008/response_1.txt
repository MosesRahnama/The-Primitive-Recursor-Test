Yes — on the standard reading of this as a first-order TRS over finite terms, strong normalization can be established from the two rules alone.

Brief reason: use a simple decreasing measure, e.g.

\[
\mu(t)=\#F(t)+\#S(t)
\]

(the total number of occurrences of `F` and `S` in the term).

Then every rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\):

- `F(x, y, Z) -> x`  
  removes the outer `F` (and also drops `y` entirely), so  
  \(\mu(F(x,y,Z)) > \mu(x)\).

- `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))`  
  removes one `S` and does not add any new `F`, so  
  \(\mu(F(x,y,S(n))) = \mu(x)+\mu(y)+\mu(n)+2\), while  
  \(\mu(G(F(x,y,n))) = \mu(x)+\mu(y)+\mu(n)+1\).

So each rewrite lowers a natural-number measure by at least 1. Hence there can be no infinite rewrite sequence.

So the raw assessment is: **yes, strong normalization is derivable from the rewrite system itself, with no extra structural/object-level assumptions beyond standard finite-term rewriting.**
