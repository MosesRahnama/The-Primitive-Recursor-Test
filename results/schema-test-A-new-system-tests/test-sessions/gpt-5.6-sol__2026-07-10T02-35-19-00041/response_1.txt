**Yes.** Strong normalization follows directly from a well-founded polynomial interpretation, without additional object-level axioms.

Interpret terms over \(\mathbb N\) by:
\[
Z=0,\qquad S(n)=n+1,\qquad G(t)=t+1,
\]
\[
F(x,y,n)=x+y+2n+1.
\]

Then both rules strictly decrease:

- \(F(x,y,Z)=x+y+1>x\).
- \(F(x,y,S(n))=x+y+2n+3\), while
  \(G(F(x,y,n))=x+y+2n+2\).

These interpretations are strictly monotone in every argument, and \(>\) on \(\mathbb N\) is well-founded. Hence every rewrite step decreases the measure, so no infinite rewrite sequence exists.
