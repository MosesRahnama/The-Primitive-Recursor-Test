1. **Primary method**: Lexicographic measure on syntactic symbol counts: $\mu(t) = (\#_S(t),\ \#_F(t))$, ordered lexicographically over $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$.

2. **Reliance on external assumptions?** Yes. The proof requires:  
   - The well-foundedness of $\mathbb{N}$ under $<$,  
   - The well-foundedness of the lexicographic order on $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$,  
   - The validity of symbol-counting as a mapping into this order.  
   None of these are derivable from the rewrite rules themselves. The rules specify only pattern-directed reductions; they contain no ordering, numerical interpretation, or meta-mathematical structure.

3. **Outside the stated boundary?** Yes. The boundary explicitly prohibits importing "structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given rules." The well-foundedness of $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$ is a structural axiom of arithmetic/meta-theory, not derivable from the syntactic rewrite rules. Thus, the method violates the constraint.

4. **Is the system strongly normalizing?** Yes. As a mathematical property of the TRS (with signature $\{F/3, G/1, S/1, Z/0\}$ and finite terms), every reduction sequence terminates: the third argument of any $F$-redex has finite $S$-nesting depth, which strictly decreases per applicable rule, and rule application removes $F$-nodes. This termination fact holds independently of proof methodology. However, *establishing* it formally requires importing a well-founded order axiom—consistent with standard TRS theory but outside the strict boundary posed.
