**Reasoning:**  
A lexicographic measure over term structure suffices:  
- Let $ \mu(t) = (\#_S(t),\ \#_F(t)) $, where $ \#_S $ counts occurrences of symbol `S`, and $ \#_F $ counts occurrences of symbol `F` in term $ t $.  
- Order pairs lexicographically over $ \mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N} $ (well-founded).

**Rule analysis:**  
1. **`F(x, y, Z) → x`**:  
   - Removes the outer `F`, the subterm `y`, and constant `Z`.  
   - $ \#_S $: Decreases by $ \#_S(y) \geq 0 $ (all `S` in `y` are discarded; `x`'s `S` were already counted pre-reduction).  
   - $ \#_F $: Decreases by $ 1 + \#_F(y) \geq 1 $ (outer `F` and all `F` in `y` removed; `x`'s `F` were already present).  
   - Thus $ \mu $ strictly decreases: if $ \#_S $ unchanged ($ \#_S(y)=0 $), $ \#_F $ drops by ≥1.

   - Replaces `S(n)` with `n` inside a new `F` wrapped by `G`.  
   - $ \#_S $: Decreases by exactly 1 (the top `S` is removed; `n`, `x`, `y` retain prior `S` counts).  
   - $ \#_F $: Unchanged (one `F` removed, one `F` introduced; `x`, `y`, `n` subterms unchanged).  
   - Thus $ \mu $ strictly decreases via the first component.
