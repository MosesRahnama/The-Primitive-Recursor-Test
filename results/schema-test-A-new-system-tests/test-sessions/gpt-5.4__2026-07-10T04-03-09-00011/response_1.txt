Yes — from the rewrite rules alone, strong normalization can be established.

Why:
- The system has only two rules:
  - `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))`
- The only recursive behavior is in the second rule, where `F(..., S(n))` rewrites to `G(F(..., n))`.
- This strictly decreases the third argument of `F` from `S(n)` to `n`.
- No rule rewrites `G(...)`, `S(...)`, `Z`, variables, or arbitrary terms back into something larger.
- So every rewrite sequence starting from an `F` term can only peel off finitely many `S` constructors until it reaches `Z`, after which the first rule applies and rewriting stops.

A simple measure is: count the number of `S` constructors in the third argument of each reducible `F`-term. Each rewrite step decreases that count by 1, and nothing increases it again.

So the TRS is strongly normalizing on its own; no extra axioms are needed.
