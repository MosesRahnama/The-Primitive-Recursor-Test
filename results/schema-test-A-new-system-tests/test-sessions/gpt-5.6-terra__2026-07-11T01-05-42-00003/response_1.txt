Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the two rules alone, under the ordinary first-order TRS reading where `Z`, `S`, `F`, and `G` are distinct function symbols and terms are finite.

A standard termination ordering suffices. For example, a lexicographic path ordering can use a precedence such as:

\[
F > G
\]

with the third argument of `F` compared lexicographically. Then:

- `F(x,y,Z) -> x` decreases by the subterm property.
- `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(F(x,y,n))` decreases because the recursive `F` call has third argument `n`, strictly smaller than `S(n)`, and the outer symbol changes from `F` to lower-precedence `G`.

Thus every rewrite step decreases a well-founded ordering, so no infinite rewrite sequence exists.

No structural equations, induction principles, or object-level assumptions are needed beyond the syntactic first-order rewrite rules themselves.
