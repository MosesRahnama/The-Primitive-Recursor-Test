**Assessment:** Yes, strong normalization can be established directly from the given rewrite rules.

**Reasoning:** The only symbol that creates redexes is `F`, and the two rules both reduce the “complexity” of `F`-occurrences in a well-founded way. Define a measure on terms, for example:
\[
\mu(t) \;=\; \sum_{\text{occurrences }F(u,v,w)\text{ in }t} |w|,
\]
where \(|w|\) is the number of symbols in \(w\).  
- **Rule 1** removes an `F` whose third argument is `Z`, so the summand \(|Z| = 1\) disappears and no new `F` is created; hence \(\mu\) strictly decreases.  
- **Rule 2** replaces `F(x, y, S(n))` with `G(F(x, y, n))`. The new `F` has third argument `n`, which is a proper subterm of `S(n)`, so \(|n| < |S(n)|\). The surrounding `G` introduces no new `F`; thus the corresponding summand decreases by at least one, and again \(\mu\) strictly decreases.

Since \(\mu(t)\) is a non-negative integer for every finite term and each rewrite step lowers it, no infinite reduction sequence is possible. This argument uses only the syntactic form of the two given rules and the standard subterm ordering; it does not require any imported induction principles, axioms about `S`/`Z` being natural-number constructors, or other object-level assumptions.
