### Reasoning
The given TRS generates a rewrite relation whose only root steps are the two displayed rules (plus the usual congruent closure under contexts and substitutions). Both rules are non-size-increasing, and the second rule is size-preserving; consequently a naïve induction on term size does not go through. Any successful termination argument must therefore supply an auxiliary well-founded order that decreases on every rewrite step.  

Typical such orders are:
- the lexicographic combination \((\#_S(t),|t|)\), where \(\#_S\) counts occurrences of the symbol \(S\),
- a linear polynomial interpretation over the naturals (e.g. \([Z]=0\), \([S](n)=n+1\), \([G](x)=x+1\), \([F](x,y,n)=x+y+2n+1\)),
- recursive path order with precedence \(F>G\).

Each of these orders rests on the well-foundedness of the natural numbers (or of the free term algebra under a suitable simplification ordering). That well-foundedness is a structural property of the ambient universe of finite first-order terms; it is not an equational consequence of the two rewrite rules themselves, nor can it be derived from them by pure equational or first-order reasoning. Importing it therefore constitutes an additional structural/object-level assumption lying outside the presented system.  

Hence strong normalisation, while true for the TRS, cannot be established from the rewrite rules alone.
