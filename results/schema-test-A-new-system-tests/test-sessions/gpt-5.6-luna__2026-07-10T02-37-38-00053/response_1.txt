Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

The recursive rule
\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(F(x,y,n))
\]
strictly decreases the third argument from \(S(n)\) to its subterm \(n\), while the other rule eliminates the \(F\)-redex entirely:
\[
F(x,y,Z)\to x.
\]
No rule creates a larger recursive \(F\)-term or duplicates a redex. A suitable well-founded term ordering—e.g. a recursive path ordering with \(F\) above \(G\) and the usual subterm ordering—can orient both rules strictly. Hence there are no infinite rewrite sequences, including for open terms, and strong normalization follows without adding structural axioms or object-level assumptions.
