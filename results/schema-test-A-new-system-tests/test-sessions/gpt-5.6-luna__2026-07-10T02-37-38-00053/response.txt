1. **Primary method:** A recursive path ordering (RPO), using a chosen precedence such as \(F > G\), together with subterm ordering.

2. **Dependence on extra choices:** Yes. The precedence and the well-founded ordering are meta-level proof choices; they are not specified by the rewrite rules.

3. **Boundary:** Under a strictly literal reading—allowing only facts fixed by the rules—that places the particular RPO argument outside the stated boundary. These are not object-level axioms, but they are external proof structure.

4. **Strong normalization itself:** Yes, I still think the system is strongly normalizing. Every recursive \(F\)-step changes the third argument from \(S(n)\) to its proper subterm \(n\), and the \(Z\)-case removes the \(F\)-redex. Thus an ordinary well-founded meta-level termination proof exists, though it necessarily supplies such a measure or ordering.
