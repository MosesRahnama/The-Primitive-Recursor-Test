**Assessment:** Yes, strong normalization can be established directly from the given rules.

**Reasoning:** Each rewrite step strictly decreases the total number of occurrences of the symbols `F` and `S` in the term:
- Rule `F(x, y, Z) -> x` removes one `F`.
- Rule `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))` removes one `S` and introduces no new `F` or `S`.

No rule creates additional `F` or `S` symbols. Hence the quantity  
`μ(t) = (# of F symbols in t) + (# of S symbols in t)`  
is a natural-number measure that drops by exactly one on every rewrite. Because the natural numbers are well-founded, no infinite reduction sequence is possible. This is a purely syntactic termination argument that does not require importing induction axioms, structural assumptions, or any external theory.
