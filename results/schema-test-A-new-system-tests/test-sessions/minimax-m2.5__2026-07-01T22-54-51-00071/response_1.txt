**Assessment:** Yes – the system is strongly normalising.

**Reasoning:**  
The two rules define a recursion on the third argument of \(F\): the term \(F(x,y,S(n))\) rewrites to \(G(F(x,y,n))\), reducing the “\(S\)‑depth’’ of that argument by one, and the base rule \(F(x,y,Z)\to x\) eliminates the \(F\)‑term altogether.  

Define a measure \(m(t)\) = total number of occurrences of the constructor \(S\) in the third argument of any subterm of the form \(F(\_, \_, -)\) in \(t\).  
- Rule \(F(x,y,S(n))\to G(F(x,y,n))\) strictly decreases \(m\) (the outer \(F\) loses one \(S\) while the inner \(F\) has one fewer \(S\)).  
- Rule \(F(x,y,Z)\to x\) also decreases \(m\) (the \(F\) disappears, so its contribution to \(m\) is removed).  

No rule increases \(m\). Since \(m\) is a natural number that strictly lowers each step, any rewrite sequence must be finite. Hence the rewrite system is strongly normalising without invoking any external axioms or additional assumptions beyond the given rules.
