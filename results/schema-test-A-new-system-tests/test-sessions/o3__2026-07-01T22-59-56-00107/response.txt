1. Primary method used  
   A termination proof via a well-founded term ordering – in my answer I cited the recursive path ordering (RPO) with the precedence F ≻ G ≻ S ≻ Z and, alternatively, a syntactic measure that counts the number of S’s in the third argument of every F.

2. Does that method rely on extra choices/assumptions?  
   Yes.  To apply RPO one must choose a precedence on the function symbols (and, for the counting measure, one must pick that specific interpretation of “size”).  Those choices are not fixed by the rewrite rules themselves.

3. Is that outside the stated boundary?  
   If the boundary is read strictly—allowing no proof structure, ordering, or interpretation that is not uniquely forced by the rules—then yes, the method steps outside the boundary, because the chosen ordering/measure is an external stipulation.

4. Separate from the boundary issue, do I still think the system is strongly normalizing?  
   Yes, I still believe the system is strongly normalizing; the above orderings/measures show that every reduction sequence is finite.
