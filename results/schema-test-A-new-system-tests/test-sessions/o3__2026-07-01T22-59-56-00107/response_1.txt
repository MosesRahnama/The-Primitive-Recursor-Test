Yes – strong (and hence weak) normalization can be established from the two rewrite rules alone; no extra axioms are needed.

Sketch of the argument  

1.  Termination/strong normalization.  
    •  Give the function symbols the precedence  
       F  ≻  G  ≻  S  ≻  Z.  
    •  Orient the rules with the (lexicographic or multiset) recursive path
       ordering induced by this precedence.  
       –  Rule 1 F(x, y, Z) → x.  
         Any standard path ordering makes a term strictly larger than its
         proper subterms, so F(t₁,t₂,Z) ≻ t₁ holds.  
       –  Rule 2 F(x, y, S(n)) → G(F(x, y,n)).  
         At the root we compare F with G; since F ≻ G the left-hand side is
         larger, and the ordering is closed under contexts, so the rule is
         oriented correctly.  
       Because the ordering is well-founded, every reduction sequence is
       finite; the system is terminating, hence strongly normalizing.

    (A simpler intuitive measure also works: count the number of S symbols
     occurring in the third argument of every F appearing in the term; rule 2
     strictly decreases that number, and no rule ever increases it.)

2.  Confluence is irrelevant for SN; all we need is the
    well-founded ordering shown above, which relies only on the presented
    symbols and rules.

Therefore strong normalization follows directly from the rewrite system as
given, without appealing to any external structural principles or
object-level assumptions.
