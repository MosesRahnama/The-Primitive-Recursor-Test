### Assessment: Yes

The given first-order TRS (signature containing at least the symbols \(F/3\), \(G/1\), \(S/1\), \(Z/0\), with the two rules shown) is strongly normalizing, and this can be established by a purely syntactic argument that refers only to the rules themselves together with the inductive definition of finite terms. No additional object-level axioms (e.g., an induction principle for an abstract sort of “natural numbers”, a freeness or exhaustiveness assumption “every term is \(Z\) or \(S(\ldots)\)”, injectivity of \(S\), etc.) are required.

### Reasoning
Consider the two measures on any term \(t\):
- \(\lvert t\rvert\) = number of symbol occurrences in \(t\) (the usual term size),
- \(\#S(t)\) = number of occurrences of the symbol \(S\) in \(t\).

Both are natural numbers. The rewrite relation decreases the lexicographic combination \((\lvert t\rvert,\#S(t))\):

- The first rule \(F(x,y,Z)\to x\) strictly decreases size (the left-hand side contains at least the extra symbols \(F\), \(y\) and \(Z\)) and does not increase \(\#S\).
- The second rule \(F(x,y,S(n))\to G(F(x,y,n))\) preserves size exactly (it merely replaces one unary symbol \(S\) by another unary symbol \(G\)) while strictly decreasing \(\#S\) by 1.  
  No rule ever introduces a fresh occurrence of \(S\).

Consequently every rewrite step decreases a pair of natural numbers in the lexicographic order, which is well-founded. Hence there are no infinite reduction sequences.

(The same conclusion can be reached by a recursive path order with precedence \(F>G\) and lexicographic status on the third argument of \(F\), again a method that inspects only the two rules.)

The argument nowhere relies on the third argument of an \(F\)-redex being a pure numeral, on the absence of other function symbols, or on any equational theory beyond the rewrite rules themselves; it holds for arbitrary terms of the free term algebra.
