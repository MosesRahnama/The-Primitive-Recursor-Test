Assessment: YES – strong normalization can be proved from the two rewrite rules alone, without appealing to any extra axioms.

Reasoning (sketch)

1.  Let μ(t) be the multiset containing, for every occurrence of a term of the form F(…, …, u) in t, the natural number  
   depth(u) = the length of the S-chain in the third argument  
   (depth(Z)=0, depth(S(u))=depth(u)+1, depth(v)=0 for every other v).

2.  Order multisets of natural numbers by the multiset extension of the usual “>” on ℕ (which is well-founded).

3.  Inspect the rules:

   •  F(x,y,Z) → x  
      – The redex F(…,Z) disappears altogether, so μ strictly decreases.

   •  F(x,y,S(n)) → G(F(x,y,n))  
      – The redex F(…,S(n)) is replaced by one new F whose third argument is n, hence its depth is one smaller.  
      – No other new F is created, so μ again strictly decreases.

   Thus every rewrite step lowers the well-founded measure μ, so no infinite reduction sequence exists.

4.  Consequently every term has only finite reduction sequences: the system is terminating, hence strongly normalizing.

All this is obtained using nothing but the two given rules and the standard well-foundedness of ℕ, so no additional structural or object-level axioms are required.
