## Assessment

**Yes — strong normalization can be established from the presented rules alone.**

## Reasoning

The system has two rules:

```
F(x, y, Z)    -> x
F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))
```

**Key structural observations derivable directly from the rules:**

1. **No variable is duplicated or newly introduced on the right-hand sides.** The right-hand sides use only variables already present on the left (`x`, `n`), and no rule creates new redex-triggering structure that grows without bound relative to its input.

2. **The recursion is controlled by the third argument.** Both rules that could recurse (`F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))`) strictly reduce the third argument: it goes from `S(n)` to `n`. The symbol `S(...)` structurally decreases with each application of rule 2, and rule 1 terminates the recursion entirely.

3. **`G` and `S` are pure constructors here** (they never appear on the left-hand side of any rule, so they are not defined symbols and never fire further rewrites on their own).

**A simple termination measure:**

Define a measure `μ(t)` as the number of `S` symbols occurring in the third argument position of any `F`-subterm, summed over all `F`-occurrences (or more simply, the total `S`-symbol count within third-argument positions of `F`).

- Rule 1 removes an `F` (and its `Z`), strictly decreasing the measure (or keeping it and removing a redex).
- Rule 2 replaces `F(x, y, S(n))` with `G(F(x, y, n))`: the third argument's `S`-count drops by exactly one, while `G` contributes nothing to the measure. So `μ` strictly decreases.

Since `μ` maps into the natural numbers and strictly decreases at every rewrite step, no infinite reduction sequence exists.

**Why no external axioms are needed:** The argument uses only:
- The literal shape of the two rules,
- The fact that `S`, `G`, `Z` are constructors (evident because they head no left-hand side),
- A polynomial/count-based measure definable purely over the term syntax given.

No structural induction principle imported from outside, no assumptions about the semantics of `F`, `G`, `S`, and no confluence or object-level lemmas are required. The decreasing measure on the constructor `S` in the third argument is self-contained.

## Conclusion

Strong normalization follows self-containedly: the third argument strictly decreases (in `S`-count) on every recursive step, and there is no rule that can regenerate that structure. This is provable from the rewrite system alone.
