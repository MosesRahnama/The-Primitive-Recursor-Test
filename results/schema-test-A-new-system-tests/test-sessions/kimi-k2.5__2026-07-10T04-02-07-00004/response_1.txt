**Yes.** Strong normalization can be established from the given rules alone.

**Reasoning:**  
The rewrite system defines primitive recursion over the third argument with a well-founded decreasing measure. Define a weight function `w` on terms by:
- `w(variable) = w(Z) = 1`
- `w(S(t)) = w(t) + 1`
- `w(G(t)) = w(t)`
- `w(F(a,b,c)) = w(a) + w(b) + 2·w(c)`

For both rules, the left-hand side has strictly greater weight than the right-hand side:
- Rule 1: `w(F(x,y,Z)) = w(x)+w(y)+2 > w(x)`.
- Rule 2: `w(F(x,y,S(n))) = w(x)+w(y)+2(w(n)+1) = w(x)+w(y)+2w(n)+2 > w(x)+w(y)+2w(n) = w(G(F(x,y,n)))`.

Since the weight is a positive integer that strictly decreases with every rewrite step and is monotone in all argument positions, no infinite reduction sequence is possible. The recursion on the third argument (peeling off `S` constructors until reaching `Z`) guarantees that every `F`-redex is eliminated after finitely many steps, and the constructors `G`, `S`, `Z` have no rules that could introduce non-termination.
