1. Method used: a standard termination‐ordering argument, namely a recursive-path (simplification) order with a chosen precedence F ≻ G ≻ S ≻ Z.

2. That proof relies on selecting a particular well-founded ordering and precedence.  The ordering is a meta-level choice; it is not dictated by, nor derivable from, the rewrite rules themselves.

3. Because the ordering choice is external to the object-level rules, one could say it lies outside the stated boundary if the boundary is interpreted to forbid any extra meta-level structure.  If the boundary is meant only to forbid additional object-level axioms, then the method is still acceptable.  So: yes, it does introduce an extra assumption; whether that violates the boundary depends on the intended strictness of “from the rules alone.”

4. Independent of that boundary issue, I still judge the system to be strongly normalizing (terminating).
