Assessment:  Yes – strong normalization (termination of every reduction sequence) can be shown using the two rewrite rules themselves, without importing any extra axioms.

Reasoning

1.  Every rewrite step acts only on the outermost symbol F.
    a.  Base rule  
       F(x , y , Z) → x  
       replaces the redex by a subterm of the redex, so it is strictly
       decreasing in every simplification order.
    b.  Step rule  
       F(x , y , S(n)) → G(F(x , y , n))  
       keeps the F-call but makes its third argument the immediate
       predecessor n of S(n); no rule ever introduces a new S.
       Hence the number of S-nestings in the third argument of that F
       strictly decreases.

2.  A standard simplification order (e.g. recursive path order) with the
    precedence  
       F  ≻  G  ≻  S  ≻  Z  
    shows both rules to be decreasing:
       • In rule 1 the left–hand side F(…) is a proper super-term of the
         variable x.  
       • In rule 2 the heads differ and F ≻ G, so
         F(x,y,S(n)) ≻ G(F(x,y,n)).

3.  Because all rules are decreasing in such a well-founded order, the
    system is terminating; for first-order TRSs termination is equivalent
    to strong normalization.

Therefore the system is strongly normalizing on its own; no extra
structural or object-level assumptions are needed.
