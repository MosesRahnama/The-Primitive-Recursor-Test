**Assessment: yes**

The TRS is untyped and first-order, with free constructors (distinct head symbols for Z, S, F, G) and purely syntactic matching. The only redexes are outer F-applications whose third argument is headed by Z or S. The first rule strictly decreases the total number of F symbols in a term (discarding the matched F while retaining only the F's already present in x). The second rule preserves the number of F symbols but replaces the third argument S(n) by the strictly smaller n (a proper subterm). No rule duplicates variables, introduces new S or Z symbols, or creates redexes in other positions. Consequently, reductions inside any argument (x, y, or n) are on strictly smaller terms.

By induction on term size (measured e.g. by the multiset of sizes of all subterms, or lexicographically by (# of F symbols, sum of depths of third arguments of all F's)), every reduction sequence must be finite: inner reductions terminate by the induction hypothesis; once arguments are normal, an outer F either stops (third-argument head neither Z nor S) or applies one of the two rules, both of which decrease the measure. No external equations, freeness axioms, or typing assumptions beyond the given rules are required; the syntactic distinctness of constructors and the subterm property already embedded in first-order rewriting suffice to establish that every term has a normal form and that no infinite chain exists.
