(VAR x y n)

(RULES

  F(x, y, Z) -> x

  F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))

)

It's a first-order term rewriting system with no variable binding, no imported axioms beyond these rules.

 The question is whether strong normalization (SN) can be established from this rewrite system alone, without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what's derivable from the rules.

Strong normalization means every term has a finite reduction sequence to normal form, no infinite reduction sequences.

I need to assess if SN holds based on these rules alone, or if additional assumptions are needed.

Let's analyze for possible infinite reductions.

The rewrite rules apply only to terms matching F(..., Z) or F(..., S(n)), where n is any term.

- When third arg is Z, reduces to x (first arg).

- When third arg is S(n), reduces to G(F(x,y,n)), recursing on the "predecessor" n.

Reductions can occur in any subterm, including arguments.

Since the rules are syntactic matching, no equations for equality or constructors like injectivity of S or Z != S(t).

In pure TRS, terms are reduced syntactically.
