**Assessment:** Yes – strong normalization (termination) can be established from the given rewrite system alone.

**Reasoning:**  
The only redexes are instances of the two \(F\)-rules.  
- In the first rule \(F(x,y,Z)\to x\) the redex disappears entirely.  
- In the second rule \(F(x,y,S(n))\to G(F(x,y,n))\) the outer \(F\) is replaced by a \(G\) wrapping a *strictly smaller* \(F\)-redex: the third argument changes from \(S(n)\) to \(n\), a proper subterm.

Define a measure on terms that sums, over all occurrences of \(F\), the number of \(S\)‑symbols in the third argument of that occurrence. Each rewrite step strictly decreases this measure (by at least one). Because the measure takes values in the natural numbers and the subterm relation is well‑founded for finite terms, no infinite reduction sequence is possible.  

A concrete monotone interpretation (e.g. polynomial interpretation) confirming this is:
\[
[Z]=0,\quad [S](m)=m+1,\quad [G](m)=m+1,\quad [F](x,y,z)=x+2z+1,
\]
which yields \([F(x,y,Z)]=x+1>x=[x]\) and \([F(x,y,S(n))]=x+2n+3 > x+2n+2=[G(F(x,y,n))]\).

No external axioms about the data type \(\{Z,S\}\) or induction principles are needed; the argument relies only on the syntactic structure of the two given rules and the finiteness of terms. Hence the system is strongly normalizing.
