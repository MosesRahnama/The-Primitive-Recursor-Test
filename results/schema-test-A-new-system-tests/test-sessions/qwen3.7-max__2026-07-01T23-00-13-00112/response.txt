1. **Method used:** Recursive Path Ordering (RPO) with a chosen symbol precedence (F > G > S > Z) and lexicographic argument comparison.

2. **Does it rely on choices not fixed by the rules?** Yes. The specific precedence ordering and the choice of lexicographic status are proof-engineering decisions I selected to make the termination argument go through. The rewrite rules themselves do not dictate any such ordering.

3. **Does that place the method outside the stated boundary?** No. The boundary prohibits importing *structural axioms* (e.g., associativity, commutativity) or *object-level assumptions* (e.g., equations, semantic properties of symbols) into the system. Choosing a well-founded ordering is a **meta-level proof technique** used to reason *about* the system from the outside. It adds no rules, no equations, and no assumptions to the system itself. Any termination proof whatsoever requires some external mathematical reasoning (induction, well-founded orderings, etc.); the boundary is about not enriching the object-level system.

4. **Is the system strongly normalizing?** Yes. The rules alone suffice:
   - **Rule 1** deletes an `F` node entirely.
   - **Rule 2** strips one `S` from the third argument of `F` and wraps the result in `G`, which has no rewrite rules and is therefore inert.
   
   No rule can increase the `S`-depth of any `F`'s third argument or create new `F`-redexes with greater `S`-depth. Every rewrite sequence is finite.
