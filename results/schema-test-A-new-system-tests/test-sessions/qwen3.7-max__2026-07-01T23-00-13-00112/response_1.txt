### Reasoning

The system can be shown strongly normalizing via a **Recursive Path Ordering (RPO)** with precedence **F > G > S > Z** and lexicographic status on F's arguments (left-to-right).

**Rule 1:** `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
The right-hand side `x` is a direct subterm of the left-hand side, so `F(x, y, Z) > x` holds trivially in RPO.

**Rule 2:** `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))`
This requires `F(x, y, S(n)) > G(F(x, y, n))`. Since `F > G` in the precedence, it suffices to show `F(x, y, S(n)) > F(x, y, n)` (the argument of G). Under lexicographic comparison of F's arguments:
- `x = x`
- `y = y`
- `S(n) > n` (since `n` is a subterm of `S(n)`)

So the third argument strictly decreases, and the required side conditions (`F(x,y,S(n))` is greater than each subterm `x`, `y`, `n`) are satisfied since they are all subterms.

### Intuitive summary

Every rewrite step strictly decreases the term in a well-founded ordering:
- **Rule 1** eliminates an `F` symbol entirely.
- **Rule 2** peels one `S` constructor from the third argument of `F`, strictly reducing it by the subterm relation.

No rule can reintroduce or enlarge the third argument of any `F`—`G` and `S` have no rewrite rules and act as inert constructors. Since RPO is well-founded and closed under contexts and substitutions, all rewrite sequences are finite, establishing strong normalization without any external axioms.
