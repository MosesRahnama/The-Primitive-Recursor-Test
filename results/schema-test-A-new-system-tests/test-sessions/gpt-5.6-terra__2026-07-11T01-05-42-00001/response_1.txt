Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone, under the ordinary syntactic reading of a first-order TRS: `Z` is a constant, `S`, `F`, and `G` are uninterpreted function symbols, and there are no equations identifying terms such as `Z` and `S(n)`.

For example, use a natural-number interpretation:

- \(\llbracket Z\rrbracket = 0\)
- \(\llbracket S(t)\rrbracket = \llbracket t\rrbracket + 1\)
- \(\llbracket G(t)\rrbracket = \llbracket t\rrbracket\)
- \(\llbracket F(x,y,z)\rrbracket = \llbracket x\rrbracket+\llbracket y\rrbracket+\llbracket z\rrbracket+1\)

Then both rules strictly decrease:

1. \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\):
   \[
   x+y+0+1 > x.
   \]

2. \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(F(x,y,n))\):
   \[
   x+y+(n+1)+1 = x+y+n+2
   >
   x+y+n+1
   =
   \llbracket G(F(x,y,n))\rrbracket.
   \]

All symbol interpretations are monotone, so rewriting in any context also decreases this measure. Since there is no infinite strictly descending sequence of natural numbers, there can be no infinite rewrite sequence.

Thus the presented system is strongly normalizing without needing any additional object-level axioms or structural rewrite rules.
