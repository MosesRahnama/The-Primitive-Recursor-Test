Yes — **from the rules alone, strong normalization is establishable**.

Reason (briefly):  
The only recursive step is
\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(F(x,y,n)),
\]
which strictly decreases the third argument of \(F\) from \(S(n)\) to \(n\). The base case
\[
F(x,y,Z)\to x
\]
stops that recursion. There is no rule rewriting \(G(\cdot)\), \(S(\cdot)\), \(Z\), or variables further, and no rule recreates a larger third argument. So every rewrite sequence from any \(F(x,y,t)\) can only peel finitely many \(S\)-constructors (if any) and then terminates; all other terms are already normal or become so after finitely many steps.

So the TRS is strongly normalizing without needing extra axioms.
