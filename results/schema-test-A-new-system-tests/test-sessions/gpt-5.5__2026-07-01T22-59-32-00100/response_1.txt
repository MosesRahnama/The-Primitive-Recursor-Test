Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the given rewrite system alone, for ordinary finite first-order terms.

Reasoning:

The only recursive rule is

```text
F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))
```

and it strictly removes one outer `S` from the third argument of `F`. The wrapper `G` does not rewrite, and no rule reintroduces an `S` around that third argument. The base rule

```text
F(x, y, Z) -> x
```

eliminates an `F` headed by `Z`.

This can be made formal using a standard simplification order, e.g. a recursive path order with precedence `F > G`. Then:

- `F(x, y, Z) > x` by the subterm property.
- `F(x, y, S(n)) > G(F(x, y, n))` because `F > G`, and `F(x, y, S(n)) > F(x, y, n)` since `S(n) > n`.

Since both rules decrease under a well-founded rewrite order, the TRS is terminating/strongly normalizing.

No structural axioms about natural numbers, induction principles, or object-level assumptions are needed; the argument is purely syntactic over the displayed first-order rewrite rules.
