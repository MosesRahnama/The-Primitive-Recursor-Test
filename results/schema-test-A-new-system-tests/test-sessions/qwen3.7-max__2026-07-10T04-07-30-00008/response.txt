1. **Primary method:** A semantic interpretation (a weight/termination function) mapping terms to natural numbers, specifically using an exponential weight to show that each rewrite step strictly decreases the term's weight.

2. **External reliance:** Yes. The method relies on an externally chosen interpretation (the specific weight function), an ordering choice (the standard $>$ relation on natural numbers), and the structural axiom that the natural numbers with $>$ are well-founded. None of these are fixed by or derivable from the syntactic rewrite rules themselves.

3. **Boundary compliance:** Yes. Because termination is a meta-property, proving it inherently requires importing an external well-founded mathematical structure (like $\mathbb{N}$ or a path ordering). Doing so violates the strict boundary of using *only* the presented rewrite system and what is derivable from its pure syntax.

4. **Actual SN status:** Yes. Separately from the boundary constraints, the system is strongly normalizing. Syntactically, the rules do not duplicate subterms (preventing redex proliferation), and the recursive rule strictly consumes the outermost constructor (`S`) of the third argument, which bounds the maximum reduction length for any finite term.
