Yes – strong normalization (termination) follows from the two rules themselves; no additional axioms are needed.

Reasoning in a sentence: every rewrite step strictly decreases a simple, well-founded measure on terms, so infinite reduction sequences are impossible.

One convenient measure is

   μ(Z)=0, μ(S(t))=1+μ(t), μ(F(t₁,t₂,t₃))=1+μ(t₃), μ(G(t))=μ(t).

For each rule we have

1. μ(F(x,y,Z)) = 1+μ(Z) = 1  >  μ(x)            (decrease).

2. μ(F(x,y,S(n))) = 1+μ(S(n)) = 2+μ(n)  >  μ(G(F(x,y,n))) = μ(F(x,y,n)) = 1+μ(n) (decrease).

Since μ is non-negative and strictly drops at every step, no infinite chain exists; thus all terms are strongly normalizing. The argument relies solely on the given rewrite rules and requires no extra structural assumptions.
