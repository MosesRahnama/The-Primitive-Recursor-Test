Yes – the rules themselves already guarantee strong normalisation; no extra (structural or object-level) axioms are needed.

Sketch of the argument

1.  The only rules that can fire are the two shown, and both rewrite a term headed by F.

2.  Rule 2,
      F(x, y, S(n)) → G(F(x, y, n)),
    removes one outer S from the third argument of that particular F.  
    Hence every application of rule 2 strictly shortens the S-chain occurring in that position.

3.  Rule 1,
      F(x, y, Z) → x,
    eliminates the whole F-node whose third argument has length 0.  
    (The replacement x cannot re-introduce that very same F-node because terms are finite; it only exposes a sub-term that was already present.)

4.  Consider the multiset M(t) consisting of the lengths of the outermost S-chains that occur as third arguments of all F-occurrences inside t.  
    • Rule 2 decreases one element of M(t) by 1, hence M(t) becomes strictly smaller under the well-founded multiset extension of the usual order on ℕ.  
    • Rule 1 removes one element from M(t) altogether.  
    Therefore every rewrite step strictly decreases M(t).

5.  Because M(t) is drawn from a well-founded ordering, an infinite strictly descending sequence of such multisets is impossible; consequently no infinite reduction sequence exists.

Thus the system is terminating, i.e. strongly normalising, on its own.
