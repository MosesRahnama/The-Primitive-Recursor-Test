Yes — strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

Reason: there is a simple well-founded interpretation into the natural numbers that strictly decreases on every rewrite step. For example, define

- \(|Z| = 0\)
- \(|S(t)| = |t| + 1\)
- \(|G(t)| = |t|\)
- \(|F(a,b,c)| = |a| + |b| + |c| + 1\)

Then the two rules decrease strictly:

1. \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\)

\[
|F(x,y,Z)| = |x| + |y| + 1 > |x|
\]

2. \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(F(x,y,n))\)

\[
|F(x,y,S(n))| = |x| + |y| + |n| + 2
\]
\[
|G(F(x,y,n))| = |F(x,y,n)| = |x| + |y| + |n| + 1
\]

So every rewrite step lowers a natural-number measure, hence no infinite reduction sequence exists.

Intuitively: the recursion on the third argument strictly peels off one \(S\) at a time, and \(G\) is inert since it has no rewrite rules. No extra structural axioms or object-level assumptions are needed.
