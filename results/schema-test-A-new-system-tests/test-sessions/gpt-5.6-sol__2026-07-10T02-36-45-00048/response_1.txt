**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone.

For example, orient both rules using a lexicographic path ordering with \(F \succ G\) and lexicographic status for \(F\):

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\) by the subterm property.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n)\), since the first two arguments agree and \(S(n) \succ n\) by the subterm property.
- Hence \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ G(F(x,y,n))\), using \(F \succ G\).

This ordering is well-founded and closed under substitutions and contexts, so every rewrite step strictly decreases it. Therefore no infinite rewrite sequence exists; the system is strongly normalizing without additional object-level or structural assumptions.
