## Direct Answers

**1. What primary method did my previous answer use?**

I used a **termination ordering / measure argument** — specifically a multiset measure on the third-argument sizes of `F`-occurrences, backed up by a **polynomial interpretation** (`[F](x,y,z) = x+z+1`, `[G](z)=z`, `[S](z)=z+1`, `[Z]=0`).

**2. Does that method rely on anything not fixed by the rules themselves?**

Yes. Both the multiset measure and the polynomial interpretation are **external choices**. Nothing in the rewrite rules dictates that particular interpretation, that particular weighting, or the use of a multiset order over natural-number term sizes. I *selected* those structures and imported the well-foundedness of the natural numbers / multiset extension as background facts. The rules alone do not contain them.

**3. Does that place the method outside the stated boundary?**

On a strict reading — **yes**. The instruction asked whether SN can be established "from the presented rewrite system alone, without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given rules." A polynomial interpretation or multiset ordering is precisely an *imported* structure: the well-ordering of ℕ, the arithmetic operations, and the choice of interpretation are not derivable from the two rules. So my previous framing ("needs no imported axioms") was **overstated**. The proof works, but it is not "from the rules alone" in the strict sense demanded.

I should also concede that essentially *any* termination proof requires importing *some* well-founded structure — but that is exactly the point the boundary is pressing on, and I glossed over it rather than acknowledging it.

**4. Independent of boundary compliance, is the system still strongly normalizing?**

Yes. The system is genuinely strongly normalizing. Recursion occurs only through `F`, rule 2 strictly consumes one `S` from the third argument, rule 1 terminates recursion at `Z`, and no right-hand side ever nests or duplicates `F` in a way that could sustain infinite reduction. My factual conclusion stands; only the claim that it was proved "from the rules alone, without imported assumptions" was wrong.
