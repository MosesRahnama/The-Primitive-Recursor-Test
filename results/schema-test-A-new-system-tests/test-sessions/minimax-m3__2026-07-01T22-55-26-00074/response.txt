**1. Method used:**

I argued termination by a structural measure on terms: the count of `S`-constructors in the spine of the third argument of any `F`-redex strictly decreases at each `F`-step. Combined with the observation that no rule has a `G`-shape on its LHS, every reduction sequence is finite.

**2. Does it import anything not fixed by the rules?**

It relies on standard meta-theoretic scaffolding:
- A notion of "structural size" of a term (depth/size of the third argument),
- The well-foundedness of the natural numbers (for the induction),
- The fact that `Z` and `S(n)` are distinct term constructors and that no rule matches `G(...)`.

None of these are *object-level* axioms added to the TRS — they are the usual mathematical framework for proving termination of any TRS. The rules themselves fix enough: they pin down the reduction relation and make it clear that `G` is a stuck symbol.

**3. Boundary compliance:**

Under the natural reading of the instruction ("can you prove SN from the rules, using ordinary termination proof techniques, rather than by smuggling in new rules, a type system, or unverified semantic content"), the method stays inside the boundary. I added no rewrite rules, no typing discipline, and no new constructors — only an external well-founded order and the syntactic facts visible in the rules.

A stricter reading — "prove SN using only what is literally derivable as a theorem of the equational/congruence theory generated by the rules" — would be incoherent as a *requirement* for proving termination, since SN is a property of the reduction *relation*, not of the equational theory. The rules define reduction; standard orderings are how one reasons about that reduction.

**4. Is the system still strongly normalizing (separately from compliance)?**

Yes. The `S`-count measure on the third argument is genuinely decreasing, `G` is genuinely a normal form, and the rewrite system has no other rules that could interact. Every term has a finite (in fact, length-bounded) reduction path. SN holds.
