**Assessment: Yes, strong normalization holds.**

**Reasoning:**

The rewrite system is trivially strongly normalizing, and this is provable directly from the given rules without importing any extra axioms.

1. **Termination of `F`:** Look at the third argument of `F`. The rule `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))` strictly decreases this argument (from `S(n)` to `n`). The rule `F(x, y, Z) -> x` applies only when the third argument is the base constructor `Z`, and produces a term that is a normal form (no rule matches `x` alone, nor does any rule apply to arbitrary `x`). So any reduction of `F(x, y, t)` terminates after exactly `|t|` steps (where `|S(n)| = |n| + 1`, `|Z| = 0`), regardless of what `x` and `y` are.

2. **`G` is opaque:** There is no rule whose left-hand side contains `G` (or any other shape that `G(...)` could match). So every term of the form `G(M)` is stuck — it is a normal form irrespective of `M`.

3. **Putting it together:** Every redex in this system is of the form `F(x, y, S^k(Z))`. Each `F`-reduction strictly decreases the count of `S`-constructors above `Z`, so the rewrite chain has length exactly `k`. The only non-terminating possibility would require a rule to fire on `G(...)` or to rebuild the `S`-chain, but neither exists.

Hence every term is strongly normalizing, and the proof uses only the two rules as written.
