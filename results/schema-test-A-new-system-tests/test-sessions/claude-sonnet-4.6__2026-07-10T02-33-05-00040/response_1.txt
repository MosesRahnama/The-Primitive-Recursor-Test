## Assessment

**Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.**

### Reasoning

**Structure of the system:**

The two rules are:
1. `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))`

**Why strong normalization holds:**

1. **The third argument strictly decreases.** In rule 2, `F(x, y, S(n))` reduces to `G(F(x, y, n))`. The third argument goes from `S(n)` to `n` — a strict structural decrease on a term built from constructor `S` and base case `Z`.

2. **No rule introduces new redexes for `F` in a non-decreasing way.** Rule 1 eliminates `F` entirely (replaced by `x`, a variable). Rule 2 replaces `F(..., S(n))` with `G(F(..., n))` — the only new `F`-redex has a strictly smaller third argument. `G` has no rewrite rules, so it is inert/normal.

3. **A simple polynomial/ordinal interpretation works.** Assign to `F(x, y, n)` a measure based on the size of `n` (e.g., the natural number encoded by `n`). Each application of rule 2 decreases this measure by 1. Rule 1 reduces it to 0. Since the measure is a natural number, it is well-founded.

4. **No circularity or self-referential growth.** `G` is a constructor with no rules; `x` and `y` are passive. There is no way for a term to grow the third argument of `F`.

**Conclusion:** The system is strongly normalizing by a straightforward lexicographic/size argument on the third argument of `F`, derivable entirely from the given rules with no additional axioms needed.
