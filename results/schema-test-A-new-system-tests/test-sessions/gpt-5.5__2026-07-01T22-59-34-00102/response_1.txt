Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the presented first-order rewrite system alone.

Reason: the only recursive rule

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(F(x,y,n))
\]

strictly decreases the third argument of `F` by removing one leading `S`. The wrapper `G` does not create new redexes by itself, since there are no rules for `G`. The base rule

\[
F(x,y,Z) \to x
\]

also terminates immediately.

A simple well-founded measure proves termination, e.g. interpret terms over natural numbers by:

- \(|Z| = 0\)
- \(|S(t)| = |t| + 1\)
- \(|G(t)| = |t|\)
- \(|F(a,b,c)| = |a| + |b| + |c| + 1\)

Then for every substitution:

- \(|F(x,y,Z)| = |x| + |y| + 1 > |x|\)
- \(|F(x,y,S(n))| = |x| + |y| + |n| + 2 > |G(F(x,y,n))| = |x| + |y| + |n| + 1\)

The interpretation is monotone under contexts, so every rewrite step strictly decreases a natural-number measure. Therefore no infinite rewrite sequence exists.

So, under the standard first-order finite-term rewriting interpretation, this TRS is strongly normalizing without requiring any extra object-level axioms or structural assumptions.
