Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

A standard recursive path ordering proof suffices. Give `F` higher precedence than `G`, and use lexicographic status for `F`. Then:

- `F(x,y,Z) -> x` decreases because a term is greater than each of its proper subterms/variables.
- `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(F(x,y,n))` decreases because:
  - the outer symbol changes from `F` to lower-precedence `G`; and
  - the recursive `F(x,y,n)` has a strictly smaller third argument than `F(x,y,S(n))`, since `S(n) > n`.

There are no rules expanding `G` or `S`, and the only recursive occurrence of `F` is on the structurally smaller third argument. Thus no infinite rewrite sequence can be generated by these rules.

This assumes the displayed syntax is read as an ordinary first-order TRS. In particular, if `Z` is not declared as a variable in `(VAR x y n)`, then it is simply a constant symbol in the first rule; that does not undermine termination.
