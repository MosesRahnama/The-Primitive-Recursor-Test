Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

For example, orient the system using a lexicographic recursive path ordering with precedence \(F > G\). The first rule decreases because its left-hand side contains \(x\) as a proper subterm. For the second rule,

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(F(x,y,n)),
\]

the outer \(F\) is greater than \(G\), and the recursive \(F(x,y,n)\) is smaller than \(F(x,y,S(n))\) because \(n\) is a proper subterm of \(S(n)\). Recursive path ordering is well-founded and stable under substitutions and contexts, so it proves termination—and hence strong normalization—without requiring any additional structural or object-level axioms.
