Assessment:  Yes – strong normalization (termination of every reduction sequence) follows from the two given rules themselves, with no need to assume any extra axioms.

Reasoning in brief

1.  Only F-symbols rewrite.  
    •  Rule-1 removes an F once its third argument is Z.  
    •  Rule-2 keeps one F but peels off one outer S from its third argument and wraps the whole redex in a new, inert G.

2.  Define a simple well-founded measure on terms, for instance  
        μ(t) = total number of S symbols occurring in t.  
    –  Rule-2 reduces μ by exactly one (one S disappears).  
    –  Rule-1 leaves μ unchanged but strictly decreases the number of F-redexes in the term.  
      Combine the two components in a lexicographic pair (μ, #F-redexes).  
      This pair is strictly lowered by every rewrite step, so an infinite sequence is impossible.

3.  No rule ever creates new S symbols, and G never rewrites at all, so neither component of the measure can increase anywhere in a derivation.

Therefore every reduction sequence is finite; i.e. the system is strongly normalizing, and this fact is provable directly from the rewrite rules that were presented.
