**Assessment: Yes.** Strong normalization can be established from the two rewrite rules alone, without importing any external axioms about the symbols \(Z\), \(S\), or \(G\).

**Reasoning:**  
Define a numeric weight on terms by summing, over **every** occurrence of an \(F\)-subterm \(F(u,v,w)\), the size (number of symbols) of its third argument \(w\):
\[
\mu(t)=\sum_{F(u,v,w)\text{ in }t}|w|.
\]

- **Rule 1:** \(F(x,y,Z)\to x\). The left-hand side contributes at least \(|Z|=1\) from this \(F\)-occurrence plus the weight of \(y\), while the right-hand side contributes neither. Hence \(\mu\) drops by at least 1.
- **Rule 2:** \(F(x,y,S(n))\to G(F(x,y,n))\). The third argument shrinks from \(S(n)\) to \(n\), so the contribution of this \(F\)-occurrence decreases from \(|S(n)|=|n|+1\) to \(|n|\). All other \(F\)-occurrences are unchanged, so \(\mu\) drops by exactly 1.

Every rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\), which is a natural number. Therefore no infinite reduction sequence is possible. The argument uses only the syntactic shapes of the given rules and elementary well-foundedness of the natural numbers.
