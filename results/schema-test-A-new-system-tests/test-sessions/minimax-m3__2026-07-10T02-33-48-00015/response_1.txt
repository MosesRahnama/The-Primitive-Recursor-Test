- The only redex shape is `F(x, y, t)` where `t` is built from `Z` and `S`.

  - If `t = S(n)`, one step gives `G(F(x, y, n))`. The inner redex `F(x, y, n)` has a strictly smaller third argument (one fewer `S` in front), and the surrounding `G` is inert forever.
- A simple termination measure works: the pair (number of leading `S`s in the third argument of the outermost `F`, 1) decreases under the second rule, and the first rule reduces to a term containing no `F`. Since this measure is well-founded (lexicographically, with a natural number first component), every reduction sequence must terminate.

No imported structural or object-level axioms (e.g., induction on `Z`/`S`) are required — the termination argument follows purely from the two given rules.
