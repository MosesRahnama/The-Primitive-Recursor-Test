1. **Primary method:** A termination measure into the natural numbers — specifically a lexicographic pair (count of `S` symbols in the third argument of the outermost `F`, plus a constant) that strictly decreases across rewrites.

2. **Reliance on external structure:** Yes. The argument depends on:
   - The well-foundedness of ℕ (to conclude that a strictly decreasing natural-number measure yields termination),
   - The interpretation of the term syntax that assigns "number of leading `S`s" a meaning as a natural number.
   
   None of this is fixed by the two rules alone; the rules contain no notion of "size" or "count" — that structure is brought in from outside.

3. **Boundary compliance:** In a strict reading, no. The proof imports a structural axiom (well-foundedness of the naturals) that is not derivable from the rewrite rules themselves. The rules do not assert that `S`/`Z` form a well-ordered inductive type, nor that term size decreases. The decrease is asserted by the proof author, not licensed by the rules.

4. **Still strongly normalizing?** Yes. The system is clearly strongly normalizing. `G` is inert (never appears as a left-hand side), so it cannot spawn further redexes. Each application of the second rule peels one `S` off the third argument of `F`; the first rule is a base case producing a closed value. So even though a fully self-contained proof "from the rules alone" is not available without some external well-foundedness principle, the termination conclusion itself is correct.
