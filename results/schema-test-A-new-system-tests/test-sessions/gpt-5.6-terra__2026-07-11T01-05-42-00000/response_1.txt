Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone, assuming the usual finite first-order term syntax and contextual rewriting.

A simple termination argument is by a lexicographic path ordering:

- Give `F` precedence over `G`.
- Use lexicographic status for `F`’s arguments.

Then:

- `F(x, y, Z) -> x` decreases because `x` is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
- `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(F(x, y, n))` decreases because the outer symbol changes from higher-precedence `F` to `G`, and the required comparison of the embedded `F(x,y,n)` with the left side holds since `S(n) > n`.

Thus every rule strictly decreases a well-founded term ordering, so no infinite rewrite sequence exists.

This does not require treating `Z` and `S` as Peano constructors, assuming numeral exhaustiveness, or importing any object-level equations. Their syntactic occurrence in the displayed rules is sufficient.
