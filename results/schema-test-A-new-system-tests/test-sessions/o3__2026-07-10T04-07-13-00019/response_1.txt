Yes – the system is strongly normalizing on its own.

Reasoning (informal sketch)

• The only recursive rule is
     F(x ,y ,S(n)) → G(F(x ,y ,n)).
  The third argument of the recursive call strictly decreases from S(n) to n.  
  Hence if we map every term to the natural number
     μ(t) = total number of S-symbols occurring as the third argument of any
            F-subterm of t,
  then μ strictly diminishes by one at every use of that rule, while rule 1
     F(x ,y ,Z) → x
  obviously removes an F altogether.  No other rule increases μ, so μ is a
  well-founded measure.

• Because every rewrite step lowers μ(t), no infinite reduction is possible;
  hence the TRS terminates, i.e. it is strongly normalizing.

• This proof needs no extra structural or object-level axioms – it relies
  only on the two given rules and the usual well-foundedness of the natural
  numbers.

Therefore, strong normalization can indeed be established from the presented
rewrite system alone.
