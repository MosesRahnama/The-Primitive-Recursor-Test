**Assessment:** Yes – the system is strongly normalizing, and this can be established directly from the given rules without extra structural axioms.

**Reasoning:**  
Every redex is an occurrence of `F` whose third argument is either `Z` or `S(n)`.  
- The rule `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(F(x, y, n))` strips one `S` constructor from that argument.  
- The rule `F(x, y, Z) → x` removes the `F` entirely when no `S` remains.

Define a measure on a term as the multiset of the numbers of `S` symbols occurring in the third argument of each `F` subterm.  
- An application of the second rule replaces a number `k+1` by `k` in the multiset, so the multiset strictly decreases in the multiset ordering induced by the usual order on natural numbers.  
- An application of the first rule deletes a `0` from the multiset, which also strictly decreases the multiset.

Since the multiset ordering is well‑founded and no rule creates new `S` symbols, every reduction sequence must be finite. Hence the rewrite system is strongly normalizing.
