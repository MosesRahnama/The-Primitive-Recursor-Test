**Yes — strong normalization can be established from these rules alone.**

## Reasoning

The system has only two rules:

```
(1) Velk(x, y, Mek)    -> x
(2) Velk(x, y, Oru(n)) -> Tarn(y, Velk(x, y, n))
```

**Key structural observation:** Reduction happens only at `Velk` occurrences, and both rules are driven by the *third argument* of `Velk`.

- Rule (1) fires when the third argument is `Mek`, eliminating the `Velk` symbol entirely (replacing it by a subterm).
- Rule (2) fires when the third argument is `Oru(n)`, and the recursive `Velk(x, y, n)` on the right has a third argument `n` that is *structurally smaller* than `Oru(n)`.

**Termination argument (measure-based):**

Define a measure on each `Velk`-redex based on the size (or `Oru`-depth) of its third argument. Rule (2) strictly decreases the `Oru`-nesting of the third argument of the `Velk` it rewrites: `Oru(n)` becomes `n`. No rule ever *increases* the third argument's structure, and no rule introduces a new `Velk` whose third argument grows (rule (2) reproduces exactly the same `x, y` but a strictly smaller third component; rule (1) removes a `Velk`).

Concretely, take the interpretation where the weight of a term is the sum over all `Velk`-subterms of `(1 + Oru-depth of its third argument)`. 

- Rule (1) removes a `Velk` node and copies subterm `x`; the `Velk` node's contribution is deleted, and `x` was already counted inside — so the total strictly decreases.
- Rule (2) turns one `Velk(x,y,Oru(n))` into `Tarn(y, Velk(x,y,n))`: `Tarn` contributes nothing to the measure, and the `Velk`'s third-argument depth drops by exactly one, so the measure strictly decreases.

This measure strictly decreases under every rewrite step and is bounded below, so no infinite reduction sequence exists.

**No hidden obstacles:**

- There is **no variable binding**, so no capture or substitution subtleties.
- There is **no duplication that grows the redex structure**: rule (2) does duplicate `x` and `y` into the recursive call, but the recursion is guarded by the strictly-decreasing third argument, and `x`, `y` themselves are not rewritten in a way that regenerates larger `Velk` redexes.
- No rule creates a fresh `Oru`, so the third argument's `Oru`-depth is monotonically non-increasing and strictly decreases whenever rule (2) applies.

## Conclusion

Strong normalization follows **purely from the presented rules** via a simple structural/polynomial termination measure. No external structural axioms or object-level assumptions are needed — the well-foundedness comes entirely from the decreasing `Oru`-depth of `Velk`'s third argument.
