**Yes.** On ordinary finite first-order terms, strong normalization follows syntactically from the rules alone.

A lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules by taking `Velk` above `Tarn` and giving `Velk` lexicographic argument status:

- `Velk(x,y,Mek) > x` by the subterm property.
- `Velk(x,y,Oru(n)) > Velk(x,y,n)` because `Oru(n) > n`.
- Therefore `Velk(x,y,Oru(n)) > Tarn(y,Velk(x,y,n))`, since the left side is also greater than `y` and `Velk` has higher precedence than `Tarn`.

This ordering is well-founded and stable under contexts and substitutions, so it also accounts for the duplication of `y` in the second rule. Hence every rewrite sequence is finite, without additional structural or object-level axioms.
