# Assessment: Yes, strong normalization can be established from the system alone.

## Reasoning

The system has two rules:

```
Velk(x, y, Mek)    -> x
Velk(x, y, Oru(n)) -> Tarn(y, Velk(x, y, n))
```

**Key structural observation:** Both rules reduce with respect to the *third argument* of `Velk`. This is essentially primitive recursion over a unary-numeral structure built from `Mek` (zero) and `Oru(·)` (successor).

**Termination argument — a simple well-founded measure suffices:**

Consider assigning to each term a measure based on the count of `Velk` symbols that can still fire, or more directly, use a polynomial/structural interpretation. A clean choice is to define a measure that strictly decreases on every rewrite step.

Take the interpretation over the naturals where each function symbol is interpreted so that a redex contraction strictly decreases the measure:

- Interpret `Mek` and `Oru(n)` by their term size (with `Oru` strictly increasing).
- The critical point is `Velk`: define `[Velk](a,b,c) = (c+1)·(a+b+1)` (or similar), which makes both rules strictly decreasing:
  - Rule 1: `[Velk](x,y,Mek) > [x]` since the left side has the extra positive factor.
  - Rule 2: `[Velk](x,y,Oru(n))` involves `Oru(n)`'s contribution `> [n]`'s, and `Tarn(y, Velk(x,y,n))` reduces the `Oru`-count in the third argument by one.

**Simpler and fully self-contained argument:** Every rewrite step **strictly decreases the total number of `Oru` and `Mek` symbols occurring in third-argument positions of `Velk` occurrences**, i.e., the third argument of any `Velk` is a *fixed, non-growing structure* that is only ever peeled down:

- Rule 1 removes a `Velk` whose third argument is `Mek`, eliminating that occurrence entirely.
- Rule 2 replaces a `Velk` on `Oru(n)` with a `Velk` on `n`, strictly shrinking that third argument's constructor depth — and crucially, **no rule ever produces a `Velk` whose third argument is larger** (the recursive call uses `n`, a strict subterm).

Since neither rule can ever construct a new, larger `Oru`/`Mek` third-argument, the multiset of `Oru`-depths of the third arguments of all `Velk` subterms strictly decreases under the multiset ordering at every step. This is a well-founded ordering, so **no infinite reduction is possible**.

## Conclusion

Strong normalization follows purely from the given rules, using only the observation that the recursion structurally descends on the third argument (a subterm-based measure). **No external structural axioms or imported object-level assumptions are needed** — the well-founded measure is derivable directly from the syntactic form of the two rules.
