# Assessment: Yes, strong normalization can be established from the given rules alone.

## Reasoning

The system has two rules operating on the symbol `Velk`:

```
Velk(x, y, Mek)     -> x
Velk(x, y, Oru(n))  -> Tarn(y, Velk(x, y, n))
```

**Key structural observations:**

1. **Only `Velk` is a redex-forming symbol.** The symbols `Mek`, `Oru`, `Tarn` never appear at the head of a left-hand side, so they are pure constructors. No rewrite can ever be triggered by them.

2. **The third argument strictly decreases in constructor size.** In rule 2, `Velk(x, y, Oru(n))` reduces to a term whose only new `Velk`-subterm is `Velk(x, y, n)`, where `n` is a proper subterm of `Oru(n)`. Rule 1 removes the `Velk` entirely. So every rewrite step involving a given `Velk` occurrence peels off exactly one `Oru` layer or eliminates the `Velk`.

3. **No new `Velk` symbols are created.** The right-hand sides contain at most as many `Velk` occurrences as the left-hand sides:
   - Rule 1: 1 `Velk` → 0 `Velk`.
   - Rule 2: 1 `Velk` → 1 `Velk` (with a strictly smaller third argument).
   
   Crucially, the variables `x`, `y`, `n` on the right are copied verbatim, and no `Velk` symbol wraps around them beyond the single recursive occurrence. No duplication of `Velk` occurs.

**A termination measure derivable purely from the rules:**

Define a weight on terms by counting `Oru`-nested-under-`Velk` structure. Concretely, assign to each `Velk`-subterm a measure equal to the number of `Oru` symbols in its third argument, and take the multiset of these measures over all `Velk` occurrences. Each rewrite step:
- Rule 2 replaces a `Velk` of measure `k+1` with one of measure `k` (strict decrease, no other `Velk` measures affected since `x`, `y` are copied unchanged).
- Rule 1 deletes a `Velk` (removes an element from the multiset).

Under the multiset ordering (well-founded, since it is built over the well-founded natural numbers), every step strictly decreases the measure. Hence no infinite reduction sequence exists.

This argument uses **only** what is derivable from the rules themselves:
- the constructor/defined-symbol partition (read off syntactically),
- the subterm relation `n ◁ Oru(n)` (a purely syntactic fact),
- non-duplication of the defined symbol (read off the right-hand sides).

## Conclusion

Strong normalization **can** be established from the presented system alone. No external structural axioms or object-level assumptions are needed — a simple syntactic/subterm-based measure (equivalently, this fits a standard recursive-path-ordering or lexicographic/multiset argument with `Velk` as the only defined symbol) suffices.
