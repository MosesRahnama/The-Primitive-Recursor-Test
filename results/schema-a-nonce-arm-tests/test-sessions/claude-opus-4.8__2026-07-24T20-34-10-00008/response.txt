Raw assessment: **Yes, strong normalization can be established from these rules alone.**

## Reasoning

The system has two rules operating on the `Velk` symbol:

```
Velk(x, y, Mek)     -> x
Velk(x, y, Oru(n))  -> Tarn(y, Velk(x, y, n))
```

**Key structural observation:** Every rule reduces on the *third argument* of `Velk`. The right-hand sides only ever recreate a `Velk` redex in the second rule, and there the third argument strictly decreases: it goes from `Oru(n)` to `n`.

**A simple measure argument suffices.** Assign to each term the count of `Velk`-symbols nested appropriately, or more directly, use a polynomial/structural interpretation. Consider the measure

- interpret `Mek` and any variable/constant as a fixed base value,
- interpret `Oru(n)` as `n + 1`,
- and note the "recursion parameter" of a `Velk` redex is the size of its third argument.

Rule 1 removes a `Velk` symbol entirely (strictly decreasing).
Rule 2 replaces `Velk(x, y, Oru(n))` by `Tarn(y, Velk(x, y, n))`: it consumes one `Oru` from the third argument. The `Oru`-depth of the third argument strictly decreases, and no rule ever *increases* the `Oru`-structure of any `Velk`'s third argument (the RHS of rule 2 reuses `n` unchanged; `Tarn` never gets rewritten).

So a lexicographic / polynomial interpretation such as
- `[Velk](a,b,c) = c·(something) + ...` with `[Oru](c) = c+1`, `[Tarn](a,b)=a+b+1`, `[Mek]=0`

can be tuned to make both rules strictly decreasing:
- Rule 1: `[Velk(x,y,Mek)] = 0·… + x-part > [x]` — achievable.
- Rule 2: each rewrite strips one `Oru`, and `Tarn` is inert, so the total interpretation drops.

Concretely, one termination proof: the multiset/ordinal measure = the `Oru`-depth of the third argument of the outermost `Velk`. Each application of rule 2 decreases it by one; rule 1 eliminates the redex. Since `Tarn` and `Oru` have no rules, no new redexes with larger measure can ever be generated. Termination follows by well-founded induction on this measure.

**Why no external axioms are needed:** 
- The system is *orthogonal* (left-linear, no critical pairs — the two `Velk` rules have non-overlapping third-argument patterns `Mek` vs `Oru(n)`).
- The recursion is *structural*: the recursive call in rule 2 is on a strict subterm (`n` is a proper subterm of `Oru(n)`).
- `Tarn`, `Oru`, `Mek` are pure constructors (no rules), so they cannot regenerate work.

This is essentially primitive recursion over the unary-numeral structure `Mek`/`Oru`, whose termination is provable purely from the rewrite rules by a subterm-based well-founded ordering — **no imported structural axioms or object-level assumptions are required.**
