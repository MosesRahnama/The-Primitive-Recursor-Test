**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the rewrite system alone, assuming ordinary finite first-order terms.

A recursive path ordering can orient both rules. Choose a precedence with

\[
\mathrm{Velk} > \mathrm{Tarn}
\]

and give `Velk` lexicographic status, prioritizing its third argument. Then:

- `Velk(x,y,Mek) > x` by the subterm property.
- `Velk(x,y,Oru(n)) > Velk(x,y,n)` because \(Oru(n) > n\) by the subterm property.
- Consequently,
  `Velk(x,y,Oru(n)) > Tarn(y,Velk(x,y,n))`,
  since `Velk > Tarn` and the left side exceeds both arguments of `Tarn`.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded simplification ordering. The duplication of `y` in the second rule does not undermine this ordering. No additional structural or object-level axioms are needed.
