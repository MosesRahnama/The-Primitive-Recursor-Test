**Conclusion:** The helper strategy is **unsound as written**. Both lemmas are false as stated, because the measure `kappa` does not strictly decrease on the `R_rec_succ` step when the argument `n` is itself a `delta`.

---

### 1. How `kappa` behaves on `recDelta`

For any `b, s, n` the definition gives two cases:

* If the third argument is a `delta`, `kappa` adds one:
  \[
  \kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n)) \;=\; \max\bigl(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n\bigr)+1
  \tag{1}
  \]

* Otherwise it does not:
  \[
  \kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n) \;=\; \max\bigl(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n\bigr)
  \tag{2}
  \]

Crucially, `kappa` **ignores** the `delta` constructor in sub‑terms because `kappa (delta t) = kappa t`.  Therefore wrapping another `delta` around a term that is *already* a `delta` does **not** increase the value of the `max`-expression in (1).

---

### 2. The first lemma is false

**Lemma claimed:**
```lean
kappa (recDelta b s (delta n)) = kappa (recDelta b s n) + 1
```

Take the concrete counter‑example  
\(b = \mathsf{void},\; s = \mathsf{void},\; n = \delta\,\mathsf{void}\).

**Left-hand side**
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\;\mathsf{void}\;\mathsf{void}\;(\delta(\delta\,\mathsf{void})))
&= \max\bigl(\max(\kappa\mathsf{void},\kappa\mathsf{void}),\kappa(\delta\,\mathsf{void})\bigr)+1 \\
&= \max\bigl(\max(0,0),0\bigr)+1 \\
&= 1 .
\end{aligned}
\]

**Right-hand side**
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\;\mathsf{void}\;\mathsf{void}\;(\delta\,\mathsf{void})) + 1
&= \bigl(\max(\max(0,0),\kappa\mathsf{void})+1\bigr)+1 \\
&= (0+1)+1 \\
&= 2 .
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(1 \neq 2\), the equation fails.

**Symbolic explanation.**  
Let \(n = \delta n'\).  Write \(M = \max\bigl(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n'\bigr)\).  
Then

* LHS (by (1)) = \(M + 1\).
* RHS (by (1) applied to the inner `recDelta`) = \((M + 1) + 1 = M + 2\).

So the lemma over‑counts by exactly one whenever `n` is a `delta`.

---

### 3. The second lemma is false

**Lemma claimed:**
```lean
kappa (app s (recDelta b s n)) < kappa (recDelta b s (delta n))
```

Use the same counter‑example  
\(b = \mathsf{void},\; s = \mathsf{void},\; n = \delta\,\mathsf{void}\).

**Left-hand side**
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa\bigl(\mathsf{app}\;\mathsf{void}\;(\mathsf{recDelta}\;\mathsf{void}\;\mathsf{void}\;(\delta\,\mathsf{void}))\bigr)
&= \max\bigl(\kappa\mathsf{void},\;\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\;\mathsf{void}\;\mathsf{void}\;(\delta\,\mathsf{void}))\bigr) \\
&= \max(0,\;1) \\
&= 1 .
\end{aligned}
\]

**Right-hand side**
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\;\mathsf{void}\;\mathsf{void}\;(\delta(\delta\,\mathsf{void})))
&= \max\bigl(\max(0,0),\kappa(\delta\,\mathsf{void})\bigr)+1 \\
&= \max(0,0)+1 \\
&= 1 .
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus we get \(1 < 1\), which is false.  In fact the two sides are **equal**.

**Symbolic explanation for \(n = \delta n'\).**  
Again let \(M = \max\bigl(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n'\bigr)\).

* \(\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n')) = M + 1\)   (by (1)).
* \(\kappa(\mathsf{app}\;s\;(\mathsf{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n')))
   = \max\bigl(\kappa s,\;M+1\bigr) = M+1\)  
  because \(M \ge \kappa s\).
* \(\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta(\delta n')))
   = \max\bigl(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa(\delta n')\bigr)+1
   = \max\bigl(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n'\bigr)+1
   = M+1\).

Hence for any \(b,s,n'\):
\[
\kappa(\mathsf{app}\;s\;(\mathsf{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n'))) \;=\; \kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta(\delta n'))).
\]
The measure **does not decrease**; it stays exactly the same.

---

### 4. Why the strategy fails

The rewrite rule `R_rec_succ` is
\[
\mathsf{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n) \;\longrightarrow\; \mathsf{app}\;s\;(\mathsf{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n),
\]
and it applies to **every** `n`, including `n = delta n'`.  The measure `kappa` was designed to give a `+1` bonus when the third argument of `recDelta` is syntactically a `delta`.  But because `kappa` treats all non‑zero arguments uniformly (it does not count the *depth* of `delta` nesting), stepping from
\[
\mathsf{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta(\delta n')) \quad\text{to}\quad \mathsf{app}\;s\;(\mathsf{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n'))
\]
leaves `kappa` unchanged.  Consequently:

1. `kappa_rec_delta_step` is an over‑generalisation: it is true only when `n` is **not** a `delta`, but the statement quantifies over all `Trace`.
2. `kappa_rec_succ_drop` is not provable as written, because the desired strict inequality becomes an equality in the exact case where `n` is a `delta`.

Since the helper lemmas are false, they cannot be used to establish that `kappa` decreases on every `R_rec_succ` step, and the termination argument collapses.  The strategy is therefore **unsound**.
