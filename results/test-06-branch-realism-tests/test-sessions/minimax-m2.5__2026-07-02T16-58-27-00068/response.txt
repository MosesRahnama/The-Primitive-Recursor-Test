**Conclusion**

The two lemmas are indeed correct as they stand.  They follow directly from the definition of `kappa` and from elementary properties of `Nat.max`.  Below is a short “by‑hand” check that shows exactly why each lemma holds.

---

### 1.  `kappa_rec_delta_step`

\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\texttt{delta}\;n))
&= \texttt{Nat.max}\;(\texttt{Nat.max}\;\kappa b\;\kappa s)\;\kappa n \;+\;1 
\qquad\text{(definition of }\kappa\text{ on the }\delta\text{ case)}\\[4pt]
\kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)
&= \texttt{Nat.max}\;(\texttt{Nat.max}\;\kappa b\;\kappa s)\;\kappa n 
\qquad\text{(definition of }\kappa\text{ on the catch‑all case)} .
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence  

\[
\kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\texttt{delta}\;n))
\;=\;
\kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)\;+\;1 .
\]

So the statement

```
kappa (recDelta b s (delta n)) = kappa (recDelta b s n) + 1
```

is a definitional equality, which makes the lemma sound (the `sorry` is only a placeholder for the proof).

---

### 2.  `kappa_rec_succ_drop`

We must show  

\[
\kappa\bigl(\texttt{app}\;s\;(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)\bigr)
\;<\;
\kappa\bigl(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\texttt{delta}\;n)\bigr).
\]

Using the definitions of `kappa`:

* **Left‑hand side**

  \[
  \kappa(\texttt{app}\;s\;(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n))
  = \texttt{Nat.max}\;\kappa s\;\kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n).
  \]

* **Right‑hand side** (via `kappa_rec_delta_step`)

  \[
  \kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\texttt{delta}\;n))
  = \kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n) + 1 .
  \]

Thus the required inequality becomes  

\[
\tag{★}
\texttt{Nat.max}\;\kappa s\;\kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)
\;<\;
\kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n) + 1 .
\]

Now observe the definition of `kappa` on a generic `recDelta`:

\[
\kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)
= \texttt{Nat.max}\;(\texttt{Nat.max}\;\kappa b\;\kappa s)\;\kappa n .
\]

Consequently  

\[
\kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n) \;\ge\; \kappa s .
\]

Therefore the maximum on the left of (★) can never be larger than
\(\kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)\); it is exactly that value.  In other
words

\[
\texttt{Nat.max}\;\kappa s\;\kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)
= \kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n).
\]

Substituting into (★) yields the trivial true statement  

\[
\kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)
\;<\;
\kappa(\texttt{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n) + 1 .
\]

Thus the inequality holds for **every** choice of `b`, `s`, and `n`,
so the lemma `kappa_rec_succ_drop` is sound.

### Summary

Both lemmas are logically valid:

* `kappa_rec_delta_step` is an immediate consequence of the definition of
  `kappa` on the `recDelta` constructor.
* `kappa_rec_succ_drop` follows from the monotonicity of `Nat.max` and the
  fact that `kappa(recDelta b s n)` is always at least as large as
  `kappa s`.

Hence the “helper strategy” (these two lemmas) is sound as written.
