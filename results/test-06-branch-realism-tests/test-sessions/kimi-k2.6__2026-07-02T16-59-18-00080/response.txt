**Assessment:** The helper strategy is **unsound as written**.  
Both lemmas are false for traces where the variable `n` is itself a `delta` term. Since the rewrite rule `R_rec_succ` quantifies over *all* traces `n`, the proposed measure `kappa` does not strictly decrease on every valid rewrite step.

---

### 1. Why `kappa_rec_delta_step` fails

The definition of `kappa` on `recDelta` is:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n)) &= \max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)+1,\\[2pt]
\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n) &= \max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)\quad\text{(if the third arg is **not** a $\delta$)}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The theorem claims

\[
\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n))=\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)+1
\]

for **every** `b, s, n : Trace`.  This holds only when `n` is *not* a `delta`.  If `n` itself has the form `delta n'`, the left-hand side uses the inner `n'` once, while the right-hand side adds an extra `+1` from the recursive call on `n = delta n'`.

**Counter-example.** Take `b = void`, `s = void`, `n = delta void`.

*LHS:*
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;\text{void}\;\text{void}\;(\delta(\delta\;\text{void})))
&= \max(\max(\kappa\text{void},\kappa\text{void}),\kappa(\delta\;\text{void}))+1 \\
&= \max(\max(0,0),0)+1 = 1.
\end{aligned}
\]

*RHS:*
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;\text{void}\;\text{void}\;(\delta\;\text{void}))+1
&= \bigl(\max(\max(\kappa\text{void},\kappa\text{void}),\kappa\text{void})+1\bigr)+1 \\
&= (0+1)+1 = 2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Since \(1\neq 2\), the equality is false.

*General calculation when \(n=\delta n'\):*
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n))
&= \max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n')+1,\\[2pt]
\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)+1
&= \bigl(\max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n')+1\bigr)+1.
\end{aligned}
\]
The right-hand side is exactly one larger than the left-hand side.

---

### 2. Why `kappa_rec_succ_drop` fails

The theorem claims

\[
\kappa(\text{app}\;s\;(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)) \;<\; \kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n)).
\]

First observe that for **any** `b, s, n` we always have
\[
\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)\ge \kappa s,
\]
because the `recDelta` case of `kappa` takes a maximum that includes `κ s`.  Consequently
\[
\kappa(\text{app}\;s\;(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n))
= \max(\kappa s,\;\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n))
= \kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n).
\]

So the desired strict inequality is equivalent to
\[
\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n) \;<\; \kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n)).
\tag{★}
\]

*If `n` is **not** a `delta`*, then
\[
\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n))
= \max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)+1
= \kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)+1,
\]
and \((★)\) holds.

*If `n = delta n'`*, however, both sides of \((★)\) compute the **same** number:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;n)
&= \kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n'))
= \max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n')+1,\\[4pt]
\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta n))
&= \kappa(\text{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\delta(\delta n')))
= \max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa(\delta n'))+1 \\
&= \max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n')+1.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the two quantities are equal, contradicting the required strict inequality.

**Counter-example (same instantiation).** With `b = void`, `s = void`, `n = delta void`:

*LHS:*
\[
\kappa(\text{app}\;\text{void}\;(\text{recDelta}\;\text{void}\;\text{void}\;(\delta\;\text{void})))
= \max(0,\;1) = 1.
\]

*RHS:*
\[
\kappa(\text{recDelta}\;\text{void}\;\text{void}\;(\delta(\delta\;\text{void}))) = 1.
\]

The statement \(1 < 1\) is false.

---

### 3. Conclusion

The rewrite rule `R_rec_succ` applies to **all** traces `n`, including those of the form `delta n'`. In exactly those cases:

* `kappa_rec_delta_step` over-counts by one, and
* `kappa_rec_succ_drop` collapses to a non-strict equality.

Because the universal quantification in both theorems admits traces where `n` is itself a `delta`, the helper lemmas are not provable and the strategy of proving termination by a strict decrease of `kappa` is **unsound as written**. To repair the argument one would need a measure that accounts for the *depth* of nested `delta`s in the third argument of `recDelta` (or a lexicographic measure), rather than the flat maximum used here.
