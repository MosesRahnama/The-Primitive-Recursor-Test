The helper strategy is **unsound**. Both theorems `kappa_rec_delta_step` and `kappa_rec_succ_drop` fail for inputs where the third argument of `recDelta` begins with a `delta` constructor. Below is the precise breakdown.

---

### **1. Failure of `kappa_rec_delta_step`**
**Claimed**:  
`kappa (recDelta b s (delta n)) = kappa (recDelta b s n) + 1`  
**Counterexample**: Let `n = delta m` for arbitrary `m`.  
- **LHS**:  
  `kappa (recDelta b s (delta (delta m)))`  
  → Matches `.recDelta _ _ (.delta _)` clause:  
  `= Nat.max (Nat.max (kappa b) (kappa s)) (kappa (delta m)) + 1`  
  `= Nat.max (Nat.max (kappa b) (kappa s)) (kappa m) + 1` (by `kappa_delta`)  
- **RHS**:  
  `kappa (recDelta b s (delta m)) + 1`  
  → `recDelta b s (delta m)` matches `.recDelta _ _ (.delta _)` clause:  
  `= [Nat.max (Nat.max (kappa b) (kappa s)) (kappa m) + 1] + 1`  
  `= Nat.max (Nat.max (kappa b) (kappa s)) (kappa m) + 2`  
- **Result**: LHS = `X + 1`, RHS = `X + 2` (where `X = Nat.max (Nat.max (kappa b) (kappa s)) (kappa m)`).  
  **Equality fails**. The theorem is false when `n` has a top-level `delta`.

---

### **2. Failure of `kappa_rec_succ_drop`**
**Claimed**:  
`kappa (app s (recDelta b s n)) < kappa (recDelta b s (delta n))`  
**Counterexample**: Same `n = delta m`.  
- **LHS**:  
  `kappa (app s (recDelta b s (delta m)))`  
  `= Nat.max (kappa s, kappa (recDelta b s (delta m)))` (by `kappa_app`)  
  `= Nat.max (kappa s, [Nat.max (Nat.max (kappa b) (kappa s)) (kappa m) + 1])`  
  Since `Nat.max (Nat.max (kappa b) (kappa s)) (kappa m) ≥ kappa s`, this simplifies to:  
  `= Nat.max (Nat.max (kappa b) (kappa s)) (kappa m) + 1`  
- **RHS**:  
  `kappa (recDelta b s (delta (delta m)))`  
  `= Nat.max (Nat.max (kappa b) (kappa s)) (kappa (delta m)) + 1`  
  `= Nat.max (Nat.max (kappa b) (kappa s)) (kappa m) + 1` (by `kappa_delta`)  
- **Result**: LHS = RHS = `X + 1`.  
  **Strict inequality fails**. The rewrite step `R_rec_succ` does *not* decrease `kappa` when `n` has a top-level `delta`.

---

### **3. Critical Implication for Termination**
The rewrite rule `R_rec_succ`:  
`Step (recDelta b s (delta n)) (app s (recDelta b s n))`  
requires `kappa` to *strictly decrease* for termination. However:  
- When `n = delta m`, `kappa` is **unchanged** (as shown above).  
- Concrete instance:  
  `recDelta b s (delta (delta void)) → app s (recDelta b s (delta void))`  
  Both sides have identical `kappa` value (`max(max(kappa b, kappa s), 0) + 1`).  
- Since the rewrite system applies rules *only at the top level* (no contextual rules in `Step`), this step is valid but **non-decreasing** in `kappa`.  
- A termination measure *must* strictly decrease on every rewrite step. This violation invalidates `kappa` as a termination measure for `R_rec_succ`.

---

### **Why the Strategy Fails Fundamentally**
The definition of `kappa` for `.recDelta b s (.delta n)` adds `+1` *only* for the outermost `delta`, ignoring nested `delta` structure in `n`. Thus:  
- `kappa (recDelta b s (delta (delta void))) = kappa (recDelta b s (delta void))`  
  (both equal `max(max(kappa b, kappa s), 0) + 1`).  
- The measure does **not** reflect the "size" of the third argument beyond its top constructor.  
- Consequently, `kappa` cannot distinguish terms with differing depths of `delta` in the recursion argument, breaking the well-foundedness required for termination.

---

### **Conclusion**
The helper theorems are **mathematically incorrect** for non-trivial inputs (where `n` has a top-level `delta`), and the underlying measure `kappa` **fails to decrease** on valid rewrite steps of `R_rec_succ`. The strategy is unsound for proving termination. No modification to the proofs (e.g., filling `sorry`) can salvage the theorems—the definitions of `kappa` and the rewrite rules are inherently mismatched for this termination argument. A sound approach would require redefining `kappa` to account for the *full depth* of `delta` nesting in the third argument of `recDelta` (e.g., summing `+1` per `delta`), but as written, the strategy is invalid.
