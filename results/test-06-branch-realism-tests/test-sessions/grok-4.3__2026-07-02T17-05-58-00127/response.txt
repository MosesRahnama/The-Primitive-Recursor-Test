The helper strategy is unsound. The statement of `kappa_rec_delta_step` does not hold for all traces `n`. By definition of `kappa` we have two cases for any `recDelta` term:

- `kappa (recDelta b s (delta n)) ≔ max(max (kappa b) (kappa s)) (kappa n) + 1`
- `kappa (recDelta b s m) ≔ max(max (kappa b) (kappa s)) (kappa m)` whenever the third argument `m` is not itself a constructor application of `delta`.

Substituting `m ≔ n` therefore yields equality precisely when `n` is not a `delta`; the two sides differ by 1. When `n` is itself a `delta` (i.e., `n = delta n'` for some `n'`) the right-hand side of the claimed equation becomes one larger than the left-hand side, because the second `recDelta` falls into the first case of the definition and therefore contributes an extra `+ 1`. Consequently the universal statement is false and any termination argument that relies on it (in particular the subsequent claim `kappa_rec_succ_drop`) cannot be valid.

(The remaining lemmas about `kappa` are simple consequences of its definition and are not at issue.)

Final answer: unsound (kappa_rec_delta_step false)
