### Assessment
The helper strategy is **not sound** as written. Both theorems are false (they do not hold for arbitrary `n`), so they cannot be used in a termination argument. The second theorem additionally depends on the first via `rw`, which is invalid.

### Calculations
Write \(M := \max(\kappa b, \kappa s)\) and \(K_n := \kappa n\) for brevity. By definition of \(\kappa\):
- \(\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\mathsf{delta}\,n)) = \max(M, K_n) + 1\),
- \(\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n)\) depends on the shape of \(n\).

#### Failure of `kappa_rec_delta_step`
The claimed equality is
\[
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\mathsf{delta}\,n)) = \kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n) + 1.
\]
- **Case \(n = \mathsf{delta}\,m\)** (so \(K_n = \kappa m\)):  
  \[
  \text{LHS} = \max(M, K_n) + 1, \qquad
  \text{RHS} = \bigl(\max(M, K_n) + 1\bigr) + 1.
  \]
  These are unequal.
- **Case \(n\) is not a \(\mathsf{delta}\)**: the equality holds, but the universal claim does not.

Concrete counter-example: take \(n = \mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void}\). Then both sides evaluate to \(M+1\) on the left of the claimed equality and \(M+2\) on the right.

#### Failure of `kappa_rec_succ_drop`
The claimed inequality is
\[
\kappa(\mathsf{app}\,s\,(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n)) < \kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\mathsf{delta}\,n)).
\]
The left-hand side is \(\max(\kappa s, \kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n))\).  
- **Case \(n = \mathsf{delta}\,m\)**: both sides equal \(\max(M, K_n) + 1\), so the strict inequality fails.  
- **Case \(n\) is not a \(\mathsf{delta}\)**: the inequality holds, but again the universal claim does not.

(The same concrete counter-example as above yields equality rather than a strict decrease.)

Thus neither lemma is true for all inputs, and the rewrite in the second lemma is unjustified.
