## Assessment: the strategy is **unsound**.

### Issue with `kappa_rec_delta_step`

The lemma asserts, for arbitrary `b s n : Trace`,
$$\kappa(\mathrm{recDelta}\ b\ s\ (\delta\ n)) \;=\; \kappa(\mathrm{recDelta}\ b\ s\ n) + 1.$$

This is **false** in general, because $\kappa$ is *invariant* under the $\delta$ constructor (`kappa_delta`) and the `recDelta` case split depends on whether the *outer* argument is a $\delta$.

**Counterexample.** Take $b = s = \mathsf{void}$ and $n = \delta\ \mathsf{void}$.

- LHS: $\kappa(\mathrm{recDelta}\ \mathsf{void}\ \mathsf{void}\ (\delta\ (\delta\ \mathsf{void})))$. The outer argument is a $\delta$, so the *first* `kappa` clause fires:
$$\max(\max(0,0),\kappa(\delta\ \mathsf{void}))+1 \;=\; \max(0,0)+1 \;=\; 1.$$

- RHS: $\kappa(\mathrm{recDelta}\ \mathsf{void}\ \mathsf{void}\ (\delta\ \mathsf{void}))+1$. Now $n = \delta\ \mathsf{void}$ is itself a $\delta$, so $\kappa(\mathrm{recDelta}\ b\ s\ n)$ again matches the *first* clause and equals
$$\max(\max(0,0),0)+1 \;=\; 1,$$
giving RHS $= 1+1 = 2$.

So $1 \neq 2$, contradicting the lemma. The pattern that "wrapping in $\delta$ adds exactly $1$ to $\kappa$" breaks as soon as $n$ is itself a $\delta$-term — wrapping collapses arbitrarily many $\delta$s into a single $+1$ in $\kappa$.

### Cascade into `kappa_rec_succ_drop`

Even ignoring the first lemma, `kappa_rec_succ_drop` itself fails in the same regime. With $b = s = \mathsf{void}$ and $n = \delta\ \mathsf{void}$:

- $\kappa(\mathrm{app}\ \mathsf{void}\ (\mathrm{recDelta}\ \mathsf{void}\ \mathsf{void}\ (\delta\ \mathsf{void}))) = \max(0,\, 1) = 1$.
- $\kappa(\mathrm{recDelta}\ \mathsf{void}\ \mathsf{void}\ (\delta\ (\delta\ \mathsf{void}))) = 1$.

The two sides are *equal*, so the strict inequality `<` does not hold either. The intended rewrite after `kappa_rec_delta_step` would only make things worse — and the underlying step is unsound.

### Why the strategy cannot be patched locally

Any fix needs $\kappa$ to record the number of $\delta$-wrappers (a "$\delta$-depth" measure), since $\kappa$ deliberately ignores them. As written, neither `kappa_rec_delta_step` nor `kappa_rec_succ_drop` is provable in general, so the termination strategy collapses at its first step.
