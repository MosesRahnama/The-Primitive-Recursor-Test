The helper strategy is not sound as written: both intended helper theorems are false for traces where `n` is itself a `delta`.

Let

\[
B := \max(\kappa b)(\kappa s).
\]

By definition,

\[
\kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ b\ s\ (\delta n))
= \max(B,\kappa n)+1.
\]

However, `kappa (recDelta b s n)` depends on the *outer syntactic form* of `n`.

If `n = delta m`, then

\[
\kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ b\ s\ n)
= \kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ b\ s\ (\delta m))
= \max(B,\kappa m)+1.
\]

Since `kappa (delta m) = kappa m`, the left side of `kappa_rec_delta_step` becomes

\[
\kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ b\ s\ (\delta(\delta m)))
= \max(B,\kappa m)+1,
\]

while its claimed right side is

\[
\kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ b\ s\ (\delta m))+1
= \max(B,\kappa m)+2.
\]

These are unequal.

A concrete counterexample is `b = void`, `s = void`, and `n = delta void`:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ void\ void\ (\delta(\delta void)))
&= 1,\\
\kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ void\ void\ (\delta void)) + 1
&= 1+1=2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus `kappa_rec_delta_step` asserts `1 = 2` in this instance.

The second theorem fails on the same example. Its left side is

\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa(\operatorname{app}\ void\ (\operatorname{recDelta}\ void\ void\ (\delta void)))
&= \max(0,1)\\
&=1,
\end{aligned}
\]

whereas its right side is

\[
\kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ void\ void\ (\delta(\delta void))) = 1.
\]

So the desired inequality becomes

\[
1 < 1,
\]

which is false.

The underlying issue is that `kappa` treats a `recDelta` term specially only when its third argument is syntactically of the form `delta _`, while `kappa_delta` erases `delta` in the measure. Consequently, adding an outer `delta` does not consistently add one to the measure: when the argument was already a `delta`, the old `recDelta` already received the special `+ 1`.

Moreover, `kappa` cannot be a globally strict decreasing measure for all the displayed rewrite rules anyway. For example,

\[
\kappa(\operatorname{merge}\ void\ t)=\kappa t,
\]

so `R_merge_void_left` preserves `kappa`; likewise `R_merge_void_right` and `R_merge_cancel` can preserve it. Hence a termination argument requiring strict decrease of this `kappa` on every `Step` would fail independently of the two `recDelta` lemmas.
