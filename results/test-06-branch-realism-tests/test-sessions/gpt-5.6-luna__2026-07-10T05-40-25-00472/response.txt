The helper strategy is not sound as written. Both theorems are false for some syntactic shapes of `n`.

Let

\[
A = \max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\qquad x=\kappa n.
\]

By definition,

\[
\kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ b\ s\ (\delta n))
= \max(A,x)+1.
\]

However, the right-hand side of `kappa_rec_delta_step` depends on whether `n` itself is syntactically a `delta`:

- If `n` is not of the form `delta m`, then
  \[
  \kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ b\ s\ n)=\max(A,x),
  \]
  so the claimed equality holds.
- If `n = delta m`, then
  \[
  \kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ b\ s\ n)
  = \max(A,\kappa m)+1
  = \max(A,x)+1.
  \]
  Thus the claimed equation would assert
  \[
  \max(A,x)+1 = \max(A,x)+2,
  \]
  which is false.

A concrete counterexample is `b = s = void` and `n = delta void`:

\[
\kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ void\ void\ (\delta(\delta\ void)))=1,
\]
whereas
\[
\kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ void\ void\ (\delta\ void))+1
=1+1=2.
\]

The second theorem is also false. Take `b = s = void` and `n = delta void`. Then

\[
\kappa(\operatorname{recDelta}\ void\ void\ (\delta(\delta\ void)))=1,
\]

while

\[
\begin{aligned}
\kappa(\operatorname{app}\ void\
  (\operatorname{recDelta}\ void\ void\ (\delta\ void)))
&=\max(0,1)\\
&=1.
\end{aligned}
\]

So the claimed strict inequality becomes \(1<1\), which is false.

Consequently, rewriting with `kappa_rec_delta_step` cannot establish the intended termination decrease: that equality is invalid precisely when the predecessor argument is already syntactically a `delta`.
