Yes.  Both helper lemmas follow immediately from the definition of the structural
measure `kappa`, so the strategy that plans to use them in a termination proof is
sound.

Notation.  Put  

    B := kappa b,     S := kappa s,     N := kappa n,  
    M := Nat.max (Nat.max B S) N.

1.  Expansion of the two terms that occur in `kappa_rec_delta_step`.

    kappa (recDelta b s (delta n))
      = Nat.max (Nat.max B S) N + 1    by the first equation of `kappa`
      = M + 1.

    kappa (recDelta b s n)
      = Nat.max (Nat.max B S) N        by the second equation of `kappa`
      = M.

    Hence                             M + 1 = M + 1
    and therefore                     kappa (recDelta b s (delta n))
                                     = kappa (recDelta b s n) + 1.

    So `kappa_rec_delta_step` is correct.

2.  Expansion of the two sides that occur in `kappa_rec_succ_drop`.

    Left-hand side:

      kappa (app s (recDelta b s n))
        = Nat.max (kappa s) (kappa (recDelta b s n))    by `kappa_app`
        = Nat.max S M                                   (definition of M)
        = M                                             because M ≥ S.

    Right-hand side:

      kappa (recDelta b s (delta n)) = M + 1            from (1).

    Because, for natural numbers, M < M + 1 always holds, we obtain  

      kappa (app s (recDelta b s n)) = M < M + 1
      = kappa (recDelta b s (delta n)).

    Thus `kappa_rec_succ_drop` is also valid.

Since the two lemmas are immediate consequences of the definition of `kappa`,
their use as helper facts in a termination proof is sound.
