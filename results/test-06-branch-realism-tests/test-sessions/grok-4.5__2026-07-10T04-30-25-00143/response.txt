The helper strategy is unsound.

The definition of \(\kappa\) is by pattern-matching on the third argument of \(\mathsf{recDelta}\):
\[
\begin{align*}
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\mathsf{delta}\,n))
&=\max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)+1,\\
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n)
&=\max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)
\quad\text{(when \(n\) is not headed by \(\mathsf{delta}\))}.
\end{align*}
\]
Consequently the claimed identity
\[
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\mathsf{delta}\,n))
=\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n)+1
\]
holds only when the third argument of the right-hand side is not itself headed by \(\mathsf{delta}\).  It fails as soon as that argument is a \(\mathsf{delta}\)-term.  Concrete counter-example (\(b=s=\mathsf{void}\), \(n=\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void}\)):
\[
\begin{align*}
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,\mathsf{void}\,\mathsf{void}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void})))
&=\max(\max(0,0),\kappa(\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void}))+1=1,\\
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,\mathsf{void}\,\mathsf{void}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void}))+1
&=(\max(\max(0,0),0)+1)+1=2.
\end{align*}
\]
(The two sides are unequal, so \(\mathsf{kappa\_rec\_delta\_step}\) is false.)

The second helper is obtained from the first by a single rewrite, therefore it is likewise false.  The same counter-example yields
\[
\begin{align*}
\kappa(\mathsf{app}\,\mathsf{void}\,(\mathsf{recDelta}\,\mathsf{void}\,\mathsf{void}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void})))
&=\max(0,1)=1,\\
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,\mathsf{void}\,\mathsf{void}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void})))
&=1,
\end{align*}
\]
so the required strict inequality does not hold.

Because the two lemmas are the only devices offered for showing that rule \(\mathsf{R\_rec\_succ}\) decreases \(\kappa\), the termination argument that rests on them is not valid.
