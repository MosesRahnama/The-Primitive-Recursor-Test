The helper strategy is not sound.

The definition of \(\kappa\) pattern-matches on the third argument of \(\mathsf{recDelta}\), so the two equations
\[
\begin{align*}
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\mathsf{delta}\,n))
&=\max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)+1,\\
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n)
&=\max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)
\end{align*}
\]
hold simultaneously only when \(n\) is not itself headed by \(\mathsf{delta}\).  Consequently the claimed identity
\[
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\mathsf{delta}\,n))
=\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n)+1
\]
fails as soon as \(n=\mathsf{delta}\,m\).  Concretely, take \(b=s=\mathsf{void}\) and \(n=\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void}\).  Then
\[
\begin{align*}
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,\mathsf{void}\,\mathsf{void}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void})))
&=\max(\max(0,0),\kappa(\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void}))+1=1,\\
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,\mathsf{void}\,\mathsf{void}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void}))+1
&=(\max(\max(0,0),0)+1)+1=2.
\end{align*}
\]
(The same numerical counter-example shows that the second helper is likewise false: both sides evaluate to \(1\).)

Even if the first identity were repaired, the second helper would still be inadequate for a termination argument.  The rewrite rule \(\mathsf{R_{rec\_succ}}\) produces a term whose \(\kappa\)-value is identical to that of the redex whenever the third argument is headed by \(\mathsf{delta}\).  Because \(\kappa\) therefore fails to decrease on every one-step reduction, the two lemmas cannot be used (as written) to discharge a well-founded-order obligation.

Final answer: not sound
