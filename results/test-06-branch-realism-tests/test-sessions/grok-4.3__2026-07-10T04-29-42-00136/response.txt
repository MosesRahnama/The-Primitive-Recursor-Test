From the definition of `kappa`, the LHS of the claimed equality is always
\[
\max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)+1.
\]
The RHS is \(\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n)+1\). The value of the latter depends on the head constructor of its third argument:
- When that argument is not a `delta`, we have \(\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n)=\max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)\), so equality holds.
- When that argument *is* a `delta m` (i.e., \(n=\mathsf{delta}\,m\)), we instead have
  \[
  \kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n)=\max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa m)+1.
  \]
  But \(\kappa n=\kappa m\), so the claimed equality becomes
  \[
  \max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa m)+1\qquad=\qquad\bigl(\max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa m)+1\bigr)+1,
  \]
  which is false.

Consequently the rewrite in the second helper is not semantics-preserving, and the termination argument that relies on these two helpers cannot be completed.

Final answer: unsound (kappa_rec_delta_step false)
