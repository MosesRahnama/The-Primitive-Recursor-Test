Based on a detailed analysis of the definition of `kappa` and the proposed helper theorems, **the helper strategy is unsound**. 

Both helper theorems, `kappa_rec_delta_step` and `kappa_rec_succ_drop`, are false as written. 

---

### Analysis of the Definitions

The measure `kappa` behaves as follows on `delta` and `recDelta` constructors:
1. `kappa (delta t) = kappa t` (it collapses nested `delta` wrappers).
2. For `recDelta`, if the third argument is of the form `.delta n`, it adds `+1` to the maximum of the measures of its subterms:
   $$\kappa(\text{recDelta } b\ s\ (\text{delta } n)) = \max(\max(\kappa(b), \kappa(s)), \kappa(n)) + 1$$
3. Otherwise, for any other term $n$:
   $$\kappa(\text{recDelta } b\ s\ n) = \max(\max(\kappa(b), \kappa(s)), \kappa(n))$$

---

### 1. Counterexample for `kappa_rec_delta_step`

The theorem asserts:
$$\kappa(\text{recDelta } b\ s\ (\text{delta } n)) = \kappa(\text{recDelta } b\ s\ n) + 1$$

Let $b = \text{void}$, $s = \text{void}$, and $n = \text{delta void}$. 

* **Left-Hand Side (LHS):**
  $$\kappa(\text{recDelta void void (delta (delta void))})$$
  This matches the pattern `.recDelta b s (.delta n)` where $n = \text{delta void}$.
  $$\begin{aligned}
  \text{LHS} &= \max(\max(\kappa(\text{void}), \kappa(\text{void})), \kappa(\text{delta void})) + 1 \\
  &= \max(\max(0, 0), 0) + 1 \\
  &= 1
  \end{aligned}$$

* **Right-Hand Side (RHS):**
  $$\kappa(\text{recDelta void void (delta void)}) + 1$$
  $$\begin{aligned}
  \text{RHS} &= (\max(\max(\kappa(\text{void}), \kappa(\text{void})), \kappa(\text{void})) + 1) + 1 \\
  &= (0 + 1) + 1 \\
  &= 2
  \end{aligned}$$

Since $1 \neq 2$, the theorem `kappa_rec_delta_step` is **unsound**.

---

### 2. Counterexample for `kappa_rec_succ_drop`

The theorem asserts:
$$\kappa(\text{app } s\ (\text{recDelta } b\ s\ n)) < \kappa(\text{recDelta } b\ s\ (\text{delta } n))$$

Let $b = \text{void}$, $s = \text{void}$, and $n = \text{delta void}$.

* **Left-Hand Side (LHS):**
  $$\begin{aligned}
  \kappa(\text{app void (recDelta void void (delta void))}) &= \max(\kappa(\text{void}), \kappa(\text{recDelta void void (delta void)})) \\
  &= \max(0, \max(\max(0,0), 0) + 1) \\
  &= \max(0, 1) \\
  &= 1
  \end{aligned}$$

* **Right-Hand Side (RHS):**
  Using our calculation from above:
  $$\kappa(\text{recDelta void void (delta (delta void))}) = 1$$

Since the LHS ($1$) is not strictly less than the RHS ($1$), the strict inequality $1 < 1$ fails. Thus, `kappa_rec_succ_drop` is also **unsound**.

---

### Conclusion

The strategy fails because `kappa` ignores the depth of nested `delta` constructors (since $\kappa(\text{delta } t) = \kappa(t)$). Consequently, wrapping a term in multiple `delta`s does not increase its `kappa` value. For any nested induction on `delta`, the termination measure $\kappa$ remains flat after the first step, preventing a proof of strict decrease.
