### Assessment
The helper strategy is **not sound** as written. Both claimed theorems fail to hold for arbitrary `n : Trace` (they are not valid for all inputs), so they cannot support a termination argument for the `R_rec_succ` rule (or any other use of `kappa` as a measure).

### Calculations
By definition of `kappa`:
- \(\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\mathsf{delta}\,n)) = \max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)+1\).
- \(\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,m)\) is *not* uniformly equal to \(\max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa m)\). The second equation holds only when the third argument fails to match the pattern \(\mathsf{delta}\,\_\); otherwise the first equation applies and an extra \(+1\) appears.

**Failure of `kappa_rec_delta_step`.**  
The claimed equality expands to
\[
\max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)+1 \;=\; \kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n)+1,
\]
i.e. we would need \(\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n)=\max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)\).  
This is false whenever \(n\) itself has the form \(\mathsf{delta}\,m\):
\[
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\mathsf{delta}\,m))
  = \max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa m)+1
  = \max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)+1.
\]
Concrete counter-example (\(b=s=\mathsf{void}\), \(n=\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void}\)):
\[
\begin{align*}
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,\mathsf{void}\,\mathsf{void}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void})))
  &= 1,\\
\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,\mathsf{void}\,\mathsf{void}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void}))+1
  &= 2.
\end{align*}
\]
(The two sides differ by 1.)

**Failure of `kappa_rec_succ_drop`.**  
The claimed strict inequality expands to
\[
\max(\kappa s,\;\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n))
  \;<\;
  \max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)+1.
\]
- When \(n\) is *not* of the form \(\mathsf{delta}\,\_\), both sides evaluate to \(M\) versus \(M+1\) (where \(M=\max(\max(\kappa b,\kappa s),\kappa n)\)) and the inequality holds.
- When \(n=\mathsf{delta}\,m\), the left-hand side becomes exactly \(M+1\), so the two sides are equal and the strict inequality fails.

The same concrete values as above give
\[
\kappa(\mathsf{app}\,\mathsf{void}\,(\mathsf{recDelta}\,\mathsf{void}\,\mathsf{void}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void})))
  = 1
  \not<\;
  1
  =
  \kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,\mathsf{void}\,\mathsf{void}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,(\mathsf{delta}\,\mathsf{void}))).
\]
(The attempted proof of the second lemma via `rw [kappa_rec_delta_step]` is therefore also invalid: it rewrites with a false equality.)

### Conclusion
Because the two lemmas do not hold in general, the “helper strategy” cannot be used to discharge a termination obligation that relies on a strict decrease of `kappa` under `R_rec_succ`. A different measure (or a corrected definition of `kappa` that forces \(\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\mathsf{delta}\,n))=\kappa(\mathsf{recDelta}\,b\,s\,n)+1\) for every \(n\)) would be required.
