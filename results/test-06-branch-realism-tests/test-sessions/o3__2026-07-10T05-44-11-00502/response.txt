Let  

  M  := max (max (kappa b) (kappa s)) (kappa n).

(1)  kappa (recDelta b s (delta n))  
     = M + 1                                                    (by definition)

(2)  kappa (recDelta b s n)  
     = M                                                        (by definition)

Hence  

  kappa (recDelta b s (delta n)) = kappa (recDelta b s n) + 1,  

so `kappa_rec_delta_step` is provable.

For the second lemma

  kappa (app s (recDelta b s n))  
    = max (kappa s) (kappa (recDelta b s n))              (by definition)  
    = max (kappa s) M                                     (from (2))  
    = M                                                   (because M ≥ kappa s).

Therefore  

  kappa (app s (recDelta b s n)) = M                      (3)

and, using (1),

  kappa (app s (recDelta b s n)) = M < M + 1
        = kappa (recDelta b s (delta n)),

so `kappa_rec_succ_drop` is also correct.

------------------------------------------------------------
Is the overall “measure-decreases” strategy sound?

To obtain a termination proof from a numeric measure one normally shows

  ∀ t u,  Step t u  →  kappa u < kappa t.

But with the present definition of `kappa` this fails for several rules:

•  R_merge_void_left:  
   kappa (merge void t) = max 0 (kappa t) = kappa t.

•  R_merge_void_right:  
   kappa (merge t void) = kappa t.

•  R_merge_cancel:  
   kappa (merge t t) = kappa t.

•  R_eq_diff:  
   kappa (eqW a b) = max (kappa a) (kappa b)  
   = kappa (integrate (merge a b)).

•  R_int_delta with t = void:  
   kappa (integrate (delta void)) = 0 = kappa void.

In each of these cases a rewrite step leaves `kappa` unchanged, so `kappa` is not
strictly descending along every rewrite.  Consequently the measure by itself
cannot serve as a global termination argument, even though the two helper
lemmas for the `recDelta` rules are valid.

Conclusion:  
– The two stated helper lemmas are correct.  
– However, the strategy of proving termination solely by showing that `kappa`
  decreases on every rewrite step is unsound as written, because several rules
  do not diminish `kappa`.  Additional structure or a refined measure would be
  required to turn the informal termination idea into a complete proof.
