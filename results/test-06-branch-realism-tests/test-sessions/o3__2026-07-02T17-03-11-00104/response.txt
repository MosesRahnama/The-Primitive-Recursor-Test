Yes, the two auxiliary lemmas are correct; the numerical facts they state follow directly from the definition of the structural measure `kappa`.

1.  kappa_rec_delta_step  

   By unfolding the definition of `kappa` we obtain
   •  κ (recDelta b s (delta n))
      = max (max (κ b) (κ s)) (κ n) + 1               (definition, “+ 1”-case)  
   •  κ (recDelta b s n)
      = max (max (κ b) (κ s)) (κ n)                   (definition, ordinary case)

   Hence  
      κ (recDelta b s (delta n)) = κ (recDelta b s n) + 1.  
   This is exactly the content of `kappa_rec_delta_step`.

2.  kappa_rec_succ_drop  

   Abbreviate  

        M := κ (recDelta b s n) =
             max (max (κ b) (κ s)) (κ n).
   
   Observe that
        κ s ≤ max (κ b) (κ s) ≤ M,        -- by definition of max
   so in particular κ s ≤ M.

   Now compute both κ–values that occur in the inequality:

   •  κ (app s (recDelta b s n))
         = max (κ s) (κ (recDelta b s n))   (definition of κ on `app`)
         = max (κ s) M
         = M                                 (because κ s ≤ M).

   •  κ (recDelta b s (delta n))
         = M + 1                             (from lemma 1).

   Consequently  

        κ (app s (recDelta b s n)) = M
        < M + 1 = κ (recDelta b s (delta n)),

   which proves `kappa_rec_succ_drop`.

Because both calculations rely only on the explicit clauses of the `kappa` definition, the helper strategy is arithmetically sound exactly as written.
