The proposed termination measure is not sound, as several rewrite rules can increase the measure.

The measure is interpreted lexicographically on pairs \((p, c)\), where \(p\) is the phase and \(c\) is the cost (i.e., \((p_1, c_1) > (p_2, c_2)\) iff \(p_1 > p_2\) or (\(p_1 = p_2\) and \(c_1 > c_2\))). For the measure to be a valid termination argument, every rule must therefore produce a strictly smaller pair.

- `R_rec_succ`, `R_int_delta`, `R_eq_refl` and `R_eq_diff` all yield a strictly smaller pair.
- `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel` and `R_rec_zero` do not. In each of those four rules the right-hand side may be a term whose phase is 1 (the sole case `recDelta _ _ (delta _)`). The left-hand side of each rule has phase 0, so the first component of the pair increases and the measure grows.

Because there exist one-step derivations that increase the measure, it does not justify termination.

Final answer: not sound (phase can increase)
