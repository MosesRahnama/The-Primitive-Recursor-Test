The proposed measure is not sound: it fails to decrease (in the lexicographic order on \(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\)) under every application of \(\mathsf{Step}\).

To see this explicitly it is enough to examine a single rule. Consider
\[
\mathsf{merge}\;\mathsf{void}\;(\mathsf{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\mathsf{delta}\;n))
\quad\longrightarrow\quad
\mathsf{recDelta}\;b\;s\;(\mathsf{delta}\;n)
\]
(the instance of \(\mathsf{R\_merge\_void\_left}\) whose second argument has phase 1). The left-hand side is headed by \(\mathsf{merge}\), so
\[
\mathsf{phase}(\mathsf{lhs})=0.
\]
The right-hand side matches the sole clause of \(\mathsf{phase}\) that yields 1, so
\[
\mathsf{phase}(\mathsf{rhs})=1.
\]
Hence
\[
\mathsf{measure}(\mathsf{rhs})\;=\;(1,\_)\;\not<_{\mathrm{lex}}\;(0,\_)\;=\;\mathsf{measure}(\mathsf{lhs}).
\]
(The same counter-example works verbatim for \(\mathsf{R\_merge\_void\_right}\) and \(\mathsf{R\_merge\_cancel}\); a similar increase of the phase component occurs for \(\mathsf{R\_rec\_zero}\).)

Because a well-founded measure must decrease on every rewrite step, the pair \((\mathsf{phase},\mathsf{cost})\) does not prove termination of the calculus.

Final answer: not sound
