The proposed lexicographic measure \(( \text{phase}(t), \text{cost}(t) )\) is **not sound** for the given rewrite system. The measure fails because there exist instances where the left‑hand side has a strictly smaller measure than the right‑hand side, violating the requirement of decreasing measures.

Specifically, the rules `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, and `R_rec_zero` can all produce a right‑hand side whose `phase` is 1 while the left‑hand side has `phase` 0. Since the lexicographic order considers the first component first, a change from 0 to 1 increases the measure regardless of the second component. For example:

- `R_merge_void_left` with \( t = \text{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\text{delta}\,n) \):  
  Left: \(\text{phase}=0\), \(\text{cost}=3+\text{cost}\,b+\text{cost}\,s+\text{cost}\,n\);  
  Right: \(\text{phase}=1\), \(\text{cost}=2+\text{cost}\,b+\text{cost}\,s+\text{cost}\,n\).  
  Here \((0, \dots) < (1, \dots)\), so the measure increases.

Thus the measure does not strictly decrease on all rewrite steps and cannot guarantee termination.
