The proposed measure \(\operatorname{measure}(t) = (\operatorname{phase}(t),\operatorname{cost}(t))\) (ordered lexicographically) is not a valid termination measure for \(\operatorname{Step}\).

To see this it is enough to exhibit a single counter-example step on which the measure fails to decrease. Consider the terms
\[
t_1 = \operatorname{merge}\;\operatorname{void}\;(\operatorname{recDelta}\;\operatorname{void}\;\operatorname{void}\;(\operatorname{delta}\;\operatorname{void}))
\]
and
\[
t_2 = \operatorname{recDelta}\;\operatorname{void}\;\operatorname{void}\;(\operatorname{delta}\;\operatorname{void}).
\]
Rule \(\operatorname{R\_merge\_void\_left}\) yields the one-step reduction \(t_1\to t_2\). Direct evaluation of the measure gives
\[
\operatorname{measure}(t_1)=(0,3),\qquad\operatorname{measure}(t_2)=(1,2).
\]
Because the first components satisfy \(0<1\), one has \(\operatorname{measure}(t_1)<\operatorname{measure}(t_2)\) in the lexicographic order, so the measure has increased.

(The same phenomenon occurs for every rule whose right-hand side is an arbitrary subterm that may itself be a \(\operatorname{recDelta}\)-redex of phase 1, i.e. for \(\operatorname{R\_merge\_void\_left}\), \(\operatorname{R\_merge\_void\_right}\), \(\operatorname{R\_merge\_cancel}\) and \(\operatorname{R\_rec\_zero}\). The remaining rules do decrease the measure, but a single increasing rule already renders the candidate measure unsound.)

Final answer: unsound
