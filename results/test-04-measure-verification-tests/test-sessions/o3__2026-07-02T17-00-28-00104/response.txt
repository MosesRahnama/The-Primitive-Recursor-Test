No.  
The pair  

  measure t = (phase t , cost t)

does not decrease strictly along all rewrite steps of Step, therefore it cannot be used as a sound
termination measure.

Why it fails
------------

phase takes value 1 only when the *outermost* constructor is

  recDelta _ _ (delta _)

and 0 otherwise.  
Several rules remove a constructor that is outside such a recDelta term and
thereby move the recDelta-with-delta term to the root of the tree.  
Consequently the first component of the measure can grow from 0 to 1.

Concrete counter-examples

1.  R_merge_void_left  

  merge void t  →  t

   Choose  

  t = recDelta b s (delta n).

   Then

   measure (merge void t) = (0 , 1 + cost t)  
   measure t               = (1 ,        cost t)

   Lexicographic order on pairs of Nats looks at the first component first, so

  (1 , cost t) ≱ (0 , 1 + cost t)

   The measure has increased.

2.  R_merge_void_right and R_merge_cancel fail for the same reason (put such a
   recDelta term at the right or both sides of merge).

3.  R_rec_zero  

  recDelta b s void  →  b

   Take  

  b = recDelta b' s' (delta n').

   Again the phase component goes from 0 to 1.

Because there are legitimate one-step rewrites for which measure(rhs) is not
lexicographically smaller than measure(lhs), the proposed (phase , cost)
measure is not a well-founded descent function for Step.  Hence it is not
sound for proving termination of this rewrite calculus.
