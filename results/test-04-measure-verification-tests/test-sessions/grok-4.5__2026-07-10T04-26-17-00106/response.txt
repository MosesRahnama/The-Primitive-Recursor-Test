The proposed measure \(\operatorname{measure}(t)=(\operatorname{phase}(t),\operatorname{cost}(t))\) is intended to decrease (in the lexicographic order on \(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\)) under every rule of \(\operatorname{Step}\). This fails for several rules, as shown by the following counter-examples (all of which are instances of the given inductive rules).

- \(\operatorname{merge}\,\operatorname{void}\,(\operatorname{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\operatorname{delta}\,n))\to\operatorname{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\operatorname{delta}\,n)\)  
  yields \((\operatorname{phase},\operatorname{cost})=(0,3+\operatorname{cost}(b)+\operatorname{cost}(s)+\operatorname{cost}(n))\mapsto(1,2+\operatorname{cost}(b)+\operatorname{cost}(s)+\operatorname{cost}(n))\).  
  The first component strictly increases, so the pair increases in lexicographic order.

- \(\operatorname{merge}\,(\operatorname{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\operatorname{delta}\,n))\,\operatorname{void}\to\operatorname{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\operatorname{delta}\,n)\)  
  and \(\operatorname{merge}\,t\,t\to t\) (when \(t=\operatorname{recDelta}\,b\,s\,(\operatorname{delta}\,n)\)) produce analogous increases of the first component.

- \(\operatorname{recDelta}\,b\,s\,\operatorname{void}\to b\) (when \(b=\operatorname{recDelta}\,b'\,s'\,(\operatorname{delta}\,n)\))  
  likewise sends phase \(0\) to phase \(1\).

(The remaining rules do decrease the measure, but a single increasing rule already renders the measure unsound for termination.)

Thus the proposed lexicographic measure does not prove termination of \(\operatorname{Step}\).

Final answer: not sound
