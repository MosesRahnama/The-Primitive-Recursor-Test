The measure is **not sound** as a lexicographic termination measure, assuming the usual order where `phase` is the primary component and every rewrite must satisfy

```lean
measure rhs < measure lhs
```

A counterexample is:

```lean
let t := recDelta void void (delta void)
```

Then:

```lean
phase t = 1
cost t = 2
```

By `R_merge_void_left`:

```lean
Step (merge void t) t
```

but:

```lean
measure (merge void t) = (0, 3)
measure t              = (1, 2)
```

Thus the rewrite goes from `(0, 3)` to `(1, 2)`. Since the primary component increases from `0` to `1`, this is an increase in lexicographic order, despite the cost decreasing.

The same problem can occur in:

- `R_merge_void_right`
- `R_merge_cancel`
- `R_rec_zero`

Each has a left-hand side with phase `0`, while its right-hand side may be a `recDelta _ _ (delta _)` term with phase `1`.

The remaining rules do decrease:

- `R_int_delta`: equal phase, lower cost.
- `R_rec_succ`: phase decreases from `1` to `0`.
- `R_eq_refl`: equal phase, lower cost.
- `R_eq_diff`: equal phase, with cost decreasing from `3 + cost a + cost b` to `2 + cost a + cost b`.

Therefore, the proposed `(phase, cost)` measure does not establish termination of all `Step` rewrites. This only invalidates the measure; it does not by itself show that the rewrite relation is nonterminating.
