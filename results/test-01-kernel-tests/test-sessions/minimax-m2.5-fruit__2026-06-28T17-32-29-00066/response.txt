**Reasoning:** Define a triple of measures on terms  

1. \(C(t)\) = number of `cherry` constructors occurring in \(t\)  
2. \(G(t)\) = number of `grape` constructors occurring in \(t\)  
3. \(S(t)\) = the total size (number of constructors) of \(t\)

Order triples lexicographically \((C,G,S)\); this is a well‑founded order on \(\mathbb N^3\).

Now check each rewrite rule:

| Rule | Effect on \((C,G,S)\) |
|------|-----------------------|
| `mango (grape t) → plum` | \(G\) decreases (the only `grape` disappears); \(C\) unchanged; \(S\) also decreases. |
| `peach plum t → t`, `peach t plum → t`, `peach t t → t` | \(C,G\) unchanged; \(S\) decreases. |

Every rule reduces the triple \((C,G,S)\) in the lexicographic order, and no rule ever increases any of these three components while leaving the earlier ones unchanged. Consequently every rewrite sequence must terminate after finitely many steps.
