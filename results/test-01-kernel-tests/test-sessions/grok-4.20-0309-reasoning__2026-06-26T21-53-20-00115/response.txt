**Yes, SN can be established from the given syntax and Step rules alone.**

The inductive definitions fix a ground TRS whose only redexes are terms whose *root* constructor matches one of the eight listed schemas. (No congruence, substitution, or contextual closure is derivable from the presented inductive Step, so none may be assumed.)

Every term is a finite tree. No finite term t satisfies any equation identifying it with a proper subterm of one of the rule RHSs (e.g., no t = merge t t, no t = integrate(merge a b), distinct root constructors rule out t = app s (recΔ …), etc.). Consequently no cycle t →⁺ t is possible.

Moreover, the only rules whose RHS can itself be a redex are:
- the three merge rules (RHS is the surviving argument t; if that t happens to be a root redex the sequence continues);
- R_rec_zero (RHS = b; again, only if b is itself a root redex).

All other rules produce a term whose root constructor matches none of the LHS patterns (void, app …, or integrate(merge …) with argument not headed by delta). Those RHS are therefore normal forms with respect to the given Step.

A straightforward structural induction on term size therefore shows that every one-step reduct is SN:
- merge/rec-zero cases strictly decrease size, so the inductive hypothesis applies directly to the reduct;
- integrate-delta, rec-succ, eq-refl, and eq-diff cases land in an immediately observable normal form (different root constructor), even when size increases.

Hence every reduction sequence is finite. The argument uses only pattern-matching on the inductive constructors, the stated Step rules, and the absence of finite solutions to the forbidden equations—nothing beyond what is derivable from the given file.
