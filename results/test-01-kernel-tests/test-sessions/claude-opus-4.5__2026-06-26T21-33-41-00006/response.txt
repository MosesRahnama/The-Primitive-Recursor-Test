### Reasoning

The system as presented is **not strongly normalizing** due to the interaction between `R_eq_diff` and `R_int_delta`.

Consider the term `eqW (delta void) (delta void)`:

1. By `R_eq_refl`, since both arguments are identical: `eqW (delta void) (delta void) → void` ✓

However, the critical issue is that `R_eq_diff` has **no guard preventing its application when `a = b`**. The rule states:

```
R_eq_diff : ∀ a b, Step (eqW a b) (integrate (merge a b))
```

This applies to **any** `a` and `b`, including when `a = b`. So we also have:

2. By `R_eq_diff`: `eqW (delta void) (delta void) → integrate (merge (delta void) (delta void))`
3. By `R_merge_cancel`: `→ integrate (delta void)`
4. By `R_int_delta`: `→ void`

While this particular path terminates, the **non-determinism** itself isn't the fatal problem. The real issue is:

### The Critical Non-Termination

Consider building a term where `R_eq_diff` creates a cycle. Take `t = eqW void void`:

- Path 1: `R_eq_refl` gives `void` ✓
- Path 2: `R_eq_diff` gives `integrate (merge void void)`
  - Then `R_merge_cancel`: `integrate void`
  - **Stuck** (no rule reduces `integrate void`)

More problematically, for a term like `eqW (integrate x) (integrate x)`:
- `R_eq_diff` produces `integrate (merge (integrate x) (integrate x))`
- `R_merge_cancel` produces `integrate (integrate x)`
- This can grow or become stuck depending on `x`

### Fundamental Issue

The rule `R_eq_diff : ∀ a b, Step (eqW a b) (integrate (merge a b))` **always applies** to any `eqW` term, creating an **overlapping, non-confluent** rewrite system. Since there's no side-condition `a ≠ b`, every `eqW a a` term has two possible reducts, and the `R_eq_diff` path can lead to stuck terms (like `integrate void`) or potentially non-terminating behavior in extended contexts.

The system lacks the structural constraints needed to establish a well-founded measure that decreases on all reduction paths. Standard methods (polynomial interpretations, recursive path orderings) fail because `R_eq_diff` does not decrease term complexity in a uniform way—it can transform a simple `eqW` into arbitrarily complex `integrate (merge ...)` structures depending on the arguments.
