## Assessment: Yes, strong normalization is provable from this calculus alone.

### Reasoning

The system is a first-order rewrite system with no variable binding, and every rule is "size-decreasing" in a defensible well-founded sense. No structural rule is needed beyond what the syntax and the eight `Step` rules provide.

**Per-rule analysis (no rule generates unbounded redexes):**

1. **`R_int_delta` / `R_merge_*` / `R_eq_refl`** — each fires at most once on a given subterm and strictly reduces term size, producing a normal form (`void`) or a non-redex.
2. **`R_rec_succ`** — `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`. The outer `app` wrapper is not a redex for any rule, so it stays inert. The only remaining redex is the inner `recΔ b s n`, whose counter argument has strictly smaller delta-depth. This is the only rule that can appear to "grow" the term, but the recursive call is structurally smaller on the meaningful measure.
3. **`R_eq_diff`** — `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`. After this step, the redex is gone. The residual `integrate(merge a b)` either becomes `void` (one further step if the merge collapses to a `delta`) or is a normal form.

**Why an infinite reduction is impossible:**

The only rule whose application doesn't immediately shrink the term is `R_rec_succ`. But that rule strictly decreases the *delta-depth of the recursion counter* — a natural-number measure. Define, e.g., a lexicographic measure

`μ(t) = ⟨ max{ δ-depth of every recΔ-counter in t }, |t| ⟩`

ordered lexicographically. Every rule decreases `μ`:
- merge/eq_refl/integrate/eq_diff reduce `|t|`.
- rec_succ keeps `|t|` bounded but reduces the δ-depth of its own counter (and can't reintroduce a deeper one elsewhere, since none of the rules build new `delta` nodes under a `recΔ`).

`μ` is well-founded (lexicographic product of `ℕ` and `ℕ`), so by induction on `μ` every reduction sequence is finite. The non-deterministic overlap between `R_eq_refl` and `R_eq_diff` on `eqW a a` is irrelevant — both branches terminate and reach normal forms (possibly distinct).

**Method:** a standard first-order termination argument — no extra rewrite rules, no guards, no side conditions, no imported axioms. The proof is entirely intrinsic to the eight given rules.
