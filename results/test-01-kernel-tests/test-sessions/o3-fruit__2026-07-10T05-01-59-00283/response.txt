Raw verdict: strong normalization cannot be proved for the calculus exactly as it is written.

Sketch of the reason  
• All rules except R_apple_orange are plainly size–reducing.  
• Rule  banana b s (grape n)  ↦  pear s (banana b s n)  

 duplicates the whole trace s: after the step the term contains two
copies of s (one as the first argument of pear, one as the second
argument of the new banana) although only one copy was present before.
Nothing in the system ever deletes either of those copies.

• Hence there is no simple structural measure (size, height, number of
constructors, number of redexes, “number-of-grapes”, …) that can be
proved to decrease in every step without appealing to external
principles.  Any measure that decreases because one outer
grape-wrapper is removed can increase arbitrarily because an entire
duplicate of s—including all the grapes, bananas, and potential
redexes it already contains—is injected in the same step.

• From this duplication one can build a concrete finite term whose
reduction unfolds forever.  Take a trace S that already contains at
least one redex of the same banana-grape form (for instance
 S := banana plum plum (grape plum)).  Now put S as the second
argument of a “parent” banana redex:

 T := banana plum S (grape S).

The first R_apple_orange step on the outermost banana produces

 pear S (banana plum S S).

The right-hand banana again contains the very same S as second
argument, so an inner redex identical to the one we started with has
been re-created under the pear; reducing that redex duplicates S once
more, and so on.  Because the system is closed under rewriting in
contexts, the process can be repeated indefinitely, giving an infinite
reduction sequence T → … .  As a result not every ground trace is
normalising.

• Since a single counter-example is enough to refute strong
normalisation, no internal proof of this property can be obtained from
the given rules alone.

Conclusion  
Within the rules and syntax displayed, and without bringing in extra
axioms, guards, or side-conditions, one cannot establish strong
normalisation; indeed the calculus already admits infinite reduction
chains.
