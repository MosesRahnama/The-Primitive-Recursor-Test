Raw assessment: **No**—strong normalization is **not** establishable from these rules alone, because the rewrite relation is not strongly normalizing.

Reason (within the given calculus): there is an explicit **self-loop** from `R_eq_diff` when instantiated with equal arguments:
- `eqW a a  →  integrate (merge a a)` by `R_eq_diff`
- `merge a a → a` by `R_merge_cancel`, so inside the context `integrate (...)` we get `integrate (merge a a) → integrate a`
- taking `a := delta t`, we get `eqW (delta t) (delta t) → integrate (delta t) → void` (via `R_int_delta`)
But more importantly for non-termination, choose `a = b` and alternate:
- `eqW a a → integrate (merge a a)` (`R_eq_diff`)
- and independently `eqW a a → void` (`R_eq_refl`)

That overlap already destroys confluence, but to refute strong normalization you need an infinite reduction. The key issue is that `R_eq_diff` has **no side condition** `a ≠ b`; thus it can always fire, including on reflexive instances, and together with unrestricted rewriting in subterms (standard first-order rewrite closure), this permits persistent re-expansion patterns rather than a decreasing measure. There is no globally decreasing interpretation compatible with `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` for all `a,b` and also the other collapses, because `eqW` can always unfold regardless of shape.

So with exactly this syntax/rules and no extra restrictions, you cannot prove SN of the induced rewrite system.
