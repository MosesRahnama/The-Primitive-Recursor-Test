**Assessment:** **Yes** – strong normalization can be established from the calculus as presented.

**Reasoning:**  
The rewrite system is first-order and finitely branching. All eight rules can be oriented by a standard reduction order (for instance, a recursive path order with a suitable precedence and status), which implies the one-step relation is well-founded and therefore strongly normalizing.

Key observations:
1. **Subterm decrease.** Several rules replace a term by a proper subterm:
   - `merge void t → t`, `merge t void → t`, `merge t t → t`
   - `recΔ b s void → b`
   By the subterm property of RPO, each of these decreases.

2. **Symbol precedence decrease.** The remaining rules reduce a term to one with a smaller root symbol or to a constant:
   - `integrate (delta t) → void` (requires `integrate > void`)
   - `eqW a a → void` (requires `eqW > void`)
   - `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` (requires `eqW > integrate > merge`)

3. **Recursion decrease.** The critical rule is the iterator:
   - `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`
   Give `recΔ` higher precedence than `app` and a lexicographic status with the third argument dominant. Then:
   - `recΔ(b, s, delta n) > s` by the subterm property,
   - `recΔ(b, s, delta n) > recΔ(b, s, n)` because `delta n > n` (subterm property) and the status compares the third argument first,
   - Hence `recΔ(b, s, delta n) > app(s, recΔ(b, s, n))`.

Because no right-hand side introduces a new `delta` at the root, the third argument of any `recΔ` can only undergo rule 6 finitely many times (once per nested `delta` in its original spine). The RPO ordering captures this well-foundedness globally.

Since every rule strictly decreases in this well-founded order and the order is compatible with arbitrary contexts, no infinite reduction sequence exists. Therefore the system is strongly normalizing without any imported axioms, extra rules, or side conditions.
