Yes. Strong normalization can be established from these rules alone; no extra structural or object-level axioms are needed.

A standard recursive path ordering / lexicographic path ordering argument suffices. For example, choose a precedence with at least

\[
\mathsf{recΔ} \succ \mathsf{app}, \qquad
\mathsf{eqW} \succ \mathsf{integrate} \succ \mathsf{merge},
\]

and place `void` below the relevant constructors. Give `recΔ` lexicographic status on its arguments. Then:

- The `integrate`, `merge`, and `eqW` rules either reduce to a proper subterm or replace a higher-precedence root by lower-precedence roots.
- `recΔ b s void → b` is a subterm reduction.
- The potentially concerning rule
  \[
  \mathsf{recΔ}\ b\ s\ (\delta n)
  \to
  \mathsf{app}\ s\ (\mathsf{recΔ}\ b\ s\ n)
  \]
  is oriented because `recΔ` is above `app`, the left side is greater than `s` by the subterm property, and
  \[
  \mathsf{recΔ}\ b\ s\ (\delta n)
  >
  \mathsf{recΔ}\ b\ s\ n
  \]
  lexicographically, since `delta n > n` by the subterm property.

Thus every rule strictly decreases a well-founded simplification ordering. This also covers ordinary rewriting at arbitrary subterm positions, not merely the root-only `Step` relation literally declared in the file.

The duplication of `s` in `R_rec_succ` does not create a loop: the recursive `recΔ` occurrence has a strictly smaller third argument, and the path-ordering proof accounts for the duplicated argument.
