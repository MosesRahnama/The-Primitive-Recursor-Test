Yes. Strong normalization is derivable from the presented syntax and rules alone.

A standard recursive/lexicographic path ordering can orient every rule. Choose precedence constraints such as:

- `banana > pear`
- `cherry > mango`, `cherry > peach`
- `mango > plum`

with lexicographic status for `banana`. Then:

- The `peach` rules and `R_banana_zero` reduce to proper subterms.
- `mango (grape t) → plum` and `cherry a a → plum` decrease by precedence.
- `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)` decreases because `cherry` dominates both `mango` and `peach`.
- In  
  `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`,  
  `banana` dominates `pear`, while the nested `banana` decreases lexicographically since `grape n` is greater than its proper subterm `n`.

Thus all rules strictly decrease a well-founded syntactic ordering. This proves strong normalization without additional rewrite rules, guards, axioms, or object-level assumptions. For the literal Lean `Step` relation, which has no contextual-closure constructors, termination is even more immediate; the same ordering also handles the usual compatible contextual closure.
