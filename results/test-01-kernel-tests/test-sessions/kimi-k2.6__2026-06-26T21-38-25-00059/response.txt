**Assessment: Yes – strong normalization can be established from the calculus alone.**

**Reasoning.**

The relation `Step` is given as an inductive predicate with *only* the listed root-reduction rules; there are no congruence constructors, so a redex must appear exactly at the top level.

Group the rules by what their right-hand side looks like:

1. **Immediate normal forms.**  
   - `R_int_delta` produces `void`.  
   - `R_rec_succ` produces an `app`-headed term.  
   - `R_eq_refl` produces `void`.  
   - `R_eq_diff` produces `integrate (merge a b)`.  

   None of these right-hand sides match any left-hand side of `Step`:
   - `void` has no associated rule.
   - `app` never appears as the head of a left-hand side.
   - `integrate (merge a b)` cannot match `integrate (delta t)` because `merge` and `delta` are distinct constructors of the inductive type `Trace`, and distinct constructors are disjoint (this follows from the standard “no confusion” property of inductive types, which is already inherent in Lean’s logical infrastructure).

   Hence any step taken by one of these four rules terminates the reduction sequence immediately.

2. **Proper subterm reductions.**  
   - `R_merge_void_left`: `merge void t → t`
   - `R_merge_void_right`: `merge t void → t`
   - `R_merge_cancel`: `merge t t → t`
   - `R_rec_zero`: `recΔ b s void → b`

   In each case the reduct is a *proper subterm* of the redex. Therefore the size (number of constructors) strictly decreases.

Since every reduction step either
- lands on a normal form, or
- strictly decreases the term size,

no infinite reduction sequence is possible. A simple well-founded size measure (or just the subterm relation on finite trees) suffices, and this argument uses nothing beyond the syntax and rewrite rules presented—no extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or hidden axioms are required.
