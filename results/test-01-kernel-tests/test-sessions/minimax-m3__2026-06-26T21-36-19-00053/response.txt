# Assessment: **Yes**, strong normalization can be established from the calculus alone.

## Key observations about the rewrite system

1. **No rule produces `delta` or `eqW` as a constructor of its result.**
   - `R_int_delta` *consumes* `integrate (delta _)`.
   - `R_rec_succ` produces `app`, not `delta`.
   - `R_eq_refl` and `R_eq_diff` *consume* `eqW`.
   
   This means the count `E(t) =` number of `eqW` subterms and the count `D(t) =` number of `delta` subterms in a term can only stay the same or decrease along every rewrite step.

2. **`app` is a permanent "absorbing" wrapper.** No rule has `app` on the LHS, so once an `app` is produced (only by `R_rec_succ`), it is a normal form at the top and only its subterms can keep reducing.

3. **`merge` always reduces to a proper subterm.** Rules `R_merge_void_left/right` yield one of the children; `R_merge_cancel` collapses `merge t t` to `t` (size drops from `2|t|+1` to `|t|`). Hence any `merge` chain is finite.

4. **`recΔ` is structurally recursive on the delta-depth of its third argument.** Each `R_rec_succ` strictly decreases the `delta`-count of the third argument (by exactly 1) and wraps the residual in `app`; `R_rec_zero` terminates the recursion. So `recΔ` cannot loop.

5. **`eqW` is fully resolved in bounded time.** 
   - `R_eq_refl`: `eqW a a → void` (1 step, dead end).
   - `R_eq_diff`: `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`, then `merge a b` reduces to some subterm `c` of `a` or `b` (or `void`), giving `integrate c`. Since `delta` is never *produced*, `c` cannot become `delta _` unless it already was, and if it was, the next step is `void`. Otherwise `integrate c` is a normal form.
   
   In either branch, an `eqW` is destroyed in O(1) further steps and never re-introduced.

## Why this suffices for strong normalization

A well-founded measure on `Trace` that witnesses termination:

- Let `E(t)` = number of `eqW` subterms of `t`. 
- Let `R(t)` = the term obtained by stripping all `app` wrappers and reducing all `merge`s to subterms (a well-defined normal form for the merge-subsystem, since merges are confluent and terminating on their own).
- Now `R(t)` contains only `void`, `delta`, `integrate`, `recΔ`, `eqW`. On this restricted language define
$$M(t) \;=\; \bigl(E(R(t)),\; D(R(t)),\; |R(t)|\bigr)$$
  with **lexicographic** ordering, where `D` is the `delta`-count.

One checks each rule:
- `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_*`, `R_rec_zero`, `R_rec_succ`, `R_eq_refl`: all strictly decrease `M` (the first three decrease `|R|`, `R_rec_succ` decreases `D(R)`, `R_eq_refl` decreases `E(R)`).
- `R_eq_diff`: decreases `E(R)` strictly (from `E(a)+E(b)+1` to `E(a)+E(b)`), so the triple drops lexicographically regardless of how `D` and `|R|` move.

Since `M` maps into `ℕ³` with the standard lex order (well-founded) and strictly decreases at every step, every reduction sequence is finite.

## Conclusion

No external axiom (function extensionality, classical choice, well-foundedness of `Nat`, etc.) beyond what is derivable from the inductive syntax and the rewrite rules themselves is needed. The system is strongly normalizing, and a standard termination proof (well-founded measure on `(E, D, size)` after collapsing `merge`/`app` to their normal forms) establishes this from the calculus alone.
