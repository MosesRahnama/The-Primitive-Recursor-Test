Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the displayed syntax and rewrite rules alone.

A standard recursive-path ordering / lexicographic path ordering proof suffices. Choose a well-founded precedence, for example with the essential comparisons

- `banana > pear`
- `cherry > mango` and `cherry > peach`
- `mango > plum`

and otherwise extend it to any finite total precedence on the symbols.

Then every rule strictly decreases in the ordering:

- The `peach` and `banana_zero` rules return a proper subterm.
- `mango (grape t) → plum` decreases by precedence.
- `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)` decreases because `banana > pear`, and the recursive `banana` occurrence has the same first two arguments but a strictly smaller final argument (`grape n > n`).
- Both `cherry` rules decrease because `cherry` is above the symbols on their right-hand sides; in particular, `cherry a b` dominates `mango (peach a b)` since it dominates both `a` and `b`.

Recursive/path orderings are well-founded on these finite first-order terms, including under arbitrary rewrite contexts. Hence there can be no infinite reduction sequence. The stated `Step` relation itself is only root rewriting, so its strong normalization is an immediate weaker consequence; the same ordering also establishes termination for its usual context closure. No extra object-level equations, structural axioms, guards, or additional rewrite rules are needed.
