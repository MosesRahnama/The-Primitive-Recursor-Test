Yes – strong normalization can be proved for this rewrite system without adding any further rules or side-conditions.

Sketch of a standard proof.

1.  Work with the ordinary, context–closed one-step reduction relation generated by the rules declared in Step (the union of the eight rules).

2.  Choose an ­(L)exicographic (R)ecursive (P)ath (O)rder (LPO/RPO) on the set of ground terms
   Trace, with the following precedence on the root symbols  

        recΔ   ≻   app   ≻   eqW   ≻   integrate   ≻   merge   ≻   delta   ≻   void .

   (Any total precedence satisfying  
        recΔ ≻ app,  
        eqW ≻ integrate,  
        integrate ≻ void,  
   and keeping the remaining symbols below them, works as well.)

3.  Verify that every left–hand side of a rule is strictly greater than its right–hand side
   with respect to this order.

   •  R_int_delta: integrate (delta t)  ≻  void  
      because integrate … has higher root symbol than void.

   •  R_merge_void_left / right: merge void t  ≻  t  (t is a sub-term of the left side).

   •  R_merge_cancel: merge t t  ≻  t  (same reason).

   •  R_rec_zero: recΔ b s void  ≻  b  (b is a sub-term of the left side).

   •  R_rec_succ:
          recΔ b s (delta n)
        ≻ app s (recΔ b s n)

        – app is lower in the precedence than recΔ,  
        – every argument of the right side (s,  recΔ b s n) is smaller than the
          whole left side:
             •  s is a sub-term of the left side;  
             •  recΔ b s n is smaller because the two terms have the same root
               symbol recΔ and their argument tuples compare lexicographically:
                     (b, s, delta n) ≻ (b, s, n) since delta n ≻ n.

   •  R_eq_refl: eqW a a  ≻  void (equals integrate case).

   •  R_eq_diff:
          eqW a b  ≻  integrate (merge a b)

        eqW has higher precedence than integrate, and for every sub-term of the
        right side (merge a b, a, b) the inequality with the left side holds
        (either by precedence or because they are sub-terms).

   All variable occurrences in the right-hand sides are contained in the
   corresponding left-hand sides, so the variable condition of LPO is also
   satisfied.

4.  The LPO/RPO is a well-founded, monotone, context-closed ordering; because
   every rule decreases that ordering, the induced rewrite relation is
   well-founded.  Hence every reduction sequence terminates.

Therefore the calculus is strongly normalizing, and the proof uses only a
standard path-ordering argument derivable from the syntax and the eight given
rewrite rules – no extra axioms, guards, or structural principles are needed.
