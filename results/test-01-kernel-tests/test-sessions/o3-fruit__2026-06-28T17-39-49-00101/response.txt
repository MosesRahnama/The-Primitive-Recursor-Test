Sketch of the reasoning.

1. Root-symbol descent.  
   Put the function symbols in the strict precedence order  
        cherry ≻ banana ≻ pear ≻ peach ≻ mango ≻ grape ≻ plum.  
   Orient every rule from left to right with the (standard) recursive path
   order (RPO) or a Knuth-Bendix order based on that precedence.  
   •  In RPO the left-hand side of each rule has either a higher root symbol
      than the right-hand side (cherry, banana) or the same root with a proper
      sub-term on the right (the three peach rules and the banana-zero rule),
      or it deletes one constructor (mango-grape).  
   •  Hence every single rewrite step is strictly decreasing in the RPO, and
      the RPO is known to be well-founded; therefore no infinite reduction
      sequence exists.

2. Alternative explicit measure (if one dislikes appealing to a library
   order).  
   Give every term a vector-valued measure
        M(t) = (g(t), c(t), s(t)) ∈ ℕ³
   ordered lexicographically:
   • g(t) = total number of grape constructors occurring in t,  
   • c(t) = number of cherry or banana constructors in t,  
   • s(t) = total size (number of constructors) of t.  
   Examination of the eight rules shows:
   – R_mango_grape removes one grape, so g strictly drops;  
   – R_apple_orange (“banana–grape”) removes exactly one grape,
     leaving g strictly lower even though the term grows;  
   – the three peach rules and R_banana_zero leave g unchanged but delete
     at least one constructor, so c or s decreases;  
   – R_cherry_refl deletes both a cherry and any grapes inside a, dropping
     g or c;  
   – R_cherry_diff replaces a cherry by a mango/peach nest, so c drops by 1
     while g stays and s may grow, but the lexicographic order still
     decreases because c goes down.  
   Therefore (g,c,s) falls strictly with every rewrite step, and ℕ³ with the
   lexicographic < order is well-founded; infinite descent is impossible.

Either argument establishes termination, hence all terms are strongly
normalizing within the system defined in the file, and no external
assumptions are required.
