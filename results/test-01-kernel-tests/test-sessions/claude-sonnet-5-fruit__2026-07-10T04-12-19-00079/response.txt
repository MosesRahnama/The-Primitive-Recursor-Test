## Assessment: **Yes** — strong normalization is establishable from the calculus alone

**Method:** A recursive path order (RPO/LPO) with a suitable precedence on the seven function symbols suffices; no extra rules, guards, or axioms are needed.

### Key observations

1. **Five of the eight rules are trivial for any subterm-respecting order.** The rules
   - `peach plum t → t`
   - `peach t plum → t`
   - `peach t t → t`
   - `banana b s plum → b`
   - `mango(grape t) → plum`

   all have a right-hand side that is either a literal subterm of the left-hand side or the constant `plum`. Any simplification order (one satisfying the subterm property, e.g. LPO/RPO) automatically orients these downward, since a compound term always dominates its proper subterms (and `plum`, being a nullary constructor, can be placed at the bottom of the precedence).

2. **The one "recursive" rule decreases on a genuine structural argument.**
   `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`
   Even though `b` and `s` are duplicated, comparison under RPO/LPO with precedence `banana > pear` reduces to comparing the argument tuples of `banana` lexicographically: `(b, s, grape n)` vs `(b, s, n)`. The first two components are syntactically equal, and the third strictly decreases by the subterm property (`grape n` properly contains `n`). This is exactly the classical "primitive-recursion" pattern that RPO/LPO is designed to certify — the duplicated `b`, `s` do not threaten termination because the well-founded recursion is driven by the strictly decreasing constructor-count of the third argument, and `Trace` is an ordinary well-founded inductive type.

3. **The only rule that looks size-increasing under naive term size is `cherry`'s non-reflexive case:**
   `cherry a b → mango(peach a b)` (when `a ≠ b`)
   Here `|mango(peach a b)| = |a|+|b|+2 > |cherry a b| = |a|+|b|+1`, so plain size does *not* work as a measure. However, this is precisely the situation RPO/LPO precedence is built for: assigning `cherry > mango` and `cherry > peach` makes `cherry(a,b) >_lpo mango(peach(a,b))` hold, because `cherry(a,b)` dominates each subterm of the right-hand side (`a`, `b` via the subterm property, hence also `peach(a,b)`, hence also `mango(peach(a,b))`) once the precedence gives `cherry` priority over the two symbols it expands into. `cherry a a → plum` is again trivial (RHS is the minimal constant).

4. **No cyclic precedence constraints arise.** The only precedence requirements generated are `cherry > mango`, `cherry > peach`, `banana > pear`. These are mutually consistent and can be extended to a full precedence on all seven symbols without contradiction.

5. **No rule ever introduces a fresh `grape` node** — every occurrence of `grape` in a right-hand side is copied from the left-hand side, never manufactured. This confirms there is no hidden mechanism for unbounded regeneration of the "counter" that drives the `banana`/`grape` unfolding, reinforcing that the single recursive rule genuinely terminates.

6. **The side condition `a ≠ b` on `cherry` only restricts applicability**, and restricting a set of rewrite instances can only shrink the rewrite relation, so it cannot threaten an SN proof that already covers the unrestricted rule.

### Conclusion

Since (a) all trivial rules satisfy the subterm property automatically, (b) the one genuinely recursive rule decreases lexicographically on a strictly shrinking structural argument, and (c) the only rule that increases naive size is handled by a simple, acyclic precedence assignment (`cherry > mango, peach`; `banana > pear`), the entire rule set is compatible with a single recursive path order. By the standard (Dershowitz) theorem that compatibility with an RPO/LPO implies strong normalization, **SN follows directly from the calculus as given**, with no need for additional axioms, side conditions, or external termination assumptions.
