## Assessment

**Yes — strong normalization holds, and it can be proved directly from the calculus as written, using nothing beyond a simple size measure and case analysis on the eight rules.**

The reason is not some deep combinatorial argument about recursion or equality — it is a structural fact about how `Step` is defined in this file.

### Key observation: `Step` has no congruence/compatibility closure

The inductive `Step` only contains the eight rules listed. There is **no** rule of the form "if `Step a b` then `Step (delta a) (delta b)`" (or similarly for `integrate`, `merge`, `app`, `recΔ`, `eqW` in other argument positions). Consequently `Step` only ever fires when the **entire** term matches one of the eight left-hand-side patterns at the root; it never rewrites inside a context. This is a genuine restriction of the calculus as given (not an assumption I am adding), and it is what makes the strong-normalization proof easy.

### Classifying the eight rules by effect on term size

Using term size `|·|` (number of constructors), inspect each rule's right-hand side:

- `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`, `R_eq_refl`: the result is `void` or a **proper subterm** of the redex. Each application **strictly decreases** term size.
- `R_rec_succ`: `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`. This *increases* size (an extra copy of `s`). But the resulting term has `app` as its head constructor, and **no rule in the list has `app` as a left-hand-side head symbol** — so this result is a Step-normal form, unconditionally, regardless of `b, s, n`. No further Step can ever apply to it.
- `R_eq_diff`: `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`. This also increases size, but the result's head is `integrate` applied to a term headed by `merge`, and the *only* rule with head `integrate` (`R_int_delta`) requires its argument to be headed by `delta`. Since `merge ≠ delta` as constructors, this result too is unconditionally Step-normal.

### Why this gives strong normalization

So every application of `Step` either:
1. strictly decreases the size of the whole term (five rules + `R_int_delta`), or
2. is one of the two "growing" rules, whose result is *provably* a normal form (no matching left-hand side exists for `app(_,_)` or for `integrate(merge(_,_))`), hence the reduction sequence terminates immediately afterward.

Any reduction sequence therefore consists of a (finite, size-decreasing) chain of case-(1) steps, optionally followed by at most one case-(2) step, after which nothing more can fire. Since term size is a natural number and case-(1) steps strictly decrease it, no infinite `Step`-sequence can exist from any starting term. A straightforward well-founded induction on term size (with the case split above handling `R_rec_succ`/`R_eq_diff` separately) proves strong normalization entirely within the given syntax and rules, without adding axioms, side conditions, or extra rewrite/congruence rules.

### Caveat

This conclusion is specific to `Step` exactly as defined — a root-only, non-compatible rewrite relation. If one were to *additionally* close `Step` under term congruence (rewriting under `delta`, `integrate`, `merge`, `app`, `recΔ`, `eqW`) — which is typically what one *means* by a "rewrite calculus" — the situation changes: `R_rec_succ` genuinely duplicates `s`, and if `s` itself contains further `recΔ`/`delta` redexes, or if congruence lets one rewrite occurrences deep inside `s` or `n`, a full strong-normalization proof would need a real termination measure (e.g. a recursive path order or a size measure sensitive to duplication) rather than the trivial size argument above. But that congruence closure is *not* part of the presented file, so within the calculus exactly as given, strong normalization is established by the simple size argument above.
