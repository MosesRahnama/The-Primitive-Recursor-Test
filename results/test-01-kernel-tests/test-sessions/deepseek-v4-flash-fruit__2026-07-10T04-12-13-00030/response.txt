**Yes**, strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone.  

Define a measure \( \mu(t) = (g(t), c(t), s(t)) \) where  
- \( g(t) \) is the number of `grape` constructors in \( t \),  
- \( c(t) \) is the number of `cherry` constructors in \( t \),  
- \( s(t) \) is the total number of constructors (size) of \( t \).  

Order these triples lexicographically by the natural numbers.  

**Key observation**: No rewrite rule introduces `grape` or `cherry` on the right‑hand side. Hence every reduction step either  
- decreases \( g \) (rules `R_mango_grape`, `R_apple_orange`),  
- decreases \( c \) (rules `R_cherry_refl`, `R_cherry_diff`), or  
- keeps \( g \) and \( c \) unchanged but strictly decreases \( s \) (all remaining rules).  

Thus for any one‑step reduction \( t \to t' \) (whether at the top level or in a context) we have \( \mu(t) >_{\text{lex}} \mu(t') \).  

Since the lexicographic order on natural numbers is well‑founded, every reduction sequence must be finite. This argument uses only the given syntax and rewrite rules, and can be formalised by recursion on `Trace` and induction on the reduction relation. No external axioms or additional assumptions are required.
