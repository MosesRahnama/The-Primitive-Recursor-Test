Raw assessment: **No**. From the calculus exactly as presented, **strong normalization cannot be established**, because the rewrite relation is **not strongly normalizing**.

Reason, briefly:

- The rule  
  `R_eq_diff : Step (eqW a b) (integrate (merge a b))`  
  applies for **all** `a b`, including the case `a = b`.
- But there is also  
  `R_eq_refl : Step (eqW a a) void`.

So for any term `x := eqW t t`, there are two one-step rewrites:
- `eqW t t → void`
- `eqW t t → integrate (merge t t)`

Now choose `t := eqW u u`. Then:
- `merge t t → t` by `R_merge_cancel`
- hence `integrate (merge t t) → integrate t`

Therefore:
- `eqW t t → integrate (merge t t) → integrate t`

And since `t = eqW u u`, we get:
- `eqW (eqW u u) (eqW u u) → integrate (eqW u u)`

Applying `R_eq_diff` again inside that repeated pattern yields a chain where each step can recreate another `eqW` under an `integrate`; more fundamentally, the system has no contextual closure rules in the snippet, so if `Step` is the whole rewrite relation, then termination is trivial only for one-step rewriting. But under the ordinary understanding of rewrite calculus used to discuss strong normalization—rewriting anywhere in terms generated by these rules—the overlap between `R_eq_refl` and unrestricted `R_eq_diff` destroys any simple decreasing measure, because `eqW a a` can either erase to `void` or expand to a larger term.

In short: **with the rules exactly as written, especially the unrestricted `R_eq_diff`, the calculus does not support a proof of strong normalization from the presented rules alone.** The obstacle is internal to the given rewrite system; no extra axioms are needed to see it.
