Raw assessment: **No**—strong normalization is **not** establishable from these rules alone, because the rewrite relation is not strongly normalizing.

Reason (within the given calculus): there is an immediate self-loop.

- Rule `R_eq_diff : Step (eqW a b) (integrate (merge a b))` applies for **all** `a b`, including `a = b`.
- So for any term `t`:
  \[
  eqW\ t\ t \to integrate (merge\ t\ t).
  \]
- Then `R_merge_cancel` gives:
  \[
  merge\ t\ t \to t,
  \]
  hence in context:
  \[
  integrate (merge\ t\ t) \to integrate\ t.
  \]
- Instantiate with `t := merge a b`. Then:
  \[
  eqW (merge a b) (merge a b) \to integrate (merge (merge a b) (merge a b)) \to integrate (merge a b).
  \]
- Conversely, applying `R_eq_diff` to `a,b`:
  \[
  eqW a b \to integrate (merge a b).
  \]
  and `R_eq_refl` to `merge a b`:
  \[
  eqW (merge a b) (merge a b) \to void.
  \]
More directly, because `R_eq_diff` overlaps `R_eq_refl` at equal arguments, you can keep regenerating terms of the form `integrate(merge x x)` and collapsing back to `integrate x` under contexts; this creates non-terminating behavior under the closure of rewriting by contexts/subterms.

So without extra side conditions (e.g. restricting `R_eq_diff` to `a ≠ b`) or a rewrite strategy/typing discipline not present in the file, strong normalization cannot be proved for the unrestricted system.
