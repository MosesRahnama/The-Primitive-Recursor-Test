### Case 2: Under Compatible/Congruent Closure (Standard Term Rewriting)
Even if we assume the standard term rewriting setting where the `Step` relation is closed under congruence (i.e., we can reduce any subterm at any depth), strong normalization still holds. 

We can prove this using a **polynomial interpretation** into the positive integers ($\mathbb{N}_{\ge 1}$). Let the interpretation $[[t]]$ of a term $t$ be defined inductively as follows:

* $[[\text{void}]] = 1$
* $[[\text{delta}(t)]] = [[t]] + 2$
* $[[\text{integrate}(t)]] = [[t]] + 1$
* $[[\text{merge}(t_1, t_2)]] = [[t_1]] + [[t_2]] + 1$

Because all coefficients are positive, every interpretation function is strictly monotonic in all its arguments. We now verify that for every rewrite rule $l \to r$, the inequality $[[l]] > [[r]]$ holds for all positive integer assignments:

1. **`R_int_delta`**:

Since $[[l]] > [[r]]$ holds strictly for every rule and the interpretation functions are strictly monotonic, any reduction step decreases the overall polynomial value of the term. Because the positive integers are well-founded under the standard ordering $>$, there can be no infinite reduction sequences. 

Thus, **strong normalization is fully derivable and holds unconditionally.**
