Raw assessment: no, strong normalization cannot be established from the presented calculus alone.

Reason, briefly: the rewrite system as given is not strongly normalizing because it admits an infinite rewrite chain. The key issue is the unrestricted rule

- `R_eq_diff : Step (eqW a b) (integrate (merge a b))`

which overlaps with

- `R_eq_refl : Step (eqW a a) void`.

Since `R_eq_diff` has no side condition requiring `a ≠ b`, it also applies when `a = b`. Then for any term `t` we get:

- `eqW t t → integrate (merge t t)` by `R_eq_diff`
- `merge t t → t` by `R_merge_cancel`

and rewriting inside contexts yields, for example with `t := eqW u u`:

- `eqW (eqW u u) (eqW u u)`
- `→ integrate (merge (eqW u u) (eqW u u))`
- `→ integrate (eqW u u)`
- `→ integrate (integrate (merge u u))`
- `→ integrate (integrate u)`
- etc.

Taking `u = void`, this gives the explicit infinite chain

- `eqW void void`
- `→ integrate (merge void void)`
- `→ integrate void`
- `→ integrate (integrate (merge void void))`
- `→ integrate (integrate void)`
- `→ integrate (integrate (integrate (merge void void)))`
- ...

So there exists a term with an infinite reduction sequence. Therefore the system is not strongly normalizing, and no proof of strong normalization is available from the calculus alone without adding restrictions or assumptions not present in the file.
