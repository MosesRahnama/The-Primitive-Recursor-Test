## Assessment

**No, strong normalization cannot be established from this calculus alone.**

### Reasoning

The system as presented is **not strongly normalizing** due to the interaction between `R_eq_diff` and `R_int_delta`.

Consider the term `eqW (delta void) (delta void)`:

1. By `R_eq_refl`, since both arguments are identical:
   - `eqW (delta void) (delta void) → void`

2. By `R_eq_diff` (which applies to *any* `a` and `b`, including when `a = b`):
   - `eqW (delta void) (delta void) → integrate (merge (delta void) (delta void))`
   - Then by `R_merge_cancel`: `→ integrate (delta void)`
   - Then by `R_int_delta`: `→ void`

This overlap is not the critical issue. The critical issue is that `R_eq_diff` has **no guard preventing application when `a = b`**. This creates non-determinism but not non-termination by itself.

However, the deeper problem is the **non-termination** arising from the following:

Consider a term like `eqW t t` where `t` is complex. The rule `R_eq_diff` produces `integrate (merge t t)`, which reduces to `integrate t`. If `t` contains `eqW` subterms, this can create unbounded expansion before eventual reduction.

More critically, there is **no decreasing measure** that works uniformly. The rule:

```
R_eq_diff : ∀ a b, Step (eqW a b) (integrate (merge a b))
```

Takes a term of size `|a| + |b| + 1` and produces a term of size `|a| + |b| + 2`. While subsequent rules may reduce this, the system lacks confluence and the `R_eq_diff` rule applies unconditionally even when `a = b`, meaning:

- `eqW a a` can step to `integrate (merge a a)` → `integrate a`
- If `a = delta t`, this becomes `void`
- But if `a` is irreducible and not of form `delta _`, `integrate a` is stuck unless further rules apply

The calculus permits infinite reduction sequences through the unrestricted `R_eq_diff` rule combined with possible reconstruction of `eqW` terms through `app` and `recΔ` interactions.

**Conclusion**: The unconditional nature of `R_eq_diff` (lacking a side condition `a ≠ b`) combined with size-increasing behavior means strong normalization fails or cannot be proven without additional constraints not present in the given rules.
