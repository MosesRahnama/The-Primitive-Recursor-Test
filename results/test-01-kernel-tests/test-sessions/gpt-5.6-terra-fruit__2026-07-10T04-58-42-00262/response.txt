Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the given syntax and rules alone.

A standard route is to orient all rules by a well-founded recursive/lexicographic path ordering on the finite `Trace` terms. For example, take a constructor precedence such as

\[
\texttt{banana} \succ \texttt{cherry} \succ \texttt{mango} \succ
\texttt{peach} \succ \texttt{pear} \succ \texttt{grape} \succ \texttt{plum},
\]

with lexicographic status for `banana`.

All rules decrease in such an ordering:

- The `peach` and `banana_zero` rules return strict subterms.
- `mango (grape t) → plum` and the `cherry` rules decrease by constructor precedence.
- For
  \[
  \texttt{banana b s (grape n)}
  \to
  \texttt{pear s (banana b s n)},
  \]
  the source is greater than `s` by the subterm property, and greater than `banana b s n` because its third argument `grape n` is greater than `n`. Since `banana` is above `pear`, the whole right-hand side is smaller.
- The side condition `a ≠ b` on `R_cherry_diff` only restricts applicability; it does not create an increasing case.

Thus every permitted rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded term ordering, so no infinite rewrite sequence exists. This uses no additional rewrite rules, object-level axioms, guards, or assumptions. It also establishes termination for the usual context-closed interpretation of these root rules; for `Step` exactly as declared, which is only a root relation, the result is no harder.
