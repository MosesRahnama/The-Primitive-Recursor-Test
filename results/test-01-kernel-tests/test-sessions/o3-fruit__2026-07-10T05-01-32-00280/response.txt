Assessment: Yes – every term in the given calculus is strongly normalising; no infinite rewrite sequence can be generated from the eight rules that constitute Step.

Sketch of the argument (informal but sufficient for a “from-the-calculus-alone” justification)

1  Root–only rewriting  
   The inductive definition of Step lists only the eight patterns shown, with no congruence or
   context-closure constructor.  Consequently each rewrite step acts exclusively on the root
   constructor of the current term; sub-terms are never rewritten while they are beneath any
   outer constructor.

2  Constructors that can fire  
   A rewrite can occur only when the root constructor is one of  
      mango •, peach • •, banana • • •, cherry • •.  
   If the root is plum, grape •, pear • •, or any mango whose
   argument is not of the form grape •, the term is already in normal form.

3  Per-rule termination reasoning  

   • R₁ (mango (grape t) → plum)  
     After the step the root becomes plum, which is irreducible ⇒ no further steps.

   • R₂, R₃, R₄ (three peach–rules)  
     Each of these removes the outer peach node, strictly decreasing the ordinary
     size |·| (number of constructors) of the term by at least 1.  
     Because no rule ever increases |·| when the root is peach, a sequence of successive
     peach-reductions is bounded above by the original size of the term and therefore finite.

   • R₅ (banana b s plum → b)  
     This removes the banana node and a grape node, so |·| drops by ≥2 ⇒ no loop.

   • R₆ (banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n))  
     Here |·| can rise, but only one step can ever fire: the root constructor changes from
     banana to pear, and pear is never a left-hand side of any rule.  Thus the increase
     cannot lead to an infinite chain.

   • R₇ (cherry a a → plum)  
     Replaces the root by plum ⇒ terminates immediately.

   • R₈ (cherry a b  (a ≠ b) → mango (peach a b))  
     The root becomes mango, but its argument is peach a b, not grape •;
     therefore R₁ cannot fire, and no other rule has mango as a left-hand side.
     Hence the resulting term is in normal form after at most this single step.

4  Global bound on reduction length  
   Any reduction sequence starts with some term T.  
   • At most one application of R₁, R₅, R₆, R₇, or R₈ can occur, because each of them
     produces a root constructor that is not on the left-hand side of any rule.  
   • Repeated applications are possible only for the peach-rules, and each such step
     lowers |·| by at least 1.  Since |T| is finite, the number of peach-steps is
     bounded by |T|.  
   Therefore every reduction sequence issuing from T is finite, and the system is
   strongly normalising.

No extra rewrite rules, side conditions, or external axioms are needed; the argument uses only the syntax of Trace and the eight given rewrite rules.
