Yes. The rules are strongly normalizing on the finite `Trace` syntax; no additional object-level axioms are needed.

A standard termination argument is to orient the rules with a recursive path ordering (or an equivalent multiset/path order). Choose a precedence with, for example,

- `recΔ` above `app`,
- `eqW` above `integrate` and `merge`,
- `integrate` above `void`,

with the remaining symbols placed below as needed.

Every rule then decreases:

- the merge, zero-recursion, and reflexive-equality rules reduce to a proper subterm;
- `integrate (delta t)` reduces to `void`;
- `eqW a b` reduces to a term whose components are proper subterms of the left side;
- `recΔ b s (delta n)` reduces to `app s (recΔ b s n)`, where the recursive argument decreases from `delta n` to `n`.

Thus the rules are compatible with a well-founded term ordering, which gives strong normalization for the corresponding rewrite relation. Literally, the declared `Step` relation only rewrites at the root, making termination even more immediate; if contextual closure is intended, the same ordering still proves termination of that closure.
