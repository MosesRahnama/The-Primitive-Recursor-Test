Yes.

A well-founded recursive path ordering on finite `Trace` terms can orient every rule strictly downward. For example, choose precedence satisfying:

- `cherry ≻ mango ≻ peach`
- `banana ≻ pear`
- `mango ≻ plum` and `cherry ≻ plum`

with lexicographic status for `banana`. The only potentially expanding rule,

```lean
banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)
```

still decreases: the outer head drops from `banana` to `pear`, and the nested `banana b s n` is smaller than `banana b s (grape n)` because `n` is a proper subterm of `grape n`. The peach and zero rules reduce to subterms, while the mango and cherry rules are also oriented by the precedence. The disequality premise on `R_cherry_diff` merely restricts applicable instances and does not affect termination.

Thus every `Step` decreases a well-founded ordering derived solely from the finite syntax. In fact, this argument also handles the usual contextual closure, although the displayed `Step` relation itself contains only root steps. No additional rewrite rules, guards, or object-level assumptions are needed.
