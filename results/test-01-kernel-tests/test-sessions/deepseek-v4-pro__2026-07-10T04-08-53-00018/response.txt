**Assessment:** Yes, strong normalization (SN) can be established from the presented calculus alone, without additional axioms or hidden assumptions.

**Reasoning:** The reduction relation `Step` operates only at the root of terms—there are no congruence rules that allow reduction of subterms. Consequently, a term reduces only if it exactly matches a left‑hand side. Inspection of the rules shows:

- Rules `R_int_delta`, `R_eq_refl`, `R_eq_diff` produce normal forms directly (`void` or `integrate (merge a b)`, which is not a redex).  
- `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, and `R_rec_zero` replace a term by one of its strict subterms, potentially yielding a new redex at the root, but always reducing the term’s size.  
- The only size‑increasing rule is `R_rec_succ`, which produces an `app`-headed term. `app` has no reduction rule, so this step is immediately followed by no further steps.

Since all terms are finite trees, any sequence of root reductions either strictly decreases the term’s size or makes exactly one size increase that ends the sequence. Hence there are no infinite reduction sequences. A formal SN proof can proceed by induction on the structure of terms (or by a suitable well‑founded measure) using only the given inductive definitions.
