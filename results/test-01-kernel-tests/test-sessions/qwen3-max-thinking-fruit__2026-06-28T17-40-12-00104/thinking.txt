Moreover, after a reduction that does not reduce to a subterm (i.e., the "expanding" rules like R_apple_orange and R_cherry_diff), the resulting term is in normal form, so the sequence terminates immediately after that step.

But wait: is it possible that after reducing to a subterm, that subterm is reducible, and then we apply a rule that again reduces to a subterm, and so on, and this chain could be infinite? No, because each time we take a proper subterm, and the term size (number of constructors) strictly decreases. Since the size is a natural number, it cannot decrease infinitely. Therefore, any sequence of reductions that only uses the "subterm-reducing" rules (peach rules, banana_zero, and also note: R_peach_cancel reduces to t, which is a subterm) must terminate after at most size(T0) steps.

However, we must check if there is any rule that reduces to a term that is not a subterm but is still reducible at the root. We argued that R_apple_orange and R_cherry_diff produce terms that are not reducible at the root. But is that always true?

For R_apple_orange: result is `pear s (banana b s n)`. Top symbol is pear. Is there any rule with left-hand side starting with pear? Looking at the rules: none. So indeed, no root reduction is possible from a term starting with pear. Therefore, it is a normal form.

For R_cherry_diff: result is `mango (peach a b)`. Top symbol is mango. The only rule for mango is R_mango_grape, which requires the argument to be of the form `grape t`. But the argument is `peach a b`, which is a term starting with peach. Since constructors are distinct, `peach a b` cannot be equal to `grape t` for any t. Therefore, no rule applies. So it is a normal form.

What about R_mango_grape? Result is plum, which is normal form.

R_cherry_refl: result is plum, normal form.

Therefore, the only rules that can be followed by another reduction are the ones that reduce to a subterm: specifically:
- R_peach_plum_left: reduces to the second argument (t)
- R_peach_plum_right: reduces to the first argument (t) [note: in R_peach_plum_right, it's `peach t plum` → t, so t is the first argument]
- R_peach_cancel: reduces to t (which is both arguments, so it's a subterm)
- R_banana_zero: reduces to b (the first argument)

Additionally, could there be a case where a term matches multiple rules, and one rule reduces to a subterm and another rule reduces to a non-subterm? For example, consider a term that is both `peach t t` and also matches another pattern? But `peach t t` only matches the peach rules. If t is such that the term also matches, say, if t is plum, then `peach plum plum` matches R_peach_plum_left, R_peach_plum_right, and R_peach_cancel, all reducing to plum. So no issue.

Another point: what about the condition in R_cherry_diff? It requires a≠b. But if a and b are such that after reduction to `mango (peach a b)`, and if by some miracle `peach a b` equals `grape t`? But as constructors are injective and distinct, `peach a b` cannot be equal to `grape t` because the top symbols are different. So no.

Therefore, in all cases, any reduction sequence must consist of a (possibly empty) sequence of reductions that reduce to a subterm (each time decreasing the term size), followed by at most one reduction that produces a normal form (either by an expanding rule or by a rule that produces a constant). Since the subterm-reducing steps are bounded by the initial term size, and after that we might have one more step that terminates, the entire sequence is finite.

But wait: could there be a reduction that reduces to a subterm, and that subterm is itself reducible by an expanding rule? For example, suppose we have a term T = `peach plum U`, and we reduce to U by R_peach_plum_left. Now, if U is of the form `banana b s (grape n)`, then U is reducible at the root by R_apple_orange. So the sequence would be: T → U → V, where V = `pear s (banana b s n)`. Then V is normal form. So the sequence has two steps: first a subterm-reducing step (size decreases from size(T) to size(U)), then an expanding step that produces a normal form. The expanding step does not reduce to a subterm, but it is applied to U, which is a subterm of T. However, after the first step, we have U, and then we apply R_apple_orange to U. But R_apple_orange is not a subterm-reducing rule; it produces a term V that is not a subterm of U. However, V is in normal form, so the sequence stops. So the total number of steps is finite.

Could there be a case where after reducing to a subterm U, U is reducible by a rule that again reduces to a subterm of U, and so on, and then eventually we hit an expanding rule? But the expanding rule, when applied, produces a normal form, so it terminates the sequence. The key is that the expanding rules (R_apple_orange, R_cherry_diff, R_mango_grape, R_cherry_refl) all produce terms that are normal forms (no further root reduction). Therefore, once an expanding rule is applied, the sequence ends. The only way to have multiple expanding rules in a sequence is if after an expanding rule, the result is reducible, but we argued that the results of expanding rules are not reducible. Therefore, at most one expanding rule can be applied in a sequence, and it must be the last step. All preceding steps must be subterm-reducing steps. Since subterm-reducing steps strictly decrease the term size (in terms of number of constructors), and the size is a natural number, there can be at most size(initial term) subterm-reducing steps. Therefore, the entire reduction sequence is bounded by size(initial term) + 1, hence finite.

But we must confirm that the subterm-reducing steps indeed strictly decrease the size. Let's define size formally:
- size(plum) = 1
- size(grape t) = 1 + size(t)
- size(mango t) = 1 + size(t)
- size(peach t1 t2) = 1 + size(t1) + size(t2)
- size(pear t1 t2) = 1 + size(t1) + size(t2)  [though pear only appears on RHS]
- size(banana b s t) = 1 + size(b) + size(s) + size(t)
- size(cherry a b) = 1 + size(a) + size(b)

Now, for the subterm-reducing rules:
- R_peach_plum_left: left size = 1 + size(plum) + size(t) = 1 + 1 + size(t) = 2 + size(t). Right size = size(t). Since size(t) >= 1 (as t is a term, minimum size 1 for plum), left size >= 3, right size = size(t) <= left size - 2. So size decreases by at least 2.
- Similarly, R_peach_plum_right: same.
- R_peach_cancel: left size = 1 + size(t) + size(t) = 1 + 2*size(t). Right size = size(t). Decrease by at least 1 + size(t) >= 2 (since size(t)>=1).
- R_banana_zero: left size = 1 + size(b) + size(s) + size(plum) = 1 + size(b) + size(s) + 1 = size(b) + size(s) + 2. Right size = size(b). Decrease by size(s) + 2 >= 1 + 2 = 3 (since size(s)>=1).

So yes, size strictly decreases.

For the expanding rules:
- R_mango_grape: left size = 1 (mango) + [1 (grape) + size(t)] = 2 + size(t). Right size = 1 (plum). Decrease by 1 + size(t) >= 2. Wait, this actually decreases size! I earlier thought it might not be a subterm, but size decreases. However, the result is plum, which is normal form. So it is a size-decreasing rule that also produces a normal form. Similarly, R_cherry_refl: left size = 1 + 2*size(a) >= 3, right size=1, decrease. R_cherry_diff: left size = 1 + size(a) + size(b), right size = 1 (mango) + [1 (peach) + size(a) + size(b)] = 2 + size(a) + size(b). So size increases by 1. But as argued, the result is normal form. R_apple_orange: left size = 1 (banana) + size(b) + size(s) + [1 (grape) + size(n)] = size(b) + size(s) + size(n) + 2. Right size = 1 (pear) + size(s) + [1 (banana) + size(b) + size(s) + size(n)] = size(b) + 2*size(s) + size(n) + 2. Compare: left size = size(b)+size(s)+size(n)+2, right size = size(b)+2*size(s)+size(n)+2. So right size = left size + size(s). Since size(s) >= 1, size increases. But result is normal form.

So the only rule that increases size is R_cherry_diff and R_apple_orange. However, as established, after applying either of these, the term is in normal form, so no further reduction is possible. Therefore, even though size increases in that step, it is the last step. So the sequence cannot continue.

Therefore, any reduction sequence must be finite. Hence, the system is strongly normalizing.

But wait: is there a possibility of a term that matches an expanding rule, and after reduction, the result is reducible? We argued no for R_apple_orange and R_cherry_diff. But let's double-check R_apple_orange: result is `pear s (banana b s n)`. Could this term match any rule? The rules require specific top symbols: mango, peach, banana, cherry. Pear is not among them. So no. Similarly, for R_cherry_diff: result is `mango (peach a b)`. The only rule for mango is R_mango_grape, which requires the argument to be `grape _`. But `peach a b` is not `grape _` because the constructor is peach, not grape. Since the type is inductive and constructors are distinct, in Lean, `peach a b` and `grape t` are not equal for any a,b,t. Therefore, no match. So indeed, normal form.

What about the possibility of a term that is reducible by multiple rules, and choosing one rule leads to a longer sequence than another? But strong normalization requires that all reduction sequences are finite, regardless of the choice of rule at each step. We need to ensure that no matter which applicable rule we choose at each step, the sequence terminates. In our analysis, we considered that after any step, the resulting term either is in normal form or is a proper subterm (in the case of subterm-reducing rules). However, if a term matches both a subterm-reducing rule and an expanding rule, we must check both possibilities.

Is there a term that matches both a subterm-reducing rule and an expanding rule? Let's see:

- Consider a term that is `peach t t` and also matches another pattern? For example, if t is such that `peach t t` also matches `peach plum _`? Only if t=plum, then `peach plum plum` matches R_peach_plum_left, R_peach_plum_right, and R_peach_cancel. All of these reduce to plum, which is a subterm-reducing step (to plum). There is no expanding rule that applies to `peach plum plum` because the only rules for peach are the ones listed, and they all reduce to a subterm (plum). So no conflict.

- Consider a term that is `banana b s plum`. This matches R_banana_zero, which reduces to b (subterm-reducing). Could it also match R_apple_orange? R_apple_orange requires the third argument to be `grape n`, but here it is plum, so no.

- Consider a term that is `banana b s (grape n)`. This matches R_apple_orange (expanding rule). Could it also match R_banana_zero? No, because third argument is grape n, not plum.

- Consider a term that is `cherry a b`. If a=b, it matches R_cherry_refl (which reduces to plum, size-decreasing and normal form). If a≠b, it matches R_cherry_diff (expanding rule, size-increasing, but normal form). There is no other rule for cherry. So no overlap between subterm-reducing and expanding for cherry.

- Consider a term that is `mango (grape t)`. Only matches R_mango_grape, which reduces to plum (size-decreasing, normal form). Not expanding in the sense of increasing size, but it is a terminating step.

- What about a term that is `peach plum t` where t is such that the whole term also matches another rule? For example, if t = plum, then it matches multiple peach rules, but all reduce to plum. If t is something else, only R_peach_plum_left applies. No expanding rule applies to peach terms.

Therefore, there is no term that can be reduced by both a rule that leads to a reducible term (subterm-reducing) and a rule that leads to a normal form (expanding) in a way that choosing the expanding rule would prevent termination? But actually, if a term matches only expanding rules, then applying it leads to normal form, so sequence length 1. If it matches only subterm-reducing rules, then applying it leads to a smaller term, and we continue. If it matches both types? From above, it seems no term matches both a subterm-reducing rule and an expanding rule. Let's verify thoroughly:

List of rules by type:
- Subterm-reducing (result is a proper subterm of the original term, and the result may be reducible): 
  * R_peach_plum_left: result = second argument (t)
  * R_peach_plum_right: result = first argument (t)
  * R_peach_cancel: result = t (which is both arguments, so a subterm)
  * R_banana_zero: result = first argument (b)
- Terminating rules (result is a normal form, i.e., no root reduction possible):
  * R_mango_grape: result = plum (normal form)
  * R_cherry_refl: result = plum (normal form)
  * R_cherry_diff: result = mango(peach a b) (normal form, as argued)
  * R_apple_orange: result = pear s (banana b s n) (normal form)

Now, check if any term can be an instance of both a subterm-reducing rule and a terminating rule.

- For a term to match a subterm-reducing rule, its top symbol must be peach or banana (with specific arguments). For terminating rules, top symbol must be mango, cherry, or banana (for R_apple_orange) or cherry (for R_cherry_diff). Wait, R_apple_orange has top symbol banana, and R_banana_zero also has top symbol banana. So a banana term could potentially match both R_banana_zero and R_apple_orange? But R_banana_zero requires third argument = plum, R_apple_orange requires third argument = grape n. Since plum and grape n are distinct constructors, a term cannot have third argument both plum and grape n. So no overlap.

- Could a peach term match a terminating rule? The terminating rules do not have peach as top symbol, so no.

- Could a cherry term match a subterm-reducing rule? No, because subterm-reducing rules require top symbol peach or banana.

- Could a mango term match a subterm-reducing rule? No.

Therefore, the sets of terms matched by subterm-reducing rules and terminating rules are disjoint. Moreover, within the subterm-reducing rules, the result is always a proper subterm, and the size decreases. Within the terminating rules, the result is always a normal form (no further reduction). Therefore, any reduction sequence must consist of zero or more applications of subterm-reducing rules (each time decreasing the size of the term), followed by at most one application of a terminating rule (which then stops). Since the size is a natural number and decreases with each subterm-reducing step, the number of such steps is bounded by the initial size. Hence, all reduction sequences are finite.

Therefore, strong normalization holds.

But wait: what about the condition in R_cherry_diff? The rule requires a proof of a ≠ b. In the context of the rewrite relation as a mathematical relation, for any two distinct terms a and b, the step exists. However, in a reduction sequence, when we have a term `cherry a b` with a≠b, we can apply R_cherry_diff. But is there any scenario where a and b are not syntactically distinct but we cannot prove a≠b? In the meta-theory, since terms are syntactic objects, if a and b are distinct terms, then a≠b is true, and the step exists. If a and b are the same term, then R_cherry_refl applies. So for every cherry term, exactly one rule applies. Therefore, no issue.

Another subtle point: the rule R_cherry_diff has a premise `a ≠ b`, which in Lean is a proposition. In the inductive definition of Step, to construct a Step for `cherry a b` to `mango (peach a b)`, we need to provide a proof of `a ≠ b`. In the context of proving strong normalization, we are reasoning about the existence of reduction sequences. For any concrete terms a and b that are distinct, such a proof exists (by decidability of equality on the inductive type Trace). Therefore, the step is available. So in the rewrite relation, the step is present for all distinct a,b. Hence, our analysis holds.

Therefore, from the given calculus alone, we can establish strong normalization by a simple size argument combined with the observation that all rules either reduce to a proper subterm (and thus decrease a well-founded measure) or produce a normal form. No additional axioms or assumptions are needed beyond the syntax and the rewrite rules as given.

However, we must be cautious: the measure we used is the size of the term (number of constructors). For the subterm-reducing rules, size strictly decreases. For the terminating rules, even if size increases (R_cherry_diff, R_apple_orange), the result is a normal form, so no further steps. Therefore, the maximum length of a reduction sequence starting from a term of size n is at most n (from subterm-reducing steps) plus possibly one terminating step. Since n is finite, the sequence is finite.

But is it possible that a subterm-reducing step reduces to a term that has larger size than the original? No, because we checked that for subterm-reducing rules, the result is a proper subterm, and in term algebras, a proper subterm has strictly smaller size. For example, in R_peach_plum_left, the result is t, which is a direct subterm of the original term `peach plum t`, and size(t) < size(peach plum t) because the latter has at least the peach constructor and plum. Similarly for others.

Therefore, the system is strongly normalizing.
