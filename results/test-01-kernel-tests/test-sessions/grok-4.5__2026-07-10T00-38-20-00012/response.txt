### Assessment
**Yes**, strong normalization (SN) of the given system can be established directly from the presented syntax and the inductive definition of `Step` alone.

### Reasoning
The relation `Step` consists solely of the eight root-level rules shown (no contextual/congruence closure is present in the inductive definition). Consequently, a term reduces if and only if it is literally an instance of one of the left-hand sides; reduction never occurs under a constructor.

Proceed by structural induction on an arbitrary term \(t : \mathsf{Trace}\), showing that every one-step reduct (if any) is itself SN.

- If \(t\) matches none of the eight patterns, it is irreducible, hence SN.
- The only rules whose right-hand sides are not immediate subterms of \(t\) are
  - \(\mathsf{rec}\Delta\, b\, s\, (\mathsf{delta}\, n)\;\to\;\mathsf{app}\, s\, (\mathsf{rec}\Delta\, b\, s\, n)\),
  - \(\mathsf{eqW}\, a\, b\;\to\;\mathsf{integrate}\,(\mathsf{merge}\, a\, b)\).
  In both cases the reduct has a head constructor (`app` or `integrate`) that matches no left-hand side, so the reduct is irreducible and therefore SN.
- Every remaining rule projects onto a strict subterm of \(t\) (or onto \(\mathsf{void}\)). The induction hypothesis supplies SN of that subterm.

All reduction sequences are therefore finite. The argument uses only the inductive definitions of \(\mathsf{Trace}\) and \(\mathsf{Step}\), ordinary structural induction, and the observation that certain head forms are irreducible; no additional rewrite rules, side-conditions, size measures external to the syntax, or object-level axioms are required.
