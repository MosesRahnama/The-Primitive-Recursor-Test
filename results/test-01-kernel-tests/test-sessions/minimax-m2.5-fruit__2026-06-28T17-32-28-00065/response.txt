**Yes – strong normalization can be proved from the calculus alone.**

The rewrite relation `Step` consists of eight one‑step rules.  Define a measure  
\(M : \mathsf{Trace} \to \mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\) by  

\[
M(t)\;:=\;\bigl(\#\mathsf{grape}(t),\;|t|\bigr)
\]

where  

* \(\#\mathsf{grape}(t)\) is the number of occurrences of the constructor `grape` in \(t\) (computed by simple recursion), and  
* \(|t|\) is the total size (the number of constructors) of \(t\).

One then proves by case analysis on the constructors of `Step` that every rule decreases \(M\) in the lexicographic order:

* Rules 1, 5, 7, 8 strictly reduce \(\#\mathsf{grape}\).
* Rules 2–4 leave \(\#\mathsf{grape}\) unchanged but reduce the size.
* Rule 6 removes one `grape` from the third argument of `banana`; depending on whether the second argument `s` already contains grapes, either \(\#\mathsf{grape}\) drops or, if the numbers of grapes are equal, the size drops (the right‑hand side adds an extra `pear`, so the total size grows, making the second component of the pair smaller in the lexicographic sense).

Thus each one‑step reduction makes \(M\) smaller.  Since \(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\) with the lexicographic order is well‑founded, there can be no infinite descending chain of `Step`‑steps.  Consequently the system is strongly normalizing.

The proof uses only the given inductive definitions and ordinary recursion; no additional rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or hidden axioms are required.
