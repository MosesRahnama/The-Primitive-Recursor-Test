Yes—under the calculus exactly as presented, strong normalization can be established.

The `Step` relation contains only root rewrite rules; it does not include contextual closure or any other structural congruence. Every possible nonterminal rewrite behaves as follows:

- `merge ...` rewrites to one of its proper subterms.
- `recΔ b s void` rewrites to the proper subterm `b`.
- `recΔ b s (delta n)` rewrites to an `app`, whose root is irreducible.
- `integrate (delta t)` rewrites to `void`.
- `eqW a b` rewrites either to `void` or to `integrate (merge a b)`, whose root is irreducible.

Although `R_eq_diff` can increase the syntactic size of the term, its target cannot take another root step. Thus it cannot participate in an infinite sequence. A well-founded induction on term size, with the irreducible-target cases handled directly, proves that every `Step` sequence is finite.

This conclusion relies only on the given syntax and constructors. Adding an unstated contextual rewrite rule would define a different system, but no such rule is present here.
