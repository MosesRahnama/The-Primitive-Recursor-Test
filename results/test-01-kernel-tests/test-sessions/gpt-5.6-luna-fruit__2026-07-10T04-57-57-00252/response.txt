Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the calculus as given; no additional rewrite rules, guards, or object-level axioms are needed.

A standard termination argument is to orient every rule by a well-founded recursive path ordering on `Trace`. Choose a precedence in which, at minimum,

- `cherry > mango`,
- `banana > pear`,
- `mango > plum`,

with the remaining symbols ordered arbitrarily.

The rules reducing to a proper argument (`peach`, `banana_zero`) decrease immediately by the subterm property. The `cherry` rules and `mango` rule decrease by the chosen precedence. The only size-preserving-looking rule,

```lean
banana b s (grape n) ↦ pear s (banana b s n)
```

also decreases: `banana` is above `pear`, and the inner `banana b s n` is smaller than the original left-hand side because its third argument changed from `grape n` to the proper subterm `n`.

A recursive path ordering is well-founded and is preserved under contexts, so every rewrite step strictly decreases it. Hence the induced rewrite relation is strongly normalizing. Literally, the declared `Step` relation is even weaker because it contains only root steps; its strong normalization follows as well.
