**Assessment:** Yes – strong normalization can be proved within the given calculus alone.

**Reasoning:** Define a measure \(m:\mathbf{Trace}\to\mathbb{N}^3\) by, for example,
\[
m(t)=\bigl(\#\text{cherry}(t),\;\#\text{grape}(t),\;\text{size}(t)\bigr)
\]
where \(\#\text{cherry}(t)\) counts the number of cherry constructors in \(t\), \(\#\text{grape}(t)\) counts the number of grape constructors, and \(\text{size}(t)\) is the total number of constructors in \(t\).  

Each rewrite rule decreases this triple lexicographically:
- `R_cherry_refl` and `R_cherry_diff` eliminate a cherry, so the first component drops.
- `R_mango_grape`, `R_apple_orange`, `R_banana_zero`, `R_peach_*`, and `R_cherry_refl` either remove a grape or shrink the term, lowering the second or third component.
- `R_cherry_diff` also removes a cherry (first component) even though the term size (third component) may increase.

Thus every step reduces the measure, and since \(\mathbb{N}^3\) with the lexicographic order is well‑founded, no infinite reduction sequence exists. No extra axioms or rewrite rules are required beyond the inductive type definitions and the given Step rules.
