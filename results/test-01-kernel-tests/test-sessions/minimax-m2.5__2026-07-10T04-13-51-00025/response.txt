**Yes.**  
The rewrite rules can be shown to be terminating by assigning a simple well‑founded measure that decreases at every step. For instance, define a weight

\[
\begin{aligned}
w(\text{void}) &= 0,\\
w(\delta(t)) &= 1+w(t),\\
w(\text{integrate }t) &= w(t),\\
w(\text{merge }t_1 t_2) &= w(t_1)+w(t_2),\\
w(\text{app }t_1 t_2) &= w(t_1)+w(t_2),\\
w(\text{recΔ }t_1 t_2 t_3) &= 1+w(t_1)+w(t_2)+w(t_3),\\
w(\text{eqW }t_1 t_2) &= 1+w(t_1)+w(t_2),
\end{aligned}
\]

and consider the lexicographic order \((c_{\text{eqW}},\,c_{\delta},\,c_{\text{recΔ}},\,w)\) where \(c_X\) counts the occurrences of the constructor \(X\).  

- **R_int_delta**, **R_merge_***, **R_rec_zero**, **R_rec_succ**, **R_eq_refl** all strictly reduce one of the components of this measure.  
- **R_eq_diff** replaces an \(\text{eqW}\) (the leftmost component) by \(\text{integrate}(\text{merge }a b)\); the \(\text{eqW}\) count drops, so the measure still decreases, and no rule ever creates a new \(\text{eqW}\) or \(\text{recΔ}\) term.  

Thus every rewrite step makes the measure smaller, and the measure is a well‑founded ordering on terms. Consequently the Step relation is terminating (strongly normalising) without adding any extra axioms or rewrite rules beyond those given.
