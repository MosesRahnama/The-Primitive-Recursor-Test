## Assessment: **Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone.**

### Reasoning

The key observation is that the `Step` inductive defines exactly 8 rewrite rules with **no congruence (context-closure) rules**. Reduction can therefore only occur at the root of a term. We can classify every rule by what its right-hand side looks like:

**Group A — Reduces to a strict subterm (size strictly decreases):**
- `merge void t → t`
- `merge t void → t`
- `merge t t → t`
- `recΔ b s void → b`

Each result (`t` or `b`) is a proper subterm of the left-hand side, so the term size strictly decreases.

**Group B — Reduces to a term whose root symbol matches no rule's left-hand side (immediate normal form):**
- `integrate (delta t) → void` — `void` matches no LHS.
- `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)` — `app` is the root; no rule has `app` at the head.
- `eqW a a → void` — `void` matches no LHS.
- `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` — `integrate` is the root, but the only `integrate`-headed rule requires `integrate (delta t)`, and `merge a b` can never equal `delta t` since `merge` and `delta` are distinct constructors.

### Conclusion

Every single rewrite step either (a) strictly decreases the size of the term, or (b) produces a term that is already in normal form (no rule applies at the root). Since the original term is a finite tree, case (a) can only occur finitely many times before we either reach `void` or a subterm that falls into case (b). Therefore, all reduction sequences are finite—bounded by the size of the initial term—and **strong normalization holds** without needing any additional axioms, guards, or structural assumptions.
