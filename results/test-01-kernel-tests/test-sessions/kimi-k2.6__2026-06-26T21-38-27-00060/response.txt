The rewrite system admits a strictly monotone polynomial interpretation over the natural numbers, which orients every rule from left to right. Assign the following weights to constructors (written as functions of the weights of subterms):

- `void ↦ 1`
- `delta(x) ↦ x + 1`
- `integrate(x) ↦ x + 1`
- `merge(x, y) ↦ x + y + 1`
- `app(x, y) ↦ x + y + 1`

- `recΔ(b, s, n) ↦ (s + 2)·n + b + 1`

- `integrate (delta t)` has weight `t + 2` > `1` = `void`.
- The three `merge` rules map `x + y + 1` down to `x` or `y` (or `2x + 1` > `x`).

- `eqW a b` has weight `2a + 2b + 3` > `a + b + 2` = `integrate (merge a b)`.

Because every rule is strictly decreasing under a monotone interpretation, no infinite reduction sequence exists. The rewrite relation is finitely branching, so termination implies strong normalization. No additional axioms, side conditions, or imported lemmas are needed—only the syntax and the listed rules.
