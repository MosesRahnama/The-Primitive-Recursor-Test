## Assessment: Yes — strong normalization follows directly, and in fact rather easily, from the given definitions alone.

## Reasoning

The crucial structural fact about this file is that `Step` is **not closed under context/congruence**. There are no constructors like `Step t t' → Step (delta t) (delta t')`, `Step t t' → Step (merge t s) (merge t' s)`, etc. `Step` only relates whole terms that match one of the eight fixed root patterns:

```
integrate (delta t),  merge void t,  merge t void,  merge t t,
recΔ b s void,  recΔ b s (delta n),  eqW a a,  eqW a b
```

Because of this, every reduction sequence is forced to re-match the *entire current term* against one of these patterns at each step — there is no way to "reduce inside" a subterm that isn't already exposed at the root.

This makes the termination argument split cleanly into two cases, using only facts that come for free from the `inductive Trace` declaration (constructor disjointness/injectivity, and well-foundedness of the immediate-subterm relation — both standard, requiring no extra axioms):

**1. Six of the eight rules are structurally decreasing.**
`R_int_delta`, `R_eq_refl` produce `void` (terminal: `void` matches none of the eight head patterns). `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero` all produce a term that is literally a *proper subterm* of the redex. Any chain built purely from these six rules is bounded by the depth of the original term, by well-founded induction on the subterm order — no special termination technique needed beyond structural induction.

**2. The two rules that could increase size are immediately terminal.**
- `R_eq_diff : eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`. The result can *never* match `integrate (delta t)`, because its argument has head symbol `merge`, and `merge ≠ delta` by constructor injectivity/disjointness — regardless of what `a`, `b` are. So this output is unconditionally a normal form.
- `R_rec_succ : recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`. The result has head symbol `app`, and `app` never occurs as the head of any left-hand side among the eight rules. So this output, too, is unconditionally a normal form (even though it contains an "unreduced" `recΔ b s n` inside — it can never be reached, since there is no congruence rule to look inside `app`).

**Conclusion.** Every reduction path from any `t : Trace` consists of a (possibly empty) sequence of subterm-shrinking steps — bounded by the finite depth of `t` — followed by at most one application of `R_eq_diff` or `R_rec_succ` (or a direct hit of `void`/normal form), which necessarily ends the sequence. Hence every reduction sequence is finite, uniformly bounded by (depth of `t`) + 1.

This is a complete strong-normalization proof obtainable purely by:
- case analysis on the eight given rules,
- the automatic disjointness/injectivity of `Trace`'s constructors, and
- well-founded induction on the (automatically well-founded) subterm order,

none of which is an "extra" axiom — they are exactly what the `inductive Trace` and `inductive Step` declarations already give you. No additional rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or external hypotheses are needed.

(Note: this ease is essentially an artifact of `Step` lacking congruence closure. A version of this calculus closed under contexts — the more usual reading of a "rewrite calculus" — would require a genuinely different, non-trivial termination argument, and indeed the size-increasing behavior of `R_eq_diff`/`R_rec_succ` would then be a real threat to normalization. But *as literally presented*, with only root-level `Step`, strong normalization holds and is provable from the calculus alone.)
