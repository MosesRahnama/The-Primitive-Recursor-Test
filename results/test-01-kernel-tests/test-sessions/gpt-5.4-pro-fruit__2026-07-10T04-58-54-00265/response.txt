Yes — **for the exact `Step` relation shown in the file, strong normalization should be provable from the calculus alone**.

Why, briefly:

- `Step` is **not closed under contexts** in the file. It only rewrites at the root of the whole term.
- Under that exact relation, the rules split into two kinds:

  1. **Strict-subterm rules**
     - `peach plum t → t`
     - `peach t plum → t`
     - `peach t t → t`
     - `banana b s plum → b`

     These always drop to a **proper subterm** of the source.

  2. **Immediate-normal-form rules**
     - `mango (grape t) → plum`
     - `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`
     - `cherry a a → plum`
     - `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)` for `a ≠ b`

     Their targets have no further root step in the presented system:
     - `plum` has no rule,
     - `pear ...` has no rule,
     - `mango (peach a b)` cannot match `mango (grape t)` because constructors are distinct.

So any reduction sequence is just:
- some number of moves to strict subterms, and then possibly
- one final move to a root-normal form.

Since terms are finite trees and the **strict subterm relation is well-founded**, there cannot be an infinite reduction chain.

So my raw assessment is:

> **Yes, strong normalization is establishable from the given syntax and rules alone, with no extra structural/object-level assumptions — as long as we mean exactly the `Step` relation written in the Lean file.**

If one intended the usual **context-closed** rewrite relation instead, that would be a different system and would require a separate analysis.
