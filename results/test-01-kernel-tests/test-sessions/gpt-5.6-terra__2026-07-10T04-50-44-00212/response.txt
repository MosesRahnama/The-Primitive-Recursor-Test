Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the displayed syntax and rules alone; no structural or object-level axioms are needed.

The rules admit a standard well-founded recursive/path ordering. For example, use a lexicographic path ordering with a precedence such as

```text
recΔ > eqW > integrate > merge > app > delta > void
```

with `recΔ` compared lexicographically in its arguments.

Each rule strictly decreases under such an ordering:

- The `merge` rules and `recΔ b s void → b` discard a proper subterm context.
- `integrate (delta t) → void` and the equality rules decrease by symbol precedence.
- `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` decreases because `eqW` is above both `integrate` and `merge`, while `a` and `b` are already subterms of the left-hand side.
- The only potentially delicate rule,
  ```text
  recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n),
  ```
  decreases because `recΔ` is above `app`, and the recursive occurrence has third argument `n` instead of the strictly larger `delta n`.

Thus there is no infinite descending rewrite sequence. This remains true even if one takes the usual compatible/contextual closure of `Step`; for the literal `Step` relation shown, which only rewrites at the root, it is an even more immediate consequence.

In particular, the unguarded `R_eq_diff` rule does not create a loop: it replaces an `eqW` root by an `integrate`/`merge` term and no rule reconstructs an `eqW` node.
