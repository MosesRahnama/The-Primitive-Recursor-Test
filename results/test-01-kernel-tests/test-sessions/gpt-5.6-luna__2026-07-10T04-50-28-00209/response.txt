Yes—**for the `Step` relation exactly as presented**, strong normalization can be established from the calculus alone.

The relation contains only root rewrite rules; it does not include contextual closure or congruence rules. Every possible rewrite sequence therefore has a simple structural descent:

- `merge void t`, `merge t void`, and `merge t t` reduce to the subterm `t`.
- `recΔ b s void` reduces to the subterm `b`.
- `integrate (delta t)` and `eqW a a` reduce directly to `void`.
- `recΔ b s (delta n)` reduces to an `app` term, which is not the left-hand side of any rule.
- `eqW a b` with unequal syntactic arguments reduces to `integrate (merge a b)`, which is also root-normal because `merge a b` is not syntactically a `delta` term.

Thus any continuing reduction can only proceed through a structurally smaller subterm, and the other rules terminate immediately. A structural induction or an equivalent well-founded accessibility argument proves that no infinite `Step` chain exists.

If contextual closure under subterms was intended, that would be a different rewrite relation not defined in this file; the conclusion above is for the displayed calculus itself.
