### Assessment and Reasoning

The system is strongly normalizing. This can be demonstrated by constructing a well-founded ordering on the `Trace` terms, which proves that every reduction sequence must be finite. A lexicographical ordering based on two measures is sufficient.

Let the measure for any term `t` be the pair `(N, M)` where:
1.  `N` is the number of `eqW` constructors in `t`.
2.  `M` is a measure for the remaining system, which is strongly normalizing on its own (as explained in step 2 below).

The argument proceeds in two main parts:

**1. Reductions involving `eqW` always terminate.**

We first look at the rules that reduce a term with an `eqW` at its head: `R_eq_refl` and `R_eq_diff`.

*   `R_eq_refl`: `Step (eqW a a) void`
*   `R_eq_diff`: `Step (eqW a b) (integrate (merge a b))`

In both cases, an `eqW` constructor at the root of the redex is eliminated. The right-hand side of `R_eq_refl` contains no `eqW`s. The right-hand side of `R_eq_diff` contains only the `eqW`s that were already present in `a` and `b`. Crucially, no other rule introduces a new `eqW` constructor.

Therefore, any reduction step that applies `R_eq_refl` or `R_eq_diff` strictly decreases the total count of `eqW` constructors in the overall term. Any reduction step involving other rules does not increase this count.

This means that the first component `N` of our measure `(N, M)` strictly decreases whenever an `eqW` rule is applied, and does not increase otherwise. Since `N` is a natural number, it can only decrease a finite number of times. Any infinite reduction sequence must therefore eventually consist only of steps where the number of `eqW` constructors is constant.

**2. The remaining subsystem is also strongly normalizing.**

We now consider the system without the `eqW` rules. If a reduction step does not decrease the `eqW` count, it must be an application of one of the other six rules. For these cases, we must show that the second measure `M` decreases. This is equivalent to showing that the subsystem consisting of these six rules is strongly normalizing.

Let's analyze this subsystem:
*   `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, and `R_rec_zero` all reduce to a term that is a subterm or is otherwise strictly smaller in size. These rules are trivially terminating.
*   `R_rec_succ`: `Step (recΔ b s (delta n)) (app s (recΔ b s n))` is the only rule that can increase the size of the term. However, this rule models primitive recursion. The recursive call `recΔ b s n` is performed on a direct structural subterm (`n`) of the argument in the redex (`delta n`). This is a standard pattern of well-founded structural recursion. Any sequence of reductions that unfolds a `recΔ` term must terminate because the third argument becomes structurally smaller with each step, eventually ceasing to be of the form `delta ...`.

The strong normalization of systems with primitive recursion is a standard result in the theory of term rewriting. While a simple term-size measure is insufficient, a proof can be constructed using standard techniques like Recursive Path Ordering (RPO) or by defining a semantic interpretation into a well-founded domain (like the natural numbers).

By using a lexicographical combination of these two measures—`(number of eqW nodes, measure for the primitive recursive subsystem)`—we have a well-founded ordering. For any reduction step `t → t'`:
