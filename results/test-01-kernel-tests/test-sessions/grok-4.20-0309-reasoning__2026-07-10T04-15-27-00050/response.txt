**YES**

The inductive definitions of `Trace` and `Step` supply the complete syntax and one-step relation. Because `Step` has no congruence constructors, a term reduces only when it matches one of the eight listed left-hand sides exactly; every reduct is therefore the whole term produced by one of the eight right-hand sides. The possible successors are:

- `void` (normal),
- an arbitrary `t` or `b` (continues only if that subterm itself matches a left-hand side),
- `app s (recΔ …)` (head `app`, matches nothing),
- `integrate (merge a b)` (head `integrate` but argument head is `merge`, so never matches `R_int_delta`).

Consequently the only multi-step chains arise from successive top-level merges that cancel `void` or duplicate arguments, or from a `recΔ` whose third argument is exactly `void` or `delta _` whose reduct happens to be headed by another reducible constructor. Each such chain peels a finite number of outer constructors (`merge`, `delta`, `integrate`, `eqW`, `recΔ`) whose inductive depth is recorded in the `Trace` tree. A lexicographic measure—first the number of cancellable outer `merge`/`delta`/`integrate` layers, then ordinary term size—decreases on every concrete successor obtainable from the given rules. No rule re-introduces an `eqW`, `recΔ`, or mismatched `integrate`/`merge` pair that could regenerate a prior left-hand side, so the relation is acyclic. Ordinary structural induction on `Trace`, using only the constructors and the eight `Step` clauses, therefore shows every reduction sequence is finite. No extra rewrite rules, congruence closure, guards, or external axioms are required.
