**Yes.** Strong normalization can be established from the calculus exactly as written, without extra axioms or hidden assumptions.

**Reasoning.** The relation `Step` is defined only by root-reduction rules; there are no congruence or closure rules that would allow reduction inside a proper context. Inspection of the eight rules shows that every reduct falls into one of two categories:

1. **Proper subterms.**  
   The rules `R_peach_plum_left`, `R_peach_plum_right`, `R_peach_cancel`, and `R_banana_zero` each rewrite a term to one of its immediate proper subterms (`t` or `b`). Any sequence of such projections strictly decreases the structural depth of the term.

2. **Root-stuck terms.**  
   The remaining rules produce terms whose root constructor cannot match any left-hand side of a `Step` rule:
   - `R_mango_grape` and `R_cherry_refl` yield `plum`, which is not a redex.
   - `R_apple_orange` yields `pear s (…)`, and no rule has `pear` at its root.
   - `R_cherry_diff` yields `mango (peach a b)`. This cannot match `mango (grape t)` because the constructors `peach` and `grape` are disjoint; in Lean’s inductive type theory this incompatibility is derivable from the syntax alone (via `no_confusion`). Hence no further root step applies.

Consequently, after any non-projection step the term is irreducible, while each projection step moves to a structurally smaller term. Because `Trace` is an inductive type, well-foundedness of the structural subterm relation is built in; a simple structural induction on `Trace` shows that every term has no infinite root-reduction sequence. Thus the system is strongly normalizing by purely internal reasoning.
