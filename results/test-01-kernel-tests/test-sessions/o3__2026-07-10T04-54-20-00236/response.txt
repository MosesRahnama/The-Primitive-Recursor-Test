Assessment: Yes – strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules that are already present.  
Nothing extra has to be assumed or imported.

Sketch of the terminating measure

1. Put the weight  
   w(eqW _)  =  4,  
   w(recΔ _ _ (delta t)) = 3,  
   w(integrate (delta t)) = 2,  
   w(merge _ _) = 1,  
   w(any other constructor) = 0.  

   For a term T let |T| be the multiset-sum of the weights of every node that occurs in T.

2. Examine every rule:

   • eqW-refl and eqW-diff erase the root eqW, so |T| drops by 4 and never increases,
     because no rule ever creates a new eqW node.

   • recΔ-succ removes one outer δ from the third argument, so the 3-weight attached
     to recΔ … (delta t) is lost; no rule ever adds a δ in that position, so the 3-part
     of the weight is strictly decreasing.

   • integrate-δ removes an integrate with a δ argument, so the 2-weight is lost and
     can never be recreated (δ is never introduced at the head of an integrate).

   • All three merge rules remove a merge node, therefore the 1-weight strictly
     decreases.

   • No rule increases any of the counted constructors.

3. Hence every single rewrite step strictly lowers the multiset-order
   (|T|, size(T)) where size(T) is the ordinary syntactic size of the term.
   A strictly descending sequence in ℕ×ℕ is impossible, so no infinite
   reduction chain exists.

Therefore every term admits only finite reduction sequences; i.e. the calculus is strongly normalizing with nothing more than the rules already shown.
