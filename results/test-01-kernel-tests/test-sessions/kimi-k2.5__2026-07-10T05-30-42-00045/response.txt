Looking at this first-order rewrite system, **yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone** without importing additional axioms or structural assumptions.

**Key reasoning:**

1. **No unbounded expansion**: The only "size-increasing" rule is `R_eq_diff` (`eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`), which increases term size by +1. However, the reduct `integrate (merge a b)` is inert unless `merge a b` reduces to a `delta`-term. The `merge` rules only eliminate constructors (collapsing `void` or identical terms), and `integrate` only reduces when applied to `delta`. In all cases, any reduction chain starting from `eqW` terminates in at most 3 steps.

2. **Primitive recursion structure**: The `recΔ` rules implement primitive recursion over unary natural numbers (`void` = zero, `delta` = successor). Rule `R_rec_succ` (`recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`) preserves the first two arguments but structurally decreases the third argument by removing one `delta` constructor. Since terms are finite, each `recΔ` can unfold at most finitely many times (bounded by the nesting depth of `delta` in its third argument) before reaching the base case `R_rec_zero`.

3. **No regeneration of redexes**: 
   - `eqW` is eliminated by either `R_eq_refl` or `R_eq_diff` and never reappears in reducts (no constructor produces `eqW`).
   - `recΔ` is eliminated or produces `app s (recΔ ...)` with a strictly smaller third argument. The `app` constructor is inert (has no reduction rules), and the new `recΔ` instance has a smaller measure.

4. **Strict decrease for other rules**: Rules `R_int_delta`, both `R_merge_void` rules, `R_merge_cancel`, and `R_eq_refl` all strictly decrease structural size.

**Conclusion**: Define a well-founded measure as the lexicographic product of: (1) the multiset of depths of `eqW` positions (each elimination removes an `eqW`), (2) the multiset of "natural number heights" of `recΔ` third arguments (each `R_rec_succ` decreases this), and (3) standard term size for the remaining rules. All reductions decrease this measure, establishing strong normalization.
