**Yes**, strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone.

**Reasoning:**  
The rewrite relation `Step` only applies to the whole term; there are no context rules that allow reductions in subterms. Therefore, a term can be reduced only if its top‑level constructor matches one of the left‑hand sides of the rules. All other terms (e.g., `void`, `delta t`, `app s t`) are normal forms.

For each possible redex:
- `integrate (delta t)` → `void` (normal form).
- `merge void t`, `merge t void`, `merge t t` → `t`, which is a proper subterm.
- `recΔ b s void` → `b`, a subterm.
- `recΔ b s (delta n)` → `app s (recΔ b s n)`. The reduct has `app` as its head, and no rule applies to `app`, so it is a normal form.
- `eqW a a` → either `void` (normal) or `integrate (merge a a)`. The latter has head `integrate` but its argument is not `delta`, hence it is a normal form.
- `eqW a b` (with `a ≠ b`) → `integrate (merge a b)`, again a normal form.

Thus every reduction leads either to a subterm (strictly smaller by the usual size measure) or to a term that is already a normal form. A simple induction on the size of the term (number of constructors) then shows that every reduction sequence is finite: no infinite chain is possible. No external axioms or additional assumptions are required.
