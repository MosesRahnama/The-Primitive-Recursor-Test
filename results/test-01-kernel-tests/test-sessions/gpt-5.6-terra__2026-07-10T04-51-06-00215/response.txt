Yes. Strong normalization can be established from these rules alone; there is no apparent looping rule or required extra axiom.

A standard recursive path ordering / lexicographic path ordering can orient every rule strictly. For example, choose a constructor-symbol precedence containing

```text
eqW ≻ integrate ≻ merge
recΔ ≻ app
```

with all nonconstant symbols above `void` (and otherwise extend it to a well-founded precedence).

Then:

- `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` decreases because `eqW` is above `integrate` and `merge`, while the left side is also above its arguments.
- `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)` decreases because `recΔ ≻ app`, the left side is above `s`, and `recΔ b s (delta n)` is lexicographically above `recΔ b s n` through `delta n > n`.
- The `merge`, zero-recursion, equality-reflexivity, and integration rules decrease by subterm descent and/or precedence.

This remains true even though `R_eq_diff` applies when its arguments happen to be identical: it may choose the non-reflexive-looking branch
`eqW a a → integrate (merge a a)`, but that still strictly decreases in the ordering and cannot produce an infinite sequence.

Thus every displayed rule is compatible with a well-founded reduction ordering. Consequently the root `Step` relation is strongly normalizing, and so is its ordinary closure under term contexts if that is the intended rewrite relation. No structural axioms, guards, side conditions, or object-level assumptions are needed.
