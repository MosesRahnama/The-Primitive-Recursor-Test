Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone.  
The rewrite system is a first‑order TRS over a finite signature. All rules can be oriented by a recursive path order (RPO) with a suitable precedence, for instance:  

 eqW > integrate, eqW > merge, recΔ > app, integrate > void,  
 with all other symbols pairwise incomparable.

Under this precedence each rule is strictly decreasing in RPO:  

- R_int_delta: integrate(delta(t)) > void (integrate > void).  
- R_merge_void_left/right: merge(void,t) > t (subterm).  
- R_merge_cancel: merge(t,t) > t (subterm).  
- R_rec_zero: recΔ(b,s,void) > b (subterm).  
- R_rec_succ: recΔ(b,s,delta(n)) > app(s,recΔ(b,s,n)) because recΔ > app and the third argument strictly decreases (delta(n) > n by subterm) while the first two are equal.  
- R_eq_refl: eqW(a,a) > void (eqW > void).  
- R_eq_diff: eqW(a,b) > integrate(merge(a,b)) because eqW > integrate and eqW(a,b) > merge(a,b) (eqW > merge and a,b are subterms).

Since RPO is a well‑founded reduction order for finite signatures, every reduction sequence terminates. Hence the calculus is strongly normalizing.  

Alternatively, a lexicographic measure (number of eqW symbols, then depth of the third argument of recΔ, then term size) also yields a termination proof without external assumptions.
