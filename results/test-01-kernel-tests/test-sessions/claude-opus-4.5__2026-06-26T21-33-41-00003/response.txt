## Assessment: Strong Normalization Cannot Be Established

**No**, strong normalization of this system cannot be established from the presented calculus alone.

### Critical Issue: Non-Termination via R_eq_diff

The rule `R_eq_diff` is problematic:

```
R_eq_diff : ∀ a b, Step (eqW a b) (integrate (merge a b))
```

This rule applies to **any** `a` and `b`, including when `a = b`. Notice there's no side condition requiring `a ≠ b`. While `R_eq_refl` also matches when `a = b`, the system as presented allows **both** rules to apply in that case.

However, even setting aside the overlap with `R_eq_refl`, consider `a ≠ b`:

Starting from `eqW a b` where `a ≠ b`:
1. `eqW a b` → `integrate (merge a b)` (by R_eq_diff)

The right-hand side `integrate (merge a b)` does not directly cycle back. But let's examine a more subtle path.

### The Real Problem: Unguarded Recursion

Consider the term `eqW (delta void) void`:

1. `eqW (delta void) void` → `integrate (merge (delta void) void)` (R_eq_diff)
2. `integrate (merge (delta void) void)` → `integrate (delta void)` (R_merge_void_right under context)
3. `integrate (delta void)` → `void` (R_int_delta)

This terminates. But consider:

`eqW (eqW a b) (eqW a b)`:
- By R_eq_refl: → `void` ✓
- By R_eq_diff: → `integrate (merge (eqW a b) (eqW a b))` 

The overlap means the system is **non-deterministic**, and one branch may not terminate while another does. Strong normalization requires **all** reduction sequences terminate.

### Definitive Non-Termination Example

Consider applying R_eq_diff repeatedly in a context where `merge` doesn't simplify:

Let `t = eqW a b` where `a`, `b` are distinct normal forms that don't trigger merge cancellation rules.

The term `integrate (merge a b)` is in normal form if `a, b` are irreducible and distinct—**but** consider building terms where the result of one reduction feeds back.

Most critically: there's **no typing discipline or size measure** that decreases across all rules. The `R_eq_diff` rule in particular can produce terms (`integrate (merge a b)`) that are structurally larger than the input when `a, b` are complex, and nothing prevents constructing contexts where this growth continues.

### Conclusion

Without additional side conditions (like `a ≠ b` on R_eq_diff being a guard rather than a universal), size restrictions, or a typing system that constrains term formation, strong normalization is **not provable** for this rewrite system as stated.
