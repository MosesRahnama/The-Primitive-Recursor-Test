Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone.  
The system has no congruence rules; reduction (`Step`) is only at the root of terms.  

- Rules like `peach plum t → t`, `peach t plum → t`, `peach t t → t`, `banana b s plum → b`, and `mango (grape t) → plum` are **projections** that strictly decrease term size. Any sequence of these alone must terminate because sizes are natural numbers.  
- The **expanding rules** `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)` and `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)` (with `a ≠ b`) produce terms whose root constructors are `pear` and `mango`. There are no rewrite rules for `pear`, and `mango` only reduces when its argument is `grape`; `mango (peach a b)` has an argument of the form `peach`, not `grape`. Hence the results of these expanding rules are **root normal forms** that cannot be reduced further.  

Every redex either reduces to a subterm (strictly smaller) or reduces to a dead‑end normal form. Therefore every reduction sequence is finite, and strong normalization holds without adding extra rules, axioms, or side conditions.
