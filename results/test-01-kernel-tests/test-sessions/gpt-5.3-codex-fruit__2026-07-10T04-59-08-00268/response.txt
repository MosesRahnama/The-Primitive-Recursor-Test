Yes — from the rules shown, strong normalization is establishable without adding any extra axioms/rules.

Why (briefly): define a well-founded measure into naturals that strictly decreases on every `Step`. A simple recursive “size/weight” works if `banana` is weighted to account for primitive recursion on its third argument. For example, pick a measure `μ : Trace → Nat` with constructors weighted so that:

- usual unary/binary constructors increase by at least 1 over subterms,
- and crucially `μ (banana b s n)` is something like `C + μ b + μ s + K * μ n` with `K` large enough that
  - `banana b s plum → b` decreases, and
  - `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)` also decreases.

All other rules are plainly decreasing under ordinary size:
- `mango (grape t) → plum`,
- `peach`-simplifications/cancellation,
- `cherry a a → plum`,
- `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)` (strictly smaller than a `cherry`-node if constructor weights are chosen appropriately).

Since every rewrite step strictly decreases a natural-valued measure, there can be no infinite reduction sequence. Hence the TRS is strongly normalizing from the presented calculus alone.
