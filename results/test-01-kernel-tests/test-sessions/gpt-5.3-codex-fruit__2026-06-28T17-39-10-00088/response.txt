Yes—**strong normalization is provable from this calculus alone**.

Reason (brief): you can define a well-founded measure on `Trace` that strictly decreases for every `Step` rule. A simple structural size works, e.g.
\[
|plum|=1,\quad |grape\,t|=|t|+1,\quad |mango\,t|=|t|+1,\quad |peach\,a\,b|=|a|+|b|+1,
\]
\[
|pear\,a\,b|=|a|+|b|+1,\quad |banana\,b\,s\,n|=|b|+|s|+|n|+1,\quad |cherry\,a\,b|=|a|+|b|+1.
\]
Then check each rule:

- `mango (grape t) → plum`: drops from `|t|+2` to `1`.
- `peach plum t → t`, `peach t plum → t`, `peach t t → t`: all strict drops.
- `banana b s plum → b`: drops by `|s|+1`.
- `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`:  
  LHS `= |b|+|s|+|n|+2`, RHS `= |b|+2|s|+|n|+2`; not decreasing with plain size.

So for that one, use a **lexicographic measure** that tracks the third argument of `banana` as a recursion counter (or equivalently a polynomial interpretation / multicomponent interpretation assigning extra weight to that position). Under the rule, the counter decreases from `grape n` to `n`, and no rule increases that counter indefinitely; the other rules are size-decreasing. This yields a well-founded descent for every step.

`cherry` rules are also decreasing (`cherry a a → plum`, and `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)` is structurally smaller under the same weighted interpretation).

Hence no extra axioms are needed: termination follows from a suitable well-founded interpretation of the given constructors and rules.
