**Assessment:** Yes, strong normalization of this system can be established solely from the given syntax and rewrite rules.

**Reasoning:**  
The reduction relation `Step` is defined inductively without any congruence (i.e., no reduction under constructors). Evaluation happens exclusively at the root of a term.  

The rewrite rules split into two groups:  
1. **Size-decreasing “exposing” rules:**  
   `merge void t → t`, `merge t void → t`, `merge t t → t`, `recΔ b s void → b`, `integrate (delta t) → void`, `eqW a a → void`.  
   These strip a root constructor and expose a subterm, strictly decreasing the term’s size (or at least never increasing it, while making the term structurally smaller in a well-founded sense).  
2. **Size-increasing “terminal” rules:**  
   `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)` and `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` (for any `a,b`).  
   Both produce terms (`app ...`, `integrate (merge ...)`) that are *normal forms*: no rule matches an `app` or an `integrate` whose argument is not exactly `delta t`. Hence they cannot reduce further.

No rule ever creates a new root redex of the exposing kind (`merge ...`, `recΔ ... void`, `integrate (delta ...)`, `eqW ...`) from its result; the only terms that can be produced at the root are `void`, a pre-existing subterm, `app`, or `integrate(merge ...)`. Consequently, any reduction sequence consists of finitely many exposing steps (bounded by the nesting depth of exposing constructors in the initial term) followed by at most one terminal step. No infinite sequences or cycles can arise. Therefore, the system is strongly normalizing, and the proof requires no external axioms or hidden assumptions.
