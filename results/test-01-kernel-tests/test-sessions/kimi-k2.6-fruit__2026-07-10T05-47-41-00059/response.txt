The most direct standard argument is a **recursive path ordering** (RPO).  Choose a precedence such as  

```
cherry > banana > mango > peach > pear > grape > plum
```

with left-to-right lexicographic status.  Then every rule strictly decreases its left-hand side:

* **Precedence** handles  
  `mango(grape t) → plum`, `cherry a a → plum`, and `cherry a b → mango(peach a b)` (the last because `cherry > mango > peach`, and the LHS is greater than each argument of the RHS by the subterm property).

* **Subterm property** handles  
  `peach plum t → t`, `peach t plum → t`, `peach t t → t`, and `banana b s plum → b`.

* The recursive **banana** rule  
  `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`  
  is oriented because `banana > pear`.  The LHS is greater than the duplicated `s` by the subterm property, and greater than the inner `banana(b,s,n)` because the heads are equal and the third argument `grape n` is greater than `n` by the subterm property.  The duplication of `s` is harmless: the outer `pear` is inert, and the recursive `banana` call occurs with a structurally smaller third argument.

Since RPO is monotonic and well-founded, every rewrite step (including steps inside subterms, which the ordering is closed under) decreases the term.  The inequality guard on `R_cherry_diff` only *restricts* the rule, so it cannot create infinite reductions.
