Assessment:  Yes – every sequence of applications of the eight rules shown in Step must terminate, so the system is strongly normalising, and this can be proved from the rules themselves without importing any extra axioms.

Sketch of the argument  

1.  Rewriting is root-only.  
   The inductive definition of Step contains no context-closure constructor, so a rule can be applied only when the whole term at the root matches its left-hand side.  This makes the termination analysis much simpler, because once the constructor at the root is changed to one that never appears on a rule’s left-hand side, rewriting is finished.

2.  Constructors that are never a redex.  
   plum, grape, mango, pear and cherry never occur as the outer symbol of a left-hand side, except for the special shapes mango (grape t) and cherry ⋯  The terms

        plum,     grape t,     mango u   (u ≠ grape v),
        pear u v, cherry u v

   are therefore normal forms.

3.  What the rules really do.  
   • R_mango_grape and R_cherry_* reduce directly to a normal form (plum
     or mango(…)).  
   • R_banana_zero reduces a banana-headed term to its first argument b,
     which may or may not still be reducible.  
   • R_peach_* simply returns one of its arguments, so the outer symbol
     changes from peach to whatever that argument’s outer symbol is.  
   • The only potentially “dangerous’’ rule is

          banana b s (grape n)  →  pear s (banana b s n)     (R_apple_orange)

     because it introduces a fresh pear and re-creates another banana.  
     However the recreated banana is now protected by the outer pear and
     can never again be the root of the term (root-only rewriting!).  So
     R_apple_orange can be fired at most once in any reduction sequence.

4.  A simple well-founded measure.  
   Let   μ : Trace → ℕ   be defined by

        μ plum                 = 0
        μ (grape t)            = 1 + μ t
        μ (mango t)            = 1 + μ t
        μ (peach t u)          = 1 + μ t + μ u
        μ (pear  t u)          = 1 + μ t + μ u
        μ (banana b s n)       = 2 + μ b + μ s + μ n
        μ (cherry a b)         = 1 + μ a + μ b

   (any additive size function would do; the particular coefficients are
   irrelevant as long as they are positive).

   • R_mango_grape, R_peach_*, R_peach_cancel and R_banana_zero all
     strictly decrease μ.

   • R_cherry_refl and R_cherry_diff also strictly decrease μ.

   • R_apple_orange may increase the raw syntactic size of the term, but
     it changes the root symbol from banana to pear, and—because rewriting
     is root-only—that newly created banana can never again be exposed as
     a redex.  Hence the number of remaining possible rule applications
     strictly decreases by one.  A more precise measure that pairs

        (# root-level redexes, μ)

     in the lexicographic order decreases strictly under every rule.

   Because ℕ × ℕ with the lexicographic ordering is well-founded, there is
   no infinite descending chain, i.e. no infinite rewrite sequence.

5.  Strong normalisation follows.  
   Every term admits only a finite number of successive root rewrites, so
   all reduction sequences are finite; hence the system is strongly
   normalising without any additional axioms or side conditions.

Therefore strong normalisation can indeed be established from the
presented calculus alone.
