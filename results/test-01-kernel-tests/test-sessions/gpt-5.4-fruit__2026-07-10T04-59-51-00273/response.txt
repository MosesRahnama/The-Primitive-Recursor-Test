Raw assessment: no, strong normalization cannot be established from the presented rewrite calculus alone.

Reason, briefly:

- There is an explicit self-embedding rewrite:
  - `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`.
- Under any ordinary first-order notion of rewriting on terms, rules may fire in subterms as well as at the root. Then from a term like
  - `banana b s (grape n)`
  one step gives
  - `pear s (banana b s n)`,
  and if `n` itself is a `grape ...`, the `banana` subterm can be rewritten again inside the `pear`, yielding an arbitrarily long reduction sequence.
- More decisively, pick `n := grape n'`. Then:
  - `banana b s (grape (grape n')) → pear s (banana b s (grape n')) → pear s (pear s (banana b s n')) → ...`
  so the number of surrounding `pear s` constructors grows while a reducible `banana` persists underneath. This blocks any simple decreasing measure on term size and shows the system is not strongly normalizing if contextual rewriting is allowed, as is standard.

Even at the root-only level, the calculus as presented does not itself provide a termination argument; and under the standard rewrite interpretation it actually admits infinite/ arbitrarily extendable reduction behavior via the `banana` rule. So from the file alone, without extra restrictions or assumptions, strong normalization is not derivable.
