Yes — **for the relation exactly presented in the file**, strong normalization can be established from the calculus alone.

Reasoning:

- `Step` contains only the eight displayed root rewrite rules.
- There are **no congruence/context-closure rules**, so rewriting does not happen inside subterms unless a subterm is exposed as the whole term by a rule such as `peach plum t → t`.
- Most rules strictly collapse structure:
  - `mango (grape t) → plum`
  - `peach plum t → t`
  - `peach t plum → t`
  - `peach t t → t`
  - `banana b s plum → b`
  - `cherry a a → plum`
- The apparently size-increasing rule

  ```lean
  banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)
  ```

  does not threaten nontermination under the given `Step`, because `pear ... ...` has no rewrite rule at the root, and there is no rule allowing reduction inside `pear`.

- Likewise,

  ```lean
  cherry a b → mango (peach a b)
  ```

  for `a ≠ b` produces a term whose root is `mango`, but its argument is `peach a b`, not `grape t`, so no `mango` rule applies.

Thus every possible rewrite either decreases to a subterm/constant, or lands immediately in a root-normal form. A proof can be done by structural induction on `Trace`, or by defining a well-founded measure that treats the exceptional `banana ... (grape n)` case separately as a terminal target.

Important boundary: this assessment is for `Step` **as defined**. If one silently added contextual closure, evaluation under constructors, extra congruence rules, guards, or object-level assumptions, that would be a different system. But from the presented calculus alone, strong normalization is provable.
