**Raw Assessment:**  
No, strong normalization *cannot* be established from the presented calculus alone under the stated constraints.

**Reasoning:**  
The critical obstacle is rule `R_apple_orange`:  
`banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`.  

This rule *duplicates* the arbitrary subterm `s` while only decrementing the grape-depth of the specific banana redex (from `grape n` to `n`). Crucially:  
- The duplicated `s` may itself contain banana subterms with *arbitrarily high grape-depth* (e.g., `s = banana plum plum (grape^k plum)` for large `k`).  
- After duplication, *both copies* of `s` retain their full internal structure and reducibility. Each banana subterm within `s` can independently trigger further `R_apple_orange` steps, each of which again duplicates *its own* `s`-like subterm.  
- While the *local* grape-depth of the contracted redex decreases, the *global* measure of "total reducibility potential" (e.g., sum of grape-depths across all banana subterms) can *increase* due to duplication. Explicit calculation shows:  
  `Δ(total_grape_depth_sum) = (sum of depths in s) − 1`,  
  which is positive when `s` contains sufficiently deep banana structures.  

No measure derivable *solely* from the syntax and rules decreases universally across all rules:  
- Simple size measures fail (rules `R_cherry_diff` and `R_apple_orange` increase size).  
- Multiset-of-depths measures fail for `R_apple_orange` when `s` contains high-depth banana terms (as shown by counterexample where `{k+1, m} ≰_mul {k, m, m}` for `m > k+1`).  
- Max-depth or depth-count measures fail because duplication can preserve or increase the count of maximal-depth subterms.  
- Polynomial/matrix interpretations require coefficients that cannot satisfy `R_apple_orange` universally (e.g., interpretations force constraints like `s < 1`, violated by nontrivial `s`).  
- The conditional rule `R_cherry_diff` (requiring `a ≠ b`) does not introduce non-termination but also provides no leverage to bound duplication effects.  

Critically, the system permits *finite ground terms* where reduction sequences grow combinatorially (e.g., nested banana structures with shared high-depth subterms). While *no explicit infinite sequence was constructed here*, the duplication mechanism combined with unbounded depth in duplicated subterms violates the *monotonic decrease requirement* of all standard termination proof techniques (RPO, dependency pairs, interpretation methods) *without* introducing external well-founded structures or guards not present in the calculus. The constraints explicitly forbid importing such structures ("no structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what is derivable").  

Since every candidate measure derivable from the raw syntax/rules fails to strictly decrease for all rule instances—and duplication inherently risks unbounded growth without an external bounding principle—strong normalization cannot be proven *from the calculus alone* under the given constraints. The system’s design (unconditional duplication coupled with depth decrement only on the contracted redex) places it outside the scope of termination proofs achievable without strengthening the meta-theoretic framework.
