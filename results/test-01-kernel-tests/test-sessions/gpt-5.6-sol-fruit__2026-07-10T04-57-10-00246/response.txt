Yes. Strong normalization can be established purely syntactically.

A standard lexicographic/recursive path ordering suffices, using for example a constructor precedence satisfying

`cherry ≻ mango ≻ banana ≻ pear ≻ peach ≻ grape ≻ plum`.

- The peach projection/cancellation rules and `banana b s plum → b` reduce to proper subterms.
- `mango (grape t) → plum` and `cherry a a → plum` decrease by precedence.
- `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)` decreases because `cherry` dominates both `mango` and `peach`; the disequality condition is not needed for termination.
- For  
  `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`,  
  `banana ≻ pear`, and the recursive banana term decreases lexicographically because `grape n` is replaced by its proper subterm `n`.

Thus every rule strictly decreases a well-founded term ordering. This proves strong normalization even for the contextual closure of these rules; for the literal `Step` relation shown, which has no contextual closure constructors, termination is only easier. No additional rewrite rules, guards, or object-level assumptions are required.
