**Yes**, SN can be established from the given inductive syntax and `Step` rules alone.

Define a rank function `rank : Trace → Nat` by structural recursion on the term (justified directly by the inductive elimination principle for `Trace`):

- `rank(plum) = 0`, `rank(grape t) = 0`, `rank(pear s u) = 0`, `rank(mango u) = 0` if `u` is not of the form `grape _` (the only case where `Step` applies).
- `rank(cherry a a) = 1 + rank(plum) = 1`.
- `rank(cherry a b) = 1` whenever `a ≠ b` (reduct `mango (peach a b)` has rank 0).
- `rank(banana b s plum) = 1 + rank(b)`.
- `rank(banana b s (grape n)) = 1` (reduct `pear s (banana b s n)` has rank 0).
- `rank(peach plum t) = 1 + rank(t)`, `rank(peach t plum) = 1 + rank(t)`, `rank(peach t t) = 1 + rank(t)`.
- `rank(mango (grape t)) = 1 + rank(plum) = 1`.
- All other terms have rank 0 (they match no `Step` rule).

Constructor disjointness (no term is both a `peach` and a `grape`, no term equals two distinct constructors, etc.) and decidable equality are obtained by induction on the inductive definition of `Trace`; no external axioms are required. Consequently every one-step reduct has strictly smaller rank. By induction on `rank(t)`, every term admits only finite `Step`-chains (any descending chain in `Nat` is finite). Hence the relation is strongly normalizing.
