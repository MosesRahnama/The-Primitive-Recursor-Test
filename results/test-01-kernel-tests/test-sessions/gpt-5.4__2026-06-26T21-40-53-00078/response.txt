Raw assessment: no, not from this calculus alone.

Reason, briefly: the rewrite relation as presented is not strongly normalizing, because it contains an unconditional rule

- `R_eq_diff : Step (eqW a b) (integrate (merge a b))`

for all `a b`, including the case `a = b`. But there is also

- `R_eq_refl : Step (eqW a a) void`.

So for any term `t`, we have
- `eqW t t → void`, and also
- `eqW t t → integrate (merge t t)`.

Now choose `t := eqW u u`. Then:
- `merge t t → t` by `R_merge_cancel`,
- hence `integrate (merge t t) → integrate t`.

Therefore
- `eqW t t → integrate t = integrate (eqW u u)`,
and inside that subterm one can again rewrite `eqW u u → integrate (merge (eqW u u) (eqW u u)) → integrate (eqW u u)` in context, producing
- `integrate (eqW u u) → integrate (integrate (eqW u u)) → …`

This yields an infinite reduction sequence once closure under contexts is taken as the rewrite calculus. So strong normalization fails.

Even if one interpreted `Step` only as the listed top-level contractions, the system as given is too weakly specified to support a meaningful general strong-normalization theorem for all terms under rewriting; but under the standard notion of rewriting, it is outright not strongly normalizing.

So the answer is: strong normalization cannot be established from the presented rules alone.
