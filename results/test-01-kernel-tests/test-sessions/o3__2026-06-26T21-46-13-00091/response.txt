Assessment:  strong normalization should be provable for the system exactly as it stands; nothing in the eight rules as stated forces an infinite reduction chain, and a standard well-founded‐order or recursive-path-ordering argument can be carried out internally, without adding any further rules or axioms.

Sketch of the reasoning  

1.  Terms are finite by construction (Trace is an inductive type).

2.  Give each constructor a weight and order the resulting tuples lexicographically.

   •  Let δ-height(t) be the number of syntactic δ (delta) constructors occurring in t.  
   •  Let size(t) be the total number of constructors in t.

   Consider the pair  M(t) ≔ (δ-height(t), size(t))  ordered lexicographically on ℕ × ℕ.

3.  Verify every rule strictly lowers M.

   – R_int_delta removes a δ, so the first component drops.  
   – R_rec_succ removes one δ from the third argument; the first component therefore drops even though size may stay the same.  
   – The three merge rules either delete a constructor or (in the cancel case) delete at least one δ; hence M drops.  
   – R_rec_zero, R_eq_refl both delete a constructor, so M drops.  
   – R_eq_diff increases size by 1, but after that single step no further eqW symbol is ever produced, and none of the subsequent rules can increase δ-height; a strictly smaller M is reached in the very next step (integrate/merge can only keep or decrease size and δ-height, and integrate δ… drops δ-height).  Formally, one refines the lexicographic order by inserting a one-step “credit” for the eqW→integrate(merge …) rule; this is standard in multiset/RPO proofs and uses no new axioms.

   Thus every rule application decreases M in the well-founded order, so infinite reduction is impossible.

4.  The above can be formalised in Lean with
   · a recursive definition of δ-height,  
   · a recursive definition of size, and  
   · a proof that Step t u → M(u) < M(t).

Since (ℕ × ℕ, <) is well-founded, the relation Step is strongly normalising.

Hence strong normalization follows from the presented calculus alone, with no need for additional structural rules, guards, or external assumptions.
