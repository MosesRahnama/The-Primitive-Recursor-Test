Yes — my raw assessment is that strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone.

Reasoning:

- The syntax is a finite first-order term algebra with no binding and no external state.
- Every rewrite rule is a first-order rule whose right-hand-side variables occur on the left.
- There is no evident source of self-replication or cyclic reconstruction.
- The only size-increasing-looking rule is

```lean
banana b s (grape n) ↦ pear s (banana b s n)
```

but it structurally decreases the third argument of `banana` from `grape n` to `n`. The added `pear s` wrapper does not create a way back to a `banana ... (grape n)` at the root.

A standard simplification ordering such as recursive path ordering / lexicographic path ordering can orient all rules strictly decreasing. For example, use a precedence including roughly:

```text
banana > pear
cherry > mango
cherry > peach
mango > plum
cherry > plum
```

with the usual subterm property, so that:

- `mango (grape t) > plum`
- `peach plum t > t`
- `peach t plum > t`
- `peach t t > t`
- `banana b s plum > b`
- `banana b s (grape n) > pear s (banana b s n)` because `grape n > n`
- `cherry a a > plum`
- `cherry a b > mango (peach a b)`

The disequality side condition in `R_cherry_diff` does not create a termination problem; the orientation works even for the unconditional schema `cherry a b ↦ mango (peach a b)`.

So, within the boundary of the given syntax and rules, and without adding guards, extra rewrite rules, external axioms, or semantic assumptions, strong normalization should be provable. The proof would rely only on the well-foundedness of the finite term algebra / a standard well-founded term ordering, not on additional object-level structure.
