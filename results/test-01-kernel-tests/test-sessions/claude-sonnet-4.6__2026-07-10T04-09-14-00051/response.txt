## Raw Assessment

**Strong normalization cannot be established from this calculus alone.**

### Reasoning

**The critical obstruction is `R_eq_diff` combined with `R_rec_succ`.**

Consider `R_eq_diff`:
```
Step (eqW a b) (integrate (merge a b))
```
and `R_rec_succ`:
```
Step (recΔ b s (delta n)) (app s (recΔ b s n))
```

**Problem 1: `R_eq_diff` is not well-founded as stated.**

`R_eq_diff` fires for *any* `a b`, including the case `a = b`. But `R_eq_refl` also fires for `a = b`. This means `eqW a a` can step to either `void` (terminating) or `integrate (merge a a)` (potentially looping). More critically, the rule `R_eq_diff` has **no guard** preventing it from firing when `a = b`, so the relation is nondeterministic — but nondeterminism alone doesn't break SN. What matters is whether *every* reduction sequence terminates.

**Problem 2: `R_rec_succ` can diverge.**

The rule `R_rec_succ` reduces `recΔ b s (delta n)` to `app s (recΔ b s n)`. There is **no rewrite rule that reduces `app`**. So `app s (recΔ b s n)` is a term where `recΔ b s n` sits under `app s (·)`, but `app` has no reduction rule — meaning `recΔ b s n` cannot be further reduced from within that `app` context using any rule in the calculus.

More precisely: `recΔ b s (delta (delta ... (delta void)...))` will reduce by `R_rec_succ` repeatedly, producing:
```
app s (app s (... (app s (recΔ b s void))...))
```
and then `recΔ b s void` reduces to `b` by `R_rec_zero`. So this particular family *does* terminate.

**Problem 3: The decisive issue — `R_eq_diff` can produce `integrate (merge a b)` where `a` or `b` contains further `eqW` subterms, but more fatally, there is no termination measure.**

Consider a term like `recΔ (eqW t t) s (delta void)`. This reduces via `R_rec_succ` to `app s (recΔ (eqW t t) s void)`, then to `app s (eqW t t)`. Now `eqW t t` can reduce via `R_eq_diff` to `integrate (merge t t)`, which reduces via `R_int_delta`... but only if `merge t t` is of the form `delta _`. There is no such guarantee.

**The definitive problem:** `R_eq_diff` applies unconditionally to `eqW a b` regardless of the syntactic relationship between `a` and `b`, and `R_eq_refl` *also* applies when `a = b`. The system has **overlapping rules with no decreasing measure** connecting them. To assign a termination measure, one would need to know that `integrate (merge a b)` is strictly smaller than `eqW a b` under some well-founded order. But `a` and `b` appear in *both* `eqW a b` and `integrate (merge a b)` — the sizes are equal up to the wrapper constructors `integrate`/`merge` vs `eqW`, so no simple structural measure works. A multiset or polynomial interpretation would need to be verified, but **no such interpretation is provided or derivable from the syntax alone**.

Furthermore, without a rule reducing `app`, terms like `app s t` are **stuck** (no rule applies), meaning `app` acts as a black hole — terms can be buried under `app` and become irreducible, which is fine for SN but means the calculus is **partial** (many terms have no normal form reachable to a ground value).

### Conclusion

**Strong normalization is not provable from the presented calculus alone.** The rules `R_eq_diff` (unconditional, no size decrease guaranteed) and `R_rec_succ` (unfolds under `app` which has no reduction rule) do not admit a straightforward termination proof without importing an external well-founded order or structural induction principle beyond what the syntax provides. The calculus as written does not carry enough information to close the proof internally.
