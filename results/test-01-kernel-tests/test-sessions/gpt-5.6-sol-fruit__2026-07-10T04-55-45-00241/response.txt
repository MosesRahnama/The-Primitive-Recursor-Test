**Yes— for `Step` exactly as defined.** Strong normalization follows from a simple natural-number ranking, without additional rewrite rules or object-level assumptions.

For example, define:

- `rank plum = 0`
- `rank (grape t) = 0`
- `rank (mango t) = 1`
- `rank (peach a b) = 1 + max (rank a) (rank b)`
- `rank (pear a b) = 0`
- `rank (banana b s n) = 1 + rank b`
- `rank (cherry a b) = 2`

Every `Step x y` strictly decreases this rank:

- peach rules return a child;
- banana-zero returns `b`;
- banana-success produces a `pear`, of rank `0`;
- mango-grape produces `plum`;
- cherry rules produce rank `0` or `1`.

Thus an infinite `Step` sequence would give an infinite descending sequence of naturals, which is impossible.

This assessment is for the relation presented in the file. It has no contextual/congruence rules allowing rewriting inside constructor arguments; silently adding such closure would define a different relation.
