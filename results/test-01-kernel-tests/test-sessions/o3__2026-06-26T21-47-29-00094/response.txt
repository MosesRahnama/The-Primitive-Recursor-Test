Raw assessment:  Yes – strong normalisation of the above rewrite system can be proved from the rules that are already present, without adding any further axioms or side-conditions.

Sketch of the reasoning.

1.  Finite syntax.  
   All traces are built from a finite set of constructors, so every term is a finite tree.

2.  An easily-oriented reduction ordering exists.  
   Take the recursive-path ordering (RPO) with the precedence

        eqW  ≻  recΔ  ≻  app  ≻  integrate  ≻  merge  ≻  delta  ≻  void.

   •  Rules whose left–hand side has symbol f and right–hand side has a
      symbol g ≺ f are automatically decreasing in the RPO.  
   •  All eight rules orient from left to right under this precedence:
        – eqW → integrate(…)  (eqW ≻ integrate)  
        – recΔ → app(…)       (recΔ ≻ app)  
        – integrate → void     (integrate ≻ void)  
        – merge → t            (merge ≻ (root of t) or merge disappears)  
        – the two “void” rules and the “cancel” rule strictly drop a top
          constructor, hence decrease.  

3.  RPO is well-founded.  
   Because every rewrite step is strictly decreasing for the RPO, there
   is no infinite descending chain of terms, hence no infinite reduction
   sequence.

4.  Therefore the relation Step is terminating; i.e. the calculus is
   strongly normalising.

No supplementary rewrite rules, guards, or external axioms are needed; the argument uses only standard termination technology (well-founded orders such as RPO) that is admissible in first-order rewriting.
