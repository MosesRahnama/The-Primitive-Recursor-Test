**Yes**

Strong normalization follows by induction on term size (derived directly from the inductive `Trace` constructors, with `size plum = 1` and `size (c args) = 1 + Σ size(args)`). For any `t`:

- If `t` matches no LHS, it is already normal.
- The rules `R_cherry_diff` and `R_apple_orange` produce `mango (peach a b)` or `pear s _`, respectively. Neither matches any LHS (head constructors differ from `mango`/`peach`/`banana`/`cherry`, and `peach a b` never equals `grape _` by injectivity of constructors), so the successor is irreducible.
- Every other rule yields a proper subterm or `plum`; the size strictly decreases (by at least 1, up to 2·|t|). By the induction hypothesis every chain from the successor is finite, hence every chain from `t` is finite.

No infinite descending chain exists because `Nat` is well-founded and the only size-increasing steps are immediately terminating. The argument uses only the given inductive syntax, the eight `Step` constructors, constructor injectivity/disjointness (derivable from the inductive declaration), and ordinary Noetherian induction on `Nat`; no congruence closure, extra rewrite rules, or external lemmas are required. Self-loops are impossible because finite terms cannot satisfy equations such as `t = peach t t`.
