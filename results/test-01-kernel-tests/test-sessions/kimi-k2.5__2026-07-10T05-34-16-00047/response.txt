**Reasoning:**

The system is strongly normalizing because all reduction sequences are finite, which can be shown via a well-founded measure or by structural analysis:

1. **Simplifying rules:** Most rules strictly decrease the term size (number of constructors). `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_*`, and `R_eq_refl` all reduce complex terms to proper subterms or atomic `void`.

2. **Controlled expansion:** `R_eq_diff` (`eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`) temporarily increases size by 1, but the resulting `integrate` is inert (only reduces when its argument is `delta t`), and `merge` immediately reduces to a subterm (`a`, `b`, or void). Thus this "detour" cannot be iterated indefinitely to create infinite growth.

3. **Well-founded recursion:** `R_rec_succ` (`recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`) appears to increase size (copying `s`), but it strictly decreases the recursion depth of the third argument (stripping one `delta` wrapper). Since `recΔ` only unfolds when its third argument is `void` or `delta`, and each `rec_succ` step reduces the height of this argument, only finitely many unfoldings occur. The resulting tower of `app` constructors is inert (no rules for `app`), and the total number of `s` copies is bounded by the initial `delta`-height.

4. **No regeneration:** No rule creates "new" `eqW` or `recΔ` redexes that weren't already present as subterms. `recΔ` produces only `app` wrappers around smaller instances; `eqW` produces `integrate(merge...)` which resolves to subterms. There is no cyclic dependency where reducing one construct can regenerate a larger instance of itself.

Therefore, every reduction sequence terminates, establishing strong normalization without requiring additional axioms or guards.
