Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the displayed syntax and rules alone.

A standard lexicographic/path ordering on finite `Trace` terms orients every rule strictly downward. For example, choose a precedence satisfying at least

```text
recΔ > app
eqW > integrate > merge > void
integrate > void
```

with the remaining symbols ordered arbitrarily, and give `recΔ` lexicographic argument status.

The only superficially problematic rule is

```text
recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n).
```

Although `s` occurs both as the left argument of `app` and inside the recursive call, the left-hand side is greater than `s` by the subterm property, and it is greater than `recΔ b s n` because the third argument strictly decreases from `delta n` to `n`. Since `recΔ > app`, the whole right-hand side is smaller.

Likewise,

```text
eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)
```

strictly decreases under `eqW > integrate > merge`; the arguments `a` and `b` are subterms of the left-hand side. All other rules either discard structure or return a strict subterm.

Thus every `Step` rule strictly decreases a well-founded ordering on finite syntax trees. Consequently there can be no infinite rewrite sequence. This remains true if rewriting is understood as closed under arbitrary term contexts, since the standard path ordering is context-monotone. No associativity, cancellation laws, extensionality principles, object-level equations, guards, or additional rewrite rules are needed.
