Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone.

**Reasoning:**  
The `Step` relation is defined inductively with no congruence (compatibility) rules—reduction occurs only at the root of the term. Consequently, every step either:

1. Reaches a normal form immediately (rules `R_mango_grape`, `R_apple_orange`, `R_cherry_refl`, `R_cherry_diff`), because their right-hand sides have root constructors (`plum`, `pear`, `mango`) that are not matched by any left-hand side pattern (or in the `mango` case, only matched by `grape` which does not appear).
2. Pops the root constructor and exposes a proper subterm (rules `R_peach_plum_left`, `R_peach_plum_right`, `R_peach_cancel`, `R_banana_zero`). In each of these, the result is a strict subterm of the original, decreasing the depth of the term.

Because terms are finite inductive trees, the proper-subterm relation is well-founded. Any infinite reduction sequence would require infinitely many “pop” steps, producing an infinite descending chain of proper subterms, which is impossible. Hence every reduction sequence terminates.  

No extra structural axioms or hidden assumptions are needed; the side condition `a ≠ b` in `R_cherry_diff` is a decidable syntactic inequality on ground terms and does not affect termination.
