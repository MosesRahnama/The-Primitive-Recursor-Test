Yes – strong normalization can be proved for the given rewrite system using only the eight rules that are listed.

Sketch of a standard proof.

1.  Work with the ordinary one-step reduction relation obtained by closing
    Step under arbitrary contexts.  Because every constructor is
    free and there is no binding, the term language is the set of finite
    first-order trees built from
       plum, grape, mango, peach, pear, banana, cherry.

2.  Choose a well-founded, monotone term order such as the recursive path
    ordering (RPO) with the following precedence (any linear extension that
    puts banana strictly above pear and that puts every proper constructor
    above plum will do):

          banana ≻ pear ≻ mango ≻ peach ≻ cherry ≻ grape ≻ plum.

    Use the standard lexicographic status for all symbols.

3.  Check that every left-hand side is strictly greater than the
    corresponding right-hand side in this ordering.

    • mango (grape t)  ≻  plum
      (root symbols differ; mango ≻ plum).

    • peach plum t     ≻  t        (peach ≻ any root of t).

    • peach t plum     ≻  t.

    • peach t t        ≻  t.

    • banana b s plum  ≻  b        (banana ≻ root(b)).

    • banana b s (grape n)
        ≻  pear s (banana b s n)
      because banana ≻ pear, and the RPO is monotone in
      the arguments; the banana that survives on the right is strictly
      smaller, being a proper subterm of the left side.

    • cherry a a       ≻  plum     (cherry ≻ plum).

    • cherry a b       ≻  mango (peach a b)
      when a ≠ b, since cherry ≻ mango.

    In every case the ordering decreases; no rule increases or keeps the
    same value.

4.  RPO with a finite precedence is well-founded, so there cannot be an
    infinite strictly descending chain of terms.  Therefore every maximal
    reduction sequence in the contextual closure of Step is finite; i.e.
    the calculus is terminating, or strongly normalizing.

5.  No additional rewrite rules, side conditions, structural axioms, or
    external assumptions are used – only the syntax and the eight rules
    that are already present.

Hence strong normalization is derivable inside the system exactly as it
stands.
