## Assessment: Yes — strong normalization holds, and is provable directly from what is given, essentially for a structural reason about how `Step` is defined.

### Key observation

`Step` is defined by exactly 8 constructors, each stating a relation between two **fully concrete term shapes**. There is **no congruence/compatibility closure** anywhere in the definition — i.e. there is no rule of the form "if `Step t t'` then `Step (grape t) (grape t')`" (or the analogous rule for `mango`, `peach`, `pear`, `banana`, `cherry`). Consequently `Step t u` can only hold when the **entire** term `t` matches one of the eight root patterns; you can never reduce inside a subterm while leaving the surrounding context fixed.

This makes the system much more restrictive than an ordinary term-rewriting system (TRS) closed under contexts, and it is exactly what makes strong normalization straightforward.

### Case analysis on the eight rules

- `R_mango_grape`, `R_cherry_refl`: rewrite the whole term to the constant `plum`. Strictly decreases size; `plum` has no outgoing `Step` at all.
- `R_peach_plum_left`, `R_peach_plum_right`, `R_peach_cancel`, `R_banana_zero`: rewrite the whole term to one of its **proper subterms**. This strictly decreases the (well-founded) structural size measure inherited from the inductive definition of `Trace`.
- `R_apple_orange` (`banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`): this *can* increase term size. But crucially, the result has head symbol `pear`, and **no rule has `pear` as a left-hand side at all** — so this result is permanently `Step`-irreducible. Since there is no congruence closure, the "inner" `banana b s n` occurring under `pear` can never be reached again by `Step`. So this rule can fire at most once at the root, and then the derivation dies.
- `R_cherry_diff` (`cherry a b → mango (peach a b)`, `a ≠ b`): also can increase size, but the result `mango (peach a b)` has head `mango` applied to something headed by `peach`, not `grape`. The only rule with LHS `mango (grape t)` requires the argument to literally be `grape`-headed, which `peach a b` never is (different constructor). So again this result is permanently `Step`-irreducible.

### Conclusion

Every application of a "size-increasing" rule (`R_apple_orange`, `R_cherry_diff`) immediately produces a term with no further `Step` redex (since there is no context rule to expose or reduce any subterm), while every other rule strictly decreases the well-founded structural-size measure on the finite inductive type `Trace`. Hence every `Step`-reduction sequence is finite: it consists of finitely many size-decreasing steps, possibly followed by at most one size-increasing step that immediately terminates the sequence.

This argument uses only:
- the inductive (hence well-founded/finite) structure of `Trace`,
- direct case inspection of the 8 given constructors of `Step`,
- ordinary well-founded induction on subterm/size,

with no additional axioms, no congruence rule, and no side conditions beyond what's already stated (`a ≠ b` in `R_cherry_diff` only restricts applicability, it doesn't need extra semantic content for the argument above). So strong normalization is fully derivable from the calculus exactly as presented.
