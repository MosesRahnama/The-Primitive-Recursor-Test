**Assessment: Yes, strong normalization can be established.**

**Reasoning:**

This rewrite system is strongly normalizing (SN) because it admits a well-founded measure that strictly decreases with each reduction step. The argument proceeds by analyzing the "consumption" of critical constructors:

1. **eqW elimination**: The `eqW` constructor appears only on the left-hand sides of `R_eq_refl` and `R_eq_diff`, and appears on **no** right-hand side. Therefore, the number of `eqW` constructors strictly decreases with each eqW-reduction and is never increased. This gives a top-level measure: the count of `eqW` symbols.

2. **Delta consumption**: The `delta` constructor is consumed by `R_int_delta` and `R_rec_succ`, and is **never created** by any rule (no RHS introduces a fresh `delta`). While `R_rec_succ` duplicates the step argument `s` (which may contain deltas), it strictly consumes the **outermost** `delta` from the third argument position. Specifically, `recΔ b s (delta n)` reduces to a term where the new `recΔ` has third argument `n`—a proper subterm of `delta n`.

3. **Recursion depth**: For any `recΔ` constructor, define its "active depth" as the length of the leading `delta`-chain in its third argument (0 if the third argument is not of the form `delta t`). Rule `R_rec_succ` reduces this active depth by 1. No rule can increase the active depth of a `recΔ` third argument, because:
   - The third argument can only become a `delta` if it reduces to one (via internal reductions), but those reductions decrease the overall eqW-count or size.
   - Once the third argument is exposed as `delta n`, applying `R_rec_succ` removes that `delta` and places `n` (which has smaller active depth) inside an `app` context where it cannot regain a leading `delta` without already having one inside.

4. **Lexicographic ordering**: Combining these observations, we can define a well-founded measure as the lexicographic tuple:
   - **First**: Number of `eqW` constructors (decreases with `R_eq_*`).
   - **Second**: Multiset of active depths of all `recΔ` positions (decreases with `R_rec_succ` since one `recΔ` loses a leading `delta`).
   - **Third**: Standard term size or weight (decreases with all other rules).

Rules analysis:
- `R_eq_diff` decreases the first component (removes `eqW`).
- `R_rec_succ` preserves the first two components' ordering but decreases the active depth of the specific `recΔ` it targets.
- All other rules (`R_int_delta`, `R_merge_*`, `R_rec_zero`, `R_eq_refl`) decrease the third component (term size/weight) without affecting the first two.

Since the ordinal $\omega^3$ (or a suitable polynomial interpretation) bounds the derivation height, and no rule introduces `eqW` or creates new `delta` constructors at reducible positions, **every reduction sequence terminates**. The system is strongly normalizing without requiring additional axioms or side conditions.
