A termination measure can be defined by recursion on the syntax.  
For example, interpret `plum` as 0, `grape t` as `f(t)+1`, `mango t` as `f(t)`, `peach t s` and `pear t s` as `f(t)+f(s)+1`, `cherry a b` as `f(a)+f(b)+2`, and `banana b s n` as `2^(f(n)+1) * (f(b)+f(s)+1)`.  
One checks that every rewrite rule strictly reduces this natural number:  

- The `peach` and `cherry` rules decrease by at least 1.  
- `R_mango_grape` decreases because `f(mango(grape t)) = f(t)+1 > 0 = f(plum)`.  
- `R_banana_zero` gives `f(banana b s plum) = 2·(f(b)+f(s)+1) > f(b)`.  
- The critical case `R_apple_orange` yields  
  `f(banana b s (grape n)) = 2 · f(banana b s n)`  
  and the right‑hand side `f(pear s (banana b s n)) = f(s) + f(banana b s n) + 1`;  
  because `f(banana b s n) ≥ 2·(f(s)+1) > f(s)+1`, the left‑hand side is strictly larger.  

Thus every reduction step decrements a natural number, so infinite reduction sequences are impossible.  
This proof uses only the inductive definition of `Trace` and standard meta‑level reasoning (recursion and natural numbers); no extra rewrite rules, axioms, or side conditions are required.
