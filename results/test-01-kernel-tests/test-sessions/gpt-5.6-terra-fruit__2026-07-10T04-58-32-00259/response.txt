Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone.

The key point is that `Step` is only the inductively listed root-step relation; there is no congruence/contextual closure allowing rewrites inside arguments. Thus many apparently recursive-looking right-hand sides are immediately stuck at the root.

A termination argument can use a well-founded structural measure, or directly prove accessibility by structural induction on `Trace`:

- `R_peach_plum_left`, `R_peach_plum_right`, `R_peach_cancel`, and `R_banana_zero` return a proper subterm (`t` or `b`), so any subsequent reductions are structurally smaller.
- `R_mango_grape` reduces directly to `plum`.
- `R_banana_zero` returns `b`; `R_banana_grape` returns a term rooted by `pear`, for which there is no `Step` constructor, hence it is terminal.
- `R_cherry_refl` reduces to `plum`.
- `R_cherry_diff` yields `mango (peach a b)`. This cannot match `R_mango_grape`, because its argument is syntactically `peach a b`, not `grape t`; no other rule has a left-hand side rooted by `mango`. Hence this result is terminal.

Although the `cherry_diff` right-hand side is syntactically larger than its left-hand side, it cannot initiate a further rewrite, so it does not threaten termination. No additional structural axioms, object-level assumptions, side conditions, or rewrite rules are needed.
