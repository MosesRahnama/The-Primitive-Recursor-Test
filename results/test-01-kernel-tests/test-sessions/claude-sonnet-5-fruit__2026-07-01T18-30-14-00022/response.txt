## Assessment: Yes — strong normalization is establishable, and in fact rather directly, from the calculus exactly as given.

### The key structural fact

`Step` is defined by exactly eight constructors, each of which relates *whole* terms of specific shapes:

- `mango (grape t) ⟶ plum`
- `peach plum t ⟶ t`
- `peach t plum ⟶ t`
- `peach t t ⟶ t`
- `banana b s plum ⟶ b`
- `banana b s (grape n) ⟶ pear s (banana b s n)`
- `cherry a a ⟶ plum`
- `cherry a b ⟶ mango (peach a b)` (a ≠ b)

There is **no compatibility/congruence closure** in this file — no rule of the shape "if `Step t t'` then `Step (C t) (C t')`" for any context `C`. So `Step` only ever fires when the *entire* term matches one of the eight left-hand-side patterns; it never rewrites a proper subterm in context. Using only what is given, we must reason about exactly this shallow relation.

### Why this makes SN provable by an elementary measure

Split the eight rules into two groups:

**Size-decreasing / terminal rules** (`R_peach_plum_left`, `R_peach_plum_right`, `R_peach_cancel`, `R_banana_zero`, `R_cherry_refl`, `R_mango_grape`): each of these produces a term that is a genuine subterm of the redex (or the constant `plum`), so a natural-number size measure strictly decreases. Any chain built purely from these rules is therefore bounded by a standard well-founded induction on term size — no extra machinery needed.

**The two size-increasing rules** (`R_apple_orange`, `R_cherry_diff`): both actually *grow* the term (`pear s (banana b s n)` is bigger than `banana b s (grape n)` by `|s|`; `mango (peach a b)` is bigger than `cherry a b` by 1). This is where an infinite/unbounded chain would have to come from *if* `Step` were context-closed. But because `Step` is **not** context-closed:

- `pear s (banana b s n)` has head symbol `pear`, and no rule's left-hand side ever has head `pear` — it is a dead, irreducible term.
- `mango (peach a b)` can only continue via `R_mango_grape`, which needs the argument to literally be `grape t`. Since `peach a b` and `grape t` come from different constructors of the inductive type `Trace`, they can never be equal (constructor disjointness is automatic in Lean's inductive semantics). So `mango (peach a b)` is also dead.

Hence each of these two rules can occur **at most once, and only as the final step**, of any reduction sequence.

### Conclusion

Given the literal relation as specified (no added congruence rule, no extra axioms), every reduction sequence consists of finitely many size-decreasing steps followed possibly by one terminal, dead-ending application of `R_apple_orange` or `R_cherry_diff`. This gives strong normalization by a completely elementary structural/size induction — no reducibility-candidate method, no ordinal analysis, and no strengthening of the system are required. The result follows purely from (1) the finiteness/well-foundedness of `Trace` under subterm size, and (2) constructor injectivity/disjointness already built into the inductive definition — both of which are intrinsic to the presented syntax, not extra assumptions.
