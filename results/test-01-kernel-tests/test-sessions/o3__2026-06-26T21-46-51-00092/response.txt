Assessment: Yes – strong normalization of the presented system can be proved inside the calculus exactly as it stands; no additional rules, side–conditions, or external axioms are needed.

Reasoning (sketch):

1.  Finiteness of constructors  
   The signature is finite and every rule is first-order and constructor-based.  No rule introduces new occurrences of the symbols δ (“delta”) or recΔ that were not already present in the redex, so the depth of those constructors can never increase along a reduction.

2.  Precedence compatible with a standard termination order  
   Give the function symbols the following (strict) precedence, e.g. for a lexicographic path order (LPO) or a recursive path order (RPO):

        recΔ  ≻  app  ≻  merge  ≻  integrate  ≻  delta  ≻  eqW  ≻  void

   With this precedence every left-hand side is strictly greater than its right-hand side:

   • integrate (delta t) ≻ void                (top symbol drops from integrate to constant)  
   • merge void t       ≻ t  
   • merge t void       ≻ t  
   • merge t t          ≻ t  
   • recΔ b s void      ≻ b                    (recΔ drops)  
   • recΔ b s (δ n)     ≻ app s (recΔ b s n)   (recΔ ≻ app, and the recursive recΔ occurs only as a proper sub-term)  
   • eqW a a            ≻ void  
   • eqW a b            ≻ integrate (merge a b) (eqW ≻ integrate)

   Because LPO (or any equivalent well-founded reduction ordering) is well-founded, and every rule decreases the term with respect to it, no infinite reduction sequence exists.  Hence the rewrite system is terminating.

3.  From termination to strong normalization  
   Reduction is purely first-order (no binding, no meta-variables), so termination of the rewrite relation immediately yields strong normalization: every term has only finitely many reduction steps, regardless of the strategy chosen.

Nothing in this argument relies on anything external to the eight rules and the given syntax, so strong normalization is derivable from the calculus alone.
