**Yes.**  
The rewrite relation is terminating.  One can define a lex‑ordered measure  

\[
M(t)=\bigl(\operatorname{depth}_{\,\operatorname{grape}}(t),\;-\operatorname{pear}(t),\;-\operatorname{cherry}(t),\;|t|\bigr)
\]

where  

* \(\operatorname{depth}_{\,\operatorname{grape}}(t)\) is the maximal nesting of `grape` constructors in \(t\);  
* \(\operatorname{pear}(t)\) (resp. \(\operatorname{cherry}(t)\)) is the number of `pear` (resp. `cherry`) constructors in \(t\);  
* \(|t|\) is the total number of constructors (the size of the term).

For each rewrite rule one checks:

* R₁ removes a `grape` → depth decreases.  
* R₂–R₄ and R₅ keep depth, pear‑ and cherry‑counts, but reduce the size.  
* R₆ keeps depth (or lowers it) and **increases** the number of `pear`s, so the second component \(- \operatorname{pear}\) decreases; size may grow, but the second component dominates.  
* R₇ and R₈ keep depth and pear‑count but lower the cherry‑count (and also lower the size).

Thus for every rule \(L\!\to\!R\) we have \(M(L) > M(R)\) in the lexicographic product of well‑founded orders.  Consequently the rewrite relation is well‑founded, i.e. the calculus is strongly normalising.  No additional axioms or side‑conditions are required.
