# Assessment: Strong Normalization Cannot Be Established

**No**, strong normalization of this system cannot be established from the presented calculus alone. The system is **not strongly normalizing**.

## Reasoning

The critical issue lies in the interaction between rules `R_apple_orange` and the potential for non-termination through size increase.

Consider the rule:
```
R_apple_orange : ∀ b s n, Step (banana b s (grape n)) (pear s (banana b s n))
```

This rule rewrites `banana b s (grape n)` to `pear s (banana b s n)`. Crucially, this is a **root-level** rewrite rule — but the system implicitly allows rewriting at any subterm position (as is standard for rewrite relations defined inductively on terms, though I note the `Step` relation here is only defined at the root, with no congruence/context closure rules).

**Wait** — let me re-examine. The `Step` relation has **no congruence rules**. There are no rules of the form "if `Step a b` then `Step (grape a) (grape b)`" etc. So `Step` only applies at the **root** of a term, not under any constructor.

This is crucial. With no context closure, we only rewrite at the top level.

Let me search for a non-terminating reduction sequence. Consider `R_cherry_diff`: when `a ≠ b`, `cherry a b` steps to `mango (peach a b)`. But `mango (peach a b)` can only reduce if it matches `mango (grape t)`, which requires `peach a b` to first reduce to some `grape t` — but `peach a b` can't be rewritten because it's not at the root of the term (it's under `mango`), and there are no congruence rules.

Without congruence rules, the only terms that can step are those that **directly match** a left-hand side at the root. Each rule produces either:
- `plum` (irreducible)
- `t` (a strict subterm of the original)
- `pear s (banana b s n)` (from `banana b s (grape n)`)
- `mango (peach a b)` (from `cherry a b`)

For `pear s (banana b s n)`: the term `pear` can only reduce via `R_peach_plum_left`, `R_peach_plum_right`, or `R_peach_cancel`, not `pear`. Actually — `pear` is a different constructor from `peach`. So `pear s (banana b s n)` matches **no** left-hand side. It's irreducible.

Similarly, `mango (peach a b)` only reduces if `peach a b = grape t` for some `t`, which is impossible since they're distinct constructors.

## Revised Assessment: **Yes, the system is strongly normalizing.**

Every rule either:
1. Produces `plum` (terminal) — rules `R_mango_grape`, `R_cherry_refl`
2. Produces a strict subterm — rules `R_peach_plum_left`, `R_peach_plum_right`, `R_peach_cancel`, `R_banana_zero`
3. Produces a term that is **irreducible** — `R_apple_orange` produces `pear(...)` which matches no LHS; `R_cherry_diff` produces `mango(peach ...)` which doesn't match `mango(grape ...)`.

Since there are no congruence rules, every term admits **at most one** root-level rewrite step, and after that step the result is either irreducible or a strict subterm that may admit one more step. Every reduction sequence has length at most 1.

**Strong normalization holds.** Every term either is irreducible or steps to an irreducible term in one step. The proof is trivial: the `Step` relation, having no congruence closure, only fires at the root, and every right-hand side is either a subterm or a constructor form (`pear`, `mango(peach ...)`) that matches no left-hand side.
