## Assessment: Yes — strong normalization is derivable from the calculus as given.

**Method:** Recursive/Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO), a standard well-founded simplification ordering technique for first-order term rewriting systems with no variable binding — which is exactly the shape of this system (all rules are schematic over the ground constructors of `Trace`, quantified universally, with no substitution/binding machinery at all).

**Why it applies here.** Choose the precedence

```
eqW  >  recΔ  >  integrate  >  merge  >  delta  >  app  >  void
```

with lexicographic status on the three arguments of `recΔ`. Checking each rule:

- `integrate(delta t) → void`, `merge void t → t`, `merge t void → t`, `merge t t → t`, `recΔ b s void → b`, `eqW a a → void`: each of these has its right-hand side either a **proper subterm** of the left (subterm property, which every simplification order satisfies automatically), or a lower‑precedence constant dominated by a compound head — trivially decreasing.

- `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`: since `recΔ ≻ app` in precedence, LPO reduces this to checking `recΔ b s (delta n) ≻ s` (subterm — true) and `recΔ b s (delta n) ≻ recΔ b s n` (same head, lexicographic comparison of `(b,s,delta n)` vs `(b,s,n)`; first two components equal, and `delta n ≻ n` by the subterm property) — both conditions hold, so this "unfolding" rule, despite **duplicating** `s`, is correctly oriented.

- `eqW a b → integrate(merge a b)`: since `eqW ≻ integrate ≻ merge`, this reduces to checking `eqW a b ≻ a` and `eqW a b ≻ b`, both true by the subterm property.

Every one of the eight rules is thus strictly decreasing in this single fixed LPO. Since LPO is monotonic (closed under contexts) and well-founded by construction, **every** rewrite step of a whole term — not just the redex itself — strictly decreases the term in this order. This gives strong normalization directly: there can be no infinite `Step`-reduction sequence.

**Points that might worry one but don't threaten SN here:**
- Rule 6 duplicates `s`, and rule 8 (`eqW a b → integrate(merge a b)`) increases term size — but LPO is not a size measure, so non-size-decreasing/duplicating steps are still handled correctly, exactly as in the classical LPO termination proof for primitive recursion.
- The overlapping, non-confluent pair `R_eq_refl`/`R_eq_diff` (both apply unconditionally to `eqW a a`) affects confluence, not termination — both rules individually decrease under the ordering, and no rule ever *creates* a fresh `eqW` or `delta` node (they only appear in the original term and are consumed), so there is no mechanism for regenerating redexes indefinitely.
- There is no substitution/variable-capture mechanism (no binders at all), so there is no possibility of a fixpoint/self-application style non-termination (unlike λ-calculus, `app` itself has no reduction rule and is inert data).

**Conclusion:** Strong normalization follows purely from the presented syntax and the eight rules, via a standard, self-contained order-theoretic argument (LPO/RPO with the precedence above). No additional rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or externally imported structural axioms are needed.
