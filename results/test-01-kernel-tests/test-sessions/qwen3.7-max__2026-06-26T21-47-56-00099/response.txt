## Assessment: **Yes — strong normalization holds and is provable from the calculus alone.**

### Reasoning

Since `Step` is defined as an inductive proposition with exactly eight rules and **no congruence/context-closure rules**, every reduction step must match one of those eight rules at the **root** of the term. I can classify the rules by their reducts:

**Rules whose reduct is always a root-normal form (chain length 1):**

| Rule | LHS | RHS | Why it's stuck |
|---|---|---|---|
| `R_int_delta` | `integrate (delta t)` | `void` | `void` matches no LHS |
| `R_rec_succ` | `recΔ b s (delta n)` | `app s (recΔ b s n)` | `app` matches no LHS |
| `R_eq_refl` | `eqW a a` | `void` | `void` matches no LHS |
| `R_eq_diff` | `eqW a b` | `integrate (merge a b)` | Only rule 1 has `integrate` on LHS, requiring `delta` as the child; but `merge ≠ delta` (distinct constructors), so the result is stuck |

**Rules whose reduct is a proper subterm of the redex:**

| Rule | LHS | RHS | Size decrease |
|---|---|---|---|
| `R_merge_void_left` | `merge void t` | `t` | `size(t) < size(t) + 2` |
| `R_merge_void_right` | `merge t void` | `t` | `size(t) < size(t) + 2` |
| `R_merge_cancel` | `merge t t` | `t` | `size(t) < 2·size(t) + 1` (since `size(t) ≥ 1`) |
| `R_rec_zero` | `recΔ b s void` | `b` | `size(b) < size(b) + size(s) + 2` |

### Termination argument

Every single step either:
1. **Produces a root-normal form** (rules 1, 6, 7, 8) — the chain ends immediately, or
2. **Produces a strict subterm** of the current term (rules 2, 3, 4, 5) — the term size (number of constructors, a natural number ≥ 1) strictly decreases.

Since term size is a natural number bounded below, any sequence of type-(2) steps must be finite, and it can only end in a term that either is a normal form or takes a type-(1) step to a normal form. Therefore **no infinite `Step`-chain exists**.

### Boundary check

This proof uses only:
- **Constructor discrimination** (`merge ≠ delta`): an intrinsic property of Lean's `inductive Trace`, not an imported axiom.
- **Well-foundedness of term size**: inherent to any inductive type (all terms are finite trees).
- **Exhaustive case analysis** on the eight rules as given.

No extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, structural axioms, or object-level assumptions are needed.
