### Reasoning:

The system is a first-order term rewriting system. Strong normalization can be proven by finding a monotonic interpretation (a "measure" or "weight" function) from the set of terms `Trace` into a well-founded set, such as the natural numbers `ℕ`, where every rewrite step `t → t'` implies `measure(t) > measure(t')`.

1.  **Structural Recursion:** The `R_rec_succ` rule, `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`, is the only recursive rule. This is a standard structural recursion: the recursive call is on a structurally smaller argument (`n` instead of `delta n`). This pattern is inherently well-founded.

2.  **Size-Increasing Rule:** The only rule that appears problematic at first glance is `R_eq_diff`: `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`. This rule increases the size of the term.

3.  **Constructing a Measure:** We can still define a weight function that decreases for every rule. The key is to assign a sufficiently large weight to the `eqW` constructor to "pay for" the expansion it causes. A simple polynomial interpretation into the natural numbers works.

    - Most rules are eliminative and clearly decrease any reasonable size-based measure (e.g., `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`).
    - The structural recursion in `R_rec_succ` can be handled by giving the `delta` constructor a value greater than 0 (e.g., 1) and making the measure for `recΔ` grow exponentially or multiplicatively with its third argument.
    - For `R_eq_diff`, we can assign weights such that `weight(eqW a b)` is greater than `weight(integrate (merge a b))`. For example, `weight(eqW a b) = 2*weight(a) + 2*weight(b) + C` for a large enough constant `C`, which can be shown to be greater than a compositional weight for `integrate (merge a b)`.

Since a measure function into the natural numbers can be constructed such that every rewrite step corresponds to a strict decrease, any sequence of rewrites must form a strictly decreasing sequence of natural numbers. Such a sequence must be finite. Therefore, the system is strongly normalizing.
