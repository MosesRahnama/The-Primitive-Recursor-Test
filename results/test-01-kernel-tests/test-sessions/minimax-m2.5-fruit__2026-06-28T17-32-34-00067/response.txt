**Assessment:** Yes – strong normalisation can be proved from the given calculus alone.

**Reasoning:**  
The rewrite relation `Step` is a first‑order term‑rewriting system whose signatures consist only of the constructors of `Trace`.  Although two of the rules (the “banana” rule `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)` and the “cherry‑diff” rule `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)`) can make a term larger, one can assign a *weight* to every term that strictly decreases for every rewrite step.

A concrete weight assignment (using the standard linear‑polynomial interpretation method) is:

| constructor | weight |
|---|---|
|`plum`| 0|
|`grape x`| 1 + weight(x)|
|`mango x`| 1 + weight(x)|
|`peach x y`| 1 + weight(y)|
|`banana x y z`| 1 + weight(x) + weight(y) + weight(z)|
|`pear x y`| 0|
|`cherry x y`| 3 + 2·weight(y) |

One then checks each rule:

* `mango (grape t) → plum`                       1+weight(t) > 0  
* `peach plum t → t`                             1+weight(t) > weight(t)  
* `peach t plum → t`                            1+weight(t) > weight(t)  
* `peach t t → t`                             1+weight(t) > weight(t)  
* `banana b s plum → b`                       1+weight(b) > weight(b)  
* `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`              1+weight(b)+weight(n) > 0  
* `cherry a a → plum`                            3+2·weight(a) > 0  
* `cherry a b (a≠b) → mango (peach a b)`                             3+2·weight(b) > 2+weight(b)  

In every case the weight of the left‑hand side exceeds the weight of the right‑hand side for all possible sub‑term values.  Because the weight is a natural number and the ordering “>” on ℕ is well‑founded, any reduction sequence must terminate.  The proof can be carried out in Lean 4 by defining a recursive function that computes the weight and proving the above inequalities; no extra axioms, rewrite rules, or side conditions are required.

Hence strong normalisation follows from the calculus as presented.
