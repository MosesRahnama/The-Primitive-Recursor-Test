Yes.

As literally defined, `Step` only rewrites at the root, and every non-collapsing rule produces a term whose root has no applicable rule; the collapsing rules expose a proper subterm. Thus infinite `Step` chains are impossible.

Even if the intended relation is the usual contextual closure, termination can still be proved from the syntax alone using a recursive path ordering. For example, choose a precedence satisfying

`eqW ≻ integrate ≻ merge` and `recΔ ≻ app`

with all relevant constructors above `void`, and give `recΔ` a lexicographic or multiset status. Then:

- merge and zero-recursion rules reduce to proper subterms;
- `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)` is oriented because `delta n ≻ n` and `recΔ ≻ app`;
- `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` is oriented by `eqW ≻ integrate, merge`;
- the remaining rules are immediate.

Duplication of `s` in the recursion rule does not invalidate recursive path ordering. Therefore strong normalization is establishable without adding guards, rules, or object-level assumptions.
