Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the given syntax and rewrite rules alone; no additional object-level axioms or guards are needed.

A suitable standard proof is to orient every rule by a well-founded recursive path ordering (or a comparable multiset/path measure). For example, choose a precedence compatible with the rules, such as

\[
\texttt{cherry} > \texttt{banana} > \texttt{mango} > \texttt{peach} > \texttt{grape} > \texttt{pear} > \texttt{plum}.
\]

The potentially non-size-decreasing rule

\[
\texttt{banana } b\,s\,(\texttt{grape }n)
\to
\texttt{pear }s\,(\texttt{banana }b\,s\,n)
\]

still decreases lexicographically: the inner `banana` has `n` in its third argument instead of `grape n`, and `grape n` is strictly greater than its subterm `n`. Duplication of `s` therefore does not create a cycle.

All other rules either remove a constructor, reduce to a proper subterm, or lower the head symbol in the ordering. The side condition `a ≠ b` on `R_cherry_diff` is not needed for termination; it merely restricts applicability.

Thus the root rules, and also their usual contextual closure if that is intended, are strongly normalizing. Literally as defined, `Step` has only root rules, making the termination argument even simpler.
