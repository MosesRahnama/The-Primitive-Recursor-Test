### Reasoning

1. **No Congruence Rules**: 
   The inductive relation `Step` defines only root-level reduction rules. There are no congruence (or compatibility) rules (such as `Step a b → Step (delta a) (delta b)`). Consequently, reduction can only occur at the top level (the root) of a term.

2. **Classification of Reductions**:
   By analyzing all 8 rules in `Step`, we can see that every transition $t \to t'$ falls into one of two categories:
   
   * **Strict Subterm Reductions**: The right-hand side $t'$ is a strict subterm of the left-hand side $t$. This strictly decreases the structural size of the term.
     * `R_merge_void_left`: `merge void t → t`
     * `R_merge_void_right`: `merge t void → t`
     * `R_merge_cancel`: `merge t t → t`
     * `R_rec_zero`: `recΔ b s void → b`
     
   * **Immediate Termination (Normal Forms)**: The right-hand side $t'$ is a head-constructor term that cannot match the left-hand side of any rule, meaning no further root reduction is possible. Since there are no congruence rules, these terms are normal forms:
     * `R_int_delta`: `integrate (delta t) → void` (the constructor `void` has no reduction rules).

### Conclusion
Because every step either strictly reduces the size of the term or immediately results in a normal form, there are no infinite reduction sequences. Therefore, the system is strongly normalizing.
