**Assessment:** Yes, strong normalization can be established from the calculus as presented.

**Reasoning:**  
Define a well-founded measure on terms by the lexicographic triple

\[
M(t)=\bigl(\#_{\mathsf{eqW}}(t)+\#_{\mathsf{rec\Delta}}(t),\; \#_{\mathsf{delta}}(t),\; |t|\bigr)
\]

where \(\#_c(t)\) counts occurrences of constructor \(c\), and \(|t|\) is the standard syntactic size (number of constructors).

Inspecting the rewrite rules:

* **`eqW` reductions** (`R_eq_refl`, `R_eq_diff`) decrease the first component because the left-hand side contains an `eqW` and neither right-hand side contains one.
* **`recΔ` reductions** (`R_rec_zero`, `R_rec_succ`) decrease the first component or preserve it while decreasing the second: `R_rec_zero` removes a `recΔ`; `R_rec_succ` preserves the number of `recΔ` but consumes one `delta` (the outer `delta` of the third argument).
* **`integrate` reduction** (`R_int_delta`) preserves the first component and decreases the second by consuming a `delta`.
* **`merge` reductions** preserve the first two components but strictly decrease syntactic size.

Crucially, **no right-hand side introduces a `delta`, `eqW`, or `recΔ`**; these constructors are only eliminated or passed through unchanged. Therefore no reduction can increase either the first or the second component of \(M(t)\). Every rewrite step strictly lowers the lexicographic triple, and \((\mathbb N^3, <_{\mathsf{lex}})\) is well-founded. Hence every reduction sequence is finite, establishing strong normalization without any additional axioms, side conditions, or imported lemmas.
