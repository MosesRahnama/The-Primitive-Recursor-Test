Yes. Strong normalization follows from the presented rules alone.

A natural-number interpretation strictly decreases under every rule. Define:

- `μ(void) = 0`
- `μ(delta t) = μ(t) + 1`
- `μ(integrate t) = μ(t) + 1`
- `μ(merge a b) = μ(a) + μ(b) + 1`
- `μ(app a b) = μ(a) + μ(b)`
- `μ(recΔ b s n) = μ(b) + (μ(n) + 1) * (μ(s) + 1)`
- `μ(eqW a b) = μ(a) + μ(b) + 3`

Every displayed rewrite strictly lowers `μ`. In particular, for the potentially expanding recursion rule,

\[
\mu(\mathrm{recΔ}\ b\ s\ (\delta n))
=
\mu(b)+(\mu(n)+2)(\mu(s)+1),
\]

while

\[
\mu(\mathrm{app}\ s\ (\mathrm{recΔ}\ b\ s\ n))
=
\mu(s)+\mu(b)+(\mu(n)+1)(\mu(s)+1),
\]

so the left side exceeds the right side by exactly `1`. Likewise, `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` decreases from `μ(a)+μ(b)+3` to `μ(a)+μ(b)+2`.

Thus an infinite `Step` sequence would induce an infinite strictly descending sequence of natural numbers, which is impossible. No additional rewrite rules, guards, structural axioms, or object-level assumptions are needed. Moreover, since these interpretations are strictly monotone in every argument, the same measure also handles the usual contextual closure, should that be introduced merely as the closure of the given rules.
