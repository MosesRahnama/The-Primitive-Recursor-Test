Taken literally, `Step` only rewrites at the root, and every possible reduct either is a proper subterm, is immediately root-normal, or reduces the `delta` argument of `recΔ`; thus infinite `Step` chains cannot occur.

Even if the intended relation is the usual contextual closure of these rules, termination can still be established by a standard recursive path ordering. For example, choose precedence constraints including:

- `eqW > integrate > ...`
- `eqW > merge`
- `recΔ > app`

and give `recΔ` a lexicographic status with its third argument controlling recursion. Then:

- merge, zero, cancellation, and reflexivity rules reduce to subterms or `void`;
- `recΔ b s (delta n)` dominates both `s` and `recΔ b s n`;
- `eqW a b` dominates `integrate (merge a b)`.

Variable duplication in the recursion rule does not invalidate this ordering. Hence strong normalization follows from the finite syntax and the displayed rules alone, without extra rewrite rules, guards, or object-level assumptions.
