Raw assessment: **No**—strong normalization is **not** establishable from this calculus alone as given.

Reason (brief): there is an explicit self-loop in one step:
- `R_eq_diff : Step (eqW a b) (integrate (merge a b))` holds for **all** `a b`, including `a = b`.
- `R_eq_refl : Step (eqW a a) void`.
So for any `a`,
\[
eqW\,a\,a \to integrate(merge\,a\,a) \to integrate\,a \to \cdots
\]
But more directly, take `a = integrate (merge t t)`. Then
\[
eqW\,a\,a \to integrate(merge\,a\,a) \to integrate\,a = a,
\]
since `merge a a -> a` by `R_merge_cancel`. Hence `a -> a` in one `Step` (via `R_eq_diff` then `R_merge_cancel` in context if closure is intended; or at minimum a non-decreasing cycle at the top-level rewrite shape). This destroys well-foundedness needed for strong normalization. Also, with both `R_eq_refl` and unrestricted `R_eq_diff`, the system is non-orthogonal/overlapping in a way that permits such looping behavior rather than enforcing descent.

So without adding restrictions (e.g., side condition `a ≠ b` on `R_eq_diff`) or extra structure, you cannot prove SN for all terms.
