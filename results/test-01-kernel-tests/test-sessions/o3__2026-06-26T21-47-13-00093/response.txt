Yes – with the rewrite relation as it is declared in the file, strong-normalisation (termination) is provable from the calculus itself without importing any extra axioms.

Reasoning (sketch)

1.  “Step” is defined only for patterns that occur at the outermost (root) position of a
    term.  There is no contextual-closure rule that would allow rewriting to fire
    under the constructors `delta`, `integrate`, `merge`, `app`, `recΔ`, or `eqW`.

2.  For every constructor that can be the root of a left-hand side there is at most
    one Step reduction that can be taken, and that reduction removes that very
    constructor from the root:

    • `integrate (delta t)  →  void` removes the outer `integrate`.
    • the three `merge …` rules remove the outer `merge`.
    • the two `recΔ …` rules remove the outer `recΔ`.
    • the two `eqW …` rules remove the outer `eqW`.

    The right-hand side is always headed by a constructor (`void`, `t`, `b`,
    `app`, or `integrate`) for which no further Step rule is applicable at the
    root.  In particular, the `recΔ` successor rule produces `app …`, and `app`
    never appears on the left of any rule; the “different” equality rule produces
    an `integrate` whose immediate argument is a `merge`, not a `delta`, so the
    `integrate` rule cannot fire again.

3.  Hence from any starting term only one root reduction is possible, and after it
    fires the term is in normal form.  The length of every rewrite sequence is
    therefore bounded by `1`, so the system is strongly normalising.

Because this argument uses only the syntax of `Trace` and the eight Step rules
themselves (no additional measures, guards, or external principles), strong
normalisation is derivable entirely within the presented calculus.
