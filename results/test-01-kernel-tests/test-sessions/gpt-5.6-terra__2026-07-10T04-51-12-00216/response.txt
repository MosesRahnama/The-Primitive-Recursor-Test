Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the displayed syntax and rules alone; no extra object-level axioms, guards, or rewrite rules are needed.

A standard lexicographic recursive path ordering (equivalently, an appropriate LPO/RPO) proves termination of the contextual rewrite closure, hence also of the root-only `Step` relation as literally defined.

Choose a precedence including, for example,

- `recΔ > app`,
- `eqW > integrate`,
- `eqW > merge`,

with lexicographic status for `recΔ`. Then every rule decreases:

- The `merge` rules and `recΔ ... void → b` reduce to subterms.
- `integrate (delta t) → void` and `eqW a a → void` reduce to a constant.
- `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)` decreases because the left term dominates both `s` and `recΔ b s n`; the latter is smaller by the third argument changing from `delta n` to `n`.
- `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` decreases because `eqW` is higher than both `integrate` and `merge`, while `a` and `b` are subterms of the left-hand side.

The apparent duplication of `s` in the successor recursion rule does not by itself create an infinite reduction: the recursive `recΔ` call is structurally smaller in its final argument, and the ordering handles the whole rule.

The overlap between `R_eq_refl` and `R_eq_diff` at `eqW a a` also does not threaten strong normalization. Both possible reducts are strictly smaller in the same termination ordering.

So, on the presented finite first-order term syntax, the rewrite system is strongly normalizing.
