**Assessment:** Yes, strong normalization (SN) can be established for this system using the presented rules alone.

**Reasoning:**

The rewrite system consists of non-recursive rules (R_int_delta, R_merge_*, R_eq_*) and one recursive schema (R_rec_*) that implements primitive recursion over the inductive type of "unary natural numbers" (where `void` is zero and `delta` is successor).

Key observations:

1.  **The recursor is well-founded:** The `recΔ` rules follow the standard pattern for structural recursion. Each application of `R_rec_succ` replaces `recΔ b s (delta n)` with `app s (recΔ b s n)`, strictly decreasing the depth of the third argument (peeling off one `delta` constructor). Since no rule wraps a `recΔ` argument in additional `delta`s or increases the nesting depth of `recΔ` in a way that could create a cycle, the recursion is well-founded.

2.  **All other rules are size-decreasing (or neutral):** The rules `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`, and `R_eq_refl` all replace a term with a strict subterm or a smaller constant (`void`), strictly decreasing the term size.

3.  **`R_eq_diff` cannot create divergence:** While `R_eq_diff` (`eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`) can increase the syntactic size by 1, the resulting term cannot generate an infinite sequence. The `merge` subterm either reduces immediately (if a rule applies) to one of its arguments (decreasing size), or remains irreducible. The outer `integrate` then either reduces to `void` (if the merge result is `delta t`) or gets stuck. No cycle is introduced because neither `integrate` nor `merge` can produce an `eqW` subterm.

4.  **Termination measure:** A well-founded measure can be defined as the lexicographic product of:
    *   The multiset of "recursion depths" (number of nested `delta` constructors) of all `recΔ` subterms, ordered by the multiset ordering; and
    *   The standard syntactic size of the term.
    
    Every rewrite step decreases this measure: `R_rec_succ` decreases the first component; all other rules decrease the second component without increasing the first (they either eliminate `recΔ` subterms or leave their arguments unchanged).

Since every possible rewrite sequence strictly decreases a well-founded measure, the system is strongly normalizing. No additional axioms, guards, or side conditions are required beyond the syntax and rules shown.
