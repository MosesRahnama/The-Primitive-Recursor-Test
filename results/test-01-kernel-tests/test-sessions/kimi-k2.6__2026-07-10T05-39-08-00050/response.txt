**Assessment: Yes—strong normalization is provable from the presented calculus alone.**

**Reasoning:**

1. **Root-only reduction.** The `Step` relation is defined only by head-reduction rules. There are **no congruence or context-closure rules**, so a redex can only be reduced when it appears at the root of the whole term. Subterms are never touched.

2. **Every redex either disappears or shrinks.**
   - *Size-decreasing rules:* `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, and `R_rec_zero` each replace the entire term with a proper subterm (`t` or `b`). Hence each such step strictly decreases the total number of constructors (the term size).
   - *Terminal rules:* `R_int_delta`, `R_rec_succ`, `R_eq_refl`, and `R_eq_diff` each produce a term whose root constructor is a normal form:
     - `void` is inert.
     - `app s (recΔ b s n)` has root `app`, and there are no rules for `app`.
     - `integrate (merge a b)` has root `integrate`, but the only `integrate` rule (`R_int_delta`) demands an argument of the form `delta t`; since `merge` and `delta` are distinct constructors and there are no context rules, this term is stuck.
   Consequently these four rules can fire **at most once** in any reduction path and cannot be followed by any further step.

3. **No rule re-creates an active root.** Scanning all right-hand sides, none introduces a root `merge`, `recΔ`, or `eqW`. Therefore once a `merge`, `recΔ`, or `eqW` redex is eliminated, it can never reappear as the root.

4. **Well-founded induction suffices.** Because `Trace` is an ordinary inductive type, every term is finite and has a well-defined size. By induction on term size:
   - If the root is not a redex, the term is normal.
   - If the root is a terminal redex, the term reaches normal form in one step.
   - If the root is a size-decreasing redex, the reduct is a proper subterm, hence smaller; by the induction hypothesis it is strongly normalizing, and therefore so is the original term.

No exotic terms, hidden assumptions, or additional rewrite rules are needed—strong normalization follows directly from the given syntax and the shape of the eight rules.
