Raw assessment: no, strong normalization cannot be established from this calculus alone.

Reason, briefly: the rewrite relation as presented is not strongly normalizing because it contains an explicit non-decreasing/self-embedding rule:

- `R_eq_diff : Step (eqW a b) (integrate (merge a b))`

This rule applies for all `a b`, including the case `a = b`. So for any `a`,

- `eqW a a → void` by `R_eq_refl`
- `eqW a a → integrate (merge a a)` by `R_eq_diff`
- `merge a a → a` by `R_merge_cancel`

Hence
- `eqW a a → integrate a`

and in particular, taking `a := eqW t t`, we get
- `eqW (eqW t t) (eqW t t) → integrate (eqW t t)`.

Iterating this gives an infinite reduction chain:
- `eqW u u → integrate u`
- with `u := eqW u' u'`, so inside the `integrate` sits another redex of the same form,
and since `Step` is a first-order rewrite relation on terms, its contextual closure permits repeated contraction of these nested `eqW` redexes, yielding terms with arbitrarily many outer `integrate`s and never reaching normal form.

More fundamentally, there is no evident well-founded decrease compatible with all rules: `R_eq_diff` can increase size, and overlaps with `R_eq_refl` prevent orienting `eqW` by a decreasing measure without extra restrictions. So from the presented syntax and rules alone, without adding side conditions such as “`R_eq_diff` only when `a ≠ b`”, strong normalization fails.
