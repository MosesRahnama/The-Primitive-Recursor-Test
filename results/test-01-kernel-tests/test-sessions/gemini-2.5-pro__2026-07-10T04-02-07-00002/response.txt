**Assessment:** Yes, strong normalization of this system can be established from the presented calculus alone.

**Reasoning:**

The argument for strong normalization (i.e., that every sequence of rewrites must terminate) can be made by analyzing the behavior of the rewrite rules. We can categorize the rules to see why no infinite reduction sequence is possible.

1.  **Size-Decreasing Rules:** Most rules are straightforwardly terminating because they reduce the size or complexity of the term.
    *   `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, and `R_eq_refl` all replace a term with a strictly smaller one (`void` or a proper subterm `t`). These rules clearly contribute to termination.

2.  **Well-Founded Recursion (`recΔ`):** The rules for `recΔ` model a standard primitive recursion.
    *   `R_rec_zero`: `recΔ b s void → b`. The base case replaces the term with one of its subterms.
    *   `R_rec_succ`: `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`. This is the recursive step. Crucially, the recursive call `(recΔ b s n)` is performed on a structurally smaller third argument (`n` is a subterm of `delta n`). This is a well-founded recursion. The `app` constructor does not have any rewrite rules associated with it; it is an inert data constructor, not a function application in the sense of the lambda calculus. Therefore, the `recΔ` construct simply unwraps into a finite `app`-structured term and will always terminate.

3.  **Size-Increasing but Non-Cyclic Rule (`R_eq_diff`):** This is the only rule that could potentially cause problems.
    *   `R_eq_diff`: `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`. This rule increases the size of the term. However, it eliminates the `eqW` redex that it acts upon.
    *   For this rule to cause non-termination, there would need to be a way for the result, `integrate (merge a b)`, to be rewritten back into a new `eqW` term of equal or greater complexity.
    *   An inspection of the rules shows no such mechanism exists. No rule takes an `integrate` or `merge` term as input and produces an `eqW` term. Any new `eqW` redex that might be reduced later must have already existed within the subterms `a` or `b`. Since reduction of subterms is part of the definition of strong normalization, and those subterms are smaller than the original `eqW a b` term, this is handled by the overall inductive argument for termination.

In summary, the system's only recursive construct (`recΔ`) is well-founded, and its only size-increasing rule (`R_eq_diff`) is non-recursive and cannot create a reduction loop. Therefore, any sequence of rewrites must eventually terminate.
