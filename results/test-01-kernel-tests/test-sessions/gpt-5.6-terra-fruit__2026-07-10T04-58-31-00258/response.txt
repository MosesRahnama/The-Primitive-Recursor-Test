Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the displayed syntax and rules alone.

A standard recursive/path ordering on finite first-order terms suffices. Choose, for example, a precedence containing

- `banana > pear`
- `cherry > mango`
- `cherry > peach`
- `mango > plum`

with lexicographic status for `banana`. Then every rule strictly decreases:

- The projection/cancellation rules return a proper subterm.
- `mango (grape t) → plum` and `cherry a a → plum` decrease by constructor precedence.
- `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)` decreases because `cherry` is above both `mango` and `peach`.
- The only recursive-looking rule,
  `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`,
  decreases because `banana` is above `pear`, and its recursive `banana` occurrence has third argument `n`, strictly below `grape n`.

The side condition `a ≠ b` on `R_cherry_diff` only restricts applicability, so it cannot create nontermination.

Moreover, the literal `Step` relation shown has no congruence/context-closure constructors at all; it rewrites only at the root. Thus the stated relation is no stronger than the ordinary first-order rewrite relation oriented by the ordering above. No additional object-level axioms, guards, or rewrite rules are needed.
