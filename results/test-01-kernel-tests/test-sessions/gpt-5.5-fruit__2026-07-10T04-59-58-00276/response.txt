Yes — strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone.

For the literal Lean relation `Step`, the argument is quite direct: every possible root step either

- reduces to a strict subterm, e.g.  
  `peach plum t → t`, `peach t plum → t`, `peach t t → t`, `banana b s plum → b`;
- reduces to `plum`;
- or reduces to a term whose root constructor has no outgoing rule, e.g.  
  `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)` and there are no `pear`-root rules;  
  `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)` and `mango (peach a b)` cannot match `mango (grape t)` by constructor disjointness.

So an infinite `Step` chain cannot be generated.

No additional object-level axioms are needed. The only structural facts required are the ordinary inductive-constructor facts already provided by Lean for `Trace`: constructor disjointness, injectivity, and structural induction/well-foundedness of finite syntax trees.

Even if one considered the usual contextual closure of these rewrite rules, the system still appears orientable by a standard simplification/path ordering: the apparently size-increasing rule

```lean
banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)
```

decreases the third argument from `grape n` to `n`, and can be oriented with `banana > pear`; similarly `cherry > mango, peach` orients the `cherry_diff` rule. The disequality premise on `cherry_diff` only restricts rewriting; it does not introduce a source of nontermination.

So the raw assessment is: yes, strong normalization is provable from the given syntax and rewrite rules alone, without strengthening the calculus or importing hidden axioms.
