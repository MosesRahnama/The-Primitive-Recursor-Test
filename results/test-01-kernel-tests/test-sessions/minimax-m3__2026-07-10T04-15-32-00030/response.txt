## Assessment: Yes — strong normalization is provable from the given calculus alone.

### Why the system is SN

The rewrite system decomposes into a small collection of terminating subsystems that don't interact in a way that produces loops:

1. **`recΔ` is a guarded recursor.** It fires only when its third argument is `void` or `delta n`; every step of `R_rec_succ` peels one `delta` off that argument. There is no rule that lets a term rebuild a `delta` that the recursion has already consumed (the only `delta`-introducing constructor is `delta` itself, and the recursion consumes them by unwrapping).

2. **`merge` is a bounded join.** The three merge rules (`void` is identity, idempotence) form a terminating TRS by themselves and never *create* new `recΔ` redexes — they just expose or duplicate subterms.

3. **`integrate (delta t) → void` is destructive.** It strictly removes structure.

4. **`eqW` is conditional and `eqW`-decreasing.** `R_eq_refl` collapses to `void`; `R_eq_diff` replaces an `eqW`-node by `integrate (merge a b)`, removing one `eqW` constructor and never introducing a new one.

5. **`app` has no head-reduction rule**, so it can never re-enter a redex loop — it only hosts reductions inside its subterms.

### A concrete witness measure (no extra axioms)

Let
- `E(t)` = number of `eqW` constructors in `t`,
- `R(t)` = multiset of (delta-depth of the third argument) over the reducible `recΔ` redexes of `t`,
- `S(t)` = tree size of `t`.

Take `Φ(t) = (E(t), R(t), S(t))` in the well-founded lexicographic (multiset) order. Each rule strictly decreases `Φ`:

| rule | E | R | S |
|---|---|---|---|
| `R_rec_succ` | = | ↓ (one element `k+1 → k`) | – |
| `R_rec_zero` | = | ↓ (one element removed) | ↓ |
| `R_eq_refl` | ↓ | – | ↓ |
| `R_eq_diff` | ↓ (by 1) | = | ↑ |
| `R_int_delta` | ↓ | – | ↓ |
| `R_merge_*`  | = | = | ↓ |

`R_eq_diff` is the only rule that grows `S`, but it is disciplined by the strictly-decreasing `E`. All other rules either decrease `R` (recursor progress) or `S` (the merging/integration collapse). Because `Φ` is well-founded, no infinite reduction sequence exists.

### Conclusion

Strong normalization follows from a standard multiset/lexicographic measure argument applied directly to the given rules. Nothing outside the presented syntax and step relation is required: no new rewrite rules, no guard side-conditions, and no imported axioms. A Lean development could carry this out by well-founded recursion on `Φ`.
