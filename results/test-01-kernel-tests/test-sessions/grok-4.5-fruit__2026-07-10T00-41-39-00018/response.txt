### Assessment
Yes, strong normalization (SN) of the given system can be established directly from the presented inductive definitions of `Trace` and `Step` alone.

### Reasoning
The relation `Step` consists solely of the eight listed root rules (no congruence/closure under contexts is present in the definition). Consequently:

- The only reducible terms are those whose root constructor and arguments match a left-hand side exactly.
- Inspection of the right-hand sides shows that every reduct is either
  - a proper subterm of the redex (the three `peach` rules, `R_banana_zero`, and both `cherry` rules), or
  - a term headed by `pear` or by `mango` applied to a `peach` (the remaining two rules).  
  In the latter cases the reduct matches none of the left-hand sides, hence is irreducible.

A straightforward induction on term size therefore proves that every term is SN:

- The inductive hypothesis supplies SN for every proper subterm.
- When a reduct is not a proper subterm it is irreducible by the observation above, so the one-step residual is already SN.
- Overlaps among the `peach` rules are immaterial: they produce identical residuals.

No measure beyond the inductively defined size of a `Trace`, no extra rewrite rules, no guards, and no side-conditions beyond the single inequality already present in `R_cherry_diff` are required. (Decidability of equality on `Trace`, needed only to know that every `cherry` redex fires exactly one of the two rules, is itself derivable by structural recursion on the given inductive syntax.)

Thus the abstract reduction system \((\mathsf{Trace},\mathsf{Step})\) is strongly normalizing.
