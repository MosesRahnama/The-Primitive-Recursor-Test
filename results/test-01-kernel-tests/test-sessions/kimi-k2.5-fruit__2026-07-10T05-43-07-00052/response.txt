**Assessment: Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone.**

**Reasoning:**

The system is strongly normalizing because every reduction sequence is finite, which can be proven by induction on term size using only the given rewrite rules.

Key observations:

1. **No congruence rules**: The `Step` relation only allows reduction at the root. There are no rules permitting reduction in subterms (e.g., no rule like "if `Step t t'` then `Step (grape t) (grape t')"`).

2. **Classification of rewrite rules by their behavior**:
   - **Size-decreasing rules** (R1, R2, R3, R4, R5, R7): Each strictly decreases the number of constructors. For example:
     - R2/R3: `|peach plum t| = 2 + |t| → |t|` (decrease by 2)
     - R4: `|peach t t| = 1 + 2|t| → |t|` (decrease by 1+|t| ≥ 2)
     - R5: `|banana b s plum| = 2 + |b| + |s| → |b|` (decrease by 2+|s| ≥ 3)
   
   - **Rules producing immediate normal forms** (R6, R8): These may increase size but yield terms that cannot be reduced further:
     - R6 produces `pear s (banana b s n)`. Since no rules have `pear` as the root symbol, this is a normal form.
     - R8 produces `mango (peach a b)`. The only rule for `mango` requires the form `mango (grape t)`. Since `peach` and `grape` are distinct constructors, `mango (peach a b)` is not a redex and is a normal form.
   
   - R1 and R7 produce `plum`, which is a normal form.

3. **Termination argument**: Any reduction chain consists of:
   - At most |t|/2 steps of size-decreasing rules (R2-R5), since each reduces size by at least 2, followed by
   - At most one step of type R1, R6, R7, or R8, which produces a normal form.
   
   Since the initial term is a finite inductive value, no infinite chains exist.

The proof requires no external axioms beyond the inductive structure of `Trace` and the definition of `Step`. A simple well-founded induction on term size (number of constructors) establishes that every term reaches a normal form in finitely many steps.
