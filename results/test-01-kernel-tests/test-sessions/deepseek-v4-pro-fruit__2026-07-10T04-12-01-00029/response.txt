The `Step` relation defines only root reductions—there are no congruence rules that allow rewriting inside subterms. Analyzing the eight rules:

1. `mango (grape t) → plum`  
2. `peach plum t → t`  
3. `peach t plum → t`  
4. `peach t t → t`  
5. `banana b s plum → b`  
6. `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`  
7. `cherry a a → plum`  
8. `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)` (if `a ≠ b`)  

Rules 2–5 have a variable as the right‑hand side, which may itself be reducible, but they strictly decrease the syntactic size of the term. Rules 1 and 7 reduce to `plum`, a normal form. Rules 6 and 8 increase the term size, but their right‑hand sides (`pear ...` and `mango (peach ...)`) are irreducible because no rule matches `pear` or `mango` at the root unless the argument is `grape t`. Hence these “increasing” rules can fire at most once at the very end of a reduction sequence and do not enable any further steps.

Because every reduction either strictly shrinks the term (allowing only finitely many steps by size) or terminates immediately, there are no infinite reduction sequences. This termination argument uses only the given inductive definitions and structural induction on term size, with no extra rewrite rules, side conditions, or external well‑founded orders.
