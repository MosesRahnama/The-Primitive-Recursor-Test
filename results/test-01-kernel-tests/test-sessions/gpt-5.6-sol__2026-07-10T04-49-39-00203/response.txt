As literally encoded, `Step` only performs root rewrites and has no contextual closure, so termination is already straightforward. Even if one considers the usual closure under term contexts, the system remains strongly normalizing.

A recursive path ordering can orient every rule. For example, choose symbol precedence including

- `eqW ≻ integrate`, `eqW ≻ merge`, and `eqW ≻ void`
- `integrate ≻ void`
- `recΔ ≻ app`

with lexicographic status for `recΔ`. The collapsing rules (`merge … → …` and `recΔ b s void → b`) reduce to subterms. For the only recursive rule,

```lean
recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)
```

the recursive call decreases because `n` is a proper subterm of `delta n`; the outer `app` is lower than `recΔ`. The two expanding-looking `eqW` and `recΔ` rules are likewise strictly decreasing in this ordering. Non-left-linearity and the overlap between `R_eq_refl` and `R_eq_diff` affect confluence, but not termination.

Thus strong normalization can be established solely from the finite syntax and the presented rules, without adding guards, rules, or object-level assumptions.
