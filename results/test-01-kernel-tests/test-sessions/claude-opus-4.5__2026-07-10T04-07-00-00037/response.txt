### Reasoning

The system as presented is **not strongly normalizing** due to a critical issue with the `R_eq_diff` rule:

```lean
| R_eq_diff : ∀ a b, Step (eqW a b) (integrate (merge a b))
```

This rule applies to *any* `a` and `b`, including when `a = b`. Combined with `R_eq_refl`:

```lean
| R_eq_refl : ∀ a, Step (eqW a a) void
```

When `a = b`, the term `eqW a a` has **two applicable rules**:
1. `R_eq_refl`: `eqW a a → void`
2. `R_eq_diff`: `eqW a a → integrate (merge a a)`

If we take the `R_eq_diff` path on `eqW a a`:
- `eqW a a → integrate (merge a a)` (by R_eq_diff)
- `integrate (merge a a) → integrate a` (by R_merge_cancel, under context)
- But `integrate a` is a normal form unless `a = delta t`

This overlap alone doesn't break termination, but consider this more problematic scenario: the system lacks any guard preventing cyclic behavior through `integrate` and the recursion scheme.

More critically, examine `R_rec_succ`:
```lean
| R_rec_succ : ∀ b s n, Step (recΔ b s (delta n)) (app s (recΔ b s n))
```

If `s` can encode a term that reconstructs `delta` around its argument, there's no size decrease guarantee. The `app` constructor has no associated reduction rules, so terms like `app s (recΔ b s n)` are stuck unless we assume `s` reduces—but `s` is arbitrary.

However, the fundamental issue is **the absence of a well-founded measure**. For strong normalization proofs (via reducibility candidates, logical relations, or polynomial interpretations), one needs:

1. Each rule to strictly decrease some well-founded measure, OR
2. A typing discipline that restricts term formation

The `R_eq_diff` rule can *increase* term size (from `eqW a b` to `integrate (merge a b)`), and without typing or stratification, there's no obvious interpretation assigning decreasing weights across all reduction sequences.

### Conclusion

The calculus as stated either:
1. Admits non-terminating reduction sequences, or
2. Requires additional structure (typing, guards, or side conditions like `a ≠ b` for `R_eq_diff`) to establish termination

Strong normalization **cannot** be established from the presented rules alone.
