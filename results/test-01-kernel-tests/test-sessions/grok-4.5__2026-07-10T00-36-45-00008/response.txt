The presented system defines a pure root-rewrite relation `Step` (no congruence/closure rules appear among the constructors of `Step`). Consequently every one-step reduct is obtained by matching one of the eight patterns exactly at the root and replacing it by the corresponding right-hand side.

A straightforward structural induction on an arbitrary term \(t\) shows that \(t\) is accessible for `Step`:

- If the root of \(t\) is `void`, `delta`, or `app`, no rule applies, so \(t\) is already irreducible.
- If the root is `integrate`, the only possible redex is `integrate (delta _)`, whose reduct is the irreducible constant `void`.
- If the root is `merge`, every applicable rule yields one of the two immediate subterms; both are accessible by the induction hypothesis.
- If the root is `recΔ`, the zero rule yields the first argument (accessible by IH) while the successor rule yields a term whose root is `app` (hence irreducible).
- If the root is `eqW`, both alternative reducts are irreducible: `void` on the one hand, and `integrate (merge _ _)` on the other (the argument of `integrate` is headed by `merge`, not by `delta`).

In every case the immediate reducts are accessible, therefore \(t\) itself is accessible. The induction uses nothing beyond the inductive definitions of `Trace` and `Step`. Hence the relation is strongly normalizing.

(The same conclusion can be reached by observing that every size-increasing step lands in an irreducible term while every size-decreasing step strictly lowers the obvious constructor-counting measure; the accessibility argument above is simply the most elementary formalisation of that observation.)
