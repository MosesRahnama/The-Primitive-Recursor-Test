## Assessment: **Yes** — strong normalization is provable directly from the given definitions, using only structural induction on `Trace`.

### Key observation: `Step` has no congruence/context closure

The inductive definition of `Step` gives exactly 8 root-level rewrite patterns and **no** rule of the form "if `Step t t'` then `Step (grape t) (grape t')`" (or similarly for `mango`, `peach`, `pear`, `banana`, `cherry`). So `Step s t` can only hold when the *entire* term `s` matches one of the eight left-hand-side shapes at the root. This is the crucial structural fact that makes the SN proof tractable.

### Case-by-case fate of each rule

Classify the 8 rules by what their right-hand side looks like, and whether that right-hand side can itself be a redex:

- **Rules that hand back a fresh, syntactically fixed result whose root constructor never occurs as the outer constructor of any rule's LHS:**
  - `R_mango_grape`: result `plum` — no rule has bare `plum` as its LHS root.
  - `R_apple_orange` (banana/grape case): result `pear s (banana b s n)` — **no rule has `pear` as an outer constructor at all**, so this is unconditionally a normal form.
  - `R_cherry_refl`: result `plum` — as above, terminal.
  - `R_cherry_diff`: result `mango (peach a b)` — for this to match `R_mango_grape`'s LHS `mango (grape t)`, the argument `peach a b` would need to literally be `grape t'`; but `peach` and `grape` are distinct constructors of an inductive type, hence never equal. So this result is also unconditionally terminal.

  These four rules therefore *always* terminate reduction in exactly one more step, regardless of the subterms `t, a, b, n` involved.

- **Rules that hand back an arbitrary proper subterm of the redex:**
  - `R_peach_plum_left`, `R_peach_plum_right`, `R_peach_cancel`: result is `t`, a strict subterm of the original `peach _ _` term.
  - `R_banana_zero`: result is `b`, a strict subterm of the original `banana _ _ _` term.

  Only these four rules can possibly be followed by another `Step`, precisely because their output is an arbitrary subterm that might itself happen to match some redex pattern.

### Putting it together

Any potential infinite `Step`-chain can only consist of applications of these last four "subterm-projection" rules, since any occurrence of the other four rules terminates the chain immediately. But each such application strictly decreases the term along the **subterm relation** of the inductively defined type `Trace`. The subterm relation on any Lean inductive type is well-founded (this is exactly what structural/well-founded recursion on `Trace` gives for free, with no extra axioms). A strictly descending chain in a well-founded order cannot be infinite.

Hence:
- No infinite `Step`-sequence exists,
- and the proof uses only: case analysis on the 8 constructors of `Step`, injectivity/distinctness of `Trace`'s constructors, and well-founded structural induction on `Trace` — all of which are automatically available from the `inductive` declarations themselves.

### Conclusion

Strong normalization of this exact system is establishable **purely from the calculus as presented**, with a fully elementary argument (structural induction / well-founded subterm ordering), requiring no additional rewrite rules, side conditions, or external axioms. The absence of contextual/congruence closure in `Step` is what makes the system trivially terminating rather than requiring a sophisticated reducibility or interpretation-based termination proof.
