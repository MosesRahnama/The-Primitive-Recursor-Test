Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the presented syntax and rules alone.

A standard recursive path ordering suffices. For example, choose a precedence satisfying

`cherry > mango`, `cherry > peach`, `banana > pear`, and `mango > plum`

(with the remaining constructors ordered arbitrarily), and give `banana` a lexicographic argument status. Then every rule strictly decreases:

- Projection/cancellation rules decrease to a proper subterm.
- `mango (grape t) → plum` and the `cherry` rules decrease by precedence.
- For the only recursive rule,
  `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`,
  the outer symbol decreases from `banana` to `pear`, while the recursive occurrence decreases because its third argument changes from `grape n` to the proper subterm `n`.

The disequality premise on `R_cherry_diff` is irrelevant to this decrease argument. Thus even the usual context-compatible closure is strongly normalizing; the literal `Step` relation defined in the file, which has no congruence rules, is no harder. No additional rewrite rules, guards, or object-level assumptions are needed.
