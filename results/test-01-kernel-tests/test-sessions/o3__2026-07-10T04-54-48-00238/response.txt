Raw assessment:  
Yes – strong normalization can be proved for the rewrite system exactly as it stands, with no need for extra rules, side-conditions, or imported axioms.

Sketch of the argument.  
Because the system is a finite first-order term-rewrite system, it suffices to show that the one-step rules decrease a well-founded measure on ground terms.  One convenient choice is the standard recursive-path ordering (RPO).

1. Fix a precedence that puts the “recursion‐driving” symbols highest:

   recΔ  ≻  app  ≻  merge  ≻  integrate  ≻  delta  ≻  void, eqW.

   (Any total precedence that keeps recΔ above app and delta, and keeps merge above void, will work.)

2. Give every symbol the default lexicographic status.  With this ordering

   • a term is always greater than any of its (proper) sub-terms  
   • and, if the heads differ, the one whose head has higher precedence is greater.

3. Check each rule; in every case the left-hand side is strictly greater than the right-hand side:

   • integrate (delta t)  ≻  void                     – by the sub-term property  
   • merge void t         ≻  t                        – sub-term  
   • merge t void         ≻  t                        – sub-term  
   • merge t t            ≻  t                        – sub-term  
   • recΔ b s void        ≻  b                        – sub-term  
   • recΔ b s (delta n)   ≻  app s (recΔ b s n)       – because recΔ ≻ app, and  
                                                        recΔ b s (delta n) contains
                                                        recΔ b s n as a strict sub-term  
   • eqW a a              ≻  void                     – sub-term  
   • eqW a b              ≻  integrate (merge a b)    – because eqW ≻ integrate.

   Hence every rewrite step is a strict RPO descent.

4. RPO is well founded, so there can be no infinite reduction sequence; the system is terminating.  
   For a first-order system with no binding, termination is equivalent to strong normalization (every term has only finite reduction sequences).

Because the proof uses only the concrete syntax and the eight given rules, and relies on the standard well-foundedness of RPO (which is provable in Lean from the usual inductive definition of accessibility), no additional object-level assumptions or structural axioms are required.

Therefore strong normalization of the presented calculus can indeed be established from the rules alone.
