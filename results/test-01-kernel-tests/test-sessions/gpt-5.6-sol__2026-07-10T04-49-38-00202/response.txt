Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the presented syntax and rules alone.

A standard recursive/lexicographic path ordering suffices. For example, choose a precedence satisfying

`recΔ ≻ app` and `eqW ≻ integrate ≻ merge`, with `integrate ≻ void` and `eqW ≻ void`.

Then every rule strictly decreases:

- The merge and zero-recursion rules return proper subterms.
- `integrate (delta t) → void` and the equality rules decrease by precedence.
- In  
  `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`,  
  the recursive call decreases because `delta n` is greater than its proper subterm `n`; the enclosing `app` is below `recΔ`.
- `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` decreases because `eqW` dominates both `integrate` and `merge`.

Such an ordering is well-founded and stable under contexts, so this also proves termination if rewriting is understood contextually. For the literal Lean `Step` relation, which has no congruence/context rules, normalization is even more immediate. Rule overlap such as the two `eqW a a` possibilities does not create a cycle. No additional rewrite rules, guards, or object-level assumptions are needed.
