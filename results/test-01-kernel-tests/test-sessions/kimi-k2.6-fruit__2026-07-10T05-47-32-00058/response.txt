**Yes.** The system is strongly normalizing, and this can be proved from the calculus alone using standard termination machinery.

A suitable method is the **recursive path ordering (RPO)**. Choose a precedence such as  
`cherry > mango > banana > peach > pear > grape > plum`  
and give `banana` multiset status. Every rule then strictly decreases its left-hand side with respect to this well-founded order:

* **Rules 2–5 and 7** are *collapsing* rules whose right-hand side is a variable occurring in the left-hand side; such rules are always oriented by RPO because a term is greater than its proper subterms.
* **Rule 1** (`mango (grape t) → plum`) is oriented because `mango > plum` (and `plum` is a constant).
* **Rule 6** (`banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`) is oriented because `banana > pear`, and the recursive call `banana b s n` is smaller in the multiset extension: after deleting the common arguments `b` and `s`, we must compare `{grape n}` with `{n}`; since `n` is a proper subterm of `grape n`, we have `grape n > n`, so the multiset comparison holds. Duplication of `s` on the right is harmless—RPO only requires the left-hand side to exceed each *immediate* subterm of the right-hand side, which follows from the subterm property.
* **Rule 8** (`cherry a b → mango (peach a b)`) is oriented because `cherry > mango` and then `cherry a b > peach a b` follows from `cherry > peach` together with the subterm property on `a` and `b`.
* **Rule 7** (`cherry a a → plum`) is oriented because `cherry > plum`.

Simpler numeric measures (such as raw term size or symbol counts) fail because rule 6 duplicates the arbitrary subterm `s` and rule 8 increases the total size. RPO does not require any additive measure and therefore handles duplication automatically, without adding guards, side conditions, or extra rewrite rules. Consequently, no infinite reduction sequence exists.
