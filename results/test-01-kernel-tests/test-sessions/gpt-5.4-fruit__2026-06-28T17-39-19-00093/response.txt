Yes — from the rules shown alone, strong normalization is very plausibly establishable.

Brief reason: every rewrite strictly decreases a well-founded measure on `Trace`. A simple size measure is not enough because `R_apple_orange`

- `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`

does not decrease raw symbol count. But a weighted recursive measure can handle it. For example, assign a measure where the third argument of `banana` is given dominant weight, e.g. define something like

- `μ(plum) = 1`
- unary constructors add a small amount
- binary constructors add their submeasures plus a small amount
- `μ(banana b s n) = μ(b) + μ(s) + C * μ(n) + K`

with `C > 1` large enough.

Then:

- `mango (grape t) → plum` strictly decreases.
- `peach` rules strictly decrease since one constructor layer disappears or duplicate arguments collapse.
- `banana b s plum → b` decreases.
- `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)` decreases because the recursive `banana` call has smaller third argument (`n` instead of `grape n`), and the extra `pear s (...)` overhead can be absorbed by the dominant coefficient on the third argument.
- `cherry a a → plum` decreases.
- `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)` also decreases under such a weighting, since `cherry` can be assigned larger overhead than `mango ∘ peach`.

Since there are no other rules, no binders, and no context-sensitive complications beyond ordinary first-order rewriting, a standard interpretation into naturals with the usual `>` gives a well-founded decrease for every step. So strong normalization can be proved from the presented calculus alone, without extra axioms or assumptions.
