**Key structural observation.** The relation `Step` is given by exactly eight constructors, each of which fires on a *specific, fully‑formed root pattern* of a `Trace` term. Critically, **no congruence/compatibility rules are included** — there is nothing of the form `Step t t' → Step (delta t) (delta t')`, `Step t t' → Step (merge t s) (merge t' s)`, etc. So `Step` is *not* closed under contexts; it can only ever rewrite a whole term whose outermost shape literally matches one of the eight left‑hand sides. Reduction never happens "underneath" a constructor unless a previous root‑step has exposed that shape at the top.

This restriction is decisive for termination:

1. **Rules that extract a subterm** — `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`, `R_eq_refl` — all replace the whole term by one of its own immediate proper subterms (or by `void`, a constant). Any such step strictly decreases a trivial well‑founded measure such as constructor count / structural size.

2. **Rules that build new structure** — `R_rec_succ` (`recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`) and `R_eq_diff` (`eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`) — do *not* decrease size, but they are **dead ends** for further `Step`‑application:
   - `app _ _` never occurs as a left‑hand side of any rule, so a term rooted at `app` is `Step`‑irreducible.
   - `integrate (merge a b)` can only reduce via `R_int_delta`, which needs the argument to be headed by `delta`; but `merge` and `delta` are distinct constructors of the same inductive type (decidable, syntactically disjoint), so this can never match.
   
   Hence, immediately after either of these two rules fires, no further `Step` is possible, full stop — regardless of what `a`, `b`, `s`, `n` are.

**Consequence.** Every `Step`-sequence either strictly decreases a simple structural size measure or terminates in the very next step. There is no way to build an infinite chain: any infinite descent would require infinitely many size‑decreasing steps (impossible, since size is a natural number and the ordering is well‑founded) interspersed with dead-end steps that immediately stop the sequence anyway. This yields strong normalization by a completely elementary well-founded/structural induction on term size plus an 8-case case split — no reducibility candidates, no logical relations, no external axioms, and no strengthening of the rule set are needed.

But that is not the system given: as written, the absence of context rules makes the termination argument immediate and fully self-contained in the presented syntax.

**Conclusion.** Strong normalization holds and is provable directly from the stated `Trace`/`Step` definitions alone, via straightforward structural size induction combined with a finite case analysis showing every rule either decreases size or produces a term outside the domain of any further rule.
