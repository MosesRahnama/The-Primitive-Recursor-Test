Yes, strong normalization of this system can be established from the presented calculus alone.

**Reasoning:**

The system is strongly normalizing because a well-founded measure can be constructed for the terms, such that every rewrite step corresponds to a strict decrease in that measure. A lexicographical ordering of measures is sufficient here.

Let's consider a pair of metrics for any term `t`:
1.  `rec_measure(t)`: A measure of the recursive complexity, defined primarily by the third argument of `recΔ` terms.
2.  `eq_count(t)`: The total number of `eqW` constructors in the term `t`.

We can define a well-founded ordering `>` on terms where `t1 > t2` if `(rec_measure(t1), eq_count(t1))` is lexicographically greater than `(rec_measure(t2), eq_count(t2))`.

Let's analyze the rewrite rules:

1.  **Rules that primarily reduce size:** `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, and `R_rec_zero`. In all these cases, the `rec_measure` does not increase, the `eq_count` does not increase, and the overall term size decreases. They are not problematic.

2.  **`R_eq_refl` and `R_eq_diff`:**
    *   `R_eq_refl`: `eqW a a → void`. The `eq_count` decreases by one.
    *   `R_eq_diff`: `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`. The `eq_count` of the left-hand side is `1 + eq_count(a) + eq_count(b)`. The `eq_count` of the right-hand side is `eq_count(a) + eq_count(b)`. This rule strictly decreases the `eq_count` of the term at the site of the reduction. While term size may increase, the `eq_count` drop ensures progress in the lexicographical order.

3.  **`R_rec_succ`:** `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`. This is the most complex rule.
    *   It resembles a standard primitive recursion pattern. The rewrite is based on pattern matching the third argument, `delta n`, and the recursive call within the result is on the structurally smaller term `n`.
    *   While this rule can increase the overall term size and the `eq_count` (by duplicating `s`), the recursion itself is well-founded. Any sequence of `R_rec_succ` applications to a specific `recΔ` term must terminate because the third argument gets structurally smaller with each step until it becomes `void` (the base case).
    *   This suggests that the primary measure should be based on the recursive structure. For a `recΔ` term, the measure is principally the size of its third argument. For all other terms, it can be defined compositionally. An `R_rec_succ` step strictly decreases this primary recursive measure.

**Conclusion:**

The rewrite system is a combination of two terminating patterns:
- A primitive recursion combinator (`recΔ`) whose reduction is guaranteed to terminate based on the finite structure of its third argument.
- An equality-like operator (`eqW`) whose reduction rules strictly eliminate instances of itself from the term.

The expansive nature of the `recΔ` rule (duplicating `s`) is bounded by its finite recursion depth. The `eqW` rules cannot create new `recΔ` terms or form a loop with the recursion. Therefore, no infinite reduction sequence is possible. A formal proof could be constructed using a lexicographical ordering on a measure of recursive calls and the count of `eqW` symbols.
