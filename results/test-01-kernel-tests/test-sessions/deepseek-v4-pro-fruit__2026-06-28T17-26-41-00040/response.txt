Yes, strong normalization can be proved using only the given calculus (no extra axioms, no external assumptions).  The system defines a top‑level rewrite relation with no rules for reduction under binders or in subterms; reductions happen only at the root.  By straightforward structural induction on `Trace` we can show that every term is strongly normalizing:

* Rules like `peach plum t → t` and `banana b s plum → b` step directly to a proper subterm, which is strongly normalizing by the induction hypothesis.
* Rules like `mango(grape t) → plum`, `cherry a a → plum`, `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`, and `cherry a b → mango(peach a b)` (when `a ≠ b`) produce a term whose root constructor (`plum`, `pear`, or `mango`) does not match any subsequent rule, so the result is immediately irreducible and hence strongly normalizing.

Thus every reduction sequence is forced to terminate after a finite number of steps, and the proof requires nothing beyond the constructors of `Trace` and the inductive definition of `Step`.
