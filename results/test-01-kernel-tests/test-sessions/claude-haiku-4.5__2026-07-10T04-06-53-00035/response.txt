# Assessment: Strong Normalization Cannot Be Established from This Calculus Alone

## Raw Verdict
**No.** Strong normalization cannot be proven for this system from the presented rules without additional structure.

## Primary Obstruction

The critical issue is **`R_rec_succ`**:
```
Step (recΔ b s (delta n)) (app s (recΔ b s n))
```

This rule rewrites a term of the form `recΔ b s (delta n)` by:
1. Applying `s` to the result
2. Creating a recursive call `recΔ b s n`

**The problem:** There is no syntactic measure that decreases with each application of this rule.

- The argument `n` in `recΔ b s (delta n)` becomes `n` in `recΔ b s n` — no progress in the inductive structure of the third argument.
- However, `s` is applied to the result, and `s` itself may contain arbitrary `recΔ` terms.
- Critically, **the rule does not unfold or reduce the structure of `s`**: if `s` is or contains another `recΔ` redex, we can have infinite reduction chains.

## Why Standard Methods Fail

1. **Lexicographic order on term structure:** The third argument to `recΔ` doesn't decrease in depth — it stays at the same level of `delta` nesting.

2. **Size measures:** Without a bound on the evaluation of `s`, the application `app s (...)` can create unbounded growth in the reduction sequence.

3. **Dependency on `s`'s form:** The rewrite explicitly depends on applying an unevaluated term `s`. If `s` itself reduces indefinitely, or if substituting it creates new redexes, there's no guarantee of termination.

## Missing Elements

To establish strong normalization, one would typically need:
- A **decreasing measure independent of the applied term** (e.g., explicit depth/height annotations), or
- **Side conditions** restricting which terms `s` can be (e.g., "s must be in normal form" or "s must be a closed term of bounded complexity"), or  
- **Structural constraints** on the grammar that rule out problematic cycles

None of these are present in the given syntax or rules.

## Conclusion

The calculus as presented is **not known to be strongly normalizing from intrinsic properties alone**. A proof would require strengthening the system or adding external structural assumptions.
