**Termination argument:**  
Any rewrite sequence must consist of:  
- Zero or more applications of size-decreasing rules (each strictly reducing size), followed by  
- At most one application of a terminating rule (which halts the sequence).  

Since term size is a positive integer and decreases with every non-terminating step, the number of consecutive size-decreasing steps is bounded by the initial term size. Terminating steps cannot be followed by further rewrites (their outputs are syntactically normal forms, verified by constructor mismatch with all left-hand sides). Overlaps (e.g., `peach plum plum` matching multiple peach rules) all reduce to the same normal form (`plum`), preserving termination regardless of nondeterministic choice. The condition `a ≠ b` in `R_cherry_diff` is intrinsic to the rule definition and ensures mutual exclusivity with `R_cherry_refl`; it does not introduce non-termination (every `cherry` term rewrites exactly once to a normal form).

**Proof method:** Structural induction on term size suffices:  
- Base: Minimal-size terms (e.g., `plum`, size 1) are normal forms.  
- Step: For term `t` of size `n`, if `t` is a redex:  
  • If rewritten via a terminating rule → normal form (sequence ends).  
  • If rewritten via a size-decreasing rule → smaller term `u` (size < `n`); by IH, `u` is SN.  
No external axioms, guards, or semantic assumptions are required—only syntactic analysis of constructors and the given rules. The absence of context-closure is critical and explicitly supported by the Lean definition (no context rules in `Step`). Thus, strong normalization is derivable *solely* from the presented calculus.
