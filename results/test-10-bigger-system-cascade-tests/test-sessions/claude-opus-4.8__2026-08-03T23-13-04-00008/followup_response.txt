**1. Primary method used.**
A **recursive path order (RPO)** argument: I fixed a precedence on the function symbols (`exp > mult > plus > SUC > BIT0,BIT1`, `minus > if,le,PRE,…`, `le~lt`, `ge~gt`, etc.) plus term statuses (multiset for the `le/lt` and `ge/gt` classes), and claimed every rule strictly decreases in the induced order.

**2. Does it rely on something not fixed by the rules?**
Yes. The **precedence and the status assignment are choices** made by the prover during proof search. Nothing in the rewrite rules canonically singles them out — a different precedence would fail, and I selected one that works by inspecting the rules. The `le~lt` / `ge~gt` equivalence-with-multiset-status was specifically engineered to break the apparent `le→lt→le` cycle. So the *proof* carries structure beyond the bare syntax.

**3. Does that put it outside the stated boundary?**
No. The boundary forbids *strengthening the system*: adding rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or hidden object-level assumptions. A termination ordering is **external proof apparatus**, not a modification of the system — it changes nothing about which terms rewrite to which. The instruction explicitly allows "any standard proof method," and RPO with a chosen precedence is the paradigm case. The precedence is *justified by* the rules, not imported as an axiom about the objects (it makes no claim that the numerals "really" denote naturals, etc.). Hence choosing an ordering is legitimate and inside the boundary.

The honest caveat is only this: such a proof is not "canonical" in the sense of being forced by the syntax; it requires a search/choice. But that is true of essentially every termination proof and does not violate the stated constraints.

**4. Is the system still strongly normalizing?**
Yes. Independently of any particular ordering, the rules are the standard binary-numeral definitions of `SUC, PRE, plus, mult, exp, minus`, the comparisons, and parity — all recursions descend on strictly smaller `BIT0/BIT1/NUMERAL`-structured arguments, with function symbols only ever replaced by lower-layer ones (`exp`→`mult`→`plus`→`SUC`) or by copies on strictly smaller inputs (the squaring in `exp`). There is no rule whose right-hand side can regenerate a strictly larger instance of its own recursion. The RPO I gave genuinely orients all 100+ rules (I re-checked the delicate cases: `exp` duplication, `mult(BIT1,BIT1)`, `PRE/minus` with `if`, and the `le/lt`, `ge/gt` cross-references). So the system is terminating, and this is provable within the stated boundary.
