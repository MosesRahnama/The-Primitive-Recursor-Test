## Direct answers

**1. What primary method did the previous answer use?**
A **recursive path order (RPO)** argument: a fixed quasi‑precedence on the function symbols plus lexicographic (left‑to‑right) status, checking `l ≻_rpo r` for every rule.

**2. Does that method rely on anything not fixed by the rules themselves?**
Yes, in the ordinary sense that *every* termination proof does: the **precedence** (`exp > mult > plus > …`), the **status** (lexicographic, left‑to‑right), and the **equivalence classes** (`le ~ lt`, `ge ~ gt`) are choices made by the prover. They are not literally written in the rules — they are parameters supplied to the proof method and then justified against the rules.

**3. Does that place the method outside the stated boundary?**
**No.** The boundary forbids *strengthening the system*: extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or hidden object‑level assumptions that change the rewrite relation. A precedence/status assignment does none of these — it does not add rules, does not restrict any reduction, and does not import external axioms. Once a precedence is fixed, RPO is a well‑founded order defined **purely from the term syntax**, and the verification `l ≻_rpo r` uses nothing but the given left/right‑hand sides. Selecting such an order is exactly what "any standard proof method" entails; if choosing an ordering were disqualifying, *no* termination method (RPO, KBO, polynomial/matrix interpretations, dependency pairs) could ever qualify, which is not what the instruction means. So the method stays within bounds.

The one thing to be clear about: the precedence must be *validated* against the rules (which it is — every rule is oriented, the precedence is acyclic, and the mutually recursive `le/lt`, `ge/gt` cross‑calls strictly decrease a lexicographic argument). It is a *derived, checkable* artifact, not a hidden assumption.

**4. Is the system still strongly normalizing?**
**Yes.** My re‑examination confirms the RPO proof holds. Every rule falls into one of two patterns already fully accounted for:
- recursive calls strictly shrink an argument in the subterm order (`NUMERAL n, BIT0 n, BIT1 n → n`), handled by lexicographic status;
- size‑increasing right‑hand sides (the `mult`, `exp` squaring/expansion rules, `minus → PRE(BIT0 …)`, `PRE → if(eq …)`) are dominated by the precedence, then discharged by descending into arguments.

The precedence is acyclic and orients all ~100 rules, and `if` is an unrewritten free constructor that cannot seed a loop. The system is strongly normalizing, and this is established from the syntax and rules alone.
