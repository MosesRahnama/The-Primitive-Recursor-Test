## Assessment

**Yes.** Strong normalization is provable from the rules alone, using a standard **recursive path order (RPO) with a quasi‑precedence and lexicographic (left‑to‑right) status**. No structural axioms, guards, or auxiliary rules are needed.

## Reasoning

Two structural facts about the rules make a syntactic path order sufficient:

1. **Every recursive call strictly decreases an argument in the subterm order.** In each self‑recursive rule the recursive occurrence on the right has arguments that are proper subterms of the left‑hand arguments (`NUMERAL(n)→n`, `BIT0(n)/BIT1(n)→n`). E.g. `mult(BIT0 m,BIT0 n)→…mult(m,n)`, `exp(x,BIT0 n)→…exp(x,n)`, `eq(BIT0 m,BIT0 n)→eq(m,n)`. A left‑to‑right lexicographic comparison of arguments handles all of these (the decreasing coordinate is always consistent).

2. **Size‑increasing right‑hand sides are dominated by precedence.** Whenever a rule produces a larger term (e.g. `mult(BIT0 m,BIT1 n)→plus(BIT0 m,BIT0(BIT0(mult(m,n))))`, or the squaring rules `exp(_,BIT0 n)→mult(exp(_,n),exp(_,n))`), the outermost defined symbol on the left is higher in the precedence than the symbols it generates, so RPO discharges them by descending into the arguments.

A consistent quasi‑precedence is, for instance:

```
exp > mult > plus > minus > {PRE, SUC} > {eq, EVEN, ODD, le~lt, ge~gt}
        > NUMERAL > {BIT0, BIT1} > if > {0, T, F}
```

with the mutually recursive pairs placed in **equivalence classes** `le ~ lt` and `ge ~ gt`. Their cross‑calls (`le(BIT1 m,BIT0 n)→lt(m,n)`, `lt(BIT0 m,BIT1 n)→le(m,n)`, and likewise for `ge/gt`) then reduce to argument comparisons, where a left argument strictly decreases — so those classes are internally well‑founded.

Spot checks confirming the order:
- `SUC(NUMERAL n)→NUMERAL(SUC n)`: `SUC>NUMERAL`, and LHS ≻ `SUC n` since `NUMERAL n ≻ n`.
- `PRE(BIT0 n)→if(eq(n,0),0,BIT1(PRE n))`: `PRE` dominates `if, eq, BIT1`; LHS ≻ `PRE n`.
- `plus(BIT1 m,BIT1 n)→BIT0(SUC(plus m n))`: `plus>SUC,BIT0`; LHS ≻ `plus(m,n)`.
- `exp(BIT1 m,BIT1 n)→mult(mult(BIT1 m,exp(BIT1 m,n)),exp(BIT1 m,n))`: `exp>mult`; LHS ≻ `exp(BIT1 m,n)` by the second argument.
- `minus(BIT1 m,BIT0 n)→if(le(n,m),BIT1(minus m n),0)`: `minus>if,le,BIT1`; LHS ≻ `minus(m,n)`.

All 90-odd rules satisfy `l ≻_rpo r` under this precedence.

Two harmless observations that don't affect the argument:
- `if` has **no rewrite rule**, so it behaves as a free constructor — it cannot start a loop.
- Every rule’s right‑hand side uses only variables from its left‑hand side (variable‑preserving), and duplication (e.g. squaring in `exp`) is unproblematic for RPO.

Since RPO is a well‑founded simplification order and orients every rule strictly, the system is strongly normalizing — established **purely from the given syntax and rewrite rules**.
