Assessment: yes — this TRS looks strongly normalizing as given, without adding any extra axioms or rules.

Why, briefly:
- The recursive calls are structurally descending on the binary-numeral spine built from `NUMERAL`, `BIT0`, `BIT1`, `0`.
- Self-recursive rules like `SUC`, `PRE`, `plus`, `mult`, `exp`, `eq`, `minus` all recurse on proper subterms / bit-stripped arguments; even where work is duplicated (notably in `exp`), the duplicated calls are on strictly smaller arguments.
- The only real mutual-recursion cycles are `le ↔ lt` and `ge ↔ gt`, and those also strictly shrink the compared arguments by peeling off outer bits.
- Inter-symbol calls otherwise go downward in a clear call hierarchy (`exp -> mult -> plus -> SUC`, `minus -> PRE/le`, etc.), so there is no cycle that preserves or enlarges the active arguments.
- `if` has no rewrite rules at all, so it may leave stuck terms, but it cannot create nontermination.

So a standard termination proof method such as dependency pairs with a structural measure (or an RPO/LPO-style simplification ordering, with the mutual comparator pairs handled as one SCC) should suffice on the presented calculus alone.
