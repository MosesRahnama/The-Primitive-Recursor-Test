### Assessment: Yes

The given TRS is strongly normalizing, and this can be established directly from the signature and rules by a standard termination method (e.g., lexicographic path order with quasi-precedence + status, or the dependency-pair framework with a suitable reduction pair). No extra rules, guards, side-conditions or semantic assumptions on the symbols are required.

The rules are the usual structurally recursive defining equations for a binary representation of the naturals (constructors \(0\), \(\mathsf{BIT0}\), \(\mathsf{BIT1}\), wrapper \(\mathsf{NUMERAL}\)) together with the operations \(\mathsf{SUC}\), \(\mathsf{PRE}\), \(\mathsf{plus}\), \(\mathsf{mult}\), \(\mathsf{exp}\), the comparisons and the Boolean tests. Each right-hand side is strictly smaller than its left-hand side in a recursive path order once the symbols are ordered by a quasi-precedence of the shape
\[
\mathsf{exp}>\mathsf{mult}>\mathsf{plus}>\mathsf{minus}>\mathsf{PRE}>\mathsf{SUC}>\mathsf{if}
>\{\mathsf{le}\sim\mathsf{lt}\}
>\{\mathsf{ge}\sim\mathsf{gt}\}
>\mathsf{eq}
>\dots
>\{\mathsf{BIT0},\mathsf{BIT1}\}
>\mathsf{NUMERAL}
>\{0,\mathsf{T},\mathsf{F}\}
\]
(with lexicographic status). The only non-trivial points are the mutual recursion among \(\{\mathsf{le},\mathsf{lt}\}\) and among \(\{\mathsf{ge},\mathsf{gt}\}\); placing each pair in the same equivalence class of the quasi-precedence makes the lexicographic comparison go through exactly as the recursive calls decrease. Duplicating rules (the \(\mathsf{mult}\) and \(\mathsf{exp}\) cases) are handled by the fact that the duplicated recursive calls occur at strictly smaller arguments. The undefined symbol \(\mathsf{if}\) receives a place in the precedence below \(\mathsf{PRE}\) and \(\mathsf{minus}\) and never needs rewrite rules of its own.

Consequently every rewrite sequence is finite.
