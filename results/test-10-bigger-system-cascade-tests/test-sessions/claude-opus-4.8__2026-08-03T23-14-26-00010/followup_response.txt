## Direct answers

**1. What primary method did the previous answer use?**
A **recursive path order (RPO/MPO)**: a chosen precedence on the signature together with multiset status for all symbols, used to orient every rule left‑to‑right.

**2. Does it rely on anything not fixed by the rules themselves?**
Yes, in one sense: the **precedence** (`exp > mult > plus > minus > SUC > PRE > le≈lt, ge≈gt > eq > if > BIT0 > BIT1 > NUMERAL > 0 …`) and the **multiset status** are *choices*. They are not uniquely determined by the rewrite rules; they are parameters of the proof search that I supplied. RPO's soundness also invokes the general well‑foundedness/monotonicity/stability theorem for path orders.

**3. Does that place the method outside the stated boundary?**
No. The boundary forbids *strengthening the system* — no extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or object‑level/structural axioms about arithmetic. A termination precedence is none of these: it is a meta‑level proof artifact, exactly the kind of ingredient a "standard proof method" is expected to carry. Well‑foundedness of a fixed precedence on a *finite* signature is automatic, not an imported axiom. The RHS/LHS comparisons I used appeal only to the syntactic subterm structure and symbol identities visible in the rules. So choosing a precedence is legitimate proof search, not a smuggled assumption. (If one demanded a *canonical, choice‑free* certificate, that stricter reading would exclude essentially every ordering‑based method — but that is not what the instruction says; it explicitly permits any standard method.)

**4. Independently of boundary questions — is the system still SN?**
Yes. I re‑checked the constraints and they form a *consistent, acyclic* precedence:
- All "strip a bit / push NUMERAL" rules decrease the argument multiset (`{BIT_i x, …} ≻ {x, …}`).
- The mutually recursive pairs `le/lt` and `ge/gt` never cross‑reference each other, so each pair can sit in one equivalence class with multiset status.
- The symbol‑lowering rules respect `exp > mult > plus > SUC` and `minus > PRE > {if,eq}`.
- The duplicating `exp` rules cause only growth, not looping, since the second argument strictly shrinks at each `exp`‑recursion.
- `if`, `T`, `F`, `0` have no rules, so they cannot start a cycle.

Every rule is oriented, so the system is terminating.

**Net position:** My earlier conclusion stands — the system is strongly normalizing, and RPO establishes it **within** the stated boundary. The only honest refinement is transparency about point 2: the proof needs a precedence choice, which is a normal feature of the method rather than an extra assumption about the objects.
