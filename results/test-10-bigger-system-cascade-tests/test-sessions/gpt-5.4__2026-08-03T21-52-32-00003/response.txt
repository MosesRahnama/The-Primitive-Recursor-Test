Yes — my assessment is that strong normalization should be provable from this TRS alone.

Briefly: the recursion is purely syntactic and structurally descending. In every recursive SCC, the recursive call is on a proper subterm of the original “numeric” argument(s): rules strip a `NUMERAL`, `BIT0`, or `BIT1` layer, and for `exp` the exponent goes from `BIT0(n)`/`BIT1(n)` to `n`. The mutually recursive pairs `le/lt` and `ge/gt` still decrease the argument-depth measure in the same way. Cross-calls only go to lower-complexity symbols (`exp -> mult -> plus -> SUC`, `minus -> PRE/le`, `PRE -> eq`, etc.), so they do not form cycles.

So a standard termination proof method like dependency pairs with a size-change / multiset measure should work. No extra arithmetic semantics, well-formedness assumptions, or added `if`-rules are needed. The missing `if` reductions do not hurt termination; they only leave more normal forms.
