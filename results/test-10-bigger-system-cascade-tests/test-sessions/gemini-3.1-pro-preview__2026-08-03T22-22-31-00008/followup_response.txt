Here are the direct answers to your questions:

1. **What primary method did your previous answer use?** 
   The Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO) method (along with the Dependency Pair framework). 

2. **Does that method rely on any proof structure, ordering choice, interpretation, or other assumption not fixed by the rewrite rules themselves?** 
   Yes. Applying LPO requires constructing a specific, well-founded precedence (an ordering hierarchy of the function symbols, e.g., `exp > mult > plus`) and assigning statuses (lexicographic or multiset) to those symbols. These parameters are meta-level proof choices constrained by, but not strictly fixed by, the raw syntax of the rewrite rules.

3. **If yes, does that place the method outside the stated boundary?** 
   No. The boundary explicitly permits "any standard proof method provided it does not require strengthening the system with extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or hidden assumptions." A precedence ordering is an intrinsic mechanism of the LPO proof method itself; it operates entirely at the meta-level. It does not modify the object-level calculus, import structural axioms (like associativity or commutativity), impose types, or require variables to behave as specific mathematical objects. 

4. **Separately from boundary compliance, do you still think the system is strongly normalizing?** 
   Yes. The system is unequivocally and unconditionally strongly normalizing. Under purely syntactic, unconstrained first-order rewriting, every possible reduction pathway strictly decreases the structural complexity of the term with respect to a well-founded path ordering, guaranteeing termination for all terms.
