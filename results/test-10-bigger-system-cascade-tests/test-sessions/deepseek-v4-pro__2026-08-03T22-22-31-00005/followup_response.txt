1. **Primary method used**: Recursive path ordering (RPO) or polynomial interpretation, as typical termination techniques for rewrite systems.

2. **External proof structure**: Yes. Both methods require making choices not forced by the rules themselves—e.g., a precedence on function symbols (for RPO) or a polynomial assignment to function symbols (for polynomial interpretations). These choices are not uniquely derivable from the presented rewrite rules; they are externally supplied to orient the rules.

3. **Does that place the method outside the stated boundary?**  
   Strictly interpreted, *any* termination proof that needs a well-founded order or an interpretation could be seen as importing a structural assumption (the existence of a suitable order/interpretation) beyond what is “derivable” from the syntax and rules alone. Under that very strict reading, the method would be outside the boundary. However, the instruction also permits “any standard proof method provided it does not require strengthening the system with extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or hidden assumptions.” The choice of an ordering is a meta-level construction, not a new object-level axiom or rule, so it is traditionally allowed. I therefore see no definitive violation of the boundary as intended for term-rewriting termination analysis.

4. **Is the system strongly normalizing?**  
   Yes, independently of any boundary concerns. All recursive calls decrease a suitable size measure (e.g., the total number of `BIT0`/`BIT1`/`NUMERAL` constructors). There is no infinite wrapping/unwrapping via `NUMERAL`, the `if` conditional is primitive and not reduced, and the boolean predicates (`eq`, `le`, etc.) are structurally decreasing. The rules can be oriented by a lexicographic path order or a polynomial interpretation, confirming strong normalization.
