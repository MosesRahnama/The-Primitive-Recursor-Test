Assessment: yes.

Reasoning, staying purely at the rewrite-syntax level:

- The system is syntactically recursive in a decreasing way. Recursive calls always move to proper subterms or to a lexicographically smaller argument tuple:
  - `SUC`, `PRE`, `EVEN`, `ODD`, `eq`, `le`, `lt`, `ge`, `gt`, `plus`, `mult`, `minus` strip `NUMERAL`, `BIT0`, or `BIT1`.
  - `exp` keeps the base but strictly decreases the exponent argument, e.g. `exp(BIT0(m),BIT0(n)) -> ... exp(BIT0(m),n)`.
  - The mutual recursions `le <-> lt` and `ge <-> gt` also shrink the arguments by peeling bits.

- The non-recursive symbol flow is downward: informally
  `exp > mult > plus/minus > SUC/PRE > comparisons/parity > if > constructors/constants`.
  A standard simplification order such as RPO/LPO with a quasi-precedence (putting `le,lt` together and `ge,gt` together) should orient the rules.

- Duplication on the right-hand side (`mult`, `exp`) does not by itself break termination, because the duplicated recursive calls are still on smaller arguments.

- `if` has no rewrite rules here. So `if(...)` terms may get stuck, but they do not create loops; if anything, that makes termination easier.

So, from the presented TRS alone, I do not see a rewrite cycle that would block a standard termination proof. Strong normalization/termination should be provable without importing any extra axioms or side conditions.
