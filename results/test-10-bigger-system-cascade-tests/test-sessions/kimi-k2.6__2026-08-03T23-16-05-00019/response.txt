**Yes** — strong normalization can be established from the presented rules alone.

**Reasoning.**  
Every rule is *structurally recursive*: every recursive call on the right-hand side appears on a proper subterm of a left-hand side argument (modulo the `NUMERAL` wrapper, which is either stripped at `0` or pushed inward over a defined symbol without recreating a larger pattern). The arithmetic, predecessor, comparison, and subtraction functions all recurse by stripping one `BIT0` or `BIT1` constructor. Exponentiation duplicates recursive calls, but each duplicate is made on a strictly smaller exponent (a proper subterm of the original exponent), so the recursion depth is bounded by the size of the exponent. The symbol `if` has no rewrite rules and acts as an inert constructor; it blocks further evaluation but cannot introduce divergence. Because no rule can regenerate a larger instance of its own left-hand side, there is no cyclic dependency. A standard simplification order (e.g., a lexicographic path order with precedence `exp ≻ mult ≻ plus ≻ SUC, PRE ≻ minus` and treating `BIT0`/`BIT1`/`NUMERAL` as unary function symbols) or a direct structural induction over binary numerals proves termination, hence strong normalization, without extra axioms, guards, or imported assumptions.
