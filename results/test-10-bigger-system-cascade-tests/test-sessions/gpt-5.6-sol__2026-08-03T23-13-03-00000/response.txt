**Yes.** Strong normalization can be established purely syntactically for this TRS, without assuming any arithmetic meaning for the symbols.

A generalized recursive path ordering/LPO can orient all rules. Use, for example, precedence constraints such as

- `exp ≻ mult ≻ plus ≻ SUC ≻ {NUMERAL, BIT0, BIT1}`,
- `minus ≻ PRE ≻ eq`,
- `minus ≻ {le, lt, if}`,
- defined symbols above the constructors/constants occurring in their right-hand sides,
- and equivalent precedence for the mutually recursive pairs `le ∼ lt` and `ge ∼ gt`, with lexicographic status.

Every recursive call then has structurally smaller arguments: outer `BIT0`, `BIT1`, or `NUMERAL` constructors are stripped. The duplication in `exp` and `mult` is harmless for a path ordering because every duplicated recursive call is still smaller, and its enclosing root has lower precedence. The `NUMERAL`-commuting rules are similarly decreasing. The symbol `if` needs no semantics or additional rules; it is simply an inert lower-precedence symbol whose arguments may continue reducing.

Thus all rules can be strictly oriented by a well-founded, substitution- and context-stable simplification ordering, yielding strong normalization for arbitrary first-order terms, not merely ground numeral terms.
