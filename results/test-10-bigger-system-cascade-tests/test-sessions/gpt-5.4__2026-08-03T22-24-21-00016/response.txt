Assessment: Yes.

Briefly: this TRS looks strongly normalizing from the rules alone. A standard syntactic termination proof method such as recursive path ordering or dependency pairs should work without adding any extra axioms.

Why:
- The recursive calls always move to proper subterms by stripping `NUMERAL`, `BIT0`, or `BIT1`.
- The only mutual-recursion cycles (`le`/`lt` and `ge`/`gt`) also strictly shorten the bit-structure of the arguments.
- Rules for `plus`, `mult`, `exp`, `PRE`, and `minus` may enlarge or duplicate terms, but every duplicated recursive call is on strictly smaller arguments.
- `if` has no rewrite rules at all, so it cannot generate loops; it only leaves partial normal forms like `if(T,...)`.

So, although naive term-size decrease does not hold, strong normalization/termination is still provable purely from the presented rewrite system, with no extra structural or semantic assumptions.
