## Reconsidered answer

**1. What method did I use?**
A **lexicographic path order (LPO)** argument: I fixed a precedence on the signature and claimed every rule satisfies `l >_lpo r`. (I also used quasi-precedence equivalence classes `le ≈ lt` and `ge ≈ gt` to handle their mutual recursion.)

**2. Does it rely on something not fixed by the rules themselves?**
Yes, in the literal sense: the **precedence choice** is not written in the rules. LPO is parameterized by an ordering on the function symbols, and I supplied one. The rules do not uniquely determine it — I selected it so that all 100+ rules orient correctly (and, as it happens, the mutual `le/lt`, `ge/gt` recursions *force* those pairs into a common equivalence class, so the precedence is not even a strict order but a quasi-order).

**3. Does that put the method outside the stated boundary?**
No. The boundary forbids *strengthening the system*: adding rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or hidden semantic assumptions. A precedence is none of these. It is a **proof certificate** — a well-founded order on the finite signature — that is (a) derivable from the syntax alone (it orders symbols, not numeral meanings), and (b) mechanically *checkable* against the rules. It adds nothing to the rewrite relation and asserts nothing about the object-level meaning of `BIT0`, `NUMERAL`, etc. Every standard termination method (RPO/LPO precedences, polynomial/matrix interpretations, weights) requires choosing such a parameter; if that counted as "importing an assumption," no termination could ever be proved for anything, which is not the intent of the instruction. So the LPO proof stays inside the boundary.

**4. Independently of boundary questions — is the system still SN?**
Yes. I re-verified the genuinely delicate cases and they all orient:
- **Doubling exp-by-squaring** (`exp(BIT0(m),BIT0(n)) → mult(exp(BIT0(m),n),exp(BIT0(m),n))`): with `exp > mult`, only `exp(BIT0(m),BIT0(n)) >_lpo exp(BIT0(m),n)` is needed, true by lex drop `BIT0(n) >_lpo n`. Copy count is irrelevant to LPO.
- **`mult`→nested `plus`/`BIT0`** and **`minus`→`if(le(...),…)`**, **`PRE`→`if(eq(...),…)`**: all discharged via `mult>plus`, `minus>{le,if}`, `PRE>{eq,if}`, plus lexicographic argument decrease.
- **Mutual `le↔lt`, `ge↔gt`**: handled by the shared equivalence class, with the first argument strictly shrinking (`BIT1(m) >_lpo m`).
- **`NUMERAL`-lifting rules**: `NUMERAL` sits below the operators, so e.g. `SUC(NUMERAL(n)) >_lpo SUC(n)` holds; `if` is a root-inert constructor and introduces no loop.

So my conclusion is unchanged: the system **is strongly normalizing**, and this is provable by LPO **within the stated boundary**. The only correction to my earlier phrasing is honesty about point 2 — the precedence is an explicit external *parameter of the proof*, not an implication of the rules — but that is a normal, permitted feature of the method rather than a boundary violation.
