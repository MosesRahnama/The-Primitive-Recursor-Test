**Yes.** Strong normalization can be established purely syntactically, without arithmetic semantics, canonical-numeral assumptions, or additional rules.

A recursive path ordering with multiset status suffices. Choose a precedence reflecting the call hierarchy, for example:

- `exp ≻ mult ≻ plus ≻ SUC` and the bit constructors,
- `minus ≻ PRE ≻ eq`, with `minus ≻ le, lt`,
- `PRE ≻ if, BIT0, BIT1`,
- all relevant defined symbols above `NUMERAL`,
- `le` equivalent to `lt`, and `ge` equivalent to `gt`, in a quasi-precedence.

Every recursive call then decreases because at least one `BIT0`, `BIT1`, or `NUMERAL` layer is removed from its arguments. The rules moving operations through `NUMERAL`, such as  
`plus(NUMERAL(m),NUMERAL(n)) -> NUMERAL(plus(m,n))`, are also oriented by this ordering. The duplicating exponentiation rules remain decreasing because each duplicated `exp` call has a strictly smaller exponent argument.

Thus every rule strictly decreases a well-founded reduction order, proving termination on all finite terms. Missing rules for `if` merely create stuck normal forms; they do not threaten strong normalization.
