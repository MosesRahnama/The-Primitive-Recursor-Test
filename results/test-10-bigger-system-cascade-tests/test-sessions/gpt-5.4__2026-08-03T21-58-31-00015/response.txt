Yes — my raw assessment is that strong normalization should be provable from this TRS alone, under the ordinary free-term reading of first-order rewriting.

Why:
- The recursive rules are structurally descending on the binary numeral shape: they peel off `NUMERAL`, `BIT0`, or `BIT1` from the active arguments.
- When a rule calls a different function, it moves to a lower-level operation (`exp -> mult -> plus`, `minus -> PRE/le`, etc.), so there is no obvious higher-level cycle.
- The only mutual-recursive clusters, `le/lt` and `ge/gt`, still strictly shorten the bit-structure of the arguments.
- `if` has no rewrite rules here, so it is inert; it can block evaluation, but it cannot create an infinite reduction.

So I do not see a genuine rewrite loop in the presented calculus. A standard termination proof method such as dependency pairs with a suitable simplification order / quasi-precedence (or an RPO/LPO-style argument) should be enough, without adding any extra axioms or hidden assumptions.
