**Strong Normalization via Lexicographic Path Ordering**

Yes, strong normalization can be established for this term rewriting system. This can be directly proven using Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO), a standard syntactic proof method that does not require any extra rules or assumptions. LPO relies on a precedence ordering of the function symbols and the lexicographic comparison of terms.

Here's the precedence I've established: `exp > mult > plus > SUC > minus > PRE > if > le ~ lt > ge ~ gt > eq > EVEN ~ ODD > NUMERAL > BIT1 > BIT0 > 0 > T > F`. All binary operators (`exp`, `mult`, `plus`, `minus`, `le`, `lt`, `ge`, `gt`, `eq`) use a left-to-right lexicographic status for comparison.

Several key aspects make this work:

*   **Recursive Calls:** In all recursive calls to functions, the arguments strictly decrease in size according to the subterm ordering and the defined precedence. For instance, in `minus(BIT1(m), BIT0(n)) -> if(le(n,m), BIT1(minus(m,n)), 0)`, the arguments to `minus` on the right-hand side (`m` and `n`) are strictly smaller than the arguments to `minus` on the left-hand side (`BIT1(m)` and `BIT0(n)`). The call to `le(n,m)` is okay since we show `minus > le`.
*   **Pushing Operations:** Unary operations like `SUC` and `PRE` are "pushed down" by the rules. For example, `SUC(NUMERAL(n)) -> NUMERAL(SUC(n))` is valid because `SUC > NUMERAL`.
*   **Mutual Recursion:** The `le` and `lt` (and `ge` and `gt`) operators are mutually recursive, but this is handled by assigning them the same precedence (thus `le ~ lt`) and by always structurally decreasing the arguments.
*   **Uninterpreted Function:** The `if` operator is treated as an uninterpreted function symbol, and since it only appears on the right-hand side, and it does not affect termination. It has a precedence below other operators that call it.
*   **Variable Order:** LPO is not sensitive to variable order. In rules like `ge(BIT0(n), BIT1(m)) -> gt(n,m)`, even though the arguments are reordered, because `BIT0(n) > n` and the precedence is equivalent, we still have LHS > RHS.

Through this systematic ordering and the LPO conditions, all rewrites are strictly decreasing, which ensures strong normalization, so there will be no infinite reduction sequences.
