3. **Boundary violation**: Yes. The boundary forbids importing assumptions "beyond what is derivable from the given syntax and rewrite rules." Precedence orders, polynomial assignments, or reduction pairs are *invented constructs*, not syntactic consequences of the rules. Their validity depends on external verification, placing these methods outside the boundary.

For every rule, all recursive calls occur on arguments that are strict syntactic subterms of LHS arguments (e.g., `exp(0,BIT0(n))` recurses on `n` ⊏ `BIT0(n)`; `SUC(BIT1(n))` recurses on `n` ⊏ `BIT1(n)`). Well-foundedness of height on finite terms follows from the syntax alone; no external axioms, orders, or interpretations are needed. Thus, strong normalization *is* establishable from the calculus alone, though the prior answer’s cited methods were boundary-noncompliant.
