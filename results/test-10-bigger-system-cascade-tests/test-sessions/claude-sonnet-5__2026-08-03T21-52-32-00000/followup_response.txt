1. **Primary method:** I used the general idea of a polynomial/depth-based interpretation combined with an informal dependency-pair/subterm-criterion argument, asserting that a size or depth measure decreases on every rule.

2. **Reliance on external choices:** Yes. A polynomial interpretation requires *choosing* a specific interpretation function (weights, monotonicity, coefficients) for every symbol, and a dependency-pair argument requires *choosing* an argument filtering, a well-founded order on tuples, and a decomposition into SCCs. None of these choices are fixed or forced by the rewrite rules themselves — they are proof engineering decisions made by the person applying the method. The rules only specify rewriting behavior, not any ordering, weighting, or filtering scheme. In particular, rules like `minus(BIT1(m),BIT0(n)) -> if(le(n,m),BIT1(minus(m,n)),0)` and `PRE(BIT0(n)) -> if(eq(n,0),0,BIT1(PRE(n)))` introduce `if` with *no rewrite rules at all* for `if` — meaning the "evaluation" of the condition and selection of a branch is not something the rewrite system itself performs. Treating `if(b,x,y)` as eventually reducing to `x` or `y` (which is implicitly assumed when reasoning about termination "under" `if`) is a semantic/object-level assumption about `if`'s behavior not licensed by any rule in the TRS.

3. **Boundary compliance:** Yes — this places the method partly outside the stated boundary. The polynomial interpretation itself (as a proof technique) is standard and permissible, but the informal justification I gave leaned on assumptions about how `if` behaves and about which "companion" calls terminate, which are not derivable purely from the given rules (since `if` has zero rewrite rules, its normal form behavior is entirely unspecified by the system). Strictly speaking, since `if` is a completely free, unconstrained symbol with no rules, any interpretation must simply treat it as a monotone constructor-like symbol — which is a legitimate, rule-consistent treatment, not a hidden strengthening. So the *interpretation choice itself* is within bounds (interpretations are always "chosen" in termination proofs; that's inherent to the standard method, not an illicit addition). However, my earlier narrative describing "if" as selecting branches, or asserting termination of "companion functions" without proof, was an unjustified shortcut — not a rigorous derivation from the rules.

4. **Do I still think it's strongly normalizing?** Yes. Despite the gap in rigor of my earlier explanation, the system is very likely strongly normalizing: every rule strictly decreases a suitable well-founded measure (e.g., a polynomial interpretation assigning depth-based weights to NUMERAL/BIT0/BIT1/0 and monotone symbol weights elsewhere), and `if` being rule-free causes no issue since it can be interpreted as any monotone function without affecting decrease on the defining rules of other symbols. So the informal conclusion stands, even though my earlier justification was not presented with full rigor.
