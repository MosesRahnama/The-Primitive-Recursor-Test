Assessment: yes.

Reasoning: as a pure first-order TRS, this looks strongly normalizing from the rules alone. The recursive structure is syntactically decreasing:

- wrapper rules with `NUMERAL`, `BIT0`, `BIT1` always peel a constructor off the recursive arguments;
- `SUC`, `PRE`, `EVEN`, `ODD`, `eq`, `le`, `lt`, `ge`, `gt` recurse on proper subterms or on argument pairs of strictly smaller total binary depth;
- `plus`, `mult`, and `minus` recurse on smaller bit tails;
- `exp` recurses only on a smaller exponent;
- cross-calls go downward in the call graph (`exp -> mult -> plus -> SUC`, `minus -> PRE/le`, etc.), while the only mutual cycles (`le <-> lt` and `ge <-> gt`) still strictly decrease argument size.

Some rules duplicate work (`exp`, `PRE`), but only by duplicating strictly smaller recursive calls, so a standard dependency-pair / multiset measure argument should still prove termination. The missing `if` rules do not hurt termination; `if` is inert here and only causes stuck normal forms, not loops.

So, within the given calculus and without extra axioms, strong normalization should be provable.
