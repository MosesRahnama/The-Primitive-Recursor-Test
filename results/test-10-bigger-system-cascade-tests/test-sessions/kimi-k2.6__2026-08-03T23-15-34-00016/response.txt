**Assessment: Yes — strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone.**

**Reasoning**

The given TRS is a first-order system of primitive-recursive-style definitions over a binary numeral representation.  A standard structural termination argument applies directly to the rules as written, with no need for imported axioms, extra rules, or side conditions.

* Every recursive rule fires on a strict structural subterm of its left-hand side.  For example:
  * `plus(BIT0(m),BIT0(n))` calls `plus(m,n)` on proper subterms `m` and `n`.
  * `exp(x, BIT0(n))` calls `exp(x,n)` on the proper subterm `n`.
  * `SUC(NUMERAL(n))` calls `SUC(n)` on the proper subterm `n`.
  * `PRE(BIT0(n))` calls `PRE(n)` inside `BIT1(PRE(n))`, again on a proper subterm.
  * `minus` recurses on the immediate subterms `m` and `n` after stripping one `BIT0`/`BIT1` layer.

* The only rule that is not a pure constructor descent is the base-case contraction `BIT0(0) -> 0`.  This strictly decreases term size and cannot be used to build up larger terms.

* Duplication (e.g. in the `exp` rules, where recursive results are copied) is harmless because each copy is a recursive call on a strictly smaller argument.  There is no rule that re-creates a larger instance of its own left-hand side from the result of a recursive call.

* No left-hand side embeds into its right-hand side, and there are no cyclic rewrite chains: base cases rewrite to constants/constructors (`0`, `T`, `F`, `BIT1(0)`), and the recursive cases always reduce the number of `BIT0`/`BIT1`/`NUMERAL` layers in the principal argument.

Consequently, one can prove strong normalization by a straightforward well-founded induction on the depth (or a suitable lexicographic combination of the depths) of the arguments to the outermost defined symbol.  Because all recursion is structural and all base cases are terminal, every rewrite sequence is finite.
