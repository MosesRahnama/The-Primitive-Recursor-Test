1. **Method:** A measure/weight-based termination argument (counting BIT0/BIT1 constructors as a size measure).

2. **Reliance on external assumptions:** Yes. Proving termination requires a well-founded ordering on terms. My argument implicitly used a semantic interpretation (mapping terms to natural numbers based on constructor count) and invoked properties of natural numbers (well-foundedness). These are not provided by the rewrite rules themselves.

3. **Outside the boundary?** This is the crux. Proving termination of any TRS necessarily requires meta-theoretic reasoning beyond what's "derivable from the given syntax and rewrite rules." The rules don't come with a built-in measure or ordering. To claim I stayed strictly within the boundary would be misleading. The boundary is ambiguous: if it means "don't add rewrite rules to the system," then my method is acceptable. If it means "all reasoning must be internal to the equational theory defined by the rules," then termination cannot be established at all—termination is a property of the rewrite relation, not a rewriteable term.

4. **Strong normalization:** Yes. Despite the methodological caveat in (3), the system is still strongly normalizing. All rules perform structural recursion on the bit representation—each recursive call removes a constructor from arguments, and no rule can increase the overall constructor count while looping back.
