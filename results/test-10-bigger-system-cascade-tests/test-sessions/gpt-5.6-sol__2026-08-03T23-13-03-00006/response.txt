**Assessment: yes.** Strong normalization can be established syntactically from the given TRS alone.

A standard dependency-pair/structural-descent argument is available:

- Every recursive call of `SUC`, `PRE`, `plus`, `mult`, `minus`, `EVEN`, `ODD`, and `eq` is made on proper subterms of the matched arguments.
- Recursive `exp` calls strictly decrease the exponent argument. Duplication of such calls can increase term size, but cannot create an infinite dependency chain.
- The mutually recursive pairs `le`/`lt` and `ge`/`gt` always remove at least one outer `BIT0` or `BIT1`, without increasing the other arguments.
- Calls introduced by one operation go only to lower, non-returning dependency components, such as `exp → mult → plus → SUC` and `minus → PRE, le`.
- The `NUMERAL` forwarding rules strip `NUMERAL` layers from recursive-call arguments and do not form a reverse cycle.
- `if` is inert because no rewrite rules for it are present; no evaluation assumptions about it are needed.

Thus the recursive dependency components admit a well-founded structural measure, and this applies under arbitrary contexts and rewrite choices, establishing strong—not merely strategy-dependent—normalization without extra axioms or rules.
