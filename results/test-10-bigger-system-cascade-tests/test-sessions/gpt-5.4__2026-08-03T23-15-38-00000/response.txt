Assessment: yes — as written, this TRS looks strongly normalizing, and that can be argued syntactically from the rules alone.

Why: the recursive structure is uniformly decreasing. The self-recursive rules always peel off a `NUMERAL`, `BIT0`, or `BIT1` layer, or recurse on a strictly smaller argument tuple. For example:
- `SUC`, `PRE`, `EVEN`, `ODD`, `eq` recurse on proper subterms.
- `plus`, `mult`, and `minus` recurse on smaller binary components.
- `exp` recurses only on a smaller second argument (the exponent); calls to `mult` are to a lower stratum.
- The only mutual recursion is `le/lt` and `ge/gt`, and each such step removes at least one outer bit-constructor from the compared pair, so a standard lexicographic/size measure decreases.

There is no rule that recreates the same recursive call at the same size, and the extra symbols introduced on the right (`if`, `BIT0`, `BIT1`, `NUMERAL`) do not form a cycle back upward. In particular, `if` has no rewrite rules here, so it cannot generate a loop by itself. So a standard termination proof method such as dependency pairs with a suitable reduction pair, or an LPO/RPO-style simplification order, should suffice without adding any extra axioms or assumptions.
