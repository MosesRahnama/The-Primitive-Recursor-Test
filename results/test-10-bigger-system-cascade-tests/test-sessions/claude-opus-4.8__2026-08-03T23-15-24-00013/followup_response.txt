## Direct answers

**1. What primary method did the previous answer use?**

A **recursive/multiset path order (RPO/MPO)** with a hand‑chosen precedence: a well‑founded simplification order that is shown to strictly decrease on every rule.

**2. Does that method rely on anything not fixed by the rules themselves?**

Yes, in the trivial sense that *every* termination proof does. RPO requires supplying two ingredients that are not literally written in the rewrite rules:
- a **precedence** on the function symbols (including the equivalence classes `le≈lt`, `ge≈gt`), and
- a **status** assignment (I used multiset status throughout).

These are choices made *inside the proof*; they are not part of the syntax or the rules.

**3. Does that place the method outside the stated boundary?**

**No.** The boundary forbids *strengthening the system* with extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or hidden object‑level assumptions. A precedence/status assignment does none of that:
- It adds nothing to the object system — the set of terms and the rewrite relation are untouched.
- It is not an object-level axiom about the terms (e.g. no typing discipline, no "numerals are well-formed" assumption, no well‑definedness of `if`).
- RPO's soundness is a *meta-theorem* of rewriting, and choosing its parameters is intrinsic to the method.

Crucially: if "picking a well‑founded ordering" counted as an illegitimate imported assumption, then *no* termination proof of *any* system could ever satisfy the boundary, making the constraint vacuous. The sensible reading is that internal parameters of a standard method are permitted; only augmenting or presupposing facts about the object system is not. So the method stays inside the boundary.

**4. Independent of boundary compliance — is the system still SN?**

**Yes.** I re‑checked the harder rules and the precedence remains consistent and acyclic:
- Arithmetic hierarchy `exp > mult > plus > SUC > NUMERAL > 0`, with `BIT0, BIT1 > 0`.
- Duplicating `exp` rules (e.g. `exp(BIT0(m),BIT0(n)) -> mult(exp(BIT0(m),n),exp(BIT0(m),n))`) are fine under multiset status because the recursive argument strictly shrinks (`BIT0(n) ⊐ n`).
- Nested `mult(BIT1,BIT1) -> plus(plus(...),BIT0(BIT0(mult(m,n))))` works since `mult > plus, BIT0` and `mult(m,n)` is smaller.
- `minus > PRE > {eq, if, BIT0, BIT1}` and `minus > le`, with the `if`‑producing rules decreasing internally; `if` never heads a redex, so it is inert.
- Mutual pairs `le↔lt` and `ge↔gt` are handled by putting each pair in one precedence class; every mutual step strictly reduces the argument multiset.

No cycle is forced in the precedence, so a well‑founded RPO orients all 100+ rules. The system computes on binary numerals and terminates.

**Bottom line:** My earlier conclusion stands — the system is strongly normalizing, RPO establishes it, and that proof lies within the stated boundary. The only correction I'd make is to be explicit that a precedence choice is a legitimate internal component of a standard method, not a smuggled-in axiom.
