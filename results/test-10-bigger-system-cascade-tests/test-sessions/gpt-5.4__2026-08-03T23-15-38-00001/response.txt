Yes — my assessment is that this TRS is strongly normalizing/terminating on its own, and a standard syntactic proof should suffice.

Why:
- The recursive calls are always on structurally smaller arguments: rules for `SUC`, `PRE`, `plus`, `eq`, `minus`, and the mutual pairs `le/lt` and `ge/gt` all strip a `NUMERAL`, `BIT0`, or `BIT1` layer.
- The higher-level functions form a descending call hierarchy: `exp` calls smaller `exp` and lower-ranked `mult`; `mult` calls smaller `mult` and lower-ranked `plus`; `plus` may call `SUC`, but there is no path back up.
- The only mutual recursion (`le` with `lt`, and `ge` with `gt`) still decreases the bit-structure of the arguments, so it is well-founded.
- `if` has no rewrite rules. That means some terms get stuck as `if(...)`, but that does not create loops; it only limits evaluation.

So, from the raw rewrite rules alone, I do not see a rewrite cycle. A dependency-pair proof with a recursive path ordering / suitable simplification ordering should be enough, without adding any extra axioms or assumptions.
