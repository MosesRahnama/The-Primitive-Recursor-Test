## Assessment: Strong normalization is derivable from the calculus as given.

**Why this system is amenable to a standard termination proof (no extra axioms needed):**

**1. Structural recursion is uniform across all defined symbols.**
Every rule dispatches on the *outer* constructor pattern of its argument(s) — `0`, `BIT0(_)`, `BIT1(_)`, `NUMERAL(_)` — and every recursive call in a right-hand side is made on the immediate subterm exposed by that pattern match (`n` or `m`, never on a reconstructed or enlarged term). This holds for `SUC`, `PRE`, `plus`, `mult`, `exp`, `EVEN`, `ODD`, `eq`, `le`, `lt`, `ge`, `gt`, `minus` uniformly. Hence a well-founded measure based on subterm size (e.g. counting `BIT0`/`BIT1`/`NUMERAL` occurrences) strictly decreases along every recursive call.

**2. The "higher" arithmetic operations only call "lower" ones on subterms.**
- `mult` calls `plus` (and itself) only on the immediate digit-subterms, never on the reconstructed `BIT0(...)`/`BIT1(...)` terms.
- `exp` calls `mult` (and itself) similarly, always with a strictly smaller second argument.
- `minus` calls `PRE` and `le` on subterms.

This gives a clean **precedence hierarchy** `exp > mult > plus, minus > PRE, SUC > (eq, le, lt, ge, gt, EVEN, ODD) > BIT0, BIT1 > NUMERAL, 0`, compatible with a lexicographic/recursive-path-order style argument (LPO/multiset extension), where every right-hand side is dominated by the left-hand side either by precedence-plus-subterm domination or by same-head argument decrease.

**3. The mutually recursive comparison predicates (`le`/`lt`, `ge`/`gt`) still terminate.**
Although `le` calls `lt` and `gt` calls `ge` (and vice versa), every such call is accompanied by a strict decrease in the size of *both* arguments (subterm extraction from `BIT0`/`BIT1`). This is exactly the pattern captured by a polynomial or matrix interpretation assigning strictly positive weight to `BIT0`, `BIT1`, `NUMERAL` and treating the mutually recursive symbols as equal-weight "siblings" — a standard reduction-pair argument in the dependency-pair framework, requiring no additional rules.

**4. The `if` symbol is inert, not a threat to termination.**
`if` occurs only in right-hand sides (`PRE(BIT0(n))`, `minus(BIT1(m),BIT0(n))`) and has **no rewrite rules at all** in this system. It therefore can never be the redex position of any rewrite step; only its arguments can reduce, and those arguments are themselves built from already-terminating subcomputations (`eq`, `le`, `minus`, `PRE`). Its presence can only *remove* possible reduction paths relative to a system where `if` were defined, so it cannot introduce non-termination. No semantic assumption about `if(T,x,y)=x` etc. is needed to prove *strong normalization* — only to prove confluence/completeness, which is a separate question not being asked here.

**Conclusion:**
Given the strictly structural-recursive shape of every rule, a monotone algebra (polynomial or matrix interpretation) or an LPO/RPO-style precedence argument — applied purely to the rules as written — suffices to certify strong normalization. No additional axioms, guards, or semantic postulates about `if`, `T`, `F` are required; the proof is fully internal to the given syntax and rules.
