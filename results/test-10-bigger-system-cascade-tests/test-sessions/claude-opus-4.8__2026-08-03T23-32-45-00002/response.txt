## Assessment

**Yes.** Strong normalization of this system can be established from the rules alone, using nothing beyond the given syntax. A **lexicographic path order (LPO)** with a suitable quasi‑precedence and left‑to‑right lexicographic status orients every rule. LPO is a well‑founded simplification order, so orientability of all rules immediately yields SN, with no added rules, guards, side conditions, or semantic assumptions.

## Precedence used

Take the quasi‑precedence (all listed above are also greater than the constants `0`, `T`, `F`):

- `exp > mult > plus > SUC > {BIT0, BIT1}`
- `minus > PRE > {if, eq, BIT0, BIT1}`, and `minus > {le, lt, if, BIT0, BIT1}`
- every defined symbol `> NUMERAL > 0`
- `le ≈ lt` (equal), `ge ≈ gt` (equal); `EVEN, ODD, eq` above their result constants

This is an acyclic quasi‑order (the two equalities absorb the only genuine mutual recursions).

## Why each family orients

- **NUMERAL‑pushing rules** (`SUC(NUMERAL n)→NUMERAL(SUC n)`, `plus(NUMERAL,NUMERAL)→NUMERAL(plus)`, …): the outer symbol outranks `NUMERAL`, and the LPO subterm case gives `NUMERAL(x) > x`, so the LHS dominates the inner call.
- **Digit recursion** (`SUC(BIT1 n)→BIT0(SUC n)`, `plus(BIT1 m,BIT1 n)→BIT0(SUC(plus m n))`, `eq/le/lt/ge/gt/minus` bit rules): the recursive call has the same (or a `≈`) root and structurally smaller arguments (`BITi(x) > x` as a subterm), so the lexicographic argument comparison decreases.
- **Mutual pairs** `le↔lt`, `ge↔gt`: with equal precedence and lex status, e.g. `le(BIT1 m,BIT0 n) → lt(m,n)` compares tuples `(BIT1 m, BIT0 n) >_lex (m,n)` because `BIT1 m > m`; symmetric for `lt→le`, `ge→gt`, `gt→ge`.
- **Duplicating rules** (`exp(BIT0 m,BIT0 n)→mult(exp(BIT0 m,n),exp(BIT0 m,n))`, the `mult` and `exp` cases feeding `plus`): here LPO's *syntactic* nature is essential. Each duplicated subterm `exp(BIT0 m, n)` is strictly below the LHS (second argument `n < BIT0 n`), and `exp > mult > plus`, so the whole RHS is dominated regardless of how large the computed numeral is. No size/polynomial argument is needed (and indeed none could work, since these rules compute exponentials).
- **Auxiliary calls** (`PRE(BIT0 n)→if(eq(n,0),0,BIT1(PRE n))`, `minus(BIT1 m,BIT0 n)→if(le(n,m),BIT1(minus m n),0)`): the head symbol outranks `if`, `eq`, `le`, `BITi`, and every argument of the RHS is either a proper subterm of the LHS or a same‑root call with smaller arguments.

## Conclusion

All 100+ rules are strictly decreasing under this single LPO. Since LPO is well‑founded and closed under contexts and substitutions, the rewrite relation is contained in it, hence terminating. This is entirely internal to the presented calculus — it relies only on the term structure and the rules themselves, importing no structural axioms or object‑level assumptions.
