**Yes.** Strong normalization can be established syntactically from the given rules alone.

A recursive path ordering (or an equivalent dependency-pair proof) can orient the rules. A suitable precedence includes, for example,

- `exp ≻ mult ≻ plus ≻ SUC`,
- `minus ≻ PRE ≻ eq`,
- `minus ≻ le ≈ lt`,
- `ge ≈ gt`,

with the defined symbols placed above the inert constructors/constants they produce, such as `BIT0`, `BIT1`, `if`, `T`, and `F`. The equivalences handle the mutually recursive `le`/`lt` and `ge`/`gt` pairs.

All recursive calls structurally decrease one or more arguments by removing a `NUMERAL`, `BIT0`, or `BIT1` layer. The duplicating `exp` rules still decrease the exponent in every duplicated recursive call, so duplication does not prevent orientation by recursive path ordering. Nested right-hand-side calls are controlled by the precedence hierarchy.

Thus a well-founded rewrite ordering, stable under substitutions and contexts, can cover all rules. No arithmetic interpretation of the symbols, typing assumption, behavior for `if`, or additional rewrite rules is required.
