## Direct Answers

**1. What primary method did the previous answer use?**

A **lexicographic path order (LPO)** — a syntactic simplification order built from a precedence on the function symbols plus a status (lexicographic) assignment. I exhibited a concrete precedence and checked that every rule satisfies `lhs >_lpo rhs`.

**2. Does that method rely on anything not fixed by the rules themselves?**

Yes, in a limited sense. LPO is not uniquely determined by the rules: it requires **choosing** (a) a precedence on the signature and (b) a status for each symbol. Different choices succeed or fail. So the *particular ordering* is a parameter supplied by the prover, not something the rules pin down. What the rules *do* fix is the signature and the term structure over which the order is defined; the existence of a suitable precedence is then a derivable fact about that syntax.

**3. Does that place the method outside the boundary?**

**No.** The boundary forbids *strengthening the system*: adding rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or hidden object-level assumptions. LPO does none of these. A precedence/status choice is an internal ingredient of a standard termination method — a parameter of the proof search, not an axiom imported into the calculus. The rewrite relation being analyzed is unchanged; no term becomes reducible or irreducible that was not already so. Choosing a precedence is analogous to choosing the induction variable in an inductive proof: it is part of *doing* the proof, not part of *augmenting* the object. So the method stays within the stated boundary.

(If one adopted a stricter reading in which *any* prover-supplied parameter counts as an "assumption not fixed by the rules," then essentially every termination method — RPO, polynomial/matrix interpretations, dependency pairs with orderings, etc. — would be excluded, since all require some chosen ordering or interpretation. Under that reading no nontrivial SN proof would be admissible, which is not a sensible reading of the instruction. The natural reading concerns not strengthening the *rewrite system*, and LPO respects that.)

**4. Independent of the boundary question — is the system still strongly normalizing?**

**Yes.** My assessment is unchanged. Every recursive call strictly decreases the binary/`BITi` structure of its arguments, and every cross-symbol call descends in a well-founded, acyclic precedence; the duplications (e.g. squaring in `exp`, argument reuse in `mult`/`minus`) do not defeat a path order, which compares each copy independently. The concrete LPO I gave orients all rules, so SN holds. The system encodes standard HOL numeral arithmetic, which is terminating, and this is confirmed constructively by the ordering.
