## Direct answers

**1. What primary method did the previous answer use?**
A syntactic **recursive/lexicographic path ordering (RPO/LPO)**: a fixed precedence on the function symbols (with `le≈lt`, `ge≈gt` as equivalence classes), inducing a well-founded, rewrite-compatible reduction order under which every rule strictly decreases.

**2. Does it rely on structure not fixed by the rules themselves?**
Yes, in the trivial sense that the *witness* is chosen by the prover: the precedence ordering (`exp>mult>plus>SUC>{BIT0,BIT1}`, `minus>PRE>{if,eq,le,lt}`, every symbol `>NUMERAL`), the equivalence classes for the mutually recursive comparison symbols, and the term-status (lexicographic/multiset) are not literally written among the rules. They are parameters of the proof search.

**3. Does that put the method outside the stated boundary?**
**No.** The boundary forbids *strengthening the system* — extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or object-level/hidden assumptions. A precedence is none of those. It is an internal proof witness whose validity is *checked against the given rules*: each rule's strict decrease is a purely syntactic fact about the LHS and RHS as written. Nothing is imported into the calculus; the system rewrites identically with or without the ordering. Since essentially every termination proof (even a simple polynomial measure) selects such a witness, treating "choosing an ordering" as a boundary violation would forbid all termination proofs — which is clearly not the intent of "you may use any standard proof method." So the precedence is *derivable/verifiable from the syntax*, not an assumption beyond it.

The one honest caveat: the argument's force depends on the precedence being **globally consistent** (acyclic) across all rules simultaneously. I re-checked this — the required constraints form a cycle-free DAG (`exp>mult>plus>SUC>BIT0/BIT1`, `minus>PRE>if/eq`, `minus>le/lt`, all `>NUMERAL`), with no conflicting demands — so a single valid RPO instance exists. That consistency is itself a fact about the rules, not an added assumption.

**4. Independent of boundary compliance — is the system still strongly normalizing?**
**Yes.** The structural invariant is robust: in every rule, recursive occurrences of a defined symbol take proper subterms of the LHS arguments (the `m`,`n` extracted from `BIT0/BIT1/NUMERAL`), and any head change moves strictly downward in the precedence. The duplicating `exp`/`mult` rules only duplicate calls with a strictly smaller exponent/argument, so no infinite chain arises. I re-verified the hard cases (`mult(BIT1,BIT1)`, the `exp(_,BIT0)` squaring rules, the `le/lt` and `ge/gt` mutual recursions, and the `minus`/`PRE` cases feeding `if`), and each is strictly decreasing. My conclusion is unchanged: the system is strongly normalizing, and this is establishable from the presented calculus alone.
