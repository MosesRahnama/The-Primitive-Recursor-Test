**Assessment:** Yes – the system is strongly normalising.

**Reasoning:**  
All rewrite rules define the operations by *structural* (primitive) recursion on one or both arguments. For every rule one can assign a well‑founded measure (e.g. the total size of the arguments or a lexicographic pair of argument sizes) that strictly decreases when the rule is applied. Consequently no infinite rewrite sequence can be built.

* The arithmetic operators (SUC, PRE, plus, mult, exp, minus) as well as the predicates (EVEN, ODD, eq, le, lt, ge, gt) are each defined by cases that replace a constructor (BIT0, BIT1, NUMERAL, 0) by a term built from a proper sub‑term of the original argument(s).  
* The only “non‑constructor’’ that appears on right‑hand sides is the ternary symbol **if**, but no rewrite rule for **if** is given, so it never reduces and therefore cannot be the source of a loop. (Even if the usual equations  
`if(T,x,y) → x` and `if(F,x,y) → y` were added, they would still preserve termination because they replace the term by one of the branches without increasing any measure.)  
* The base‐case rules (e.g. `SUC(0) → BIT1(0)`, `BIT0(0) → 0`) are non‑recursive; they can be applied only a finite number of times because their left‑hand sides contain no variables.

A standard termination proof technique—such as a polynomial interpretation or a recursive path order with a precedence giving all defined functions higher priority than the constructors—witnesses this decrease. No extra axioms or hidden assumptions are needed; the proof follows solely from the given syntax and rewrite rules. Hence strong normalisation holds.
