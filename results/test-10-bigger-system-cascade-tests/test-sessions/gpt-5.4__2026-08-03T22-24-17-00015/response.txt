Assessment: **yes** — under ordinary first-order TRS semantics, this system looks **strongly normalizing / terminating** from the rules alone.

Why:
- The recursion is syntactic and descending. In every recursive cluster, calls are made on **proper subterms / shorter bit-structure**:
  - `SUC`, `PRE`, `EVEN`, `ODD`, `eq`, `le`, `lt`, `ge`, `gt`, `minus` peel off `NUMERAL`, `BIT0`, or `BIT1`.
  - `plus` recurses on `m,n` from `BIT*(m), BIT*(n)`.
  - `mult` recurses on `mult(m,n)` from `mult(BIT*(m),BIT*(n))`.
  - `exp` recurses by strictly decreasing the **exponent** argument (`... , BIT0(n)` or `... , BIT1(n)` to `... , n`).
- The only mutual cycles, `le ↔ lt` and `ge ↔ gt`, still decrease the leading bit constructors, so they do not form a genuine loop.
- `if` has **no rewrite rules**, so it can only create stuck terms, not infinite rewriting.
- No rule recreates the same recursive call at the same structural size; duplication happens (`exp`, `mult`) but only on strictly smaller recursive arguments.

So a standard termination method such as **dependency pairs** or a suitable **recursive path ordering / simplification order** should be enough, with no extra axioms or hidden assumptions.  
Caveat: this says termination/SN, **not** confluence or completeness; the missing `if` rules mean some terms just get stuck.
