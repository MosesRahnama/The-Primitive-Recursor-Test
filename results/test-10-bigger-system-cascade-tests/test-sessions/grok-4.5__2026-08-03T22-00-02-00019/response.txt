The given TRS is strongly normalizing. This follows by a standard multiset/path order (or equivalently dependency-pairs) argument that uses only the explicit function symbols and rewrite rules:

- Assign a precedence \(\mathrm{exp} > \mathrm{mult} > \mathrm{plus} > \mathrm{SUC} > \mathrm{PRE} > \mathrm{minus} > \dots\) (all defined symbols) strictly above every constructor (\(0\), \(\mathrm{BIT0}\), \(\mathrm{BIT1}\), \(\mathrm{NUMERAL}\), \(\mathrm{if}\), \(\mathrm{T}\), \(\mathrm{F}\), \ldots).
- Every right-hand side is then strictly smaller than its left-hand side in the induced lexicographic path order: recursive calls occur exclusively on proper subterms (the bit-vectors \(m,n\)), while the outer constructors/operations introduced on the right are smaller in the precedence.
- The mutually recursive comparison symbols (\(\mathrm{le}/\mathrm{lt}\), \(\mathrm{ge}/\mathrm{gt}\)) likewise decrease on the same subterm measure, so they generate no infinite chains.
- Symbols lacking rules (\(\mathrm{if}\), \(\mathrm{T}\), \(\mathrm{F}\), \ldots) are treated as free constructors and do not affect the well-founded order.

Consequently every rewrite sequence is finite. No additional rewrite rules, side-conditions or semantic axioms are required.
