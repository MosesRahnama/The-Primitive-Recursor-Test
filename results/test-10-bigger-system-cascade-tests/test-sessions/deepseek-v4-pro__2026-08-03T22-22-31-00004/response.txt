Strong normalization can be established from the given system alone. All recursive definitions (SUC, PRE, plus, mult, exp, minus, eq, le, lt, ge, gt, EVEN, ODD) perform structural recursion on the binary number constructors 0, BIT0, BIT1, with well-founded decreases in every recursive call. The mutual recursions (e.g., le/lt) also reduce the arguments strictly. The NUMERAL wrapper introduces no infinite reductions: it either is stripped and re-wrapped (pushing evaluation outward) or collapses to 0, and once a term is wrapped in NUMERAL it either stays wrapped as a normal form or reduces to 0. The special rules BIT0(0) → 0 and the presence of the uninterpreted if-symbol do not create loops, because BIT0 only collapses a redex to a smaller term and if acts as a constructor normal form. No rule increases the size of recursive arguments in a way that could lead to unbounded growth, and no cyclic reduction sequences are possible. Thus the calculus is strongly normalizing.
