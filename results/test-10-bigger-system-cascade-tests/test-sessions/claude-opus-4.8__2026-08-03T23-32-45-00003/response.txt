## Assessment

**Yes — strong normalization can be established from the presented system alone, with no added axioms or assumptions.** A lexicographic path order (LPO), a completely standard syntactic method, orients every rule.

## Reasoning

The signature splits naturally into constructors (`0`, `BIT0`, `BIT1`, `NUMERAL`, `if`, `T`, `F`) and defined symbols (`SUC`, `PRE`, `plus`, `mult`, `exp`, `EVEN`, `ODD`, `eq`, `le`, `lt`, `ge`, `gt`, `minus`). In *every* rule, the recursive occurrences of a defined symbol on the right are applied to **strict subterms** of the left-hand arguments (`m`, `n` under a stripped `BIT0`/`BIT1`/`NUMERAL`), while any newly introduced defined symbols are lower in a fixed precedence. This is exactly the situation an LPO handles.

Take the precedence (with left‑to‑right lexicographic status):

- `exp > mult > plus > SUC`, and `SUC > BIT0, BIT1, NUMERAL`
- `mult, plus > BIT0`
- `PRE > if, eq, BIT0, BIT1, NUMERAL`
- `minus > PRE, if, le, BIT0, BIT1, NUMERAL`
- `SUC, PRE, plus, mult, exp, minus > NUMERAL`; `NUMERAL, BIT0 > 0`
- `le ≈ lt` and `ge ≈ gt` (same precedence class)

Some representative checks:

- **`SUC(NUMERAL(n)) → NUMERAL(SUC(n))`**: `SUC > NUMERAL`, and `SUC(NUMERAL(n)) > SUC(n)` since `NUMERAL(n) > n`. The same pattern handles all `plus/mult/exp/PRE/minus` versions of the "push through `NUMERAL`" rules.
- **`mult(BIT1(m),BIT1(n)) → plus(plus(BIT1(m),BIT0(n)),BIT0(BIT0(mult(m,n))))`**: `mult > plus, BIT0`; the deep `mult(m,n)` is smaller by lexicographic comparison (`BIT1(m) > m`); the reappearing `BIT1(m)`, `BIT0(n)` are subterms.
- **`exp(BIT1(m),BIT1(n)) → mult(mult(BIT1(m),exp(BIT1(m),n)),exp(BIT1(m),n))`**: `exp > mult`, and `exp(BIT1(m),BIT1(n)) > exp(BIT1(m),n)` lexicographically (`BIT1(n) > n`) — the squaring/duplication is irrelevant to a path order.
- **The mutually-recursive comparisons** `le(BIT1(m),BIT0(n)) → lt(m,n)` and `lt(BIT0(m),BIT1(n)) → le(m,n)`: these would break a *strict* precedence, but putting `le ≈ lt` (and `ge ≈ gt`) in one equivalence class resolves it — with equal precedence and lexicographic status the arguments strictly decrease (`BIT1(m) > m`, `BIT0(m) > m`), and the second right-hand argument is a subterm of the left. All `eq/le/lt/ge/gt` rules likewise decrease their arguments to strict subterms.
- **`PRE(BIT0(n)) → if(eq(n,0),0,BIT1(PRE(n)))`** and **`minus(BIT1(m),BIT0(n)) → if(le(n,m),BIT1(minus(m,n)),0)`**: `if`, `eq`, `le` are below `PRE`/`minus`, and the recursive calls are on strict subterms.

The precedence is acyclic (the `le≈lt`, `ge≈gt` classes are the only equivalences, and they have no other constraints among themselves), so it defines a legitimate LPO. Since LPO is a simplification order derived purely from the signature and the rewrite rules, orienting all rules with it gives strong normalization **using only the given syntax** — no extra rules, guards, side conditions, or imported axioms are needed.

**Conclusion:** The system is strongly normalizing, and this is provable from the calculus alone via a standard lexicographic path order.
