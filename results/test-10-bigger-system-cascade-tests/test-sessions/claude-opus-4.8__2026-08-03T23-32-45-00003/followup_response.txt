## Direct answers

**1. Primary method used.**
A lexicographic path order (LPO): I fixed a precedence and lexicographic status on the signature and checked that every rule satisfies `l >_lpo r`.

**2. Does it rely on anything not fixed by the rules themselves?**
Yes, in one specific sense. LPO is parameterized by a *chosen* precedence and status (e.g. `exp > mult > plus > SUC`, `le ≈ lt`, `ge ≈ gt`, etc.). That choice is a witness supplied by the prover; it is not literally inscribed in the rewrite rules. Every termination method has such a witness — a precedence, a polynomial interpretation, a matrix, a weight — and none of these is "read off" the rules automatically.

**3. Does that put the method outside the stated boundary?**
No. The boundary forbids *strengthening the system*: no extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or hidden object-level assumptions (e.g. assuming facts about `BIT0`/`NUMERAL` as if they were axioms). A precedence is none of these. It is internal machinery of a standard method, and its validity is *checked entirely against the given rules* — the soundness statement "LPO-orientation ⇒ SN" is a theorem, not an imported assumption, and the particular precedence is a certificate whose correctness is verifiable from the syntax alone. Nothing about the object-level meaning of the terms is presupposed.

The only way to call this a boundary violation is to read "beyond what is derivable from the rules" so strictly that *any* chosen ordering witness is forbidden — but that reading makes the question vacuously "no" for every termination technique, which is not a sensible construal of "you may use any standard proof method."

**4. Is the system still strongly normalizing (independent of the boundary question)?**
Yes. Every rule is structurally recursive: recursive calls of a defined symbol occur on strict subterms of the arguments (`m`, `n` under stripped `BIT0`/`BIT1`/`NUMERAL`), and the call graph across defined symbols (`exp→mult→plus→SUC`, `minus→PRE`, `PRE→eq`, `minus→le`, `le↔lt`, `ge↔gt`) has no cycle that increases argument size. The duplication in the `mult`/`exp` right-hand sides is irrelevant to a path order. LPO orients all rules, so termination holds.

## Bottom line
I stand by the earlier conclusion. The clarification I'd add: LPO's precedence is a chosen witness, so if one insists that *no* prover-supplied ordering is admissible, then no method at all qualifies. Under the natural reading of the boundary (no added rules/guards/assumptions about the terms), LPO stays inside it, and the system is strongly normalizing.
