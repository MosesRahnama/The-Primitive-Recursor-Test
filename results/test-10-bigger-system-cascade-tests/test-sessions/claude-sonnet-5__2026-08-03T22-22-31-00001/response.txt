### Reasoning

1. **Structure of the recursion.** Every defined symbol (`SUC, PRE, plus, mult, exp, EVEN, ODD, eq, le, lt, ge, gt, minus`) is defined by case analysis on the *constructor* skeleton `0 / BIT0(_) / BIT1(_) / NUMERAL(_)`, and every recursive call in the right-hand side is applied to a **proper subterm** of the matched argument(s). E.g.:
   - `SUC(BIT1(n)) -> BIT0(SUC(n))`, `PRE(NUMERAL(n)) -> NUMERAL(PRE(n))`
   - `plus(BIT1(m),BIT1(n)) -> BIT0(SUC(plus(m,n)))`

   In each case the "hard" recursive argument (the numeral being decomposed) strictly shrinks in size, even though the surrounding term may temporarily grow (e.g. duplicated `exp` calls, or `mult` wrapping `plus`). This is exactly the shape that a **well‑founded structural/size measure** handles: assign each numeral term a natural‑number weight that strictly decreases along the "peeled" argument, and make the interpretations of `plus`, `mult`, `SUC`, `PRE`, `eq`, `le`, etc. monotone enough (e.g. via a polynomial or simple multiset/lexicographic path order with a suitable precedence: `exp > mult > plus > SUC/PRE > BIT0/BIT1/NUMERAL`) to dominate the right-hand sides.

Hence a stratified ordering (precedence-based path order, or nested polynomial interpretation) suffices; this is a standard technique requiring no assumption beyond reading off the rule shapes.

(It would matter for confluence/computational completeness, not for strong normalization.)

4. **This falls within admissible methods.** Polynomial interpretations, RPO/KBO with argument filtering, and dependency-pair based decompositions are standard termination proof techniques that operate purely on the given signature and rules; they do not add rewrite rules, side conditions, or external axioms — they only exhibit a well-founded order compatible with the rules, which is licensed by ordinary rewriting theory.
