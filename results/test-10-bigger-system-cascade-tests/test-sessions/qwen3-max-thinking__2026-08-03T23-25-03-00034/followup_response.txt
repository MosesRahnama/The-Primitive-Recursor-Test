1. **Primary method**: Recursive Path Order (RPO) / Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with manually selected precedence and status, leveraging the subterm property and structural recursion patterns visible in the rules.

2. **Reliance on non-derivable choices**: **Yes**. The proof requires *explicit human choices* not fixed or derivable from the rules:  
   - Precedence ordering among defined symbols (e.g., `exp > mult`, `SUC > BIT0`, treatment of `le`/`lt` mutual recursion)  
   - Status assignments (lexicographic vs. multiset) per symbol  
   These choices are *not* logically entailed by the syntax or rules. The rules alone do not determine which symbol must precede another; multiple incompatible precedences are syntactically possible. Selecting a working precedence constitutes an *external proof artifact*, not a property derivable from the calculus.

3. **Boundary violation**: **Yes**. The instruction forbids importing "assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given syntax and rewrite rules." While RPO itself is a permitted *method*, the *specific precedence/status choices* required to orient the rules are **not derivable** from the system. They are externally supplied assumptions about symbol ordering. This violates the boundary: termination cannot be *established from the calculus alone* without injecting this non-derivable structural choice. (Contrast: if all rules were *strictly subterm-decreasing* with no symbol-order dependencies—e.g., every RHS recursive call occurred on a direct subterm with no root-symbol comparisons—termination would be trivially derivable. Here, rules like `SUC(BIT1(n)) → BIT0(SUC(n))` require `SUC > BIT0` to orient; this inequality is not derivable from rules/syntax.)

4. **Strong normalization status**: **Yes, the system is strongly normalizing**. All recursive calls occur on *syntactic subterms* of original arguments (e.g., `n` in `BIT0(n)`, `m,n` in `BIT1(m),BIT0(n)`). No rule creates cycles or non-subterm recursion. Duplication (e.g., in `exp`, `mult`) occurs only on structurally smaller subterms. Mutual recursion (`le`/`lt`, `ge`/`gt`) strictly reduces argument size. This structural property—verifiable by direct rule inspection—is sufficient for termination. However, *proving* it formally requires an external ordering choice, placing the *proof* outside the strict boundary, even though the *property* holds.
