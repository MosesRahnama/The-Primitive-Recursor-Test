**Verdict: yes.** Termination can be established from the displayed rewrite rules alone. A direct polynomial interpretation over the natural numbers proves that every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded measure, including under arbitrary term contexts.

Interpret symbols over \(\mathbb N\) as follows:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\llbracket 0 \rrbracket &= 0,\\
\llbracket s(t) \rrbracket &= \llbracket t\rrbracket+1,\\
\llbracket plus(u,v)\rrbracket &= \llbracket u\rrbracket+2\llbracket v\rrbracket+1,\\
\llbracket times(u,v)\rrbracket
 &=2\llbracket u\rrbracket\llbracket v\rrbracket
   +2\llbracket u\rrbracket+\llbracket v\rrbracket+1,\\
\llbracket p(u)\rrbracket &=2\llbracket u\rrbracket+1.
\end{aligned}
\]

All of these interpretations are strictly monotone in each argument position. Therefore, if a rule strictly decreases this interpretation at its root, it also strictly decreases when applied inside any context.

Let \(X=\llbracket x\rrbracket\) and \(Y=\llbracket y\rrbracket\).

### `plus` rules

\[
plus(x,0)\to x
\]

\[
\llbracket plus(x,0)\rrbracket=X+1>X.
\]

\[
plus(x,s(y))\to s(plus(x,y))
\]

\[
\llbracket plus(x,s(y))\rrbracket
=X+2(Y+1)+1=X+2Y+3,
\]

while

\[
\llbracket s(plus(x,y))\rrbracket
=(X+2Y+1)+1=X+2Y+2.
\]

Hence the left-hand side is strictly larger.

### `times` rules

\[
times(0,y)\to 0
\]

\[
\llbracket times(0,y)\rrbracket=Y+1>0.
\]

\[
times(x,0)\to 0
\]

\[
\llbracket times(x,0)\rrbracket=2X+1>0.
\]

\[
times(s(x),y)\to plus(times(x,y),y)
\]

The left-hand side has value

\[
\begin{aligned}
\llbracket times(s(x),y)\rrbracket
&=2(X+1)Y+2(X+1)+Y+1\\
&=2XY+2X+3Y+3.
\end{aligned}
\]

The right-hand side has value

\[
\begin{aligned}
\llbracket plus(times(x,y),y)\rrbracket
&=(2XY+2X+Y+1)+2Y+1\\
&=2XY+2X+3Y+2.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus the left-hand side exceeds the right-hand side by \(1\).

### `p` rules

\[
p(s(s(x)))\to s(p(s(x)))
\]

\[
\llbracket p(s(s(x)))\rrbracket
=2(X+2)+1=2X+5,
\]

whereas

\[
\llbracket s(p(s(x)))\rrbracket
=(2(X+1)+1)+1=2X+4.
\]

So this rule strictly decreases.

Finally,

\[
p(s(0))\to 0
\]

has

\[
\llbracket p(s(0))\rrbracket=2\cdot1+1=3>0.
\]

Since every rule strictly decreases a natural-number interpretation, and strict descent on \(\mathbb N\) is well-founded, there can be no infinite rewrite sequence.

Therefore the presented system is terminating, with no need for additional axioms, equations, assumptions about the intended arithmetic meaning of the symbols, or added rewrite rules.
