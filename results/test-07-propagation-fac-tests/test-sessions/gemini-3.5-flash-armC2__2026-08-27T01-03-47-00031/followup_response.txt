I chose the **Dependency Pair (DP) Framework** combined with **Polynomial Interpretations** for several key reasons, primarily because standard, monolithic termination techniques fail on this specific system. 

Here is the detailed breakdown of why this method was chosen:

### 1. Simple Path Orders (like LPO or RPO) Fail Due to a Precedence Conflict
If we try to use a standard Lexicographic Path Order (LPO), we run into a contradiction in the operator precedence ($\succ$):
* To orient the rule for `p`: 
  $$p(s(s(x))) \to s(p(s(x)))$$
  the root symbol of the left-hand side must be greater than the right-hand side, requiring **$p \succ s$**.
* To orient the rule for `fac`: 
  $$fac(s(x)) \to times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))$$
  we must show that the arguments of the recursive call decrease. This requires showing $s(x) \succ p(s(x))$, which is only possible if **$s \succ p$**.

Because we cannot have both $p \succ s$ and $s \succ p$, **no single LPO can prove termination for this system.**

### 2. The Recursion is Non-Structural
In the rule:
$$fac(s(x)) \to times(fac(\underline{p(s(x))}), s(x))$$
the recursive call is made on $p(s(x))$, which is not a syntactic subterm of the input $s(x)$. To prove termination, we must *evaluate* $p(s(x))$ to show it is semantically "smaller" than $s(x)$. 

Standard structural induction or simple syntactic orderings cannot easily capture this "decrease-by-evaluation" property.

### 3. Modularity of the DP Framework
The DP framework is the state-of-the-art methodology because it decomposes the global termination problem into localized sub-problems (Strongly Connected Components, or SCCs):
* **Isolation of Concerns:** It separates the proof for `plus`, `times`, `p`, and `fac` into four independent loops. 
* **Elimination of Irrelevant Rules:** When proving that the `fac` loop terminates, we only need to consider the "usable rules" (the rules for `p`). We can completely ignore the rules for `times` and `plus` during this step, which vastly simplifies the search for a valid mathematical ordering.

### 4. Polynomial Interpretations Excel at "Evaluation"
Once isolated by the DP framework, the loop for `fac` simplifies to showing that $FAC(s(x)) \to FAC(p(s(x)))$ decreases, given the behavior of $p$. 

Polynomial interpretations are perfect here because they allow us to map the symbols to actual algebraic operations:
* By mapping $s$ to $+1$ and $p$ to $-1$, the rule for $p$ becomes a trivial algebraic identity ($x + 1 = x + 1$), while the recursive call of $fac$ is shown to strictly decrease:
  $$[FAC(s(x))] = x + 1 \quad > \quad [FAC(p(s(x)))] = x$$

### Summary
The DP framework was chosen because it **bypasses the precedence conflict** of syntactic orders and **modularizes the system**, allowing a simple polynomial interpretation to easily prove that `p` acts as a decrement function for `fac`.
