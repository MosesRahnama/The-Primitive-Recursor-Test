### Why RPO
The system is a pure first-order TRS on free terms (no binders, no AC symbols, no conditions, no built-in arithmetic). For such systems the classical simplification orders—recursive path order (RPO) or its lexicographic variant (LPO)—are the most direct decision procedures that stay strictly inside the given syntax: they require only a precedence on the finite signature and then compare terms by a purely syntactic, well-founded relation.  

I chose RPO (multiset status) for three concrete reasons visible from the rules alone:

- The defined symbols line up in a natural strict hierarchy  
  \(\texttt{times} > \texttt{plus} > \texttt{p} > \texttt{s} > 0\)  
  that makes every left-hand side larger than its right-hand side by a single root-symbol comparison plus the subterm property. No auxiliary interpretations, polynomial weights, or dependency-pair graphs are required.

- All seven rules are oriented uniformly by that one precedence; there is no need to case-split, to add extra rewrite rules, or to impose guards/side-conditions that would strengthen the original system.

- RPO is known to be a simplification order, hence well-founded on the set of finite ground terms generated by the signature. Once the inequalities are verified (as done in the previous answer), termination follows at once, with no appeal to external semantic domains or object-level axioms.

Any other standard method that also works from the rules alone (e.g., a simple polynomial interpretation or Knuth-Bendix order) would have been admissible; RPO is simply the shortest syntactic certificate that covers every rule without remainder.
