### Verdict
Yes, termination is established from the rules alone.

### Termination Argument
We orient the TRS by the recursive path order (RPO) induced by the precedence
\[
\texttt{times} > \texttt{plus} > \texttt{p} > \texttt{s} > 0
\]
(with the usual multiset status on arguments). RPO is a well-founded simplification order on the set of finite terms; it is therefore enough to verify that every left-hand side is strictly larger than the corresponding right-hand side. We check the seven rules in turn.

- \(\texttt{plus}(x,0) > x\)  
  Immediate (every term is strictly larger than each of its proper subterms).

- \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y)) > \texttt{s}(\texttt{plus}(x,y))\)  
  Root symbol \(\texttt{plus} > \texttt{s}\), so it is enough that the left-hand side exceeds every immediate subterm of the right-hand side, i.e. \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y)) > \texttt{plus}(x,y)\). The latter holds by the subterm property on the second arguments (\(\texttt{s}(y) > y\)) together with identical first arguments.

- \(\texttt{times}(0,y) > 0\) and \(\texttt{times}(x,0) > 0\)  
  Immediate (root \(\texttt{times}\) is larger than the constant \(0\)).

- \(\texttt{times}(\texttt{s}(x),y) > \texttt{plus}(\texttt{times}(x,y),y)\)  
  Root \(\texttt{times} > \texttt{plus}\), so it is enough that the left-hand side exceeds both immediate subterms of the right-hand side.  
  - \(\texttt{times}(\texttt{s}(x),y) > \texttt{times}(x,y)\) holds by the subterm property on the first arguments.  
  - \(\texttt{times}(\texttt{s}(x),y) > y\) holds because \(y\) is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.

- \(\texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(\texttt{s}(x))) > \texttt{s}(\texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(x)))\)  
  Root \(\texttt{p} > \texttt{s}\), so it is enough that the left-hand side exceeds the single immediate subterm of the right-hand side, i.e. \(\texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(\texttt{s}(x))) > \texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(x))\). The latter holds by the subterm property on the arguments (\(\texttt{s}(\texttt{s}(x)) > \texttt{s}(x)\)).

- \(\texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(0)) > 0\)  
  Immediate (root \(\texttt{p}\) is larger than the constant \(0\)).

All rules are therefore strictly decreasing in RPO. Consequently every rewrite sequence is finite.

(The same orientation is obtained with the lexicographic path order under any left-to-right or right-to-left lexicographic status; the multiset variant above is merely the most concise to write down.)
