## Yes, the method complies — and here's a careful audit of why

I'll go through the prompt's requirements one by one and check whether LPO/RPO actually meets them.

### Requirement 1: "from the presented rules alone"

LPO's input is just the **signature** (the function symbols and their arities, fixed by the rules) plus a **precedence** and **status** I choose as proof parameters. I never reference any equation between the function symbols (e.g., commutativity of `+`, idempotence of something) — those aren't in the rules, and LPO doesn't ask for them.

### Requirement 2: "without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions"

This is where I want to be precise. LPO's soundness rests on **Kruskal's tree theorem** — that the homeomorphic embedding is a well-quasi-order on finite trees. Is Kruskal's theorem an "imported structural axiom"?

I'd say **no, and here's the distinction that matters**:

| Thing LPO needs | Is it an import? |
|---|---|
| The signature `{plus/2, times/2, p/1, fac/1, s/1, 0/0}` | No — given by the rules. |
| The fact that terms are finite trees | No — that's the *definition* of "term." |
| Kruskal's tree theorem | No — it's a *theorem* about those trees, derivable from first-order set theory / arithmetic, not an *axiom* imported about the object domain. |
| The "meaning" of 0 as a natural number | Not used. |
| The well-foundedness of ℕ | Not used. |
| Any property of `+`, `×`, `p`, `fac` beyond what the rules rewrite | Not used. |

Compare this with the method I *rejected* — polynomial interpretation over ℕ. That one *would* smuggle in the well-foundedness of ℕ and the monotonicity of arithmetic operations. LPO doesn't.

### Requirement 3: "no extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or hidden assumptions"

- **No extra rewrite rules**: I added zero rules to the system. The eight rules in the prompt are the entire object of study.
- **No guards**: I didn't gate any step on a "when this term is in normal form" condition.
- **No side conditions**: I didn't attach preconditions like "provided the second argument of `plus` is not `0`."
- **No hidden assumptions**: The precedence and status are *parameters* I am free to pick, and I exhibit them explicitly. A hidden assumption would be something like "assume `p` is monotone" or "assume `s(x) ≠ 0`" — I asserted nothing of the kind. LPO's check is purely **syntactic**: does one term's root symbol dominate the other's in the precedence, and do the children relate in the chosen status? That's a mechanical inspection of the terms' tree shapes, not a semantic claim about the functions.

### A potential objection I considered

One could argue: "Kruskal's tree theorem is itself a non-trivial commitment." Fair — but the prompt permits **any standard proof method**, and LPO is textbook-standard in rewrite theory (Baader & Nipkow, *Term Rewriting and All That*, is the canonical reference). The prompt isn't asking for a proof from Peano arithmetic with no metatheory; it's asking that the proof not *strengthen the system* or *import object-level content*. LPO does neither.

### Verdict

LPO satisfies all three constraints. If you want something even more elementary, the same proof can be done with a hand-rolled **nested measure** (e.g., a lexicographic pair `(arg-decrease-flag, structural-size)`), but that's just LPO spelled out with extra notation. The substantive content — that the recursive calls live on strictly smaller arguments — is identical, and the compliance reasoning is the same.
