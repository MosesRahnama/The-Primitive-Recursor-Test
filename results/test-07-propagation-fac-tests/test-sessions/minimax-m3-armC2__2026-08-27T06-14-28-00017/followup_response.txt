## Why LPO/RPO

I picked the **lexicographic path order** (LPO) — or its multiset variant RPO — for three reasons that line up with the prompt's constraints.

### 1. It stays purely syntactic on the term algebra

The prompt explicitly forbids importing object-level axioms. Methods that "obviously" work for this system — like appealing to the well-foundedness of ℕ, or using polynomial/matrix interpretations interpreted *over the natural numbers* — smuggle in the very structural assumptions the question rules out (ℕ is a well-order, + and × are monotone, etc.). LPO's termination guarantee is grounded in **Kruskal's theorem** applied to the term algebra generated by the signature. It needs no external model.

### 2. It matches the system's natural recursive shape

The rules are written in a textbook form where each function recurses on a *specific argument*:

- `plus` decreases in its 2nd argument
- `times` decreases in its 1st argument  
- `p` decreases in its sole argument
- `fac` decreases in its sole argument (via the call to `p`)

This is exactly what LPO's **status** (lexicographic vs. multiset on the argument tuple) is designed to express. The nesting of calls (`fac → times → plus`) is handled by an appropriate **precedence** (`fac > times > plus > p > s > 0`):

- `times(s(x), y) → plus(times(x, y), y)`: `times > plus` in precedence, and the recursive `times(x, y)` decreases in its 1st argument (`x < s(x)`). LPO accepts this.
- `fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`: `fac > times` in precedence, and the recursive `fac(p(s(x)))` decreases in its argument (`p(s(x)) < s(x)`, which LPO verifies via `p > s` and `x < s(x)`).

### 3. It's the right tool for the difficulty

The system isn't pathological — no mutual recursion, no infinitely branching rules, no nested recursion that needs dependency pairs to disentangle. Pulling out the **dependency pair framework**, **semantic labeling**, or **higher-order recursion schemes** would be overkill. LPO is the lightest-weight technique that yields a clean, auditable proof.

### What I deliberately did *not* do

- **No appeal to ℕ-well-foundedness.** Even though the rules "are clearly" computing on natural numbers, that intuition is a theorem here, not a premise.
- **No interpretation method.** A polynomial interpretation with `0 ↦ 0, s(x) ↦ x+1, plus(x,y) ↦ x+y, times(x,y) ↦ x·y, p(x) ↦ x, fac(x) ↦ x!` would work, but it leans on the arithmetic of ℕ, which is exactly the kind of object-level assumption the prompt disallows.
- **No "obvious by inspection" hand-wave.** The overlapping `times(s(x), 0)` could in principle cause confluence issues; I noted that both reducts terminate rather than asserting it.

In short: LPO is the most conservative method that still produces a rigorous, self-contained proof of termination from the rules as given.
