**Verdict:** Yes. Termination is established from the two presented rewrite rules alone. No algebraic properties of `Z` or `S`, no induction principle, and no extra rules are needed.

Use a lexicographic path ordering (LPO), a standard well-founded term ordering.

Choose a precedence with

\[
F \succ G
\]

and give `F` lexicographic status. The relative precedence of `S` and `Z` is irrelevant here.

Let

\[
L = F(x,y,S(n)).
\]

For the second rule,

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)),
\]

we show \(L\) is greater than its right-hand side in LPO.

1. \(L > y\), because \(y\) is a proper subterm of \(L\).

2. \(L > F(x,y,n)\), since both terms have root symbol `F`, their first two arguments are identical, and their third arguments satisfy

   \[
   S(n) > n
   \]

   by the strict subterm property of LPO. Hence, by lexicographic comparison at the `F` root,

   \[
   F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n).
   \]

3. Since \(F \succ G\), and \(L\) is greater than each argument of

   \[
   G(y,F(x,y,n)),
   \]

   LPO gives

   \[
   F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n)).
   \]

Thus the second rewrite rule strictly decreases under LPO.

For the first rule,

\[
F(x,y,Z) \to x,
\]

the right-hand side \(x\) is a proper subterm of the left-hand side. Therefore

\[
F(x,y,Z) > x
\]

by the subterm property of LPO.

Both rules strictly decrease in a well-founded ordering. Consequently, the rewrite system is terminating.

This argument depends only on the displayed first-order syntax and rewrite rules. In particular, it does not assume that `Z` and `S` are natural-number constructors, that every term is either `Z` or `S(n)`, or any external/object-level axiom about them.
