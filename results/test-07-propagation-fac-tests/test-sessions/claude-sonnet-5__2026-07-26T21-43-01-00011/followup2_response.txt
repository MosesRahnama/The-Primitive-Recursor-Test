**1. Dependency pairs / dependency graph / usable rules — these are proof-search machinery, not new axioms.**

**2. The one place that needed real scrutiny: the interpretation `β` used for {D6}.**

I defined
`β(0)=0, β(s(t))=β(t)+1, β(p(t))=max(β(t)-1,0)`,
and left `β` **undefined on `plus`, `times`, `fac`**, treating variable positions (like `x` in `FAC(s(x)) -> FAC(p(s(x))))`) as free natural-number parameters rather than unfolding what `x` might structurally be.

This is legitimate *only* because of how reduction pairs work: an algebra used to compare two sides of a rule/DP needs to be defined (and monotone) only for the function symbols that actually occur in that comparison, with variables ranging freely over the carrier (here, ℕ). Since `x` in D6 never gets decomposed by `β` — it's abstracted immediately to a numeric parameter — I never need to know what `β` does to `fac`, `times`, or `plus`-headed instantiations of `x`. This is standard in the DP/reduction-pair framework (it's exactly what makes "usable rules" a sound restriction), **not** an extra assumption smuggled in about the semantics of `p`. The equalities I verified for rules 6 and 7 (the usable rules) are the *only* facts about `p` actually used, and both are checked directly against the given rules, term by term, with no appeal to "p is predecessor" as an imported fact — that behavior is *derived* from rules 6–7, not assumed.
