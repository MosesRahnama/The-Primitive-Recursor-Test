I chose the **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)** for several specific reasons that align with both the structural properties of your rewrite system and the strict constraints of your prompt:

### 1. Purely Syntactic (No External Axioms)
Your prompt strictly prohibited importing external axioms, mathematical structures, or assumptions. 
* If I had used a **Polynomial Interpretation** (e.g., mapping $S(n)$ to $n+1$), I would have had to import the algebraic axioms of the natural numbers $(\mathbb{N}, +, >)$ to prove that the interpretations are well-founded and monotonic.
* **LPO**, by contrast, is a **purely syntactic, relation-based formalism**. It operates solely on the structure of the terms and a finite tree of operator precedence. It does not require mapping terms to any external semantic domain, making it 100% self-contained within the syntax of your rules.

### 2. Handling Symbol Creation ($F \to G$)
In the second rule:
$$\text{F}(x, y, \text{S}(n)) \rightarrow \text{G}(y, \text{F}(x, y, n))$$
the right-hand side introduces a brand-new outer function symbol, $G$, which is not present at the root of the left-hand side. 
* LPO handles this effortlessly through **precedence** ($F \succ G$). This precedence allows the system to "trade" an $F$ at the root for a $G$, provided all arguments of the new $G$-term are strictly bounded by the original $F$-term. 

### 3. Direct Mapping to Structural Recursion
The second rule represents a classic structural recursion on the third argument (from $S(n)$ to $n$), while the first two arguments ($x, y$) remain constant.
* LPO with a **lexicographic status** is designed precisely to model this behavior. By looking at the arguments from left to right (or right to left):
  1. $x$ is compared to $x$ (equal)
  2. $y$ is compared to $y$ (equal)
  3. $S(n)$ is compared to $n$ (strictly decreases)
* This makes LPO the most natural and direct mathematical tool to capture the "decrease" in the recursion.

### 4. Occam’s Razor (Avoiding Overkill)
While more powerful frameworks like **Dependency Pairs** or **Matrix Interpretations** exist, they are designed for complex, mutually recursive, or non-simply terminating systems. Your system is simply terminating and can be solved at the topmost level of the term rewriting hierarchy. Choosing LPO kept the proof concise, elegant, and easy to verify.
