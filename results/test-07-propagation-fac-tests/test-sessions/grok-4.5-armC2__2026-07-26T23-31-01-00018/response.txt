### Assessment
**No**, termination cannot be established from the given rewrite system alone.

### Reasoning
The rules for `plus`, `times` and `p` are standard and (individually) unproblematic for termination. The difficulty is entirely concentrated in the `fac` rule
```
fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x)).
```
The recursive invocation is **not** on a proper subterm of the left-hand side.  Consequently every simplification order (RPO/LPO, KBO, \ldots) and every simple polynomial or matrix interpretation fails: one cannot justify
```
s(x)  ≻  p(s(x))
```
from the signature and the rules alone.

The only way the argument can be shown to decrease is to appeal to the semantic property
```
p(s(t))  =  t   (or at least  |p(s(t))| < |s(t)|)
```
for the values that actually arise.  That equality (or inequality) is **not** a consequence of the presented rewrite relation: `p(s(x))` and `x` are distinct normal forms.  Deriving it requires an external inductive argument on the constructors `0`/`s` (or an imported ordered-semiring axiom, a measure function, \ldots).  None of those ingredients is derivable from the eight rules.

Hence any termination proof necessarily imports structural/object-level assumptions that go beyond the pure TRS.
