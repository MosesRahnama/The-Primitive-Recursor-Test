### Why this assessment method

I chose a direct, syntax-first analysis of the TRS (focusing on the shape of recursive calls, what simplification orders / interpretations can justify, and what equalities are actually forced by the rules) for three tightly related reasons:

1. **The question is meta-theoretic, not “find a proof”**  
   You asked whether termination is *establishable from the presented rules alone*, without imported structural/object-level assumptions. That is a question about the pure rewrite relation, not a request to run a particular termination tool or to exhibit a well-founded order that works after adding axioms. The quickest way to answer it is to inspect whether any of the standard, purely syntactic sufficient conditions (LPO/RPO with any precedence/status, KBO, simple polynomial/matrix interpretations, dependency-pair processors that stay inside the given signature, \ldots) can succeed, and whether the crucial decrease is even a consequence of the rewrite relation.

2. **The obstruction is local and obvious**  
   All rules except the `fac` rule are non-problematic (they are constructor-based and decrease under any reasonable measure). The single recursive call
   ```
   fac(s(x)) → \ldots fac(p(s(x))) \ldots
   ```
   is *not* on a proper subterm.  Consequently every simplification order fails on the same elementary comparison `s(x) ≻ p(s(x))`.  Showing that this comparison nevertheless holds for the ground terms that arise requires the semantic fact `p(s(t)) = t` (or at least a strict decrease in a measure), which is *not* entailed by the eight rules: `p(s(x))` and `x` are distinct normal forms.  Once that gap is visible, one already knows that any termination argument must import an external inductive principle or an ordered-semiring axiom.  No further global method is required to reach the “no” answer.

3. **Avoiding unnecessary machinery**  
   Full automation (TTT, AProVE, NaTT, \ldots), semantic labelling, higher-order rankings, etc. would only obscure the point.  Those techniques either (a) also fail for the same reason or (b) succeed only after the missing equations/lemmas have been added—precisely the extra assumptions the question forbids.  A minimal manual inspection already separates “derivable from the rules” from “needs external arithmetic facts.”

In short, the method is the most economical one that directly addresses the exact claim being evaluated: pure syntactic/semantic self-containedness of the given TRS.
