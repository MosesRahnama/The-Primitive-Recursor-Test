**Yes.** The LPO proof complies with those requirements.

The choices

\[
F \succ G
\]

and lexicographic status for \(F\) are **metalevel parameters of the termination ordering**, not new object-level axioms or rewrite rules. They do not restrict when rewriting may occur, add guards, or assign meanings to \(F,G,S,\) or \(Z\).

Likewise, using

\[
S(n) > n
\]

does not assume that \(S\) is arithmetic successor or that \(n\) is a natural number. It follows purely from the syntactic fact that \(n\) is a proper subterm of \(S(n)\), via LPO’s subterm property.

The proof relies only on:

- ordinary finite first-order term syntax, already implicit in calling this a first-order TRS;
- the two presented rewrite rules;
- the standard metatheorem that LPO with a well-founded symbol precedence is a well-founded reduction ordering, closed under substitutions and contexts.

No assumptions are made about:

- \(Z\) denoting zero;
- \(S\) constructing natural numbers;
- \(G\)’s semantics or rewrite behavior;
- the third argument of \(F\) having any particular “datatype” shape;
- evaluation strategy or top-level-only rewriting.

The ordering covers both rules under arbitrary substitutions and in arbitrary contexts. Thus the proof establishes termination for all finite first-order terms of the signature without strengthening the rewrite system.
