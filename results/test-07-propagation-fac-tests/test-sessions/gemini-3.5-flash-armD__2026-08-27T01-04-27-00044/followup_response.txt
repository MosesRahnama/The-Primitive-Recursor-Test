I chose **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)** for several specific reasons that align with both the nature of your term rewriting system and the strict constraints of your prompt:

### 1. Purely Syntactic (No External Axioms)
Your prompt explicitly restricted the use of "external memory," "imported object-level axioms," or "strengthening the system with extra rewrite rules." 
* Other common termination proof methods, such as **Polynomial Interpretations**, require mapping terms to mathematical structures (like the natural numbers $\mathbb{N}$) and defining operations (like addition or multiplication) over those domains. This introduces external mathematical axioms.
* **LPO is a purely syntactic ordering.** It operates solely on the structure of the terms themselves and a simple precedence (a strict partial order) on the finite set of symbols already present in the signature ($\text{times}, \text{plus}, \text{p}, \text{s}, 0$). It requires absolutely no external arithmetic, set theory, or algebraic axioms.

### 2. It Directly Models the Hierarchical Dependencies
The rewrite rules have a clear, stratified hierarchical dependency where one function is defined in terms of another:
* `times` is defined using `plus` (Rule 5: `times(s(x), y) -> plus(...)`).
* `plus` is defined using `s` (Rule 2: `plus(x, s(y)) -> s(...)`).
* `p` is defined using `s` (Rule 6: `p(s(s(x))) -> s(...)`).

LPO is designed specifically to capture these "definitional dependencies" via the **precedence** relation ($\text{times} > \text{plus} > \text{s} > 0$ and $\text{p} > \text{s}$). Once this precedence is defined, the termination proof becomes a straightforward, mechanical verification.

### 3. Ability to Handle "Constructor Wrapping"
Rules like Rule 2 (`plus(x, s(y)) -> s(plus(x, y))`) and Rule 6 (`p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))`) are notorious for increasing the "height" of the term on the right-hand side by wrapping the recursive call inside a constructor (`s`). 
* Simple reduction orderings like *multiset ordering* or size-based comparisons fail here because the right-hand side term is not a subterm, nor is it strictly smaller in symbol count.
* LPO handles this effortlessly because when the root symbol of the left-hand side has higher precedence than the root symbol of the right-hand side (e.g., $\text{plus} > \text{s}$ or $\text{p} > \text{s}$), LPO allows you to peel off the outer constructor on the right and prove that the core recursive call is strictly smaller.

### 4. Decidability and Direct Verification
LPO is fully decidable. It provides a concrete, algorithmically checkable proof that guarantees well-foundedness. By showing that every rule is strictly decreasing under LPO, the termination of the system is mathematically indisputable and requires zero "hand-waving" or semantic assumptions.
