I chose the **Dependency Pair (DP) Framework** with polynomial interpretations for three main reasons: **modularity**, the presence of **non-structural recursion**, and the **flexibility of weak compatibility**.

Here is the detailed breakdown of why simpler, classical methods fail or are highly impractical for this system, and why the DP framework is the ideal fit:

---

### 1. The Challenge of Non-Structural Recursion (Why LPO/MPO fails)
In classical simplification orderings like the Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO) or Multiset Path Ordering (MPO), terms are compared structurally. For a recursive call $f(s) \to f(t)$ to be decreasing, $t$ must be structurally smaller than $s$.

In the factorial rule:
$$\text{fac}(s(x)) \to \text{times}(\text{fac}(p(s(x))), s(x))$$
The recursive call is $\text{fac}(p(s(x)))$. Under any standard path ordering:
* We must show that the argument $p(s(x))$ is strictly smaller than $s(x)$.
* However, $p$ is a defined function symbol, not a constructor. Syntactically, $p(s(x))$ is *larger* than $s(x)$ because it contains the extra symbol $p$ at the root. 
* To make $s(x) \succ p(s(x))$ under LPO, we would need the constructor $s$ to have a higher precedence than the defined symbol $p$ ($s \succ p$).
* But looking at the rule for $p$:
  $$p(s(s(x))) \to s(p(s(x)))$$
  The right-hand side has $s$ at the root. To orient this rule, we would need $p \succ s$. 

This creates a **precedence conflict** ($s \succ p$ vs. $p \succ s$), meaning standard LPO/MPO cannot prove the termination of this system.

---

### 2. Modularity via SCCs (Divide and Conquer)
The rewrite system is hierarchical: `fac` calls `times` and `p`, `times` calls `plus`, and `plus` is self-contained. 

A global method (like finding a single polynomial interpretation for all rules simultaneously) is extremely difficult because a constraint required to prove `times` might conflict with a constraint required to prove `p` or `fac`.

By using the **Dependency Pair Framework**:
* We construct a **Dependency Graph** and identify **Strongly Connected Components (SCCs)**.
* This allows us to isolate the recursive loops of each function. 
* We can prove the termination of `plus`, `times`, `p`, and `fac` **completely independently** of each other, using different, highly localized mathematical orderings (e.g., measuring only the first argument for `times`, but the second argument for `plus`).

---

### 3. Weak vs. Strict Compatibility
When proving the termination of the `fac` loop, we must account for the behavior of the helper function `p`. Under global methods, we would have to prove that the rules for `p` are *strictly* decreasing.

Under the DP Framework:
* We only need to show a **strict decrease** on the actual recursive transition: 
  $$\text{FAC}(s(x)) \to \text{FAC}(p(s(x)))$$
* For the rules defining $p$ (the "usable rules"), we only need to show a **weak decrease** ($\ge$ instead of $>$):
  $$p(s(s(x))) \ge s(p(s(x)))$$
  $$p(s(0)) \ge 0$$

This dramatically lowers the mathematical constraints. It allowed us to define $[p](x) = \max(0, x-1)$, which trivially satisfies the weak inequality $x + 1 \ge x + 1$ for the $p$ rules, while still yielding the strict inequality $x + 1 > x$ needed to eliminate the `FAC` dependency pair.
