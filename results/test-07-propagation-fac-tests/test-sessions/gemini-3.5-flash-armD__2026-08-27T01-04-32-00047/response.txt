### Termination Argument

To prove that the system terminates for all possible inputs, we can construct a **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)**, which is a well-known syntactic simplification order. Any term rewriting system compatible with an LPO is guaranteed to be terminating.

#### 1. Precedence Relation
We define a strict partial order (precedence relation) on the signature of function symbols:
$$\text{times} \succ \text{plus} \succ s$$
$$p \succ s$$

Both relations are well-defined and can be combined into a single, consistent strict partial order $\succ$ on our symbols: $\{\text{times} \succ \text{plus} \succ s, \ p \succ s\}$. We also assign a lexicographic status (left-to-right) to all binary functions.

#### 2. Verification of Each Rule

We show that for each rewrite rule $l \to r$, we have $l \succ_{lpo} r$. 

Under LPO, $f(s_1, \dots, s_n) \succ_{lpo} t$ holds if:
1. $s_i \succeq_{lpo} t$ for some $i$, or
2. $t = g(t_1, \dots, t_m)$, $f \succ g$, and $f(s_1, \dots, s_n) \succ_{lpo} t_j$ for all $j$, or
3. $t = f(t_1, \dots, t_n)$, $(s_1, \dots, s_n) \succ_{lex} (t_1, \dots, t_n)$, and $f(s_1, \dots, s_n) \succ_{lpo} t_j$ for all $j$.

##### **Rule 1: $\text{plus}(x, 0) \to x$**
* **Proof:** Since $x$ is a strict subterm of the left-hand side $\text{plus}(x, 0)$, we have $\text{plus}(x, 0) \succ_{lpo} x$ directly by the subterm property (Case 1).

##### **Rule 2: $\text{plus}(x, s(y)) \to s(\text{plus}(x, y))$**
* **Proof:** Since $\text{plus} \succ s$, we must show that $\text{plus}(x, s(y)) \succ_{lpo} \text{plus}(x, y)$ (Case 2).
* To show $\text{plus}(x, s(y)) \succ_{lpo} \text{plus}(x, y)$ (Case 3):
  * We compare their arguments lexicographically: $(x, s(y)) \succ_{lex} (x, y)$ because the first arguments are equal ($x = x$) and for the second arguments, $s(y) \succ_{lpo} y$ (by subterm).
  * We must also check that $\text{plus}(x, s(y)) \succ_{lpo} x$ and $\text{plus}(x, s(y)) \succ_{lpo} y$, both of which hold by the subterm property.
* Hence, $\text{plus}(x, s(y)) \succ_{lpo} s(\text{plus}(x, y))$.

##### **Rule 3: $\text{times}(0, y) \to 0$**
* **Proof:** Since $0$ is a strict subterm of $\text{times}(0, y)$, we have $\text{times}(0, y) \succ_{lpo} 0$ by the subterm property.

##### **Rule 4: $\text{times}(x, 0) \to 0$**
* **Proof:** Similarly, $0$ is a strict subterm of $\text{times}(x, 0)$, so $\text{times}(x, 0) \succ_{lpo} 0$ by the subterm property.

##### **Rule 5: $\text{times}(s(x), y) \to \text{plus}(\text{times}(x, y), y)$**
* **Proof:** Since $\text{times} \succ \text{plus}$, we must show that (Case 2):
  1. $\text{times}(s(x), y) \succ_{lpo} \text{times}(x, y)$

* To show $\text{times}(s(x), y) \succ_{lpo} \text{times}(x, y)$ (Case 3):

  * The subterm conditions $\text{times}(s(x), y) \succ_{lpo} x$ and $\text{times}(s(x), y) \succ_{lpo} y$ hold.

##### **Rule 6: $p(s(s(x))) \to s(p(s(x)))$**
* **Proof:** Since $p \succ s$, we must show that $p(s(s(x))) \succ_{lpo} p(s(x))$ (Case 2).

* Hence, $p(s(s(x))) \succ_{lpo} s(p(s(x)))$.

##### **Rule 7: $p(s(0)) \to 0$**
* **Proof:** Since $0$ is a strict subterm of $p(s(0))$, we have $p(s(0)) \succ_{lpo} 0$ by the subterm property.
