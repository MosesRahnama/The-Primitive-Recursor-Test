## Verdict: **Yes — termination is provable from the rules alone.**

The system terminates. The proof uses the **Lexicographic Path Order (LPO)** with the precedence

$$\text{times} \;>\; \text{plus} \;>\; s \;>\; 0.$$

LPO is a standard, classical termination technique (Kamin & Lévy, 1980) and requires no extra rules, side conditions, or structural assumptions about the signature.

---

## The LPO rules (for reference)

For ground terms, $s > t$ iff one of:

- **(Subterm)** $t$ is a strict subterm of $s$; or
- **(Precedence)** $s = f(s_1,\dots,s_n)$, $t = g(t_1,\dots,t_m)$, $f > g$ in the precedence, and $s > t_i$ for every $i$; or
- **(Status/multiset)** $s$ and $t$ have the same head $f$, and the multiset of $s$'s direct arguments is strictly greater (in the multiset extension of $>$) than that of $t$'s.

LPO is well-founded on the set of finite first-order terms whenever the precedence is well-founded (here it is, being finite).

## Rule-by-rule verification

I write $\overline{s} = (s_1,\dots,s_n)$ for the direct arguments of the head of $s$.

**Rule 1.** $\text{times}(x,0) \to 0$
- LHS head: `times`. RHS: the constant $0$.
- $0$ is a strict subterm of $\text{times}(x,0)$.
- Applies by **(Subterm)**. ✓

**Rule 2.** $\text{times}(x,s(y)) \to \text{plus}(\text{times}(x,y), x)$
- LHS head `times` $\;>\;$ `plus` in the precedence.
- Need LHS $\!>\!$ both arguments of the RHS:
  - $\text{times}(x,s(y)) > x$ — by **(Subterm)** (x is a direct subterm).
  - $\text{times}(x,s(y)) > \text{times}(x,y)$ — same head `times`; multiset $\{x,\,s(y)\}$ vs. $\{x,\,y\}$; need $s(y) > y$, which holds by **(Subterm)**.
- Applies by **(Precedence)**. ✓

**Rule 3.** $\text{plus}(x,0) \to x$
- $x$ is a strict subterm of $\text{plus}(x,0)$.
- Applies by **(Subterm)**. ✓

**Rule 4.** $\text{plus}(0,x) \to x$
- $x$ is a strict subterm of $\text{plus}(0,x)$.
- Applies by **(Subterm)**. ✓

**Rule 5.** $\text{plus}(x,s(y)) \to s(\text{plus}(x,y))$
- LHS head `plus` $\;>\;$ `s` in the precedence.
- Need LHS $\!>\!$ the single RHS argument $\text{plus}(x,y)$:
  - $\text{plus}(x,s(y)) > \text{plus}(x,y)$ — same head; multiset $\{x,\,s(y)\}$ vs. $\{x,\,y\}$; $s(y) > y$ by **(Subterm)**.
- Applies by **(Precedence)**. ✓

**Rule 6.** $\text{plus}(s(x),y) \to s(\text{plus}(x,y))$
- LHS head `plus` $\;>\;$ `s` in the precedence.
- Need LHS $\!>\!$ $\text{plus}(x,y)$:
  - $\text{plus}(s(x),y) > \text{plus}(x,y)$ — same head; multiset $\{s(x),\,y\}$ vs. $\{x,\,y\}$; $s(x) > x$ by **(Subterm)**.
- Applies by **(Precedence)**. ✓
