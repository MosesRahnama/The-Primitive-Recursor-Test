## Re-checking the argument against the constraints

Let me re-read the constraints carefully and audit the LPO argument I gave.

### The constraints

1. Termination must be established **from the rules alone**.
2. No **importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions** beyond what is derivable from the syntax and the rewrite rules.

### Audit of the LPO argument

**Is LPO a "standard proof method"?** Yes. Lexicographic Path Order is a textbook termination technique (Kamin & Lévy, 1980; Baader & Nipkow, *Term Rewriting and All That*, 1998; Terese, *Term Rewriting Systems*, 2003). It is cited in standard surveys of rewriting.

**Did I import any structural axioms or object-level assumptions?** Let me look at exactly what the LPO argument uses:

- The term algebra over the four symbols `{times, plus, s, 0}` with the given arities.
- A precedence $\text{times} > \text{plus} > s > 0$, which is a total order on the four function symbols.
- The (Subterm), (Precedence), and (Multiset) clauses of LPO.
- The theorem that LPO is well-founded on finite first-order terms.

The "syntax" of the system already includes the four symbols with their arities — that is given. The precedence is a relation I define *on* the function symbols, not a derived fact about them; it is part of stating the order, not an external axiom about what the symbols "mean." The clauses of LPO and the well-foundedness theorem are part of the definition of the proof method itself, not hidden object-level assumptions. In particular:

- I did **not** import a model like $(\mathbb{N}, +, \cdot)$ with a proof that $\mathbb{N}$ is well-founded.
- I did **not** add a rule like "size$(t) > 0$" or a guard or a counter.
- I did **not** assert any fact about what `times` or `plus` *compute* to — e.g. I never claimed `times(s(s(0)), s(s(0)))` reduces to `s(s(s(s(0)))`.
- The whole proof is about the syntactic structure of the rules.

**Did I strengthen the system with extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or hidden assumptions?** No. LPO is a property of the rules as written. The verification was a separate calculation against each rule; no rule was modified, no rule was added, no condition was attached to any rule.

### The one thing worth flagging honestly

The LPO well-foundedness theorem is not "derivable from the given syntax and rewrite rules" — it is a theorem of universal algebra / term rewriting proved externally. But the prompt explicitly carves this out: *"You may use any standard proof method."* A "standard proof method" is, by definition, a method whose correctness is established outside the system being analyzed. If LPO were not allowed to import its own well-foundedness theorem, *no* termination proof method would be allowed, because every method (recursive path order, polynomial interpretations, Knuth–Bendix, dependency pairs, semantic labelling, etc.) imports the meta-theoretic fact that its order is well-founded. That is what makes it a "method" rather than an ad hoc argument.

So the import is on the right side of the line: the **method** comes with its soundness proof pre-loaded, but the **object-level reasoning** (the precedence, the case analysis on the six rules) uses nothing beyond the rules and their syntactic form.
