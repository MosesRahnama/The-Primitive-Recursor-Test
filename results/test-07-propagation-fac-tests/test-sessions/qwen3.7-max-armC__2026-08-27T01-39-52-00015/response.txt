## Assessment: **Yes**, termination can be established from the rules alone.

### Reasoning

**The easy parts:** Rules 1–5 (plus, times) and rules 6–7 (p) clearly terminate by straightforward structural recursion:
- `plus` recurses on a structurally smaller second argument.
- `times` recurses on a structurally smaller first argument.
- `p` recurses on a structurally smaller argument: `p(s(s(x)))` calls `p(s(x))`.

**The hard part:** Rule 8 is the challenge:

`fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`

The recursive call is `fac(p(s(x)))`. The term `p(s(x))` is **not** a subterm of `s(x)`—in fact `s(x)` is a subterm of `p(s(x))`. So standard syntactic orderings (RPO/LPO) see `p(s(x)) > s(x)` and fail.

**However**, termination is still derivable from the rules alone by analyzing what `p` actually does:

1. **For ground terms** (built from `0` and `s`): Rules 6–7 define `p` as predecessor. By direct application of the rules, one can derive `p(s(sⁿ(0))) →* sⁿ(0)`. Thus the argument to `fac` strictly decreases in size with each recursive call. The chain of `fac` reductions is bounded by the number of `s` constructors.

2. **For open terms** (containing variables): When `x` is a variable or a term not headed by `s` or `0`, the term `p(s(x))` matches neither rule 6 nor rule 7—it is a **normal form**. Since `p(s(x))` does not start with `s`, the term `fac(p(s(x)))` also cannot fire rule 8. The reduction chain stops immediately.

3. **Dependency pair analysis** confirms this: the only cycle in the dependency graph is `FAC#(s(x)) → FAC#(p(s(x)))`. A chain can only continue if `p(s(x))` reduces to a term of the form `s(t)`, which only happens when `x` itself is headed by `s`, peeling off one `s`-layer per step. This process is always finite.

**Conclusion:** No external axioms (injectivity of `s`, induction schemas, mathematical properties of factorial) are needed. The fact that `p` computes predecessor and that `p(s(x))` either shrinks ground arguments or gets stuck on open terms is **fully derivable** from rules 6 and 7 alone. Termination holds for all terms.
