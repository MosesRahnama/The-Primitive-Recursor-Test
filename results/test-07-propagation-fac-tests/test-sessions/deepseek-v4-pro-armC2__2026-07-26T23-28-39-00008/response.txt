Yes, termination of this rewrite system can be established from the rules alone.

The only potential source of non-termination is the recursive call in the `fac` rule. It requires `p(s(x))` to reduce to a term of the form `s(y)` for `fac` to fire again. Analysis of `p` shows this happens exactly when `x` is `s(v)`, yielding a normal form `s(nf(p(v)))`. In that case the number of `s` symbols strictly decreases. If `x` is not `s(v)`, the normal form of `p(s(x))` either is not an `s`-term (stops `fac`) or is `0` (stops). All other rules (`plus`, `times`, `p`) are plainly terminating. Thus no infinite rewrite sequence exists, and termination is provable using the given rules without additional structural axioms.
