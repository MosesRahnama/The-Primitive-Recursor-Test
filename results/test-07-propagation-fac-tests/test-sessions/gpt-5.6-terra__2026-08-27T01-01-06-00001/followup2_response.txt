No—not as stated, my proposed **overall** argument did not fully comply with that requirement.

The `plus`/`times`/`p` part was within the boundary: using a standard recursive path ordering to prove termination of that subsystem does not add axioms, rules, guards, or semantic assumptions.

But the subsequent `fac` argument had a gap. I said that repeated `fac` recursion “consumes available successor structure” and implicitly treated that successor structure as though it were a fixed, directly visible constructor prefix of the original argument. That is not justified from the rules alone.

For arbitrary first-order terms, an argument need not initially be a numeral or even initially have the needed outer `s` form. It can reduce into one. For example:

```text
plus(0, s(0)) -> s(plus(0, 0)) -> s(0).
```

Thus a term such as

```text
fac(s(plus(0, s(0))))
```

can first reduce inside its argument so that the `p` occurring in the recursive call later sees additional outer `s` structure:

```text
fac(s(plus(0, s(0))))
->* fac(s(s(0)))
```

at the relevant recursive stage. More generally, `plus` and `times` reductions can alter constructor exposure before a `p` or `fac` redex is used.

That does not by itself prove nontermination. But it means my informal claim that each recursive `fac` continuation consumes a fixed supply of successors from the “original payload” was not established by the presented rules. To make that claim valid, I would need an explicit well-founded measure that accounts for:

- reductions within arbitrary arguments;
- constructor symbols exposed by terminating `plus`/`times` reductions;
- nested `fac` occurrences;
- duplication of subterms caused by the `fac` right-hand side and by the `times` rule.

I did not provide such a measure, nor prove that it strictly decreases for every possible rewrite step.

So the accurate assessment is:

- **The method did not add explicit rewrite rules or guards.**
- **However, its `fac` component relied on an insufficiently justified structural intuition that resembles an assumption of numeral-like successor descent.**
- Therefore it was **not a complete termination proof under the strict boundary in the initial prompt.**

My earlier categorical conclusion that termination had been established was too strong.
