I chose the dependency pair method combined with a matrix or polynomial interpretation because the rewrite system’s non‑trivial termination challenge lies in the `fac` rule:

```
fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))
```

The recursive call `fac(p(s(x)))` is **not a simple structural subterm** of `s(x)`. Direct syntactic orders like LPO or KBO cannot directly show termination here, because they typically rely on the recursive argument being a strict subterm. Instead, the dependency pair method separates the recursive call into a pair `fac(s(x)) -> fac(p(s(x)))` and allows us to prove its termination independently from the other rules, using a well‑founded order that may rely on semantic interpretations.

A polynomial or matrix interpretation can then assign a measure (e.g., a polynomial over the natural numbers) to terms such that applying `p(s(x))` yields a strict decrease in that measure, thereby making the dependency pair decreasing even though `p(s(x))` is not syntactically smaller. This combination is a standard, powerful technique for first‑order functional programs like this one, where the recursion is “guarded” by a definable predecessor function.
