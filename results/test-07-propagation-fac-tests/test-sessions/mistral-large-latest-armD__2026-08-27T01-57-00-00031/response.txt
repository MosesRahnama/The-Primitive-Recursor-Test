Let's analyze the termination of this first-order term rewriting system. We'll use the **recursive path order (RPO)** with a carefully chosen precedence to establish termination.

### System Recap:
1. `plus(x, 0) → x`
2. `plus(x, s(y)) → s(plus(x, y))`
3. `times(0, y) → 0`
4. `times(x, 0) → 0`
5. `times(s(x), y) → plus(times(x, y), y)`
6. `p(s(s(x))) → s(p(s(x)))`
7. `p(s(0)) → 0`

### Termination Argument:
We will show termination using **RPO with status** (allowing lexicographic or multiset status for symbols). The key is to choose a precedence and status that orients all rules strictly from left to right.

#### Precedence:
We choose the following precedence (where `>` means "has higher precedence than"):
- `s > p` (since `p` reduces `s`-terms)
- `s > plus` (since `plus` is defined in terms of `s`)
- `plus > times` (since `times` is defined in terms of `plus`)
- `times > 0` (base case for `times`)

So the full precedence is:
`s > p > plus > times > 0`

#### Status:
- `plus` and `times` are given **lexicographic status** (left-to-right), since their recursive calls are in the first argument.
- `p` is given **multiset status** (though lexicographic would also work here).

#### Rule-by-Rule Analysis:
We verify that the left-hand side (LHS) is strictly greater than the right-hand side (RHS) in RPO for each rule.

1. **`plus(x, 0) → x`**
   - LHS: `plus(x, 0)`
   - RHS: `x`
   - Since `plus > 0` (by precedence), `plus(x, 0) > x` in RPO.

2. **`plus(x, s(y)) → s(plus(x, y))`**
   - LHS: `plus(x, s(y))`
   - RHS: `s(plus(x, y))`
   - Since `s > plus` (by precedence), and `s(y) > y` (subterm property), the LHS is greater than the RHS in RPO with lexicographic status for `plus`.

3. **`times(0, y) → 0`**
   - LHS: `times(0, y)`
   - RHS: `0`
   - Since `times > 0` (by precedence), `times(0, y) > 0` in RPO.

4. **`times(x, 0) → 0`**
   - LHS: `times(x, 0)`
   - RHS: `0`
   - Since `times > 0` (by precedence), `times(x, 0) > 0` in RPO.

5. **`times(s(x), y) → plus(times(x, y), y)`**
   - LHS: `times(s(x), y)`
   - RHS: `plus(times(x, y), y)`
   - Since `s > plus` (by precedence), and `s(x) > x` (subterm property), the LHS is greater than the RHS in RPO with lexicographic status for `times`.

6. **`p(s(s(x))) → s(p(s(x)))`**
   - LHS: `p(s(s(x)))`
   - RHS: `s(p(s(x)))`
   - Since `s > p` (by precedence), and `s(s(x)) > s(x)` (subterm property), the LHS is greater than the RHS in RPO with multiset status for `p`.

7. **`p(s(0)) → 0`**
   - LHS: `p(s(0))`
   - RHS: `0`
   - Since `p > 0` (by precedence, since `s > p > ... > 0`), and `s(0) > 0` (subterm property), the LHS is greater than the RHS in RPO.

### Conclusion:
All rules are strictly decreasing in the **recursive path order** with the chosen precedence and status. Since RPO is a well-founded order, this proves that the system is **terminating**.

No additional axioms, rules, or assumptions were needed. The argument relies solely on the given rules and standard termination proof techniques.
