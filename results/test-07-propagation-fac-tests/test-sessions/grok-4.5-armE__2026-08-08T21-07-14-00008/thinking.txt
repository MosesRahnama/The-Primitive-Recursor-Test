The user asks about a first-order term rewriting system and whether termination can be established from the presented rules alone.
Yes, termination can be established from the given rules alone.

Consider the lexicographic path order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z (where S and Z are constructors). Define the status of F and G as multiset status. The first rule F(x,y,Z) → x is oriented because F(x,y,Z) > x holds by the definition of LPO (the left-hand side is greater than each of its arguments).  

For the second rule F(x,y,S(n)) → G(y,F(x,y,n)), the left-hand side is greater than the right-hand side because  
F(x,y,S(n )) > G(y,F(x,y,n))  
by the precedence F > G, and the remaining conditions hold:  
F(x,y,S(n)) > y (by LPO),  
F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n) (by LPO, same root symbol, and the third arguments satisfy S(n) > n by the precedence S > Z).  

Thus both rules are oriented by LPO, which is a well-founded simplification order. Every reduction sequence is therefore finite, so the system terminates.  

The argument covers both rules directly from the given syntax and rewrite rules; no additional axioms, guards, or rules are required. No infinite reduction exists because each application of the second rule strictly decreases the third argument of the remaining F (in the LPO sense), and the first rule removes an F entirely. G and the constructors S, Z never introduce new redexes.  

The system terminates.
