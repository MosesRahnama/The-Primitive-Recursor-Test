### Verdict
Yes, termination can be established from the rules alone (via a standard simplification order).

### Termination Argument
The signature consists of the function symbols \(\mathrm{F}\) (ternary), \(\mathrm{G}\) (binary), \(\mathrm{S}\) (unary) and \(\mathrm{Z}\) (nullary), together with the usual first-order variables. The rewrite relation is the ordinary one-step rewrite relation generated by the two given rules (no further rules, no conditions, no equations, and no external axioms).

Consider the lexicographic path order \(\succ_{\mathrm{LPO}}\) induced by the precedence
\[
\mathrm{F} > \mathrm{G} > \mathrm{S} > \mathrm{Z}
\]
(with lexicographic status for every symbol). This is a simplification order, hence well-founded, and it is closed under contexts and substitutions. It remains only to check that both rules are strictly oriented.

- Rule 1.  
  \[
  \mathrm{F}(x,y,\mathrm{Z})\;\succ_{\mathrm{LPO}}\;x.
  \]
  The left-hand side has root \(\mathrm{F}\) and \(x\) occurs as a proper argument, so the claim holds by the subterm property of LPO.

- Rule 2.  
  \[
  \mathrm{F}(x,y,\mathrm{S}(n))\;\succ_{\mathrm{LPO}}\;\mathrm{G}(y,\mathrm{F}(x,y,n)).
  \]
  The roots satisfy \(\mathrm{F} > \mathrm{G}\). Consequently it is enough to verify the two strict inequalities
  \[
  \mathrm{F}(x,y,\mathrm{S}(n))\;\succ_{\mathrm{LPO}}\;y
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  \mathrm{F}(x,y,\mathrm{S}(n))\;\succ_{\mathrm{LPO}}\;\mathrm{F}(x,y,n).
  \]
  The first holds again by the subterm property. For the second, the roots are identical, so LPO compares arguments lexicographically from the left:
  - first arguments: \(x\equiv x\),
  - second arguments: \(y\equiv y\),
  - third arguments: \(\mathrm{S}(n)\;\succ_{\mathrm{LPO}}\;n\) (subterm property).  
  Hence the inequality holds.

Both rules are therefore strictly decreasing in a well-founded simplification order. Every rewrite sequence is finite, i.e. the TRS is terminating.

(The same orientation is obtained with the multiset path order or with a suitable Knuth–Bendix order; the LPO argument above is merely the most concise.)
