I chose the polynomial interpretation method because it is a fully self‑contained, syntactic termination technique that works directly on the given rewrite rules, without needing to import any external structural axioms (like associativity, commutativity, or induction) and without adding guards or side conditions.  

Polynomial interpretations map each function symbol to a polynomial over the natural numbers, which automatically ensures monotonicity when all coefficients are non‑negative.  The natural numbers are well‑founded under the usual ordering, so if every rule strictly decreases some chosen polynomial measure, then infinite rewrite sequences are impossible.  The entire argument lives entirely inside the system’s own syntax and the arithmetic of the interpretation—no external assumptions are required.

This system is built from familiar constructor symbols (`0`, `s`) and defined functions (`plus`, `times`, `p`) that resemble natural number arithmetic.  A polynomial interpretation is a natural fit because:

- The constructors give a clear “size” or “value” (e.g., interpret `s` as `+1`).
- Recursive calls in the rewrite rules reduce the size of the first argument (for `plus` and `times`) or the argument of `p`.  This structural descent can be captured by polynomial inequalities.
- There are no complications like higher‑order variables or binding, so a simple first‑order interpretation suffices.

To find the specific interpretation, I followed a standard heuristic: assign a base interpretation to `0` and `s` that reflects the constructor’s role (e.g., `s(x) = x + 1`), and then assign linear or polynomial forms to the defined functions with sufficient weight on the arguments that decrease.  The coefficients were adjusted to make each left‑hand side strictly larger than the corresponding right‑hand side, while keeping all interpretations weakly monotone.  For example, `plus(x,y)` was given `x + 2y + 1` to ensure that `plus(x,s(y))` is larger than `s(plus(x,y))`, and `times` was given a quadratic form to dominate the nested `plus` in the `times(s(x),y)` rule.

Other termination methods—such as recursive path orderings (with a suitable precedence) or dependency pairs with a subterm criterion—could also work, but the polynomial interpretation was straightforward to construct and verify, and it immediately yields a complete termination certificate.
