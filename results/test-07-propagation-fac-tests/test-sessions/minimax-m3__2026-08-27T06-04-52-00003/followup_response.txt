I picked a **lexicographic/measure-based termination argument** (often called a *termination ordering* or *semantic labelling* argument) for three reasons specific to this system:

### 1. The system already supplies a well-founded domain
The terms `0, s(0), s(s(0)), …` are exactly the standard natural numbers, and `<` on them is well-founded. Any tool that asks "does this rewrite chain stop?" can be answered by mapping every term to an element of `ℕ` and showing the chain is strictly decreasing. This is the cheapest proof technique that succeeds, and it doesn't require inventing extra structure.

### 2. Each rule is structurally recursive in a first-argument sense
Looking at the rules:

- `plus` recurses on the **second** argument, base case `0`.
- `times(s(x), y) → plus(times(x, y), y)` recurses on the **first** argument, base case `0`.
- `p` recurses on its single argument, base case `s(0)`.
- `fac` recurses on `p(s(x))`, which is a strict subterm of `s(x)`.

This is the textbook pattern that a simple measure handles directly. There's no nested mutual recursion through constructors, no tricky overlapping redexes that would force a more sophisticated method like **dependency pairs** or **matrix / arctic termination**. I considered DP but it would be overkill — dependency pairs shine when rules are non-obvious or non-structural; here the structure is right there in the syntax.

### 3. It stays strictly inside the allowed boundary
You said: *no extra rewrite rules, no guards, no side conditions, no hidden assumptions.* A measure-based argument uses only:
- the syntactic size of terms (derivable from the given grammar),
- the fact that `ℕ` with `<` is well-founded (this is a meta-fact, not an extra axiom — it's the universal property of the natural numbers that the syntax `0, s(…)` is *meant* to denote).

I deliberately avoided:
- **Adding lexicographic path orders or interpretation-based methods** that would require proving polynomial bounds.
- **Induction on derivations**, which would be circular in a termination proof.
- **Importing arithmetic axioms** — I only used the well-foundedness of `ℕ`, not, say, that `plus` is associative or that `fac(n) = n!`.

### What I considered and rejected
- **Dependency pairs (Arts & Gielen):** would work, but introduces a meta-theory (DP framework) when the measure argument already fits in one paragraph.
- **Polynomial interpretations:** would work, but requires inventing an interpretation and proving it monotone — again more machinery than needed.
- **Induction on the number of `s`-symbols:** essentially what I did, but phrased as a measure so the well-foundedness is explicit rather than implicit.

The measure method is the **simplest sufficient method** here, and the instructions said to stay within bounds, so I picked the one that needed the least machinery.
