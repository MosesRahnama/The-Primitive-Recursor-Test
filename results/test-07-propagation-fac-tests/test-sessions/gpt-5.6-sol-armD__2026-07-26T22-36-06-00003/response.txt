**Verdict: Yes.** Termination follows from the rules alone via a polynomial interpretation over the well-founded order \((\mathbb N,>)\). No additional rewrite rules, structural axioms, guards, or object-level assumptions are needed.

Interpret the symbols as:

\[
\begin{aligned}
[0] &= 0,\\
[s](a) &= a+1,\\
[\operatorname{plus}](a,b) &= a+2b+1,\\
[\operatorname{times}](a,b) &= 3ab+2a+b+1,\\
[p](a) &= 2a.
\end{aligned}
\]

All interpretations are strictly monotone in every argument. In particular, for `times`, the increments in its first and second arguments are respectively \(3b+2>0\) and \(3a+1>0\).

Each rule strictly decreases:

1. \(\operatorname{plus}(x,0)\to x\):
   \[
   x+1>x.
   \]

2. \(\operatorname{plus}(x,s(y))\to s(\operatorname{plus}(x,y))\):
   \[
   x+2y+3>x+2y+2.
   \]

3. \(\operatorname{times}(0,y)\to0\):
   \[
   y+1>0.
   \]

4. \(\operatorname{times}(x,0)\to0\):
   \[
   2x+1>0.
   \]

5. \(\operatorname{times}(s(x),y)\to
   \operatorname{plus}(\operatorname{times}(x,y),y)\):
   \[
   \begin{aligned}
   [\operatorname{times}(s(x),y)]
     &=3xy+2x+4y+3,\\
   [\operatorname{plus}(\operatorname{times}(x,y),y)]
     &=3xy+2x+3y+2,
   \end{aligned}
   \]
   and the difference is \(y+1>0\).

6. \(p(s(s(x)))\to s(p(s(x)))\):
   \[
   2x+4>2x+3.
   \]

7. \(p(s(0))\to0\):
   \[
   2>0.
   \]

Thus every rewrite step, including one inside an arbitrary context, strictly lowers a natural-number interpretation. Since \(\mathbb N\) admits no infinite strictly descending sequence, the system is terminating.
