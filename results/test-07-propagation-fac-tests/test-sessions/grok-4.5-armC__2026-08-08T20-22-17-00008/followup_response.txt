The question asked specifically whether termination follows from the bare rules (no extra structural/object-level assumptions).  

I therefore examined the system in two steps that match that criterion exactly:

1. Syntactic check – ordinary simplification orders (RPO/LPO/KBO, polynomial interpretations over the naturals, etc.) orient every rule except the `fac` rule. Those orders are purely combinatorial and use nothing beyond the given rewrite relation, so they are admissible.
2. Isolation of the obstacle – the single remaining rule  
   `fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`  
   is not oriented by any such order, because the recursive argument `p(s(x))` is not a subterm of the left-hand side. The only reason the call nevertheless decreases is the semantic fact that `p` realises the predecessor function on the constructors `0`/`s`. Proving that fact requires an external well-founded order on the natural numbers together with an inductive argument; neither is derivable from the rewrite rules themselves (they do not even prove the open equation `p(s(x))=x`).

Hence the method is simply the direct test demanded by the question: “Does a termination argument exist that stays inside the equational theory of the given TRS?” The answer is no, precisely because of the gap exposed in step 2.
