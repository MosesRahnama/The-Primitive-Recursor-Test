### Assessment: No

The given TRS is terminating (as a pure first-order rewrite system over its signature), but this cannot be established from the rules alone.

The rules for `plus`, `times` and `p` are unproblematic: they admit an entirely syntactic termination argument (e.g., a recursive path order with a suitable precedence such as `times > plus > p > s > 0`). The difficulty is introduced by the single `fac` rule
```
fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x)).
```
The recursive call is *not* on a proper sub-term of the left-hand side, nor does any standard simplification order (RPO, LPO, KBO, \ldots) orient the rule. The only reason the call eventually terminates is that, on ground terms built from `0`/`s`, the helper `p` realises the predecessor function and therefore decreases a classic numeric measure. Establishing that fact requires:

- the semantic reading of the constructors `0`/`s` as the natural numbers,
- a well-founded order (usually `<` or `size`) on that domain, and
- inductive reasoning showing `p(s(t)) < s(t)` (or an equivalent measure decrease) for every ground numeral `t`.

None of these ingredients is derivable from the rewrite rules themselves. The rules do not even prove the open equation `p(s(x)) = x` (the term `p(s(x))` is already in normal form). Consequently any termination proof must import structural/object-level assumptions about the natural numbers that go strictly beyond the presented TRS.
