**Verdict: yes.** Termination can be established from the displayed rules alone. A monotone interpretation into a well-founded ordered algebra orients every rule strictly, so no additional rewrite rules, equations, constructor assumptions, or object-level axioms are needed.

Use the carrier \(\mathbb N\times\mathbb N\), ordered lexicographically:

\[
(a,b) >_{\mathrm{lex}} (c,d)
\quad\text{iff}\quad
a>c \ \text{or}\ (a=c\text{ and }b>d).
\]

This is well-founded. Interpret the symbols as follows, writing the interpretations of \(x,y\) as \((a,b)\) and \((c,d)\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
[0] &= (0,0),\\
[s(a,b)] &= (a+1,b),\\
[p(a,b)] &= (\max(a-1,0),a),\\
[\mathit{plus}((a,b),(c,d))] &= (a+c,\max(b,d)+c+1),\\
[\mathit{times}((a,b),(c,d))] &=
(ac,\max(b,d)+2a(c+1)+1),\\
[\mathit{fac}(a,b)] &= (2^{a^2},0).
\end{aligned}
\]

These operations are monotone with respect to the lexicographic order. Hence a strict decrease at a rewritten redex remains a strict decrease in every context.

Let \(x=(a,b)\) and \(y=(c,d)\).

---

### `plus`

\[
\mathit{plus}(x,0)
\]

has interpretation

\[
(a,b+1) >_{\rm lex} (a,b),
\]

so

\[
\mathit{plus}(x,0)\to x
\]

strictly decreases.

For the recursive rule,

\[
[\mathit{plus}(x,s(y))]
=(a+c+1,\max(b,d)+c+2),
\]

while

\[
[s(\mathit{plus}(x,y))]
=(a+c+1,\max(b,d)+c+1).
\]

Thus

\[
\mathit{plus}(x,s(y)) >_{\rm lex} s(\mathit{plus}(x,y)).
\]

---

### `times`

For the zero rules:

\[
[\mathit{times}(0,y)] = (0,d+1) >_{\rm lex} (0,0),
\]

and

\[
[\mathit{times}(x,0)] = (0,b+2a+1) >_{\rm lex} (0,0).
\]

Therefore both

\[
\mathit{times}(0,y)\to 0
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\mathit{times}(x,0)\to 0
\]

strictly decrease.

For the recursive multiplication rule,

\[
[\mathit{times}(s(x),y)]
=
((a+1)c,\max(b,d)+2(a+1)(c+1)+1).
\]

The right side has first component

\[
[\mathit{plus}(\mathit{times}(x,y),y)]_1
=ac+c=(a+1)c.
\]

Its second component is

\[
\max(b,d)+2a(c+1)+c+2,
\]

because the second component of \(\mathit{times}(x,y)\) is already greater than \(d\). Thus the difference between the left and right second components is

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\bigl(2(a+1)(c+1)+1\bigr)
-\bigl(2a(c+1)+c+2\bigr)\\
&=c+1>0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Hence

\[
\mathit{times}(s(x),y)
>_{\rm lex}
\mathit{plus}(\mathit{times}(x,y),y).
\]

---

### `p`

For

\[
p(s(s(x)))\to s(p(s(x))),
\]

the left side interprets as

\[
[p(s(s(x)))] = (a+1,a+2),
\]

while the right side is

\[
[s(p(s(x)))] = (a+1,a+1).
\]

So the left side is strictly larger.

For the base rule,

\[
[p(s(0))]=p(1,0)=(0,1)>(0,0)=[0].
\]

Thus both `p` rules strictly decrease.

---

### `fac`

The left-hand side has interpretation

\[
[\mathit{fac}(s(x))]
=
(2^{(a+1)^2},0).
\]

On the right,

\[
[p(s(x))]=(a,a+1),
\]

so

\[
[\mathit{fac}(p(s(x)))]
=
(2^{a^2},0).
\]

Therefore

\[
[\mathit{times}(\mathit{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x))]_1
=
2^{a^2}(a+1).
\]

It suffices to compare first components:

\[
2^{(a+1)^2}
=
2^{a^2}\cdot 2^{2a+1}
>
2^{a^2}(a+1),
\]

since \(2^{2a+1}>a+1\) for every \(a\in\mathbb N\). Hence

\[
\mathit{fac}(s(x))
>
\mathit{times}(\mathit{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x)).
\]

---

Every one of the eight rewrite rules strictly decreases this well-founded interpretation. Consequently, there can be no infinite rewrite sequence.

Therefore, **the given TRS is terminating as presented**, with no imported structural or object-level assumptions.
