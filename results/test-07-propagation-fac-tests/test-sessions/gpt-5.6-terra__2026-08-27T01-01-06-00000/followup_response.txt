I picked a **semantic interpretation into a well-founded order** because the system has several interacting recursive symbols whose termination is not especially transparent from a single simple syntactic measure.

In particular:

- `plus` recurses on the second argument.
- `times` recurses on the first argument, but introduces `plus`.
- `p` has a rule whose visible constructor depth does not simply decrease:
  \[
  p(s(s(x))) \to s(p(s(x))).
  \]
  One `s` is moved outside `p`, so ordinary term size is unchanged.
- `fac` is the difficult rule:
  \[
  fac(s(x)) \to times(fac(p(s(x))),s(x)).
  \]
  It introduces both `times` and a recursive `fac` call. The recursive argument is syntactically transformed by `p(s(x))`, rather than merely being a direct subterm such as \(x\).

A simple term-size argument therefore does not work: several rules increase size or preserve it. For example, the right-hand side of the `fac` rule is much larger than its left-hand side.

A polynomial interpretation over ordinary natural numbers is also awkward here. The `fac` rule requires the interpretation of `fac(s(x))` to dominate something roughly like

\[
times(fac(p(s(x))),s(x)),
\]

which multiplies the value of the recursive `fac` call by a quantity depending on \(x\). An exponential interpretation for `fac` naturally dominates that growth.

The lexicographic \(\mathbb N^2\) interpretation was chosen because it gives two independent decreasing resources:

1. a **primary component** for genuine decreases in constructor/numeric depth, especially the `fac` rule; and  
2. a **secondary component** for rules such as recursive `plus`, recursive `times`, and the first `p` rule, where the relevant primary quantity can remain equal but another measure decreases.

For example, in

\[
p(s(s(x))) \to s(p(s(x))),
\]

both sides can have the same first coordinate, but the second coordinate decreases. Likewise, for

\[
times(s(x),y)\to plus(times(x,y),y),
\]

the multiplication-like first component is unchanged on both sides, while the auxiliary second component strictly drops.

So the method was selected not because it assumes arithmetic properties of the represented terms, but because it provides a purely syntactic semantic ranking that can orient all rules uniformly, including the mutually interacting `times`/`plus` rules and the nested recursive `fac` rule.
