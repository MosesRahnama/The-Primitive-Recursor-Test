### Why RPO?

I chose the **recursive path order (RPO)** (with a simple precedence and lexicographic status) for these reasons:

- The TRS is a pure first-order term rewriting system with no conditions, no AC symbols, no binders, and only six rules. RPO is a standard, purely syntactic well-founded order that applies directly to exactly this setting and requires nothing beyond a precedence on the signature and the rules themselves.
- The rules have a clear “recursive” structure (the recursive calls to `times` and `plus` occur on structurally smaller arguments). RPO is purpose-built for orienting precisely such recursive definitions once a precedence such as `times > plus > s > 0` is supplied.
- Checking the six rules against the definition of RPO is immediate and local: each rule is verified by a short root-symbol comparison plus a lexicographic comparison of immediate subterms (or by the subterm property). No global analysis, no generation of extra rules, and no semantic interpretation are required.
- Other standard methods (polynomial interpretations, KBO, dependency pairs, \ldots) also work, but they are heavier. Polynomials need an explicit interpretation to be invented and verified; dependency pairs require a DP processor. RPO gives a self-contained, rule-by-rule argument with minimal machinery, which matches the request to stay inside the given syntax and rewrite rules.

In short, RPO is the lightest standard technique that orients every rule outright and therefore establishes termination from the presented system alone.
