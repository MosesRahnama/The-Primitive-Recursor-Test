I chose the dependency‑pair framework with a natural‑numbers interpretation as the **reduction pair** because it mirrors the **intended semantics** of the system perfectly.

The given rewrite rules are the recursive definitions of addition, multiplication, the predecessor function, and the factorial over Peano‑style natural numbers (constructors `0` and `s`). Each function computes exactly the usual arithmetic operation: `plus` is addition, `times` is multiplication, `p` is truncated predecessor, and `fac` is factorial. The reductions are essentially call‑by‑name evaluations of these functions.

Because the intended model is so clear, a **polynomial/arithmetic interpretation** is almost self‑suggesting:
- The interpretation `[0]=0`, `[s](n)=n+1` captures the data type.
- The functions `plus`, `times`, `p`, `fac` are interpreted by their natural arithmetic equivalents (`+`, `·`, `−1`, `!`).
- This makes every original rewrite rule an equality or a strict decrease in the model—usually a decrease of some argument in the recursive calls.

The dependency‑pair transformation separates the calling structure (the DPs) from the constructor rules, and then the arithmetic interpretation supplies a **well‑founded monotone order** where:
- The DPs all strictly decrease because each recursive call peels off at least one `s` or reduces a factorial index.
- The original rules are weakly decreasing (most become equalities).

No other technique was needed because the system is **orthogonal** and **simply typed** by nature, and the natural‑numbers semantics provides a trivial termination certificate. In short, the match between the rewrite system and elementary arithmetic made a direct interpretation the most transparent and economical proof path.
