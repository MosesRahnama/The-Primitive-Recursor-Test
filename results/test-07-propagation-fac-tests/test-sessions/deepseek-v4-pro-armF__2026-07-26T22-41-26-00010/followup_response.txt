I chose Recursive Path Ordering (RPO) because the system exhibits a simple, monotonic structural recursion that can be captured entirely by a precedence on function symbols and lexicographic comparison of arguments—no need for arithmetic interpretations, polynomial bounding, or dependency pair decomposition.  

The rules decrease in obvious ways:  
- `times` calls `plus`, and its recursive call `times(x, y)` has a structurally smaller second argument (`s(y) > y`).  
- `plus` calls itself with smaller arguments in the two successor cases (`s(y) > y` or `s(x) > x`).  
- The base cases eliminate symbols outright.  

RPO is the most direct *syntactic* termination proof for such systems: it only requires defining a precedence that mirrors this natural “call‑graph” (`times > plus > s > 0`) and verifying that each rule’s right‑hand side is strictly smaller than its left‑hand side under that ordering. No inventive numeric or polynomial interpretation is needed, and the ordering is guaranteed to be a well‑founded reduction order closed under substitution—exactly matching the “no external axioms” constraint.
