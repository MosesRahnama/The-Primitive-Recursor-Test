## The one potential concern: the precedence

A skeptical reader might object: "isn't the precedence `fac > times > plus > s > p > 0` itself a hidden assumption? Aren't you asserting that `fac` is somehow 'bigger than' `times`?"

It is not. The precedence is a **parameter of the LPO method**, not an axiom about the TRS. LPO's well-foundedness is a *parametric* theorem:

> For **any** strict partial order `>` on the function symbols, the induced LPO is a well-founded strict order on terms.

The well-foundedness comes from the fact that terms are finite trees, not from any fact I am asserting about `s`, `0`, `p`, `plus`, `times`, or `fac`. Different precedences yield different LPOs; all of them (when their induced relation on symbols is strict partial) are well-founded. I picked one that happened to *orient* the rules. Any other precedence that also oriented the rules would have given an equally valid proof.

To put it concretely: I did not choose `fac > times` because I believe `fac` is semantically larger than `times`. I chose it because in the rule `fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`, the head of the LHS (`fac`) needs to dominate the head of the RHS (`times`) for the LPO case "head of LHS > head of RHS" to apply. The precedence is a *tool* in the proof, not a *fact* about the system.
