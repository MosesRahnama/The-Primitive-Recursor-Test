## Verdict: **Terminating** — provable directly from the given rules using the Recursive Path Ordering (RPO).

## Setup

The system has exactly two rules:

1. `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`

Define a precedence on function symbols:

- `F > G`  (and `S`, `Z` are unrelated to `F`, `G`, or ranked below them — irrelevant to the argument since they never appear as head symbols on right-hand sides in a way that needs comparison against `F`/`G`).

Use the standard **RPO** definition: for the multiset/lexicographic status on arguments of the same symbol, and the usual "subterm" clause: any proper subterm of the left-hand side is smaller than the left-hand side.

## Checking Rule 1: `F(x, y, Z) -> x`

`x` is a proper subterm of `F(x, y, Z)`. By the subterm property of RPO, this immediately gives

`F(x, y, Z) >_rpo x`.

No precedence or status considerations are even needed here — this is the base case of the recursion and trivially decreasing.

## Checking Rule 2: `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`

The right-hand side has head symbol `G`, which is *strictly below* `F` in the precedence. By the RPO rule for a "greater head symbol" case, it suffices to show that `F(x,y,S(n))` dominates **every argument** of `G(y, F(x,y,n))`, i.e.:

- **Argument `y`:** `y` is a proper subterm of `F(x,y,S(n))`, so by the subterm property, `F(x,y,S(n)) >_rpo y`. ✔

- **Argument `F(x,y,n)`:** Both sides have head symbol `F`, so we compare argument tuples with the (lexicographic, left-to-right) status:
  - First argument: `x` vs `x` — equal.
  - Second argument: `y` vs `y` — equal.
  - Third argument: `S(n)` vs `n` — here `n` is a proper subterm of `S(n)`, so `S(n) >_rpo n` by the subterm property.
  
  Since the first two components are equal and the third is strictly decreasing under RPO, the lexicographic comparison yields
  
  `F(x,y,S(n)) >_rpo F(x,y,n)`. ✔

Since both arguments of `G(y, F(x,y,n))` are strictly dominated by the left-hand side `F(x,y,S(n))`, and `G <_prec F`, RPO gives

`F(x, y, S(n)) >_rpo G(y, F(x, y, n))`.

## Conclusion

Both rewrite rules are oriented strictly decreasing by a single, fixed RPO instance (precedence `F > G`, lexicographic status on `F`'s arguments, standard subterm property). Since RPO is well-founded and rule-independent of context (it is a reduction order, monotone and stable under substitution), this constitutes a complete termination proof for the system as given, using no axioms beyond the syntactic subterm relation implicit in the term structure itself.

**Every rule is covered**: Rule 1 by the subterm property alone; Rule 2 by precedence (`F > G`) combined with lexicographic argument comparison, itself grounded in the subterm property applied to `S(n)` vs `n`.

Hence: **the system terminates**, and this is established purely from the given rules via RPO, with no additional axioms, guards, or side conditions imported.
