Yes. Termination can be established from the rewrite rules themselves; it does not require assuming externally that `p` is a predecessor or that the terms denote natural numbers.

At a high level:

- `plus` recursively reduces the number of leading `s` constructors in its second argument.
- `times` recursively reduces the number of leading `s` constructors in its first argument; its calls to `plus` are in the already-terminating arithmetic fragment.
- `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))` moves the recursive `p` call inward by one `s`, and `p(s(0)) -> 0` closes that recursion. If the argument is not sufficiently `s`-headed, no `p` rule applies, so that produces a normal form rather than a loop.
- The only recursive `fac` call is on `p(s(x))`. Once the relevant `p` reductions are performed, this corresponds to reducing the available leading-`s` depth of the factorial argument. If that reduction cannot expose another `s`, the recursive `fac` call is simply stuck; it does not recurse indefinitely.

The missing rules such as `fac(0) -> ...` or `p(0) -> ...` mean that some terms are irreducible before reaching an intended numeric result, but that affects completeness/definedness, not termination. No rule recreates a recursive call at an equal or larger applicable recursion depth in a way that can yield an infinite rewrite sequence.
