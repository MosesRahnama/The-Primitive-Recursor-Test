### Verdict
Yes, termination can be established from the rules alone (via a standard simplification order).

### Termination Argument
We use the lexicographic path order (LPO) induced by the precedence
\[
\texttt{times} > \texttt{plus} > \texttt{s} > 0
\]
(with lexicographic status for the binary symbols). It is well-known that LPO is a simplification order and therefore well-founded; hence it suffices to verify \(l >_{\mathrm{LPO}} r\) for each rule \(l\to r\). (We recall the relevant clauses of the definition: a term \(f(\bar s)\) is strictly greater than a term \(t\) if (i) \(t\) is a proper subterm of \(f(\bar s)\), or (ii) \(t=g(\bar t)\) with \(f>g\) and \(f(\bar s)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}t_i\) for every \(i\), or (iii) \(t=f(\bar t)\) and \(\bar s>_{\mathrm{lex}}\bar t\) while \(f(\bar s)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}t_i\) for every \(i\).)

- \(\texttt{times}(x,0)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}0\) holds by clause (i) (proper subterm).
- \(\texttt{times}(x,\texttt{s}(y))>_{\mathrm{LPO}}\texttt{plus}(\texttt{times}(x,y),x)\): the root precedence \(\texttt{times}>\texttt{plus}\) reduces the claim to the two comparisons \(\texttt{times}(x,\texttt{s}(y))>_{\mathrm{LPO}}\texttt{times}(x,y)\) and \(\texttt{times}(x,\texttt{s}(y))>_{\mathrm{LPO}}x\). The second is clause (i). The first is clause (iii) because the argument tuples satisfy \((x,\texttt{s}(y))>_{\mathrm{lex}}(x,y)\) (as \(\texttt{s}(y)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}y\)) and the side condition \(\texttt{times}(x,\texttt{s}(y))>_{\mathrm{LPO}}y\) again holds by (i).
- \(\texttt{plus}(x,0)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}x\) holds by clause (i).
- \(\texttt{plus}(0,x)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}x\) holds by clause (i).
- \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y))>_{\mathrm{LPO}}\texttt{s}(\texttt{plus}(x,y))\): the root precedence \(\texttt{plus}>\texttt{s}\) reduces the claim to \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y))>_{\mathrm{LPO}}\texttt{plus}(x,y)\), which is clause (iii) because \((x,\texttt{s}(y))>_{\mathrm{lex}}(x,y)\) and the side condition \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y))>_{\mathrm{LPO}}y\) holds by (i).
- \(\texttt{plus}(\texttt{s}(x),y)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}\texttt{s}(\texttt{plus}(x,y))\): symmetrically, the root precedence \(\texttt{plus}>\texttt{s}\) reduces the claim to \(\texttt{plus}(\texttt{s}(x),y)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}\texttt{plus}(x,y)\), which is clause (iii) because \((\texttt{s}(x),y)>_{\mathrm{lex}}(x,y)\) and the side condition \(\texttt{plus}(\texttt{s}(x),y)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}x\) holds by (i).

Every rule is therefore strictly decreasing in a well-founded order, so the rewrite system terminates. The argument uses only the given signature and the six rewrite rules; no additional equations, sorts, guards or semantic interpretations are required.
