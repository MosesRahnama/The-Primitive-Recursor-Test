# Why the Polynomial Interpretation

I picked the polynomial interpretation over the other candidates (LPO/RPO, dependency pairs in isolation, or a hand-rolled measure function) for three concrete reasons visible in the rewrite system:

## 1. The mutual recursion between `fac` and `p` breaks syntactic structural recursion

A naive "measure on the first argument" doesn't work directly:
- `fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`

The argument going into the recursive `fac` call isn't a *subterm* of the original — it's `p(s(x))`, which the user sees as "smaller" only because of what `p`'s rules do. If I tried to argue "the argument of `fac` is a strict subterm," I'd be handwaving the semantics of `p`, exactly the kind of object-level assumption you flagged as off-limits.

A polynomial interpretation forces me to *commit* to a real number for every ground term and watch the value go down on every rule. That obligation produces an actual proof that doesn't sneak in intuitions about `p`.

## 2. The right-hand sides have nested structure that LPO doesn't naturally handle

Look at `times(s(x), y) → plus(times(x,y), y)`. The RHS has *two* occurrences of `y`. With LPO you'd need a precedence where `times > plus` to orient the top symbol, and a lexicographic status on `times`'s arguments, and then the `y` that "passes through" is going to fight you on the right side. It can be made to work, but you're tuning precedences to fit the system rather than the system admitting a clean argument.

Polynomials don't care about symbol shapes; they just compute a number. The "pass-through" of `y` in the RHS costs a bounded additive term and the LHS dominates it.

## 3. Dependency pairs without an interpretation don't deliver the punchline

I could in principle set up a DP problem for `fac` and `p` and try to prove the SCC terminating. But the DP processor still needs *some* termination argument inside, and the candidates collapse to: another polynomial interpretation, or a different reduction pair. So picking "dependency pairs" alone is just deferring the choice.

## What I would *not* pick and why

- **A hand-rolled "well-founded measure"**: This is the danger case. The moment I say "the measure is `size(x) − height(p-chain)`" or anything that references what `p` *means*, I've imported an object-level assumption. The rules don't say `p` is a predecessor — they only say it rewrites `s(s(x))` to `s(p(s(x)))` and `s(0)` to `0`. Termination must be shown from those rules *alone*, so the measure has to come from a generic termination technique applied syntactically.

- **A manual induction**: Same problem. Induction on a structural size only works if I can define "size" without referring to the semantics of `p`.

The polynomial interpretation is the cleanest of the standard TRS termination methods precisely because it treats the rules as rewrite rules and nothing more — it never asks "what does `p` compute?"
