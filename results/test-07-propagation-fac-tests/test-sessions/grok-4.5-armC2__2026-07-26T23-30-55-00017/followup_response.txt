The query asks whether termination follows *from the eight rules alone*, with no imported structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what the rules themselves derive. The most economical way to answer that is:

1. Check whether a standard syntactic method that needs *nothing but the rules* (a simplification order / RPO / LPO, Knuth-Bendix order, etc.) already orients every rule.  
   - The \(+\), \(\times\) and \(p\) rules are unproblematic.  
   - The `fac` rule is not: the recursive argument \(p(s(x))\) has \(s(x)\) as a proper subterm, violating the subterm property that every simplification order must satisfy.  
   So the system is **not simply terminating**. That already shows that pure rule-based syntactic orders are insufficient.

2. Ask what *would* be needed to finish a termination proof.  
   The only plausible well-founded order that makes the recursive call decrease is the usual order on \(\mathbb{N}\) under the intended interpretation of \(p\) as predecessor. Establishing  
   \[
   p(s(x))\;<\;s(x)
   \]
   (and the corresponding measure decrease for `fac`) requires  
   - exhaustive case analysis on the constructors \(0\) and \(s\), and  
   - the induction principle for those constructors.  

   Neither the case distinction nor the induction axiom is a logical consequence of the eight rewrite rules; both are extra structural assumptions about the domain.

Because step 1 fails and step 2 necessarily imports material that the query forbids, the answer is simply “no.”
