I selected the **Dependency Pair (DP) Framework** combined with **Polynomial Interpretations** because this combination is the standard, mathematically rigorous, and fully automated way to prove termination for this specific class of term rewriting systems. 

Here is why this choice is the most appropriate and effective:

### 1. Standard Path Orderings (like LPO or RPO) Fail Here
Simple syntactic path orderings (such as Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO) or Recursive Path Ordering (RPO)) cannot prove termination of this system on their own. 
* In the rule `fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`, the recursive call `fac` has an argument `p(s(x))`. 
* Syntactically, `p(s(x))` is not a subterm of `s(x)`. In fact, it introduces a new defined function symbol `p`.
* Because standard path orderings rely strictly on syntactic subterm properties, they cannot recognize that `p(s(x))` is "smaller" than `s(x)`.

### 2. The DP Framework Decomposes the Problem
The Dependency Pair Framework (developed by Arts and Giesl) is designed to overcome this exact limitation. 
* It extracts "dependency pairs" (representing the recursive calls) and builds a **dependency graph**.
* It decomposes the system into Strongly Connected Components (SCCs). This allows us to prove termination of `plus` and `times` independently from `p`, and then use those results to prove the termination of the trickier `fac` loop. We don't have to prove the entire system terminating in one single, monolithic step.

### 3. Polynomial Interpretations Capture the Semantics of `p(s(x))`
To solve the remaining constraint that `fac(s(x))` transitions to `fac(p(s(x)))`, we need to show that the argument strictly decreases.
* A **Polynomial Interpretation** maps terms to polynomials over natural numbers (e.g., $[[s(x)]] = x + 1$ and $[[p(x)]] = x$).
* This algebraic mapping translates the syntactic rewriting rules into inequalities of polynomials (e.g., showing that the interpretation of the left-hand side is strictly greater than the right-hand side, $LHS > RHS$, and that all rules are compatible with this ordering).
* This provides a well-founded ordering (since natural numbers cannot decrease infinitely), proving termination without needing to "know" what factorial, addition, or predecessor actually mean mathematically.

### 4. It Adheres to Your Constraints
This method is purely **syntactic and algebraic**. It does not assume any prior mathematical knowledge about arithmetic, induction, or the properties of integers. A computer program (like AProVE or TTT2) can generate this proof entirely from the raw rules of the system alone.
