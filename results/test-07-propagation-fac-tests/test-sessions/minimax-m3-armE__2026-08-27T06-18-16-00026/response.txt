### Termination argument

The system has only two rewrite rules, both root-symbol `F`, plus the unused function symbol `G` (which has no defining rules, so any term with root `G` is a normal form). Termination is established by induction on the structure of the **third argument of `F`**.

Let `M =₃ N` mean: the third argument of `F` in M is strictly greater (in the standard structural order on the term algebra `Z | S(t)`) than the third argument of `F` in N — or N has no `F` redex at all.

**Base case.** Consider a redex of the form `F(x, y, Z)`.
- Rule 1 applies: `F(x, y, Z) → x`.
- The right-hand side `x` contains no occurrence of `F`, so the derivation stops. ✅

**Inductive case.** Consider a redex of the form `F(x, y, S(n))` with `n` a proper subterm of `S(n)`.
- Rule 2 applies: `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))`.
- The new term is `G(y, F(x, y, n))`. The only possible redex inside it is the inner `F(x, y, n)`, because `G` has no rewrite rules.
- The third argument of that residual `F` is `n`, which is strictly smaller (as a term) than `S(n)`.
- By the induction hypothesis, the derivation of `F(x, y, n)` terminates.

So every rewrite strictly decreases the third argument of the (unique) `F` redex, which lives in the well-founded term algebra generated by `Z` and `S(_)`. The outer `G(_)` frame contributes no further redexes. Hence the system is **strongly normalizing** from the rules as given — no extra axioms, guards, or side conditions are needed.
