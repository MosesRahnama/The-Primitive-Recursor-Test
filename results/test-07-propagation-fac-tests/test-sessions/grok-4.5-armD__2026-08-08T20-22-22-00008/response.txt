### Verdict
Yes, termination of the given TRS is established from the rules alone (via a standard simplification order).

### Termination Argument
We use the lexicographic path order (LPO) induced by the precedence
\[
\texttt{times} > \texttt{plus} > \texttt{p} > \texttt{s} > \texttt{0}
\]
on the signature (with left-to-right lexicographic status for every symbol). LPO is a well-founded simplification order on terms; it is therefore sufficient to check that each of the seven rules is oriented by this LPO (i.e., left-hand side \(>_{\mathrm{LPO}}\) right-hand side). We verify the rules one by one.

1. \(\texttt{plus}(x,0)\to x\)  
   The right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left-hand side, hence \(\texttt{plus}(x,0)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}x\).

2. \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y))\to\texttt{s}(\texttt{plus}(x,y))\)  
   The root symbol of the left-hand side is strictly larger than that of the right-hand side (\(\texttt{plus}>\texttt{s}\)). It remains only to check the two immediate subterms of the right-hand side:
   - \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y))>_{\mathrm{LPO}}\texttt{plus}(x,y)\) holds by the lexicographic comparison of arguments \((x,\texttt{s}(y))\;>_{\mathrm{lex}}\;(x,y)\) (because \(\texttt{s}(y)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}y\));
   - \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y))>_{\mathrm{LPO}}y\) holds because \(y\) is a proper subterm.
   Consequently the whole rule is oriented.

3. \(\texttt{times}(0,y)\to 0\)  
   The right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left-hand side, hence the inequality holds.

4. \(\texttt{times}(x,0)\to 0\)  
   Identical subterm argument.

5. \(\texttt{times}(\texttt{s}(x),y)\to\texttt{plus}(\texttt{times}(x,y),y)\)  
   The root symbol of the left-hand side is strictly larger (\(\texttt{times}>\texttt{plus}\)). The two immediate subterms of the right-hand side satisfy
   - \(\texttt{times}(\texttt{s}(x),y)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}\texttt{times}(x,y)\) by the lexicographic comparison of arguments \((\texttt{s}(x),y)\;>_{\mathrm{lex}}\;(x,y)\),
   - \(\texttt{times}(\texttt{s}(x),y)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}y\) because \(y\) is a proper subterm.
   Hence the rule is oriented.

6. \(\texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(\texttt{s}(x)))\to\texttt{s}(\texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(x)))\)  
   The root symbol of the left-hand side is strictly larger (\(\texttt{p}>\texttt{s}\)). The single immediate subterm of the right-hand side satisfies
   \[
   \texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(\texttt{s}(x)))>_{\mathrm{LPO}}\texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(x))
   \]
   by the lexicographic comparison of the arguments \(\texttt{s}(\texttt{s}(x))>_{\mathrm{LPO}}\texttt{s}(x)\). (The side condition that the left-hand side also exceeds \(\texttt{s}(x)\) is immediate because \(\texttt{s}(x)\) is a proper subterm.) Thus the rule is oriented.

7. \(\texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(0))\to 0\)  
   The right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left-hand side, hence the inequality holds.

Every rule is strictly decreasing in LPO. Therefore every rewrite sequence is finite, i.e., the TRS is terminating. The argument uses nothing beyond the ordinary inductive definition of LPO on the free term algebra generated by the signature appearing in the rules.
