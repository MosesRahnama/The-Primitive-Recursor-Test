### Why LPO

I chose the lexicographic path order because it is a standard simplification order that applies directly to the seven rules as written, using only a precedence on the function symbols that appear in the signature. No interpretations, no dependency pairs, no argument filterings, no equational theories, and no extra rules or side conditions are required; each rule is checked by a purely syntactic comparison once the precedence

\[
\mathsf{times}\succ\mathsf{plus}\succ p\succ s\succ 0
\]

is fixed. For a small first-order system whose rules are essentially the usual recursive definitions of addition and multiplication together with a simple destructor-like symbol \(p\), this check succeeds immediately and therefore constitutes a self-contained termination proof. More elaborate methods would also work but are unnecessary once a simplification order already orients every rule.
