### Verdict
Yes, termination is established from the rules alone.

### Termination Argument
We establish termination of the given TRS by the lexicographic path order (LPO) induced by the precedence
\[
\times > + > \mathsf{p} > \mathsf{s} > 0
\]
(all symbols have lexicographic status, left-to-right). LPO is a well-founded simplification order on terms; hence it suffices to verify that each rule is a strict decrease \(\ell >_{\mathsf{lpo}} r\).

- \(+(x,0) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} x\) holds because every term is strictly larger than each of its proper subterms.
- \(+(x,\mathsf{s}(y)) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} \mathsf{s}(+(x,y))\): the root precedence \(+ > \mathsf{s}\) reduces the claim to \(+(x,\mathsf{s}(y)) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} +(x,y)\). The latter holds by the lexicographic comparison of arguments (\(x=x\) and \(\mathsf{s}(y) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} y\)).
- \(\times(0,y) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} 0\) and \(\times(x,0) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} 0\) hold by the root precedence \(\times > 0\).
- \(\times(\mathsf{s}(x),y) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} +( \times(x,y), y )\): the root precedence \(\times > +\) reduces the claim to the two inequalities \(\times(\mathsf{s}(x),y) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} \times(x,y)\) and \(\times(\mathsf{s}(x),y) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} y\). The second is immediate (a term is larger than any variable occurring in it). The first holds by the lexicographic comparison of arguments (\(\mathsf{s}(x) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} x\)).
- \(\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{s}(x))) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} \mathsf{s}(\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(x)))\): the root precedence \(\mathsf{p} > \mathsf{s}\) reduces the claim to \(\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{s}(x))) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} \mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(x))\), which holds by the lexicographic comparison of the unary arguments (\(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{s}(x)) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} \mathsf{s}(x)\)).
- \(\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(0)) >_{\mathsf{lpo}} 0\) holds by the root precedence \(\mathsf{p} > 0\).

Every rule is therefore oriented by \(>_{\mathsf{lpo}}\). Consequently every rewrite sequence is finite.

(Note that the argument uses only the inductive definition of LPO on the signature and the seven given rules; no additional rewrite rules, equational theories, interpretations of the function symbols, or domain-specific axioms are required.)
