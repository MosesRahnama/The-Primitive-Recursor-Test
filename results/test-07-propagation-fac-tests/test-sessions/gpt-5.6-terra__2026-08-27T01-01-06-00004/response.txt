**Verdict: yes.** Termination can be established from the displayed rewrite rules alone. No arithmetic axioms about the object language are needed; the following is a meta-level termination proof by dependency pairs and a weakly monotone interpretation over the natural numbers.

## 1. A weak interpretation for all rewrite rules

Interpret the unmarked symbols over \(\mathbb N\) as:

\[
\begin{aligned}
[0] &= 0,\\
[s](n) &= n+1,\\
[\mathit{plus}](m,n) &= m+n,\\
[\mathit{times}](m,n) &= m n,\\
[p](n) &= \max(n-1,0),\\
[\mathit{fac}](n) &= n!.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is a meta-level interpretation used to prove termination, not an added rewrite rule or an object-level assumption.

Every displayed rewrite rule is weakly decreasing—in fact, each is interpreted as equality:

* `plus(x, 0) -> x`:
  \[
  m+0=m.
  \]

* `plus(x, s(y)) -> s(plus(x, y))`:
  \[
  m+(n+1)=(m+n)+1.
  \]

* `times(0, y) -> 0`:
  \[
  0\cdot n=0.
  \]

* `times(x, 0) -> 0`:
  \[
  m\cdot0=0.
  \]

* `times(s(x), y) -> plus(times(x, y), y)`:
  \[
  (m+1)n=mn+n.
  \]

* `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))`:
  \[
  \max((m+2)-1,0)=m+1
  \]
  and
  \[
  1+\max((m+1)-1,0)=1+m=m+1.
  \]

* `p(s(0)) -> 0`:
  \[
  \max(1-1,0)=0.
  \]

* `fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`:
  \[
  (m+1)! = m!(m+1),
  \]
  because
  \[
  [p(s(x))]=\max((m+1)-1,0)=m.
  \]

Thus all ordinary rules are compatible with \(\geq\).

## 2. Dependency pairs

The recursive dependency pairs relevant to cycles are:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathit{plus}^{\#}(x,s(y))
  &\to \mathit{plus}^{\#}(x,y),\\
\mathit{times}^{\#}(s(x),y)
  &\to \mathit{times}^{\#}(x,y),\\
p^{\#}(s(s(x)))
  &\to p^{\#}(s(x)),\\
\mathit{fac}^{\#}(s(x))
  &\to \mathit{fac}^{\#}(p(s(x))).
\end{aligned}
\]

There are also cross-component pairs, for example from `times` to `plus`, and from `fac` to `times` and `p`. They do not create return edges, so they are not cyclic dependency-pair components. The cyclic components are exactly the four self-recursive components above.

For each such component, retain the unmarked interpretation from section 1 for ordinary rules, and interpret the marked root symbol as follows.

### `plus` component

Let

\[
[\mathit{plus}^{\#}](m,n)=n.
\]

Then:

\[
[\mathit{plus}^{\#}(x,s(y))]=n+1>n=[\mathit{plus}^{\#}(x,y)].
\]

### `times` component

Let

\[
[\mathit{times}^{\#}](m,n)=m.
\]

Then:

\[
[\mathit{times}^{\#}(s(x),y)]=m+1>m=[\mathit{times}^{\#}(x,y)].
\]

### `p` component

Let

\[
[p^{\#}](n)=n.
\]

Then:

\[
[p^{\#}(s(s(x)))]=m+2>m+1=[p^{\#}(s(x))].
\]

### `fac` component

Let

\[
[\mathit{fac}^{\#}](n)=n.
\]

Then:

\[
[\mathit{fac}^{\#}(s(x))]=m+1.
\]

Meanwhile,

\[
[p(s(x))]=\max((m+1)-1,0)=m,
\]

so

\[
[\mathit{fac}^{\#}(p(s(x)))]=m.
\]

Therefore:

\[
[\mathit{fac}^{\#}(s(x))]
>
[\mathit{fac}^{\#}(p(s(x)))].
\]

This is the important point for the factorial rule: although its right-hand side contains a recursive `fac`, its recursive argument is strictly smaller under the interpretation because `p(s(x))` denotes \(m\), while `s(x)` denotes \(m+1\).

## 3. Conclusion

All ordinary rewrite rules are weakly compatible with a monotone interpretation over \(\mathbb N\), and every cyclic dependency pair is strictly decreasing under the well-founded order \(>\) on \(\mathbb N\).

Hence there can be no infinite dependency-pair chain. By the dependency-pair termination criterion, the given first-order TRS is terminating.

This argument covers every listed rule and uses no added rewrite rules, no guards, no confluence assumption, no induction principle internal to the object language, and no imported object-level arithmetic axioms.
