Yes, with one clarification about terminology.

The proposed proof does **not** add any object-level rewrite rule, equation, guard, or semantic axiom such as

```text
p(s(x)) = x
```

or

```text
fac(n) = n!
```

to the TRS. It uses a standard **meta-level interpretation** to prove termination.

In termination proofs, assigning meanings such as

\[
[p](n)=\max(n-1,0)
\quad\text{and}\quad
[\mathit{fac}](n)=n!
\]

does not claim that these are additional equations of the rewrite system. Rather, it exhibits one well-founded ordered algebra in which the already-given rewrite rules are non-increasing and the recursive dependency pairs are strictly decreasing.

That distinction matters:

- The TRS itself still has only the eight rules shown.
- No reduction step is added.
- No conditional side condition such as “\(x\) is a numeral” is imposed.
- No claim of confluence, normalization to numerals, or intended arithmetic semantics is required.
- Variables range over arbitrary terms syntactically; in the interpretation proof, they range over arbitrary natural-number values, as is standard for a universally quantified rule interpretation.

For example, the factorial dependency pair is treated meta-theoretically as

\[
\mathit{fac}^{\#}(s(x))
\to
\mathit{fac}^{\#}(p(s(x))).
\]

Under the chosen interpretation,

\[
[s(x)] = n+1
\]

and

\[
[p(s(x))]
=
\max((n+1)-1,0)
=
n.
\]

Thus the interpretation proves strict descent from \(n+1\) to \(n\). This does **not** require adding the object-level equation `p(s(x)) -> x`, which is not among the supplied rules and in fact would be a strengthening of the system.

Likewise, using \(n!\) for the interpretation of `fac` is not an assumption that the TRS computes factorial in some pre-established arithmetic theory. It is just a convenient meta-level function satisfying the compatibility requirement for the given rule:

\[
[\mathit{fac}(s(x))]
=
(n+1)!
=
n!(n+1)
=
[\mathit{times}(\mathit{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x))].
\]

The only imported apparatus is ordinary meta-theory required by essentially any standard termination argument:

1. the well-founded strict order \(>\) on \(\mathbb N\);
2. a monotone algebra interpretation;
3. the dependency-pair termination criterion.

Those are proof tools, not added assumptions in the rewrite system. Under the prompt’s permission to use “any standard proof method,” that is within the stated boundary.

So the answer remains: **yes, the method complies**. My wording in the prior answer could have been clearer in saying that “\(p(s(x))\) denotes \(m\)” meant *under the selected meta-level interpretation*, not as an equation derivable or assumed inside the object-level rewrite system.
