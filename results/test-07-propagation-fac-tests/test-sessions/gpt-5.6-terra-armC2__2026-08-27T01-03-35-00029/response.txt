Yes. Under the standard first-order rewriting semantics on finite terms, termination can be established from these rules alone; no Peano-style equations, induction axioms, or assumed arithmetic meaning of `plus`, `times`, `p`, or `fac` is required.

The key point is syntactic, not semantic:

- `plus` recurses by removing one outer `s` from its second argument.
- `times` recurses by removing one outer `s` from its first argument.
- `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))` moves toward a `p(s(0))` base case by consuming an available outer `s` under the argument of `p`; it does not create an unbounded supply of such `s` symbols.
- `fac` is the only less immediate case: its recursive occurrence is `fac(p(s(x)))`. On numeral-shaped inputs this is plainly a predecessor step. More generally, even without assuming that inputs denote naturals, repeated use of the `p` and `fac` rules cannot regenerate the required leading `s` structure indefinitely: each possible continuation is controlled by finite syntactic `s`-structure already present in the term.

A formal termination proof would use an appropriate well-founded reduction ordering, or a dependency-pair/semantic-labelling style argument that handles the `fac`/`p` interaction. Such a proof is about the rewrite rules themselves, not about imported arithmetic laws.
