I picked a syntactic termination approach because the restriction rules out treating the symbols as pre-existing arithmetic operations. The relevant question is therefore whether all rewrite sequences on arbitrary finite first-order terms are finite, not whether factorial terminates on natural-number values.

More specifically:

- A direct “natural-number argument decreases” argument would be insufficient by itself, because variables may be instantiated by arbitrary terms, not just terms of the form `s^n(0)`.
- The `fac` rule is not orientable by the most naïve structural ordering: its recursive call is on `p(s(x))`, which is not visibly a syntactic subterm of `s(x)`.
- Therefore I pointed to dependency-pair / relative-rewriting or a suitably designed well-founded interpretation as the appropriate kind of method. Those methods analyze the actual possible recursive-call chains, including the intervening `p` reductions, rather than assuming arithmetic semantics.

My mention of “numeral-shaped inputs” was only intuition for the `fac`/`p` interaction, not a premise that may be imported. The formal proof would need to show that any recursive `fac`-call sequence can only continue when the `p` computation exposes another leading `s`, and that this cannot happen indefinitely in a finite term without eventually exhausting the relevant structure.
