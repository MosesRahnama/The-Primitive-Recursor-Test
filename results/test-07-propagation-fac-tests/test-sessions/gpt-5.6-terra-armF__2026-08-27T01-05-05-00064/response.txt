Use the **lexicographic path ordering** (LPO) with lexicographic status for `times` and `plus`, and precedence

\[
\mathsf{times} \succ \mathsf{plus} \succ \mathsf{s} \succ 0.
\]

LPO is a well-founded simplification ordering, so it suffices to show that every rule decreases.

Useful immediate facts under LPO:

- A term is greater than each of its proper subterms. Thus:
  \[
  s(z) > z,\qquad f(\ldots,z,\ldots)>z.
  \]
- With lexicographic status:
  \[
  \mathsf{times}(x,s(y)) > \mathsf{times}(x,y)
  \]
  because the first arguments agree and \(s(y)>y\).
- Likewise:
  \[
  \mathsf{plus}(x,s(y)) > \mathsf{plus}(x,y),
  \]
  and
  \[
  \mathsf{plus}(s(x),y) > \mathsf{plus}(x,y).
  \]

Now every rule decreases.

1. \[
   \mathsf{times}(x,0)\to 0.
   \]

   Since \(\mathsf{times}\succ 0\), the left-hand side is greater than the constant \(0\).

2. \[
   \mathsf{times}(x,s(y))\to \mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{times}(x,y),x).
   \]

   Since \(\mathsf{times}\succ\mathsf{plus}\), it is enough to show that the left-hand side is greater than both arguments of the right-hand `plus`.

   - \(\mathsf{times}(x,s(y))>\mathsf{times}(x,y)\), by lexicographic comparison and \(s(y)>y\).
   - \(\mathsf{times}(x,s(y))>x\), because \(x\) is a subterm of the left-hand side.

   Hence the left-hand side is greater than the whole right-hand side.

3. \[
   \mathsf{plus}(x,0)\to x.
   \]

   The variable \(x\) is a direct subterm of \(\mathsf{plus}(x,0)\), so this decreases.

4. \[
   \mathsf{plus}(0,x)\to x.
   \]

   Again, \(x\) is a direct subterm of the left-hand side.

5. \[
   \mathsf{plus}(x,s(y))\to s(\mathsf{plus}(x,y)).
   \]

   The root precedence gives \(\mathsf{plus}\succ s\). It remains to show

   \[
   \mathsf{plus}(x,s(y))>\mathsf{plus}(x,y),
   \]

   which holds lexicographically because \(s(y)>y\). Therefore the left-hand side is greater than the argument of the right-hand `s`, and hence greater than the entire right-hand side.

6. \[
   \mathsf{plus}(s(x),y)\to s(\mathsf{plus}(x,y)).
   \]

   Again \(\mathsf{plus}\succ s\), and

   \[
   \mathsf{plus}(s(x),y)>\mathsf{plus}(x,y)
   \]

   lexicographically because \(s(x)>x\). Thus this rule also decreases.

Since every rewrite rule strictly decreases under a well-founded LPO, there can be no infinite rewrite sequence. Therefore the presented TRS is terminating.
