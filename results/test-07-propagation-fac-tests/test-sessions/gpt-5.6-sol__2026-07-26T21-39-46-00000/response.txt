## Verdict

**Yes.** The TRS is terminating on all first-order terms, including non-ground and non-constructor terms. No additional axioms about natural numbers, predecessor, or factorial are needed.

A dependency-pair proof works.

### 1. A weak interpretation of every rewrite rule

Interpret terms over \(\mathbb N\) by

\[
\begin{aligned}
[0] &= 0,\\
[s(t)] &= [t]+1,\\
[\mathrm{plus}(t,u)] &= [t]+[u],\\
[\mathrm{times}(t,u)] &= [t]\,[u],\\
[p(t)] &= \max([t]-1,0),\\
[\mathrm{fac}(t)] &= 0.
\end{aligned}
\]

All these operations are monotone. Every rewrite rule preserves this value exactly:

- \(\mathrm{plus}(x,0)\to x\):
  \[
  [x]+0=[x].
  \]

- \(\mathrm{plus}(x,s(y))\to s(\mathrm{plus}(x,y))\):
  \[
  [x]+([y]+1)=([x]+[y])+1.
  \]

- \(\mathrm{times}(0,y)\to0\):
  \[
  0\cdot[y]=0.
  \]

- \(\mathrm{times}(x,0)\to0\):
  \[
  [x]\cdot0=0.
  \]

- \(\mathrm{times}(s(x),y)\to\mathrm{plus}(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y)\):
  \[
  ([x]+1)[y]=[x][y]+[y].
  \]

- \(p(s(s(x)))\to s(p(s(x)))\):
  \[
  \max([x]+2-1,0)=[x]+1
  \]
  and
  \[
  \max([x]+1-1,0)+1=[x]+1.
  \]

- \(p(s(0))\to0\):
  \[
  \max(1-1,0)=0.
  \]

- \(\mathrm{fac}(s(x))\to
  \mathrm{times}(\mathrm{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x))\):
  \[
  0=0\cdot([x]+1).
  \]

Thus ordinary rewrite steps are weakly nonincreasing—in fact, value-preserving.

### 2. Dependency pairs

Writing defined symbols with marked versions, the dependency pairs are:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{PLUS}^{\#}(x,s(y))
  &\to \mathrm{PLUS}^{\#}(x,y),\\
\mathrm{TIMES}^{\#}(s(x),y)
  &\to \mathrm{TIMES}^{\#}(x,y),\\
\mathrm{TIMES}^{\#}(s(x),y)
  &\to \mathrm{PLUS}^{\#}(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y),\\
\mathrm{P}^{\#}(s(s(x)))
  &\to \mathrm{P}^{\#}(s(x)),\\
\mathrm{FAC}^{\#}(s(x))
  &\to \mathrm{FAC}^{\#}(p(s(x))),\\
\mathrm{FAC}^{\#}(s(x))
  &\to \mathrm{TIMES}^{\#}(\mathrm{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x)),\\
\mathrm{FAC}^{\#}(s(x))
  &\to \mathrm{P}^{\#}(s(x)).
\end{aligned}
\]

The only cyclic components are the four self-components for
\(\mathrm{PLUS}^{\#}\), \(\mathrm{TIMES}^{\#}\), \(\mathrm{P}^{\#}\), and
\(\mathrm{FAC}^{\#}\). The other transitions only go

\[
\mathrm{FAC}^{\#}\to\mathrm{TIMES}^{\#},\qquad
\mathrm{FAC}^{\#}\to\mathrm{P}^{\#},\qquad
\mathrm{TIMES}^{\#}\to\mathrm{PLUS}^{\#},
\]

with no route back.

Each cyclic pair strictly decreases a natural-number projection:

- For \(\mathrm{PLUS}^{\#}\), project to the second argument:
  \[
  [s(y)]=[y]+1>[y].
  \]

- For \(\mathrm{TIMES}^{\#}\), project to the first argument:
  \[
  [s(x)]=[x]+1>[x].
  \]

- For \(\mathrm{P}^{\#}\), project to its argument:
  \[
  [s(s(x))]=[x]+2>[x]+1=[s(x)].
  \]

- For \(\mathrm{FAC}^{\#}\), project to its argument:
  \[
  [s(x)]=[x]+1>
  [p(s(x))]=[x].
  \]

Ordinary rewrite steps between dependency-pair steps preserve the interpretation, so they cannot undo these strict decreases. Since the inter-component dependency graph is acyclic, an infinite dependency-pair chain would eventually have to remain in one cyclic component, where it would induce an infinite descending sequence in \(\mathbb N\), which is impossible.

By the dependency-pair termination theorem, the original TRS terminates.

The interpretation of \(p\) as truncated subtraction is only a semantic termination interpretation; it does not add an object-level rule or assume that terms have a particular constructor form.
