### Yes, it complied

The dependency-pair method paired with a monotone algebraic interpretation meets every stated constraint of the initial prompt, for the following reasons.

- **Rules alone, nothing else**: The only equalities ever used are the eight rewrite rules that appear in the prompt. Dependency pairs are not added to the TRS; they are a derived proof artefact obtained by the ordinary syntactic extraction of defined-rooted subterms of right-hand sides. No rule is altered, no new rule is inserted, and the original rewrite relation is left untouched.
- **No structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond the syntax**: The interpretation is a meta-level mapping from terms into \(\mathbb{N}\). The only properties required of \(\mathbb{N}\) are that it is a set equipped with a well-founded order \(>\) and that the ordinary arithmetic operations are weakly monotone—facts that belong to the ambient mathematics in which every termination proof is conducted, not to the object theory. Nothing is assumed about the function symbols \(\mathrm{plus}\), \(\mathrm{times}\), \(\mathrm{p}\) or \(\mathrm{fac}\) beyond the eight rules themselves; in particular, no inductive theorems, no freeness axioms for \(0,s\), and no equations that are not instances of the given rules are ever invoked.
- **No strengthening, guards, side conditions or hidden assumptions**: The reduction-pair conditions are checked by direct arithmetic evaluation of the concrete interpretation functions on every rule and every dependency pair. Those checks contain no extra hypotheses, no conditional constraints, and no appeal to lemmas outside pure arithmetic. The auxiliary function \(f\) that interprets \(\mathrm{fac}\) is defined by a primitive recursion that is justified solely by the well-foundedness of \(\mathbb{N}\); it does not enlarge the rewrite system.

Consequently the argument stays inside the boundary fixed by the prompt: a standard, fully syntactic-and-semantic proof method that refers exclusively to the given signature and the eight rewrite rules.
