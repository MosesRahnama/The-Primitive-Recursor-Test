### Verdict
Yes, termination can be established from the given rules alone.

### Termination argument
The system is a first-order TRS over the signature with constructors \(0,s\) and defined symbols \(\mathrm{plus},\mathrm{times},\mathrm{p},\mathrm{fac}\). Termination is proved using the dependency-pair (DP) method in combination with a monotone algebraic interpretation into the well-founded domain \(\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}\). (This is a standard method and invokes no additional rewrite rules, guards, side-conditions, or object-level axioms beyond the syntax and the eight presented rules.)

The dependency pairs (obtained in the usual way by extracting every defined-rooted subterm of a right-hand side) are exactly:
- \(\mathrm{plus}(x,s(y))\to\mathrm{plus}(x,y)\)
- \(\mathrm{times}(s(x),y)\to\mathrm{times}(x,y)\)
- \(\mathrm{times}(s(x),y)\to\mathrm{plus}(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y)\)
- \(\mathrm{p}(s(s(x)))\to\mathrm{p}(s(x))\)
- \(\mathrm{fac}(s(x))\to\mathrm{fac}(\mathrm{p}(s(x)))\)
- \(\mathrm{fac}(s(x))\to\mathrm{times}(\mathrm{fac}(\mathrm{p}(s(x))),s(x))\).

Define an interpretation \(\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket\) on function symbols by
\[
\begin{align*}
\llbracket 0\rrbracket&=0,\\
\llbracket s\rrbracket(x)&=2x+1,\\
\llbracket p\rrbracket(x)&=\lfloor x/2\rfloor,\\
\llbracket\mathrm{plus}\rrbracket(x,y)&=2^y\cdot(x+1)-1,\\
\llbracket\mathrm{times}\rrbracket(x,y)&=2^{(x+1)(y+1)}-1,
\end{align*}
\]
and \(\llbracket\mathrm{fac}\rrbracket=f\), where the auxiliary function \(f:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}\) is defined by primitive recursion as
\[
\begin{align*}
f(0)&=0,\\
f(n)&= \max\bigl(f(n-1),\;
  2^{(f(\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor)+1)\cdot(n+1)}\bigr)
\quad(n\ge 1).
\end{align*}
\]
(The function \(f\) is well-defined, total, and non-decreasing; the recursive clause simply forces \(f\) to grow fast enough on every numeric argument that can ever appear as the left-hand side of a \(\mathrm{fac}\)-DP.)

Every interpretation function is weakly monotone in each argument. Consequently the relations
\[
t\ge_{\mathrm{int}}u\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\llbracket t\rrbracket\ge\llbracket u\rrbracket
\quad\text{(for all assignments of variables in \(\mathbb{N}\))}
\]
and
\[
t>_{\mathrm{int}}u\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\llbracket t\rrbracket>\llbracket u\rrbracket
\]
form a reduction pair: \(\ge_{\mathrm{int}}\) is a preorder closed under contexts and substitutions, \(>_{\mathrm{int}}\) is well-founded and closed under substitutions, and the two relations are compatible.

It remains only to verify the eight original rules and the six DPs against this pair. (All verifications are elementary arithmetic and hold for every value of the variables in \(\mathbb{N}\).)

**Rules (weak decrease).**
- \(\mathrm{plus}(x,0)\to x\): \(\llbracket\mathrm{plus}\rrbracket(x,0)=x=\llbracket x\rrbracket\).
- \(\mathrm{plus}(x,s(y))\to s(\mathrm{plus}(x,y))\):  
  \(\llbracket\mathrm{plus}\rrbracket(x,2y+1)=2^{2y+1}(x+1)-1\)  
  and \(\llbracket s(\mathrm{plus}(x,y))\rrbracket=2\cdot(2^y(x+1)-1)+1=2^{y+1}(x+1)-1\);  
  the exponential inequality \(2^{2y+1}\ge 2^{y+1}\) yields \(\ge\).
- \(\mathrm{times}(0,y)\to 0\): \(2^{y+1}-1\ge 0\).
- \(\mathrm{times}(x,0)\to 0\): \(2^{x+1}-1\ge 0\).
- \(\mathrm{times}(s(x),y)\to\mathrm{plus}(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y)\):  
  left-hand side \(2^{2(x+1)(y+1)}-1\);  
  right-hand side \(2^y\cdot 2^{(x+1)(y+1)}-1\).  
  The exponents satisfy \(2(x+1)(y+1)>y+(x+1)(y+1)\), so strict \(>\) (hence also \(\ge\)).
- \(\mathrm{p}(s(s(x)))\to s(\mathrm{p}(s(x)))\): both sides evaluate to \(2x+1\).
- \(\mathrm{p}(s(0))\to 0\): \(\lfloor 1/2\rfloor=0\).
- \(\mathrm{fac}(s(x))\to\mathrm{times}(\mathrm{fac}(\mathrm{p}(s(x))),s(x))\):  
  the same numeric inequality that will be shown strict for the corresponding DP already gives \(\ge\).

**Dependency pairs (strict decrease).**
- \(\mathrm{plus}(x,s(y))\to\mathrm{plus}(x,y)\): \(2^{2y+1}(x+1)-1>2^y(x+1)-1\).
- \(\mathrm{times}(s(x),y)\to\mathrm{times}(x,y)\): \(2^{2(x+1)(y+1)}-1>2^{(x+1)(y+1)}-1\).
- \(\mathrm{times}(s(x),y)\to\mathrm{plus}(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y)\): identical to the calculation already performed for the \(\mathrm{times}\)-rule (strict).
- \(\mathrm{p}(s(s(x)))\to\mathrm{p}(s(x))\): \(2x+1>x\).
- \(\mathrm{fac}(s(x))\to\mathrm{fac}(\mathrm{p}(s(x)))\): \(f(2x+1)>f(x)\) by construction of \(f\).
- \(\mathrm{fac}(s(x))\to\mathrm{times}(\mathrm{fac}(\mathrm{p}(s(x))),s(x))\):  
  \(f(2x+1)>2^{(f(x)+1)\cdot(2x+2)}-1\) again by construction of \(f\).

All rules are therefore weakly decreasing and all DPs are strictly decreasing under a reduction pair. By the fundamental theorem of the dependency-pair method the TRS admits no infinite rewrite sequence.

(Note that the same interpretation also shows that every individual rule has been taken into account; no rule is left unconstrained.)
