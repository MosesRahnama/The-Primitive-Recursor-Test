Yes. Termination can be established syntactically from the rewrite rules alone; no equation such as \(p(s(x))=x\) or assumption that all terms are natural-number constructor terms is needed.

The key points are:

- `plus` and `times` recurse by consuming an explicit leading `s` in one argument.
- `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))` moves `p` inward through a finite `s`-spine, consuming one such layer at each root step.
- A repeated `fac` call is possible only when `p(s(x))` reduces to a term headed by `s`; the same finite-spine descent then limits the chain of recursive `fac` calls.
- Duplication in the `times` rule can increase term size and derivation length, but it does not restore the consumed recursive argument or create a cycle.

Thus a suitable dependency-pair or layered well-founded measure proves termination. A simple term-size argument would not suffice, but no extra object-level structural axioms are required.
