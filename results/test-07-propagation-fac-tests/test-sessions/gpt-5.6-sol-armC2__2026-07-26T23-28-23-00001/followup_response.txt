I picked a dependency-pair/layered well-founded argument because the obvious methods do not fit this system:

- **Term size fails:** the `times`, `p`, and especially `fac` rules may preserve or increase size.
- **A simple subterm/simplification order fails:** the recursive `fac` argument `p(s(x))` contains the old argument `s(x)` as a proper subterm, so one cannot directly claim \(s(x) > p(s(x))\) in an order having the subterm property.
- **The decrease is delayed:** `fac` only makes progress after `p` is reduced. Each further `fac` call requires exposing and consuming another leading `s` from the relevant finite reduction path.
- **Duplication is present:** `times` duplicates `y`, so a single scalar measure on total syntax is inconvenient; dependency-pair reasoning separates recursive call chains from harmless duplication.

Thus the method isolates the possible infinite call chains and assigns separate decreases to `plus`, `times`, `p`, and the `fac`/`p` interaction. This is a meta-level argument about finite terms and the given rules, not an imported assumption that every object-level term is a natural number or that \(p(s(x))=x\).
