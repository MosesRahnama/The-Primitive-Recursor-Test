**Termination Argument:**
We can prove that the system is terminating using the **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)**, which is a standard syntactic proof method for term rewriting systems. LPO is a well-founded simplification ordering that guarantees termination without requiring any extra axioms, guards, or system strengthening. 

To use LPO, we must define a strict precedence relation on the function symbols. We assign the precedence **`F > G`** (the symbols `S` and `Z` do not require a specific precedence). We also evaluate the arguments of `F` left-to-right. 

Under LPO, a term $s = f(s_1, ..., s_m)$ is strictly greater than a term $t$ (written $s >_{lpo} t$) if any of the following standard conditions hold:
1. $s_i \ge_{lpo} t$ for some immediate subterm $s_i$ of $s$ (the subterm property).
2. $t = g(t_1, ..., t_k)$, with $f > g$ in our precedence, and $s >_{lpo} t_j$ for all immediate subterms $t_j$ of $t$.
3. $t = f(t_1, ..., t_m)$ (same root symbol), $s >_{lpo} t_j$ for all immediate subterms $t_j$ of $t$, and the arguments $(s_1, ..., s_m)$ are lexicographically strictly greater than $(t_1, ..., t_m)$.

Let's apply these conditions to verify that the left-hand side (LHS) is strictly greater than the right-hand side (RHS) for every rule:

**Rule 1: `F(x, y, Z) -> x`**
* The RHS `x` is an immediate subterm of the LHS `F(x, y, Z)`. 
* By LPO Condition 1, a term is always strictly greater than its proper subterms. Therefore, `F(x, y, Z) >_{lpo} x` trivially holds.

**Rule 2: `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`**
* We must show that $\text{LHS} >_{lpo} \text{RHS}$. Since the root symbol of the LHS is `F` and the RHS is `G`, and we defined **`F > G`**, we use LPO Condition 2. 
* Condition 2 requires the LHS to be strictly greater than all immediate subterms of the RHS, which are `y` and `F(x, y, n)`.
  1. **Showing $\text{LHS} >_{lpo} y$**: 
     The variable `y` is an immediate subterm of the LHS `F(x, y, S(n))`. Thus, this holds via Condition 1.
  2. **Showing $\text{LHS} >_{lpo} F(x, y, n)$**: 
     Because both terms share the root symbol `F`, we use LPO Condition 3. We must satisfy two sub-requirements:
     * *Requirement A*: The LHS must be strictly greater than all immediate subterms of `F(x, y, n)` (which are `x`, `y`, and `n`).
       - `x` and `y` are immediate subterms of the LHS, satisfying the condition.
       - `n` is an immediate subterm of `S(n)`, which in turn is a subterm of the LHS. Thus, by recursive application of the subterm property (Condition 1), the LHS is strictly greater than `n`.
     * *Requirement B*: The arguments of the LHS must be lexicographically greater than the arguments of the RHS subterm. We compare `(x, y, S(n))` against `(x, y, n)` from left to right:
       - 1st argument: `x = x`
       - 2nd argument: `y = y`
       - 3rd argument: `S(n) >_{lpo} n` (This is true by Condition 1, as `n` is a proper subterm of `S(n)`).
       Because the first mismatch strictly favors the LHS, the lexicographic comparison holds.

Since every rule strictly decreases under the Lexicographic Path Ordering (a universally well-founded relation), the term rewriting system is unconditionally terminating.

This maps Rule 1 to $x+y+1 > x$ and Rule 2 to $x+yn+y+n+1 > x+yn+y+n$, strictly decreasing in both rules while maintaining strict monotonicity).*
