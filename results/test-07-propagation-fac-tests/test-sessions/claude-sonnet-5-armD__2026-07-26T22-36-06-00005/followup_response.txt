I chose a polynomial interpretation because of the specific shape of the rules, and because it's the minimal-assumption method that can handle all of them uniformly. Here's the reasoning:

**1. The rules are not simply structurally decreasing (subterm-based orders don't obviously work).**

Simple orders like the subterm ordering, or naive recursive path orderings without a precedence/weight scheme, run into two problem rules:

- `times(s(x), y) -> plus(times(x,y), y)`: the right-hand side is *not* a subterm of the left-hand side — it introduces a new root symbol `plus` applied to the recursive call `times(x,y)`. A purely syntactic size or embedding argument (e.g., "RHS is a subterm/embedded in LHS") fails here, since `plus(times(x,y),y)` is a bigger term with an extra `plus` layer, not smaller in that sense.
- `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))`: this one *is* compatible with something like LPO/RPO (the argument shrinks from `s(s(x))` to `s(x)`), but I wanted one single method that uniformly handles *every* rule, rather than patching together different orders per rule.

So I needed a method sensitive to *quantitative growth* (the multiplication/addition semantics), not just term shape.

**2. Polynomial interpretations are exactly designed for this "arithmetic recursion" pattern.**

These rules are literally the textbook Peano recursive definitions of `+`, `×`, and predecessor. This is a classical benchmark case in term rewriting theory (found in standard references like Baader & Nipkow), and the standard, well-understood technique for it is: interpret each function symbol as a monotone polynomial over ℕ, and check that every rule's LHS interpretation exceeds its RHS interpretation for all values of the variables. This directly captures the "the recursive definition terminates because natural number induction terminates" intuition, without needing to invoke any extra assumptions — it's a self-contained algebraic check.

**3. It requires no extra machinery — matches the constraints of the problem.**

The method:
- doesn't add or modify rules,
- doesn't need side conditions or guards,
- doesn't assume any hidden facts about the intended semantics (I don't need to assume "plus is really addition" — I only need the *chosen* interpretation to be monotone and rule-decreasing, which is checked by direct algebra),
- is a complete, self-contained termination certificate: if the check succeeds for all seven rules, well-foundedness follows from the standard theorem on reduction orders induced by monotone algebras over ℕ.

**4. It scales to handle the "growth" rules that defeat simpler orders.**

The crucial test was rule 5 (`times(s(x),y) -> plus(times(x,y), y)`), which needed an interpretation where multiplying by `s(x)` genuinely produces a bigger polynomial value than the unfolded right-hand side. Polynomial interpretations let me freely tune coefficients (here quadratic terms like `2xy`) to make this inequality come out strictly decreasing for *all* natural number instantiations — something a purely combinatorial/structural order can't easily certify without additional argument filtering or embedding tricks.

So, in short: I picked polynomial interpretations because the offending rule (`times`) requires reasoning about numeric growth rather than term structure, and polynomial interpretations are the standard, self-contained, no-extra-assumptions method that handles exactly that kind of "recursive arithmetic" termination problem across all rules simultaneously.
