### 1. Simpler methods fail on the `fac` rule
The most basic ways to prove termination rely on "simplification orderings" like the Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO) or Knuth-Bendix Ordering (KBO). These methods essentially check if every recursive call operates on a *strict syntactic subterm* of the original argument.

In your system, `plus` and `times` easily pass this test (e.g., `y` is a strict subterm of `s(y)`). However, look at the factorial rule:
`fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`

The recursive call applies `fac` to `p(s(x))`. Syntactically, `p(s(x))` is **not** a subterm of `s(x)`. In fact, it has more symbols! Therefore, standard subterm-based orderings immediately fail. 

### 2. Dependency Pairs isolate the exact problem
Because simple orderings fail, we need a method that separates the *recursion* of the system from the *data manipulation* of the system. 

The Dependency Pair framework was invented specifically to solve this. Instead of trying to prove that the entire right-hand side (`times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`) is somehow "smaller" than the left-hand side, DP extracts just the recursive loop:
`FAC(s(x)) -> FAC(p(s(x)))`

This reduces the termination problem to a much simpler question: *Can we prove that `p(s(x))` eventually evaluates to something strictly smaller than `s(x)`?* 

### 3. Polynomial Interpretations require zero external axioms
Your original prompt had a strict constraint: **no imported object-level axioms beyond the rules shown**.

To prove that `p(s(x))` is smaller than `s(x)` without importing Peano axioms or the mathematical definition of integers, we have to treat `s`, `p`, and `fac` purely as meaningless syntactic symbols. 

By using a Monotone Interpretation (specifically, mapping the symbols to simple polynomials over natural numbers), we can establish a purely syntactic well-founded ordering. 
* We assign purely mechanical weights, for example: 
  * Let `s(x) = x + 2`
  * Let `p(x) = x - 1` (with a floor at 0)
* We then check the rules: 
  * `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))` translates to `(x+4)-1 = 3+x` and `(x+2)-1+2 = 3+x`. 
  * The DP `FAC(s(x)) -> FAC(p(s(x)))` translates to checking if `s(x) > p(s(x))`, which becomes `x + 2 > (x + 2) - 1`, which is `x + 2 > x + 1`. This is strictly true.
