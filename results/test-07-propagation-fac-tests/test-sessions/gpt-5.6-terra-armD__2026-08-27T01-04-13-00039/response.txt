**Verdict: yes.** Termination can be established from the displayed rules alone. No arithmetic interpretation, induction principle, constructor freeness assumption, or additional rewrite/structural rules are needed.

A direct proof uses the **lexicographic path ordering** (LPO) with lexicographic status for the binary symbols and a precedence such as

\[
\mathsf{times} \succ \mathsf{plus} \succ \mathsf{p} \succ \mathsf{s} \succ \mathsf{0}.
\]

LPO over this finite first-order signature is well-founded. It therefore suffices to verify that every rewrite rule strictly decreases under this ordering.

Let \(>\) denote this LPO.

1. \(\mathsf{plus}(x,0) \to x\)

\[
\mathsf{plus}(x,0) > x
\]

by the LPO subterm property.

2. \(\mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y)) \to \mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y))\)

The left root is \(\mathsf{plus}\), and

\[
\mathsf{plus} \succ \mathsf{s}.
\]

Further,

\[
\mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
>
\mathsf{plus}(x,y),
\]

because the first arguments agree and \(\mathsf{s}(y)>y\) by the subterm property. Hence

\[
\mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
>
\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y)).
\]

3. \(\mathsf{times}(0,y) \to 0\)

\[
\mathsf{times}(0,y)>0
\]

by the subterm property, since \(0\) is an argument of the left-hand side.

4. \(\mathsf{times}(x,0) \to 0\)

Likewise,

\[
\mathsf{times}(x,0)>0
\]

by the subterm property.

5. \(\mathsf{times}(\mathsf{s}(x),y) \to
\mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{times}(x,y),y)\)

Since

\[
\mathsf{times}\succ\mathsf{plus},
\]

it remains to establish that the left side is greater than both arguments of the right-side \(\mathsf{plus}\).

First,

\[
\mathsf{times}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)
>
\mathsf{times}(x,y),
\]

because both have root \(\mathsf{times}\), their second arguments agree, and

\[
\mathsf{s}(x)>x
\]

by the subterm property.

Second,

\[
\mathsf{times}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)>y
\]

again by the subterm property.

Therefore,

\[
\mathsf{times}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)
>
\mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{times}(x,y),y).
\]

6. \(\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{s}(x))) \to
\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(x)))\)

Since

\[
\mathsf{p}\succ\mathsf{s},
\]

it suffices that

\[
\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{s}(x)))
>
\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(x)).
\]

Both have root \(\mathsf{p}\), and

\[
\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{s}(x))>\mathsf{s}(x)
\]

because their roots agree and \(\mathsf{s}(x)>x\). Thus

\[
\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{s}(x)))
>
\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(x))).
\]

7. \(\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(0)) \to 0\)

\[
\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(0))>0
\]

by the subterm property.

Since every displayed rewrite rule strictly decreases under one well-founded ordering, there is no infinite rewrite sequence. Thus the system is terminating on all first-order terms over the given signature, including terms not intended to represent natural numbers.
