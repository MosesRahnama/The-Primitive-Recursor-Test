The distinction is between:

- **adding facts to the rewrite system**, which would violate the prompt, and
- **using a meta-level well-founded interpretation to prove termination**, which the prompt expressly permits as a standard proof method.

The argument did not add:

- a rule such as \(p(s(x))\to x\);
- equations identifying terms with natural numbers;
- constructor freeness, injectivity, or disjointness assumptions;
- restrictions on substitutions or on which terms may be rewritten;
- guards or side conditions on any rule.

The interpretation into \(\mathbb N\) is not asserted as an object-level semantics of the terms. It is merely a proof function assigned to all terms. In particular,

\[
[p(t)]=\max([t]-1,0)
\]

does **not** claim that the TRS proves \(p(s(t))=t\). It only supplies a numerical measure. Its admissibility is checked directly against every presented rewrite rule.

Likewise, dependency pairs do not strengthen the TRS. They are a meta-level analysis of possible recursive-call chains. The required decrease for `fac` is established for every valuation:

\[
[s(x)]=[x]+1>[x]=[p(s(x))].
\]

That numerical inequality is a property of the chosen proof interpretation, not an imported rewrite fact about \(p(s(x))\).
