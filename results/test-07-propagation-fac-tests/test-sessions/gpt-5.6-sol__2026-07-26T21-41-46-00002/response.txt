## Verdict

**Yes.** The TRS is terminating on all finite first-order terms, including non-ground terms. No additional rewrite rule such as \(p(s(x))\to x\), and no semantic assumption that the terms denote arithmetic, is needed.

A dependency-pair argument establishes termination.

### 1. A rewrite-invariant interpretation

Interpret terms in \(\mathbb N\) as follows:

\[
\begin{aligned}
[0] &= 0,\\
[s(t)] &= [t]+1,\\
[p(t)] &= \max([t]-1,0),\\
[\operatorname{plus}(u,v)] &= [u]+[v],\\
[\operatorname{times}(u,v)] &= [u]\,[v],\\
[\operatorname{fac}(u)] &= 0.
\end{aligned}
\]

This is only a termination-proof interpretation; it does not add object-level equations or rewrite rules.

Every rewrite rule preserves this interpretation exactly:

- \(\operatorname{plus}(x,0)\to x\):
  \[
  [x]+0=[x].
  \]

- \(\operatorname{plus}(x,s(y))\to s(\operatorname{plus}(x,y))\):
  \[
  [x]+[y]+1=[x]+[y]+1.
  \]

- \(\operatorname{times}(0,y)\to0\):
  \[
  0\cdot[y]=0.
  \]

- \(\operatorname{times}(x,0)\to0\):
  \[
  [x]\cdot0=0.
  \]

- \(\operatorname{times}(s(x),y)\to
  \operatorname{plus}(\operatorname{times}(x,y),y)\):
  \[
  ([x]+1)[y]=[x][y]+[y].
  \]

- \(p(s(s(x)))\to s(p(s(x)))\):
  \[
  \max([x]+2-1,0)=[x]+1
  =1+\max([x]+1-1,0).
  \]

- \(p(s(0))\to0\):
  \[
  \max(1-1,0)=0.
  \]

- \(\operatorname{fac}(s(x))\to
  \operatorname{times}(\operatorname{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x))\):
  \[
  0=0\cdot([x]+1).
  \]

Consequently, intervening ordinary rewrite steps preserve the interpretation of every rewritten argument.

### 2. Dependency pairs

The relevant dependency pairs are:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{PLUS}(x,s(y)) &\to \mathrm{PLUS}(x,y),\\
\mathrm{TIMES}(s(x),y) &\to \mathrm{TIMES}(x,y),\\
\mathrm{TIMES}(s(x),y) &\to
  \mathrm{PLUS}(\operatorname{times}(x,y),y),\\
\mathrm{P}(s(s(x))) &\to \mathrm{P}(s(x)),\\
\mathrm{FAC}(s(x)) &\to \mathrm{FAC}(p(s(x))),\\
\mathrm{FAC}(s(x)) &\to
  \mathrm{TIMES}(\operatorname{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x)),\\
\mathrm{FAC}(s(x)) &\to \mathrm{P}(s(x)).
\end{aligned}
\]

The last pair is included under the standard definition that takes all defined-root subterms of a right-hand side. Omitting it under a different standard candidate convention does not affect the argument.

The only cyclic components are the self-components for `PLUS`, `TIMES`, `P`, and `FAC`. The other pairs only lead from one component to a later component and cannot return.

For each cyclic component, use the indicated argument’s interpretation as a natural-number measure:

- `PLUS`: second argument
  \[
  [s(y)]=[y]+1>[y].
  \]

- `TIMES`: first argument
  \[
  [s(x)]=[x]+1>[x].
  \]

- `P`: its argument
  \[
  [s(s(x))]=[x]+2>[x]+1=[s(x)].
  \]

- `FAC`: its argument
  \[
  [s(x)]=[x]+1
  >
  [p(s(x))]=\max([x]+1-1,0)=[x].
  \]

Ordinary rewrite steps between dependency-pair steps preserve these values, while every dependency-pair step inside its cyclic component strictly decreases the corresponding natural-number measure. Hence no component admits an infinite dependency-pair chain. Since transitions between different components cannot cycle, there is no infinite dependency-pair chain at all.

By the standard dependency-pair termination criterion, the original TRS is terminating.

Notably, the crucial `fac` decrease does **not** assume the absent rule \(p(s(x))\to x\); it follows solely from the proof interpretation, under which the two terms have values \([x]\) and \([x]+1\), respectively.
