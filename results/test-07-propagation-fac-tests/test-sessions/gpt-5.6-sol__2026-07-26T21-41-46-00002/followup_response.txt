I chose **dependency pairs with a rewrite-invariant numeric interpretation** because the difficulty is not the computational rules themselves, but isolating the recursive calls—especially the `fac` call.

Three features make this method a natural fit:

1. **Several rules preserve an obvious arithmetic value rather than decrease it.**  
   For example,

   \[
   \operatorname{plus}(x,s(y))\to s(\operatorname{plus}(x,y))
   \]

   and

   \[
   \operatorname{times}(s(x),y)\to
   \operatorname{plus}(\operatorname{times}(x,y),y)
   \]

   preserve their usual numeric interpretations. Thus, demanding one global measure that strictly decreases on every rewrite step would be unnecessarily difficult. Dependency pairs let ordinary rules be value-preserving while requiring strict decrease only along recursive-call cycles.

2. **The `fac` decrease is syntactically obscured.**  
   Its recursive call is

   \[
   \operatorname{fac}(s(x))
   \to \cdots\operatorname{fac}(p(s(x)))\cdots .
   \]

   There is no rule \(p(s(x))\to x\), so it would be invalid to justify termination by informally treating those terms as equal. The chosen interpretation instead proves directly that

   \[
   [p(s(x))]=[x]<[x]+1=[s(x)].
   \]

   This establishes the recursive decrease without adding the missing predecessor equation.

3. **The dependency graph is simple.**  
   Each genuinely recursive symbol has a straightforward decreasing argument:

   - `plus`: second argument;
   - `times`: first argument;
   - `p`: its sole argument;
   - `fac`: its sole argument.

   Cross-symbol calls, such as `fac` to `times` and `times` to `plus`, do not return to the earlier component. Dependency pairs cleanly separate these acyclic transitions from the recursive cycles.

The interpretation was made **exactly rewrite-invariant** so that any ordinary rewrite steps occurring between two dependency-pair steps cannot increase or otherwise disrupt the selected measure. Defining \([\operatorname{fac}(t)]=0\) may look deliberately coarse, but that is useful: the interpretation is not intended to model factorial. It only needs to preserve ordinary rewrites and expose the decrease in recursive-call arguments.

So the method was chosen because it proves exactly what is available from the presented syntax: recursive calls descend, administrative computation preserves the relevant measures, and no arithmetic or predecessor axiom has to be imported.
