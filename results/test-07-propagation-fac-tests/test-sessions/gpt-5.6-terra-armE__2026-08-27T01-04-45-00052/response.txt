**Verdict:** Yes. Termination is provable from the two displayed rewrite rules alone. No object-level axioms, extra rules, or side conditions are needed.

Use the **lexicographic path ordering** (LPO) with a precedence in which

\[
F \;>\; G
\]

and give \(F\) lexicographic status. The relative precedence of \(S\) and \(Z\) is immaterial for these proofs (one may, for example, use \(F>G>S>Z\)).

LPO is a well-founded, substitution-stable reduction ordering, so it suffices to orient each rule strictly from left to right.

---

### Rule 1

\[
F(x,y,Z)\to x
\]

The right-hand side \(x\) is a proper subterm of \(F(x,y,Z)\). By the subterm property of LPO,

\[
F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} x.
\]

Thus rule 1 decreases.

---

### Rule 2

\[
F(x,y,S(n))\to G(y,F(x,y,n)).
\]

Let

\[
L = F(x,y,S(n)).
\]

To show

\[
L >_{\mathrm{LPO}} G(y,F(x,y,n)),
\]

since \(F>G\), it is enough to show that \(L\) is greater than each argument of the right-hand side.

First,

\[
L >_{\mathrm{LPO}} y,
\]

because \(y\) is a subterm of \(L\).

Second, compare \(L\) with \(F(x,y,n)\). Both have root symbol \(F\), and \(F\) has lexicographic status. Their first two arguments agree, while their third arguments satisfy

\[
S(n) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} n
\]

by the subterm property. Hence

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} F(x,y,n).
\]

Therefore \(L\) is greater than both arguments \(y\) and \(F(x,y,n)\) of the \(G\)-term. Since \(F>G\),

\[
F(x,y,S(n))
>_{\mathrm{LPO}}
G(y,F(x,y,n)).
\]

Thus rule 2 also decreases.

---

Both rules are strictly decreasing under a well-founded ordering. Therefore there can be no infinite rewrite sequence.

\[
\boxed{\text{The presented rewrite system is terminating.}}
\]
