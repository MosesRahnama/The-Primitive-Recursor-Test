**Verdict: Yes.** Termination follows from the rules alone for all finite first-order terms; no assumption that `Z` and `S` represent natural numbers is needed.

Use a **lexicographic path ordering (LPO)** with a well-founded precedence containing

\[
F \succ G
\]

(the relative positions of `S` and `Z` may be chosen arbitrarily), and give `F` lexicographic status.

Let \(>\) denote this LPO.

### Rule 1

\[
F(x,y,Z) \to x
\]

Here \(x\) is a proper subterm of the left-hand side. By the subterm property,

\[
F(x,y,Z) > x.
\]

### Rule 2

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)).
\]

First,

\[
S(n)>n
\]

by the subterm property. Therefore, using the lexicographic status of `F`,

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n),
\]

because the first two arguments agree and the third strictly decreases.

Also,

\[
F(x,y,S(n))>y
\]

since \(y\) is a proper subterm. Since \(F\succ G\) and the left-hand side is greater than both arguments of the right-hand-side `G`, LPO gives

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n)).
\]

Thus every instance of every rewrite rule strictly decreases under a well-founded ordering that is closed under substitutions and contexts. Consequently, no infinite rewrite sequence exists.

This argument treats \(n\) as an arbitrary finite term and uses only syntactic subterm structure; it imports no structural or object-level axioms.
