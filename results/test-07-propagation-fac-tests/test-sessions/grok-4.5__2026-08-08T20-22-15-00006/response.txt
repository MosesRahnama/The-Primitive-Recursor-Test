### Verdict
Yes, termination can be established from the given rules alone.

### Termination Argument
We employ the dependency pair (DP) method, a standard technique for first-order TRS termination. The defined symbols are \(\mathrm{plus}\), \(\mathrm{times}\), \(\mathrm{p}\) and \(\mathrm{fac}\). The dependency pairs extracted from the rules are exactly:

- \(\mathrm{PLUS}(x, s(y)) \to \mathrm{PLUS}(x, y)\)
- \(\mathrm{TIMES}(s(x), y) \to \mathrm{TIMES}(x, y)\)
- \(\mathrm{TIMES}(s(x), y) \to \mathrm{PLUS}(\mathrm{times}(x, y), y)\)
- \(\mathrm{P}(s(s(x))) \to \mathrm{P}(s(x))\)
- \(\mathrm{FAC}(s(x)) \to \mathrm{FAC}(p(s(x)))\)
- \(\mathrm{FAC}(s(x)) \to \mathrm{TIMES}(\mathrm{fac}(p(s(x))), s(x))\)

(The remaining rules contribute no DPs, as their right-hand sides contain no defined symbols.)

It is sufficient to exhibit a reduction pair \((\gtrsim, >)\) that orients every original rule weakly (\(l \gtrsim r\)) and every DP strictly (\(l > r\)). We obtain such a pair from the following interpretation into the well-founded domain \(\mathbb{N}\) (natural numbers ordered by the standard \(>\)):

\[
\begin{align*}
|0| &= 0,\\
|s|(n) &= n+1,\\
|\mathrm{plus}|(m,n) &= m+n,\\
|\mathrm{times}|(m,n) &= m\cdot n,\\
|p|(n) &= \max(n-1,0),\\
|\mathrm{fac}|(n) &= 0,\\
|\mathrm{PLUS}|(m,n) &= m+n,\\
|\mathrm{TIMES}|(m,n) &= m\cdot(n+1)+1,\\
|\mathrm{P}|(n) &= n,\\
|\mathrm{FAC}|(n) &= n+2.
\end{align*}
\]

All ten functions are weakly monotone in every argument. Consequently the interpretations induce a reduction pair on terms (via the usual homomorphic extension, with variables ranging over \(\mathbb{N}\)).

**Verification that every original rule is weakly oriented**

1. \(\mathrm{plus}(x,0)\to x\):  
   \(|\mathrm{plus}(x,0)|=|x|+0=|x|\ge|x|\).

2. \(\mathrm{plus}(x,s(y))\to s(\mathrm{plus}(x,y))\):  
   left \(=|x|+(|y|+1)\), right \(=(|x|+|y|)+1\), equal.

3. \(\mathrm{times}(0,y)\to 0\):  
   \(0\cdot|y|=0\ge0\).

4. \(\mathrm{times}(x,0)\to 0\):  
   \(|x|\cdot0=0\ge0\).

5. \(\mathrm{times}(s(x),y)\to\mathrm{plus}(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y)\):  
   left \(=(|x|+1)\cdot|y|=|x|\cdot|y|+|y|\),  
   right \(=|x|\cdot|y|+|y|\), equal.

6. \(\mathrm{p}(s(s(x)))\to s(\mathrm{p}(s(x)))\):  
   left \(=\max(|x|+2-1,0)=|x|+1\),  
   right \(=\max(|x|+1-1,0)+1=|x|+1\), equal.

7. \(\mathrm{p}(s(0))\to0\):  
   \(\max(1-1,0)=0\ge0\).

8. \(\mathrm{fac}(s(x))\to\mathrm{times}(\mathrm{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x))\):  
   left \(=0\), right \(=0\cdot(|x|+1)=0\), equal.

**Verification that every DP is strictly oriented**

- \(\mathrm{PLUS}(x,s(y))\to\mathrm{PLUS}(x,y)\):  
  left \(=|x|+|y|+1> |x|+|y|\) (right).

- \(\mathrm{TIMES}(s(x),y)\to\mathrm{TIMES}(x,y)\):  
  left \(=(|x|+1)(|y|+1)+1= |x|(|y|+1)+(|y|+1)+1\)  
  \(> |x|(|y|+1)+1\) (right).

- \(\mathrm{TIMES}(s(x),y)\to\mathrm{PLUS}(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y)\):  
  left \(=(|x|+1)(|y|+1)+1= |x||y|+|x|+|y|+2\)  
  \(> |x||y|+|y|\) (right).

- \(\mathrm{P}(s(s(x)))\to\mathrm{P}(s(x))\):  
  left \(=|x|+2> |x|+1\) (right).

- \(\mathrm{FAC}(s(x))\to\mathrm{FAC}(p(s(x)))\):  
  left \(=|x|+3\), right \(=\max(|x|,0)+2=|x|+2\), hence strict.

- \(\mathrm{FAC}(s(x))\to\mathrm{TIMES}(\mathrm{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x))\):  
  left \(=|x|+3\), right \(=0\cdot((|x|+1)+1)+1=1\), hence strict.

By the fundamental theorem of the dependency-pair method the existence of such a reduction pair implies that the original TRS has no infinite rewrite sequences.

(Note that the argument uses only the eight given rewrite rules to generate the DPs and only the ordinary arithmetic of the meta-level naturals to discharge the required inequalities; no object-level axioms, extra rules, guards or side-conditions are added to the TRS itself.)
