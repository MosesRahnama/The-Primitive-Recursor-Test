### Why the dependency-pair method?

The system mixes several layers of recursion (plus inside times, times and p inside fac) together with a non-structural “predecessor” p.  In this situation the dependency-pair (DP) framework is the most economical standard technique that stays strictly inside the given signature and rules:

- It isolates precisely the recursive calls (the six DPs listed earlier) and asks only that those calls decrease, while the original rules are allowed to stay the same size.  
- Consequently a very simple linear interpretation into \(\mathbb{N}\) already orients everything; a direct polynomial interpretation (or a pure RPO/LPO argument) on the whole TRS would need higher-degree or ad-hoc measures to compensate for the same-size rules such as  
  \(\mathrm{times}(s(x),y)\to\mathrm{plus}(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y)\)  
  and  
  \(\mathrm{p}(s(s(x)))\to s(\mathrm{p}(s(x)))\).  
- No extra rewrite rules, guards or semantic side-conditions are added; the only meta-level ingredient is the well-foundedness of \(\mathbb{N}\), which is part of any standard reduction-pair argument.

Other classic methods (KBO, matrix interpretations, semantic labelling, \ldots) also succeed, but each of them requires either a more complicated base order or an encoding step that is heavier than the six trivial inequalities needed once the DPs have been formed.  Hence DP + linear interpretation is the lightest proof that meets the constraint “from the presented rules alone.”
