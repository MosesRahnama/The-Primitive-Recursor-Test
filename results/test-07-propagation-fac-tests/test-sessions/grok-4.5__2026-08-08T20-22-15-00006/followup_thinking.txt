The user is asking why I picked the dependency pair method for proving termination of that TRS.
I chose the dependency pair method because it cleanly separates the recursive structure of the rules from the original system, making it easier to handle the nested recursions in fac and times without having to juggle complicated interpretations directly on the full set of rules. With the predecessor function and the way fac calls into times, a basic lexic order or simple polynomial interpretation can get messy when trying to ensure every rule strictly decreases while keeping the whole thing well-founded. DPs let me focus on the critical recursive calls (like fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))) and pair them with an interpretation that guarantees strict decrease exactly where needed — on the dependency pairs — while allowing weak decrease on the non-recursive parts like plus and times.
