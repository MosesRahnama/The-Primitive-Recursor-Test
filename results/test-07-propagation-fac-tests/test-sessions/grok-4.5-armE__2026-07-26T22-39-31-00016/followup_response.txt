### Why RPO

I chose the recursive path order because it is a standard, self-contained simplification order that applies directly to the two given rules and the signature they induce, with no need for auxiliary constructions, interpretations, or extensions of the system.

The rules exhibit precisely the shape that path orders are designed to handle: a recursive call on a structurally smaller argument (the third argument decreases from \(S(n)\) to \(n\)) together with a top-level symbol change (\(F\) to \(G\)) that can be oriented by a simple precedence. Checking the two strict decreases is therefore immediate from the definition of RPO (subterm property + lexicographic status) and requires nothing beyond the syntax already present. The same orientation can be obtained from other simplification orders, but RPO yields the shortest, fully explicit argument that still covers every rule.

More heavyweight techniques (dependency pairs, semantic labelling, etc.) are unnecessary for a two-rule system of this size; interpretation methods would also work but demand an extra search for concrete polynomials or matrices, which adds no insight once a simplification order already succeeds. RPO therefore stays strictly inside the mandated boundary while giving a complete termination proof.
