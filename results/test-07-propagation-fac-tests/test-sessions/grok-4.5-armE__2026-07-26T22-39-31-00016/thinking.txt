The user asks about a first-order term rewriting system and whether termination can be established from the presented rules alone.
Yes, termination can be established from the given rules alone.

The system is terminating because every rewrite step strictly decreases the “size” of the third argument of F, measured by the number of S-symbols (the depth of the natural-number term). The first rule replaces any F(…, Z) by x, which removes 0 S-symbols. The second rule replaces F(…, S(n)) by G(y, F(…, n)), which removes exactly one S-symbol and leaves a new F whose third argument is n.  

G has no rules, so once an F is rewritten to a G-term the only possible further reductions are inside the 2-ary F that appears inside that G-term; each such inner F again removes one S-symbol. Because every reduction path therefore corresponds to a finite descent on the natural-number depth of the third argument, no infinite rewrite sequence exists. All other terms (those whose third argument is neither Z nor S(·)) are 0-redexes and are already normal forms.  

This argument uses only the given syntax (F/3, G/2, S/1, Z/0) and the two supplied rules; no structural induction hypotheses, no extra axioms, and no side-conditions are required. A recursive-path-order proof with precedence F ≻ G also works: both rules are oriented because the left-hand side is larger than each of its immediate subterms under the lexicographic comparison on argument tuples. Either method suffices.
