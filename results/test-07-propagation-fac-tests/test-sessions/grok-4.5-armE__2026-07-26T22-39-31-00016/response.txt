### Termination Argument
The signature consists of the symbols appearing in the rules (\(F/3\), \(G/2\), \(S/1\), \(Z/0\)) together with any uninterpreted symbols (which are irrelevant, as they admit no rules). The rewrite relation is the standard one generated by the two rules under contexts and substitutions. No guards, extra rules, or equational theories are added.

Termination follows by the recursive path order (RPO) induced by the precedence \(F > G > S > Z\) (any total extension of this precedence works) with lexicographic status. RPO is a well-founded simplification order on terms; it therefore suffices to check that both rules are strictly decreasing.

- Rule \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\):  
  The left-hand side has \(x\) as a proper subterm, so the subterm property of RPO immediately yields \(F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{RPO}} x\).

- Rule \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\):  
  The roots satisfy \(F > G\), therefore it is enough to verify the two strict inequalities  
  \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{RPO}} y\) and \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{RPO}} F(x,y,n)\).  
  The first holds by the subterm property.  
  For the second the roots are identical, so the argument tuples are compared lexicographically: the first two components are identical and the third components satisfy \(S(n) >_{\mathrm{RPO}} n\) (again by the subterm property). Hence \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{RPO}} F(x,y,n)\).

Both rules are oriented by \(>_{\mathrm{RPO}}\). Consequently every rewrite step strictly decreases the RPO measure and no infinite rewrite sequence exists.

(The same orientation is obtained by the multiset path order or by a simple Knuth–Bendix order with suitable weights and precedence; the RPO argument above is self-contained and uses nothing beyond the syntax of the two rules.)
