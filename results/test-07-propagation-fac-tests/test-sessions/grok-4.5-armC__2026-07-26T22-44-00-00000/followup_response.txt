### Why dependency pairs?

I chose the dependency-pair (DP) method because it is a complete, modular, and fully syntactic technique that works directly on the eight rules as given, with no extra axioms, and because simpler classical orders are known to fail on this system.

- **RPO/KBO/LPO fail.**  
  The factorial rule  
  `fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`  
  homeomorphically embeds its left-hand side into its right-hand side (after the obvious unfolding of `p`). Consequently the system is not simply terminating, so every simplification order (RPO, KBO, LPO, \ldots) is inapplicable.

- **A direct polynomial/matrix interpretation on the whole TRS is possible but heavier.**  
  One can invent a single well-founded algebra that orients all eight rules at once, but the interpretation must be rather elaborate (factorials, truncated subtraction, \ldots). DP analysis splits the problem into four independent SCCs, each of which is oriented by a trivial projection or a simple linear polynomial. That is considerably less work and makes the proof easier to check by hand.

- **DP is “from the rules alone.”**  
  The method never leaves the free term algebra, never adds induction principles, freeness axioms or domain restrictions; it only inspects the recursive calls that appear in the given rewrite rules. That matches the constraint you stated.

- **It is the standard modern tool for exactly this kind of example.**  
  The system is the classic “factorial with predecessor” benchmark that appears in the DP literature precisely to illustrate the technique; using DP is therefore the most direct and transparent route.

In short: DP is the lightest method that both succeeds and stays strictly inside the presented rewrite system.
