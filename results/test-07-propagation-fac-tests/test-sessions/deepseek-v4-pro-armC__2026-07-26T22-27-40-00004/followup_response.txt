I picked the method of appealing to a **well‑founded reduction order** (specifically mentioning polynomial interpretations) because the system isn’t completely straightforward — the `fac` rule `fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))` makes a recursive call on `p(s(x))`, which is *not* a subterm of `s(x)`.  

A purely structural approach (like lexicographic path order) would fail for that rule, since `p(s(x))` is not syntactically smaller. I therefore had to invoke an interpretation where the **meaning** of `p(s(x))` is strictly less than `s(x)`. A polynomial (or more generally, a monotone well‑founded algebra) over the natural numbers can assign a value to `p` that acts like a predecessor (e.g. `[p](n) = max(n‑1,0)`) and give `fac` a fast‑growing interpretation, making every rule strictly decreasing. That’s a standard termination proof for this very system, and it doesn’t rely on any imported arithmetic axioms — the decrease is verified purely by the chosen interpretation.

So my choice of method was dictated by the need to handle the non‑structural recursion in `fac` within a self‑contained syntactic termination argument.
