I chose those methods because they are standard, sound, and complete-in-practice for proving termination of first-order rewrite systems like this one.  

The system has no higher-order features, no built-in arithmetic axioms, and all recursion is structural. That means a simplification order (like a polynomial interpretation or RPO) is sufficient. Dependency pairs are a natural extension when rules like `fac` have a nested recursive call that requires showing the argument decreases after some evaluation. A polynomial interpretation can easily assign weights that make each rule’s right-hand side smaller than its left-hand side, especially since `plus`, `times`, and `p` are obviously terminating and `fac` reduces its argument via `p`. I didn’t need to consider more complex methods (e.g., semantic labelling or usable rules) because the system is straightforward and a direct monotonic weight assignment works.
