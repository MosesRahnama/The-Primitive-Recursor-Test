**Verdict: yes.** Termination is provable from these rewrite rules alone. No associativity, commutativity, induction principle, arithmetic meaning of the symbols, or additional rewrite rules are needed.

Use the **lexicographic path ordering** (LPO) with precedence

\[
\mathsf{times} \succ \mathsf{plus} \succ \mathsf{s} \succ 0
\]

and lexicographic status for the binary symbols `times` and `plus`.

LPO is a well-founded ordering on first-order terms, is closed under substitutions and contexts, and therefore it suffices to show that every right-hand side is strictly smaller than its corresponding left-hand side.

Let \(>\) denote this LPO.

| Rule | Reason the left side is greater than the right side |
|---|---|
| `times(x, 0) -> 0` | `0` is a proper subterm of `times(x,0)`, so `times(x,0) > 0`. |
| `times(x, s(y)) -> plus(times(x, y), x)` | Since `times ≻ plus`, it is enough that the left side exceed both arguments of the RHS. `times(x,s(y)) > times(x,y)` by lexicographic comparison, since `s(y) > y` by the subterm property. Also `times(x,s(y)) > x` because `x` is a subterm. Hence it exceeds `plus(times(x,y),x)`. |
| `plus(x, 0) -> x` | `x` is a proper subterm of `plus(x,0)`. |
| `plus(0, x) -> x` | `x` is a proper subterm of `plus(0,x)`. |
| `plus(x, s(y)) -> s(plus(x, y))` | Since `plus ≻ s`, it suffices that `plus(x,s(y)) > plus(x,y)`. This holds lexicographically because the first arguments are equal and `s(y) > y`. Thus the left side exceeds the argument of the RHS `s`, hence exceeds the whole RHS. |
| `plus(s(x), y) -> s(plus(x, y))` | Again `plus ≻ s`. Moreover `plus(s(x),y) > plus(x,y)` lexicographically because `s(x) > x`. Therefore the left side exceeds `s(plus(x,y))`. |

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded LPO:

\[
l > r
\quad\text{for every rule } l \to r.
\]

Consequently, there can be no infinite rewrite sequence.

The overlap between

```text
plus(x, s(y)) -> s(plus(x, y))
plus(s(x), y) -> s(plus(x, y))
```

does not affect this conclusion: at an overlapping redex, either permitted rewrite still strictly decreases the same well-founded ordering. Therefore the presented system is terminating.
