**Verdict: Yes.** Termination follows syntactically from the six rules alone. No arithmetic meaning for `0`, `s`, `plus`, or `times`, and no associativity, commutativity, constructor-disjointness, or other object-level axioms are needed.

Use the lexicographic path ordering (LPO) with precedence

\[
\texttt{times} \succ \texttt{plus} \succ \texttt{s} \succ \texttt{0},
\]

and lexicographic status for `times` and `plus`. LPO is a well-founded reduction order on finite first-order terms.

Each rule strictly decreases:

1. \(\texttt{times}(x,0) \to 0\)

   The right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.

2. \(\texttt{times}(x,\texttt{s}(y)) \to
   \texttt{plus}(\texttt{times}(x,y),x)\)

   First,
   \[
   \texttt{times}(x,\texttt{s}(y))
   \succ_{\mathrm{LPO}} \texttt{times}(x,y),
   \]
   because the first arguments agree and \(\texttt{s}(y)\succ_{\mathrm{LPO}}y\) by the subterm property. The left side is also greater than \(x\), again by the subterm property. Since
   \(\texttt{times}\succ\texttt{plus}\), it is therefore greater than the entire right-hand side.

3. \(\texttt{plus}(x,0)\to x\)

   The right-hand side is a proper subterm.

4. \(\texttt{plus}(0,x)\to x\)

   The right-hand side is a proper subterm.

5. \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y))\to
   \texttt{s}(\texttt{plus}(x,y))\)

   Lexicographically,
   \[
   \texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y))
   \succ_{\mathrm{LPO}}\texttt{plus}(x,y)
   \]
   since \(\texttt{s}(y)\succ_{\mathrm{LPO}}y\). Because
   \(\texttt{plus}\succ\texttt{s}\), the left side is greater than
   \(\texttt{s}(\texttt{plus}(x,y))\).

6. \(\texttt{plus}(\texttt{s}(x),y)\to
   \texttt{s}(\texttt{plus}(x,y))\)

   Likewise,
   \[
   \texttt{plus}(\texttt{s}(x),y)
   \succ_{\mathrm{LPO}}\texttt{plus}(x,y)
   \]
   because \(\texttt{s}(x)\succ_{\mathrm{LPO}}x\). With
   \(\texttt{plus}\succ\texttt{s}\), this orients the whole rule strictly.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded order. Consequently, no infinite rewrite sequence exists, so the presented TRS is terminating on all finite terms.
