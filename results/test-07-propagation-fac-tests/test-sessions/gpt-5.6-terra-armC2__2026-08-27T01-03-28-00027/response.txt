Yes. Termination is a property of this rewrite system itself and can be established syntactically, for all finite first-order terms, without assuming that the symbols denote natural-number addition, multiplication, predecessor, factorial, or any structural axioms.

Briefly:

- `plus` and `times` are structurally recursive: their recursive calls are on syntactically smaller arguments (`y` for `plus`, and the predecessor `x` for the recursive `times` call).
- The `p` rules cannot loop. They move the `p` inward through a finite prefix of `s` constructors:
  `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))`.
  The special case `p(s(0)) -> 0` ends that traversal when applicable.
- `fac` is the only non-obvious rule, since it introduces another `fac`:
  `fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`.
  But the recursive `fac` argument is headed by `p`, not by `s`. It can only become `s`-headed after finite `p` reductions, and those reductions consume/move through a finite syntactic `s` structure. They do not create an unbounded recursive cycle. A standard dependency-pair or tailored well-founded syntactic measure can formalize this.

Thus no external arithmetic meaning or induction principle is required: the raw TRS is terminating.
