I picked a syntactic, well-founded-measure/dependency-pair style argument because your constraint explicitly excludes importing intended arithmetic semantics. Therefore it would not be appropriate to argue that these rules implement natural-number addition, multiplication, predecessor, and factorial and then appeal to their usual termination.

The relevant evidence has to come from the rule shapes alone:

- recursive calls in `plus`/`times` are structurally below the redex;
- `p` traverses a finite chain of leading `s` symbols and has no rule that recreates a larger such chain under the same `p`;
- the apparent recursive call in `fac` is guarded by `p`. For another `fac` step to occur, the `p` term must first rewrite to an `s`-headed term, and the `p` rules prevent this from yielding an infinite recurrence of `fac` calls.

I mentioned dependency pairs because they are a standard purely TRS-internal formalization of precisely this reasoning: one analyzes possible recursive call chains rather than assigning arithmetic meanings to symbols. A tailored lexicographic or multiset measure on recursive-call structure would also be suitable.
