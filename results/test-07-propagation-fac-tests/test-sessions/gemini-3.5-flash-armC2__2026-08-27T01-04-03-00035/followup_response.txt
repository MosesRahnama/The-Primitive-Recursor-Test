I selected the **Dependency Pair (DP) Framework** combined with **Polynomial Interpretations** because this specific Term Rewriting System (TRS) contains a classic challenge—**nested defined functions in recursive arguments**—that makes simpler methods fail. 

Here is the detailed reasoning for why this specific methodology is necessary and chosen:

### 1. The Challenge of the `fac` and `p` Rules
If we try to use simpler, purely syntactic path orderings (like the **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)** or **Recursive Path Ordering (RPO)**), they fail on this system. 

Look at the factorial rule:
$$\text{fac}(s(x)) \rightarrow \text{times}(\text{fac}(\mathbf{p(s(x))}), s(x))$$

For a standard reduction order $(\succ)$ to prove termination directly, the left-hand side must be strictly greater than the right-hand side. This requires showing that the recursive argument is smaller than the input:
$$s(x) \succ p(s(x))$$

Syntactically, $p(s(x))$ contains the defined function symbol $p$ and is actually *larger* in terms of term structure than $s(x)$. Standard LPO/RPO cannot orient this because $p$ is not a constructor, and we cannot prove $s(x) \succ p(s(x))$ without knowing what $p$ actually does.

### 2. Why the Dependency Pair (DP) Framework is Needed
The DP framework (developed by Arts and Giesl) is the standard modern technique because it solves this exact problem by:
1. **Isolating the recursion:** Instead of proving that the entire right-hand side is smaller, it focuses only on the active recursive calls (the "dependency pairs"). For `fac`, the critical dependency pair is:
   $$\text{FAC}(s(x)) \rightarrow \text{FAC}(p(s(x)))$$
2. **Utilizing "Usable Rules":** It allows us to take the rules for $p$ into account when comparing $s(x)$ and $p(s(x))$. Since the usable rules show that $p(s(x)) \rightarrow^* x$ and $s(x) \succ x$, the framework can mathematically bridge the gap and prove the decrease.

### 3. Why Polynomial Interpretations?
Once the DP framework isolates the recursion, we need a concrete way to measure the "size" of the terms. **Polynomial interpretations** are the ideal tool here because:
* **Intuitive Mapping:** They map the symbolic terms to the domain of natural numbers (where $> >$ is well-founded). For example:
  * $[0] = 1$
  * $[s(x)] = x + 1$
  * $[p(x)] = x$
  * $[\text{FAC}(x)] = x$
* **Rigorous Proof of Decrease:** Under this mapping, the dependency pair $\text{FAC}(s(x)) \rightarrow \text{FAC}(p(s(x)))$ translates to:
  $$x + 1 > x$$
  This is strictly true for all natural numbers, proving termination of the recursion.

### Summary
I chose this combination because it is the **most mathematically robust standard** for first-order rewriting. It eleganty overcomes the semantic hurdle of the predecessor function `p` inside the recursive call of `fac`, which would block simpler, purely syntactic termination proofs.
