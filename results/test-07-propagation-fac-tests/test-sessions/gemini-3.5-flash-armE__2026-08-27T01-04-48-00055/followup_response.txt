I chose the **Polynomial Interpretation** method for several specific reasons, balancing mathematical rigor, intuitive clarity, and the constraints of the prompt. 

Here is why this method was the ideal choice:

### 1. It Explains *Why* the System Terminates (Semantic Intuition)
The system is a classic representation of primitive recursion (specifically, a variation of multiplication or repeated accumulation). 
* The third argument of $F$ acts as a decrementing counter ($S(n) \rightarrow n$).
* With each step, the second argument $y$ is passed to the accumulator $G$.
* This means the "size" of the term doesn't just decrease by a constant; it decreases by an amount proportional to $y$. 

A polynomial interpretation captures this exact relationship. By defining the weight of $F(x,y,n)$ as containing the term $(y+1)n$, the math perfectly mirrors the computational behavior: decrementing $n$ by $1$ frees up $y+1$ units of "potential energy," which is more than enough to cover the cost of creating the new $G(y, \dots)$ wrapper (which only costs $y$ units).

### 2. Standard Syntactic Orders (like LPO) are Highly Abstract
While a syntactic order like the **Lexicographic Path Order (LPO)** with precedence $F \succ G$ can also prove termination here, LPO proofs are highly recursive and abstract. Proving LPO requires:
* Defining a precedence relation.
* Recursively evaluating multiple nested conditions.
* Relying on the general, complex proof of the well-foundedness of LPO itself.

By contrast, the polynomial interpretation translates the symbolic TRS problem into **basic high-school algebra** over the positive integers. Anyone can immediately verify that $(x + (y + 1)n + y + 1) > (x + (y + 1)n + y)$ without needing to understand the underlying theory of term rewriting orderings.

### 3. Absolute Self-Containment
The prompt requested a proof *"without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions."* 

By mapping the terms directly to the positive integers $(\mathbb{N}_{\ge 1}, >)$, we leverage a well-known, universally accepted well-founded set. The proof does not require the reader to trust or import the axiom systems of abstract reduction relations, Kruskal's Tree Theorem, or termination frameworks; it relies purely on the basic order properties of numbers.
