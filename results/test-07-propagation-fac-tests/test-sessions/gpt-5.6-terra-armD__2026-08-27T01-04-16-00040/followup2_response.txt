Yes. The proposed polynomial-interpretation proof complies with those requirements.

It does **not** assume that the object-level symbols `plus`, `times`, `s`, `0`, or `p` have their intended arithmetic meanings. In particular, it does not use assumptions such as:

- `plus` being associative, commutative, or identity-preserving;
- `times` distributing over `plus`;
- `s` being injective or representing a successor operation;
- terms denoting natural numbers in an object-level model;
- any unstated equations or rewrite rules.

The assignments

\[
[0]=0,\quad [s](n)=n+1,\quad [plus](a,b)=a+2b+1,
\]

\[
[times](a,b)=2ab+2a+b+1,\quad [p](a)=2a
\]

are not equations added to the rewrite system. They are a **meta-level ranking interpretation** used only to prove that every instance of every displayed rewrite rule decreases in a well-founded order.

Thus, for example, interpreting the symbol `times` by

\[
[times](a,b)=2ab+2a+b+1
\]

does not assert any object-level fact such as

\[
times(x,y) = 2xy+2x+y+1.
\]

It merely defines a numerical measure of the syntax tree rooted at `times`.

The only background fact used is the standard meta-theoretic fact that the strict order \(>\) on natural numbers is well-founded: there is no infinite sequence

\[
n_0 > n_1 > n_2 > \cdots.
\]

That is part of the ordinary proof method, not an added axiom, rewrite rule, guard, side condition, or assumption about the terms represented by the TRS.

Because the interpretation is monotone and every rule has strictly smaller interpretation on its right-hand side than on its left-hand side, every rewrite step decreases that external measure. Therefore an infinite rewrite sequence is impossible.

So the conclusion—termination of the given TRS—follows without strengthening or changing the system and without importing object-level algebraic or structural assumptions.
