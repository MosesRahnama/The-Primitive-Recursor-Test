Yes. Termination can be established from the rewrite rules themselves; no external Peano axioms or intended-number-model assumptions are required.

Syntactically, the recursive calls are controlled by decreasing constructor structure:

- `plus(x, s(y))` recurses on the second argument with one leading `s` removed.
- `times(s(x), y)` recurses on the first argument with one leading `s` removed.
- `p(s(s(x)))` moves the `p` inward while reducing the number of leading `s` symbols under that `p`; `p(s(0))` ends the process.
- `fac(s(x))` calls `fac(p(s(x)))`. By the `p` rules, if its argument has a finite leading `s`-chain, that chain is shortened; if it does not match the `p` rules, then the recursive `fac` call is stuck rather than generating another factorial step.

The system may duplicate subterms—for example in multiplication—but duplication alone does not create a rewrite cycle. A formal termination proof could be given using dependency pairs and a suitable lexicographic/structural measure. Such a proof is purely about the displayed rewrite rules and applies to arbitrary first-order terms, not merely terms denoting natural numbers.
