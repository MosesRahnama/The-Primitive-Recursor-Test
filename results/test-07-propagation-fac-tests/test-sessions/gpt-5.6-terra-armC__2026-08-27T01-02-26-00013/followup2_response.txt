The **method class** I proposed—dependency pairs with a syntactic well-founded ordering—does comply with those requirements. It is a standard termination technique for first-order TRSs and does not require adding rules, treating variables as natural numbers, or assuming arithmetic properties not represented by rewriting.

However, my **brief justification as stated** was not a complete compliant proof. In particular, saying that a `fac(p(s(x)))` call is “stuck” when `p(s(x))` does not immediately match a `p` rule was too strong. Rewriting may occur inside `x` first and later make the enclosing `p` redex applicable. For example, an argument not initially headed by `s` can reduce to one through `plus` or `p` rewriting below it.

So the accurate assessment is:

- **Yes:** a dependency-pair/termination-order proof would satisfy the stated restrictions, because it works solely from the syntax and rules.
- **But:** my earlier sketch was only an intuition, not the required rigorous proof. A real proof must account for arbitrary rewriting inside arguments and for chains involving `fac` together with intervening `p` reductions, rather than relying only on the argument’s initially visible `s`-prefix.
