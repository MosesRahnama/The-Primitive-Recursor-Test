I chose the **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)** because it is the most elegant, direct, and natural tool for this specific set of rules. When analyzing a term rewriting system by hand, the goal is to find a proof method that requires the least amount of boilerplate while remaining mathematically rigorous. 

Here is the specific breakdown of why LPO was the ideal choice over other standard methods:

### 1. Perfect Match for Structural Recursion
If you look closely at the rules, `plus` and `times` are defined via standard primitive recursion, and `p` is defined by structural unwrapping. In every recursive rule (Rules 2, 5, and 6), an `s` is "peeled off" one of the arguments on the left-hand side, and the function is called on that stripped-down argument on the right-hand side. 
LPO was explicitly designed to handle this exact pattern. Its combination of the **Subterm Property** (which knows that `s(x)` is strictly larger than `x`) and **Lexicographic Descent** (which knows that `times(x, y)` is smaller than `times(s(x), y)`) makes orienting recursive rules practically automatic. 

### 2. Purely Syntactic (No "Magic Numbers")
The prompt placed strict constraints on not importing hidden assumptions or structural axioms. LPO is purely syntactic. It relies *only* on the shapes of the terms and a simple hierarchical list of the symbols (the precedence). 
By contrast, if I had chosen **Polynomial Interpretations**, I would have had to invent arbitrary mathematical functions (e.g., setting the interpretation of $s(x)$ to $x + 1$ and $times(x,y)$ to $x \times y + x + 1$). While valid, this requires introducing external arithmetic, proving monotonicity, and doing algebra—all of which feel like "importing external models," which brushed too closely against your constraints.

### 3. Zero Overhead Compared to Other Syntactic Methods
Other purely syntactic methods exist, but they have unnecessary overhead for a system this simple:
*   **Knuth-Bendix Ordering (KBO):** KBO requires you to assign a numerical "weight" to every symbol (e.g., $w(s)=1, w(times)=0$) in addition to a precedence, and then you must verify weight balances for every rule. Because no rules in this system increase the raw variable count in a way that requires weight-balancing, KBO's weight equations are unnecessary busywork here.
*   **Dependency Pairs:** This is the modern workhorse for automated termination provers. However, for a human proof, it is highly verbose. I would have had to define dependency pairs, draw a dependency graph, and then still use a reduction pair (like LPO or polynomials) to eliminate the cycles. It is a heavy-duty tool meant for systems where LPO fails, but here, LPO succeeds in one step.

### Summary
I picked LPO because the system consists entirely of primitive recursive functions. LPO handles primitive recursion natively without requiring weight assignments (like KBO), external arithmetic (like Polynomials), or complex graph generation (like Dependency Pairs). It provided the cleanest, most unassailable proof while strictly obeying your boundary conditions.
