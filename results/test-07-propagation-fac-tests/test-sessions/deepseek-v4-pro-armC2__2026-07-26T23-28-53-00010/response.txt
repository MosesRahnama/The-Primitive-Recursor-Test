Yes, termination can be established from the given rewrite system alone.  

**Reasoning**:  
The rules for `plus`, `times`, and `p` are structurally recursive (they strictly decrease the number of `s`/`0` constructors or the depth of `p` in a way that must terminate). The `fac` rule applies only to `s(x)`. For any finite term, rewriting `fac(s(x))` produces `times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`. The argument `p(s(x))` either eventually collapses to a term `s(...)` with fewer `s` symbols (if the term is ground) or becomes stuck in a normal form without infinite regress (if a variable prevents further `p` reductions). In both cases any rewrite sequence is finite. Standard termination techniques (e.g., polynomial/matrix interpretations or the dependency pair method) can prove this without importing any external axioms about natural numbers.
