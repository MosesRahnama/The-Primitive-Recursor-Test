**Yes—the dependency-pair/minimal-chain method itself complies**, because dependency pairs are a meta-level proof technique, not additional object-level rewrite rules. It does not require assuming \(p(s(x))=x\), restricting variables to numerals, or adding guards or side conditions to the TRS.

However, my earlier explanation was somewhat too informal. In particular, saying that a finite term cannot supply infinitely many `s` layers needs care because arbitrary substituted terms may themselves contain reducible `fac`, `plus`, or `times` expressions that expose `s` constructors.

The missing formal point is supplied by the **minimal-chain property**: in a hypothetical minimal infinite dependency-pair chain, the proper argument subterms are terminating. Because the TRS is finitely branching, each such terminating term has only finitely many reducts and hence a finite maximum number of leading `s` constructors it can expose. Each transition

\[
FAC(s(x))\to FAC(p(s(x)))
\]

that enables another `FAC` step must use

\[
p(s(s(z)))\to s(p(s(z))),
\]

and thereby consumes one level of that finite potential. Thus an infinite minimal chain is impossible.

So the proposed method meets the requirements, but my original response was only a proof sketch; it should have made the minimal-chain argument explicit rather than relying on the loose phrase “a finite term cannot provide such an infinite progression.”
