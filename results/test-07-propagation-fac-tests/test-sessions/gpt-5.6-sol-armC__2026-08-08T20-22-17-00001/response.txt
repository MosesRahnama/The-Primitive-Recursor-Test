**Assessment: yes.** Termination can be established from the rewrite rules alone, although not by simply assuming the arithmetic identity \(p(s(x))=x\).

The `plus`, `times`, and `p` subsystem is terminating under standard syntactic termination orderings: their recursive calls reduce the relevant constructor structure. The only non-obvious dependency is

\[
fac(s(x)) \to fac(p(s(x))).
\]

For another `fac` step to occur, `p(s(x))` must rewrite to a term headed by `s`. By the given `p` rules, that can happen only when `x` itself exposes another leading `s`; the rule

\[
p(s(s(z))) \to s(p(s(z)))
\]

consumes one such layer. An infinite sequence of factorial calls would therefore require infinitely many successive `s` layers to be exposed. The remaining subsystem is terminating and finitely branching, so a finite term cannot provide such an infinite progression. This can be formalized with dependency-pair/minimal-chain reasoning.

Thus no external natural-number axioms or structural assumptions are needed; the conclusion follows syntactically from the presented TRS.
