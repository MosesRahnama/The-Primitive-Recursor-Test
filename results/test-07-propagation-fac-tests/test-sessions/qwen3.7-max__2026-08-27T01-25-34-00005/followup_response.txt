I chose the **Dependency Pair (DP) framework combined with a weakly monotonic semantic interpretation** because standard, purely syntactic termination methods and standard strict polynomial interpretations fundamentally fail on this specific term rewriting system (TRS). 

Here is the detailed breakdown of why alternative methods fail and why this specific combination was necessary:

### 1. Failure of Knuth-Bendix Ordering (KBO)
KBO requires that for every rule $l \to r$, every variable must occur at least as many times on the left-hand side (LHS) as on the right-hand side (RHS). 
* Look at the factorial rule: `fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`
* The variable `x` occurs **once** on the LHS.
* The variable `x` occurs **twice** on the RHS (once inside `p(s(x))` and once inside the second argument `s(x)`).
Because $1 \not\ge 2$, KBO is immediately and unconditionally ruled out.

### 2. Failure of Recursive Path Ordering (RPO / LPO)
RPO requires that the arguments of recursive calls on the RHS be strict syntactic subterms of the arguments on the LHS (or reducible to them via a precedence).
* In the `fac` rule, the recursive call is `fac(p(s(x)))`. 
* The argument `p(s(x))` is **not a syntactic subterm** of `s(x)`. 
* Because `p` is a defined symbol (not a free constructor), RPO cannot easily establish that `p(s(x))` is "smaller" than `s(x)` using just precedence and status. Syntactic path orderings choke on defined symbols acting as "decreasers" in recursive arguments.

### 3. Failure of Standard Strict Polynomial Interpretations
Standard polynomial interpretations over $\mathbb{N}$ require the interpretation functions to be **strictly monotonic** (i.e., $x > y \implies f(x) > f(y)$). 
* To prove termination of `fac`, we need the interpretation of the recursive argument to strictly decrease: $[p(s(x))] < [s(x)]$.
* If $[s](x) = x + 1$, we need $[p](x+1) < x + 1$, which implies $[p]$ must act as a predecessor function (e.g., $[p](y) = y - 1$).
* However, $y - 1$ is not a valid strictly monotonic polynomial over $\mathbb{N}$ (it can yield negative numbers, and strictly monotonic polynomials over $\mathbb{N}$ must grow at least as fast as $y + c$). Thus, standard strict polynomial interpretations cannot model the predecessor function `p` while maintaining the strict decrease required for `fac`.

### 4. Why the Dependency Pair (DP) Framework?
Because the semantic size of the *entire* RHS of the `fac` rule (`times(fac(...), s(x))`) is actually much larger than the LHS, we cannot prove $LHS > RHS$ directly. 
* The DP framework isolates the recursive calls. It allows us to ignore the "bookkeeping" context (`times`) and only require that the recursive call itself decreases: `FAC#(s(x)) > FAC#(p(s(x)))`. 
* This bypasses the size explosion caused by `times` and focuses the proof strictly on the descent of the arguments.

### 5. Why a Weakly Monotonic Semantic Interpretation?
To solve the predecessor problem that breaks strict polynomial interpretations, I used a **weakly monotonic semantic interpretation** (specifically, the exact mathematical value over $\mathbb{N}$).
* By defining $V(p(t)) = \max(0, V(t) - 1)$, we perfectly capture the semantic behavior of the predecessor function without violating the non-negativity of $\mathbb{N}$.
* Because the interpretation is *invariant* (i.e., $V(LHS) = V(RHS)$ for all rules), rewriting below the root (the chain condition in DP) never increases the semantic value. 
* This allows us to easily prove that the semantic value of the argument strictly decreases at every Dependency Pair step: $V(p(s(x))) = V(x) < V(x) + 1 = V(s(x))$. 

**Summary:** 
Syntactic methods fail due to variable duplication (KBO) and non-subterm recursive arguments (RPO). Standard strict semantic methods fail due to the monotonicity requirements of the predecessor function. The DP framework isolates the recursive descent, and the weakly monotonic natural-number interpretation cleanly handles the predecessor, making this combination the most direct and robust way to prove termination for this system.
