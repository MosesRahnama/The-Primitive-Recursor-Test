**Yes.** Termination can be established from the rewrite rules alone, although not by the simplest syntactic “recursive call is a subterm” argument.

A semantic-labeling proof is available. Interpret terms over the naturals by

- \(0=0\), \(s(n)=n+1\),
- \(p(n)=\max(n-1,0)\),
- `plus` and `times` as addition and multiplication,
- `fac` as factorial.

Every rule preserves this interpretation. Label recursive symbols by the relevant argument value. Then each recursive call has a strictly smaller label:

- `plus`: \(n+1 \to n\),
- `times`: \(n+1 \to n\) in its first argument,
- `p`: \(n+2 \to n+1\),
- `fac`: \(n+1 \to n\), since \(p(s(x))\) has value \(n\).

The resulting labeled system is orientable by a well-founded recursive path ordering, with `fac` above `times`, and `times` above `plus`. Thus the original TRS terminates on finite first-order terms. No external object-level axiom such as “predecessor is structurally smaller” is required; the interpretation is a metatheoretic termination measure verified directly against the given rules.
