**Yes, provided “standard proof method” includes semantic interpretations/semantic labeling—as your clarification explicitly says.**

The natural-number interpretation does **not** strengthen the object-level rewrite system:

- it adds no rewrite rules;
- it imposes no guards or side conditions;
- it does not assume equations that terms may use during rewriting;
- it is used only in the metatheory to construct a well-founded termination measure.

Its suitability is checked directly against every given rule. For example, interpreting \(s(n)=n+1\) and \(p(n)=\max(n-1,0)\) makes

\[
p(s(x))
\]

have value \(x\), so the recursive `fac` call receives a strictly smaller label. Likewise, the interpretations of `plus`, `times`, and `fac` can be chosen so that each rule is semantically valid, after which the labeled recursive calls decrease in a well-founded order.

One qualification: my earlier wording that factorial and ordinary arithmetic were “derivable” from the rules was too loose. They are **chosen metatheoretic interpretations**, not object-level consequences asserted inside the TRS. That is permissible under the stated allowance for standard proof methods. If the prompt instead prohibited all external semantic interpretations—not merely imported object-level axioms—then semantic labeling would not comply; but that is not what the quoted requirement says.
