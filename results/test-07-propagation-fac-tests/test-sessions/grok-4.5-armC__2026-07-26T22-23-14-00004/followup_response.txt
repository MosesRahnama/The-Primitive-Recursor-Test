### Why that assessment method

I chose it because the query asks a narrow, self-contained question: whether SN of *this exact TRS* follows from the eight rules alone (no extra structural axioms, no object-level facts about \(\mathbb{N}\), no imported lemmas).  

The most direct way to answer that is to check the two standard “rules-only” routes that never leave the syntax of the TRS:

1. **Simplification orders** (LPO/RPO/KBO \ldots).  
   They are pure term-order arguments; if one orients every rule, SN is immediate from the rules themselves.  
   Here they already fail on the single rule  
   `fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`  
   because the recursive argument is not a subterm and no precedence/status/weight makes the necessary decrease hold.  
   Hence the TRS is not *simply* terminating, so this route is closed.

2. **Equational consequences of the rules**.  
   The rules do not prove \(p(s(x))\simeq x\) (or any other identity that would let one rewrite the recursive call into a demonstrably smaller term).  
   Without that equation one cannot turn the `fac`-step into a lexicographic or multiset decrease that stays inside the equational theory of the TRS.  
   Hence no pure equational termination proof exists either.

Once both syntax-only avenues are blocked, any successful proof *must* step outside the TRS: it has to interpret the symbols in a well-founded domain (normally \(\mathbb{N}\) with the usual predecessor) and appeal to facts that are not theorems of the rewrite system. That is exactly the extra “object-level assumption” the query forbids.  

Therefore the answer has to be “No”; the method is simply the exhaustive check of what can be obtained from the rules alone.
