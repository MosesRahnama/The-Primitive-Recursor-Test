### Assessment: No

The given TRS (a variant of addition/multiplication/predecessor/factorial, with a non-base-case `fac`) is in fact SN, but this cannot be established from the rules alone.

- The rules for `plus`, `times` and `p` are oriented by a standard syntactic simplification order (e.g., LPO with precedence `times > plus > p > s > 0`), and are therefore (simply) terminating by themselves.
- The `fac` rule is the obstacle. Its recursive call is *not* on a subterm of the left-hand side. Consequently every simplification order fails to orient it: already the necessary comparison `s(x) > p(s(x))` is impossible in LPO/RPO (regardless of precedence or status) and the same rule also increases every reasonable Knuth-Bendix weight. The TRS is therefore not simply terminating.
- Any successful termination argument must therefore leave the purely syntactic realm. Typical proofs interpret the signature in the natural numbers (reading `p` as predecessor), appeal to the well-foundedness of \(>\) on \(\mathbb{N}\), and only then show that every `fac`-step decreases a suitable measure. Those semantic ingredients are object-level assumptions about the intended domain; they are not derivable from the eight rewrite rules themselves (the rules do not even prove the equation \(p(s(x))\simeq x\)).

Hence termination, while true, cannot be concluded from the presented rewrite system in isolation.
