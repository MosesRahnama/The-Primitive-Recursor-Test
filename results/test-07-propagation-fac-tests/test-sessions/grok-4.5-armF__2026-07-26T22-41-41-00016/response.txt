### Verdict
Yes, termination is established from the given rules alone (via a standard simplification order on the term syntax).

### Termination Argument
We use the lexicographic path order (LPO) induced by the precedence
\[
\texttt{times} > \texttt{plus} > \texttt{s} > \texttt{0}.
\]
LPO is a well-founded simplification order on first-order terms; hence it suffices to verify that each rule is an LPO-descent. (Variables are compared only by identity; no extra axioms, sorts, or interpretations are used.)

- \(\texttt{times}(x,0) \to 0\):  
  root \(\texttt{times} > 0\), and the left-hand side has no further arguments to check.

- \(\texttt{times}(x,\texttt{s}(y)) \to \texttt{plus}(\texttt{times}(x,y),x)\):  
  root \(\texttt{times} > \texttt{plus}\), so it is enough that the left-hand side strictly exceeds every argument of the right-hand side.  
  - \(\texttt{times}(x,\texttt{s}(y)) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} x\) holds because \(x\) is a proper subterm.  
  - \(\texttt{times}(x,\texttt{s}(y)) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} \texttt{times}(x,y)\) holds by the lexicographic status of equal roots: the argument tuples satisfy \((x,\texttt{s}(y)) >_{\mathrm{lex}} (x,y)\) (first components identical, second components \(\texttt{s}(y) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} y\)).

- \(\texttt{plus}(x,0) \to x\):  
  the right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.

- \(\texttt{plus}(0,x) \to x\):  
  likewise a proper-subterm descent.

- \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y)) \to \texttt{s}(\texttt{plus}(x,y))\):  
  root \(\texttt{plus} > \texttt{s}\), so it is enough that the left-hand side exceeds the single argument of the right-hand side.  
  \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y)) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} \texttt{plus}(x,y)\) holds by lexicographic status: \((x,\texttt{s}(y)) >_{\mathrm{lex}} (x,y)\).

- \(\texttt{plus}(\texttt{s}(x),y) \to \texttt{s}(\texttt{plus}(x,y))\):  
  identical reasoning with the symmetric argument tuple \((\texttt{s}(x),y) >_{\mathrm{lex}} (x,y)\).

Every rule is therefore an LPO-descent. Consequently every rewrite sequence is finite.

(The same precedence also orients the rules under the multiset path order, but LPO already suffices.)
