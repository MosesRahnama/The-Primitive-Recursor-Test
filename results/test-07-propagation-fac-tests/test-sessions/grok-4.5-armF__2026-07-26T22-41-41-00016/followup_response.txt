### Why LPO

I chose the lexicographic path order for three tightly related reasons that stay inside the problem constraints:

1. **It is a purely syntactic, well-founded simplification order**  
   LPO is defined directly on the first-order terms generated by the given signature \(\{0,\texttt{s},\texttt{plus},\texttt{times}\}\).  Its only parameters are a precedence on those four symbols and a lexicographic status for each symbol.  No semantic domain, no arithmetic interpretation, no induction axioms, no extra rewrite rules, and no side conditions are required.  That matches the demand that termination be read off “the presented rules alone.”

2. **The rules are already oriented by a natural precedence**  
   The two recursive rules for \(\texttt{times}\) build a \(\texttt{plus}\)-term whose recursive call is a proper subterm; the four rules for \(\texttt{plus}\) merely push a single \(\texttt{s}\) outward.  The precedence
   \[
   \texttt{times} > \texttt{plus} > \texttt{s} > 0
   \]
   therefore makes every left-hand side strictly larger than its right-hand side by the ordinary LPO recursive clauses (root comparison or lexicographic argument comparison).  Checking the six inequalities is a finite, mechanical inspection of the given syntax; nothing else has to be assumed.

3. **It is a standard, elementary method that needs no strengthening**  
   Among the classical techniques that apply to untyped first-order TRSs (KBO, RPO/LPO, polynomial interpretations, dependency pairs, \ldots), LPO is the lightest that succeeds here.  A polynomial interpretation would also work, but it imports an external well-founded order on the naturals and requires proving that the interpretation of each right-hand side is numerically smaller—an object-level arithmetic argument that the problem statement explicitly discourages.  Dependency pairs or semantic labelling would be heavier still.  LPO stays inside the pure term algebra and therefore satisfies the “no imported structural axioms” boundary while still giving a complete termination proof.

In short, LPO is the method that is simultaneously sufficient, purely syntactic, and minimal with respect to the restrictions stated in the query.
