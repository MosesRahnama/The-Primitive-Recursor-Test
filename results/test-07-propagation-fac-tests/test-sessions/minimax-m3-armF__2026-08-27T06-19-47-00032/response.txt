### Termination Argument (Recursive Path Order)

I use the **Recursive Path Order (RPO)** with a simple precedence on the defined function symbols:

$$\text{times} \;>\; \text{plus} \;>\; s \;>\; 0$$

and treat variables as the smallest terms. The RPO is a well-founded quasi-order on ground terms (and admits variables), so it suffices to show every rule is **strictly oriented** $l \rhd r$.

---

**Rule 1.** `times(x, 0) → 0`

The RHS `0` is a subterm of the LHS. By the subterm clause of RPO, `times(x,0) ≻ 0`. ✓

**Rule 2.** `times(x, s(y)) → plus(times(x, y), x)`

The top symbol of the LHS is `times` and the top symbol of the RHS is `plus`, with `times ≻ plus`. The two immediate subterms of the RHS, namely `times(x, y)` and `x`, are both subterms of the LHS, hence are each ≤ the LHS under RPO. So the "lexicographic/multiset" clause of RPO yields `times(x, s(y)) ≻ plus(times(x, y), x)`. ✓

**Rule 3.** `plus(x, 0) → x`

The RHS `x` is a subterm of the LHS. By the subterm clause, `plus(x,0) ≻ x`. ✓

**Rule 4.** `plus(0, x) → x`

The RHS `x` is a subterm of the LHS. By the subterm clause, `plus(0,x) ≻ x`. ✓

**Rule 5.** `plus(x, s(y)) → s(plus(x, y))`

The top symbol of the LHS is `plus`, the top symbol of the RHS is `s`, with `plus ≻ s`. The unique argument of the RHS's `s`, namely `plus(x, y)`, is a subterm of the LHS, so it is ≤ the LHS. The status of variables is also covered. Hence `plus(x, s(y)) ≻ s(plus(x, y))`. ✓

**Rule 6.** `plus(s(x), y) → s(plus(x, y))`

The top symbol of the LHS is `plus`, the top symbol of the RHS is `s`, with `plus ≻ s`. The unique argument `plus(x, y)` of the RHS's `s` is a subterm of the LHS, hence ≤ the LHS. So `plus(s(x), y) ≻ s(plus(x, y))`. ✓
