I chose a **polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\)** because it gives a single, syntax-directed measure that can handle all seven rules uniformly.

The main obstacle is the multiplication rule:

\[
\mathrm{times}(s(x),y)\to \mathrm{plus}(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y).
\]

A simple term-size measure fails here because the right-hand side can be larger. Likewise, a naïve “recursive argument decreases” explanation is insufficient: although \(x\) decreases inside `times`, the rule introduces `plus` and duplicates the occurrence of \(y\) in the interpreted structure.

Polynomial interpretations are designed for this situation:

- `times` can assign enough weight to the interaction between its arguments, via the product term \(3ab\);
- the coefficients can make decreasing the first `times` argument outweigh the added `plus(...,y)`;
- `p` can be weighted more heavily than `s`, handling
  \[
  p(s(s(x)))\to s(p(s(x)));
  \]
- strict monotonicity ensures that a decrease at the rewritten subterm remains a decrease inside any surrounding context;
- the inequalities hold for every natural-number valuation, so substitutions are covered automatically.

I therefore looked for coefficients satisfying all rule inequalities. The displayed interpretation is one workable solution, not a canonical or uniquely necessary one. I picked it because every rule then reduces to a short arithmetic inequality, giving a self-contained termination proof without imposing semantic assumptions about what `plus`, `times`, or `p` are “supposed” to mean.
