I chose **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)** for several specific reasons:

### 1. The System is Structurally Recursive
The core mechanism of this term rewriting system is structural recursion on the third argument of $F$ (decreasing from $S(n)$ to $n$). Path orderings like LPO are specifically designed to capture structural descent (where a term is replaced by a function of its strictly smaller subterms) without needing to map terms to external domains like natural numbers.

### 2. Handling Symbol Introduction ($G$)
In the second rule, $F(x, y, S(n)) \rightarrow G(y, F(x, y, n))$, the function symbol $G$ appears on the right-hand side but not on the left-hand side. 
* LPO handles this naturally via **precedence** ($F \succ G$). This precedence formally encodes the intuition that $F$ is the "dominant" driver of the computation, and it has the "right" to generate $G$ during its evaluation.

### 3. Syntactic and Self-Contained
Your prompt requested a proof *“without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given syntax.”* 
* Other methods, like **Polynomial Interpretations**, require defining mapping functions into the natural numbers (e.g., $[F](x,y,z) = x + y + z^2$) and then relying on the algebraic axioms of arithmetic (like addition and multiplication over $\mathbb{N}$) to prove the inequalities.
* LPO is **purely syntactic**. It operates entirely on the syntax of the terms themselves using only basic set-theoretic relations (like projection/subterms and lexicographic comparison of tuples). It requires no external mathematical machinery or arithmetic theories.

### 4. Decidability and Automation
LPO is a highly algorithmic, syntax-directed decision procedure. Once the precedence $F \succ G$ is chosen, the proof proceeds via deterministic syntactic rules, making the verification of termination absolute, transparent, and easy to follow.
