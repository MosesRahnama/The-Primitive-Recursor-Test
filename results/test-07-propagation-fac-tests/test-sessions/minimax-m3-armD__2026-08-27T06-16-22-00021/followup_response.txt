# Why LPO?

I picked the **Lexicographic Path Order (LPO)** for several reasons tied to the specific shape of this system.

## What the system actually looks like

The rewrite rules exhibit two structural patterns that drove the choice:

1. **Nested mutual recursion in `times`**: Rule 5 calls `times` on a strictly smaller first argument, and the result feeds into a `plus` that will recurse on its second argument. Termination here requires reasoning about the **call graph between `times` and `plus` together**, not just each function in isolation.

2. **Plain structural recursion in `plus` and `p`**: Rules 1, 2, 6, 7 all recurse on a strict subterm of one specific argument.

## Why LPO fits naturally

**LPO gives precedence + lexicographic comparison in one tool.** The single precedence `times > plus > p > s > 0` does two distinct jobs:

- `times > plus` lets Rule 5 "discharge" the obligation to show `times(s(x), y) > plus(…)` by precedence, and then the only thing left to check is the recursive `times(x, y)` call. Lexicographic comparison of `(s(x), y) vs (x, y)` immediately certifies that the first argument strictly decreased.
- `plus > s` lets Rule 2 reduce to checking `plus(x, s(y)) > plus(x, y)`, which lexicographic comparison resolves via `s(y) > y`.

So LPO handled mutual recursion and the "first-argument decreases, second-argument is preserved" pattern in one uniform machinery, with no auxiliary lemmas.

## Why I rejected the natural alternatives

- **Polynomial interpretations.** They would also work, but every rule here has the form "the result is structurally smaller in an obvious way." Polynomial interpretations are stronger machinery than the rules require, and finding the right polynomials (especially handling the mutual recursion in Rule 5 with a non-trivial mapping for `plus` and `times`) is more work than the structure of the rules demands.

- **Size-Change Termination (SCT).** This would handle the system, but the mutual recursion forces a global argument: each `times` step decreases the first argument, which bounds the chain of `plus` calls produced by Rule 5, which in turn bounds the next `times` step, etc. That's the right idea but it's exactly the kind of "compose two size-change arguments across the call graph" reasoning that LPO packages for free.

- **A hand-rolled measure / norm.** The mutual recursion makes this fragile: any measure that decreases per rewrite step must simultaneously track the sizes of `(x, y)` across both `times` and `plus`, and you essentially re-derive LPO on this particular system.

- **A simpler path order (e.g., plain subterm order, or a non-lexicographic variant).** These cannot certify Rule 5, because the LHS is *not* a subterm of the RHS, nor vice versa — neither contains the other, and a precedence-only order without lexicographic comparison cannot orient the rule.

## Why the verdict remains within the rules

LPO is defined purely on the term algebra of the given signature. Its well-foundedness is a meta-theorem; applying it here requires no extra rewrite rules, no side conditions, no object-level axioms — just the precedence I stated. That matches the constraint in your prompt exactly.
