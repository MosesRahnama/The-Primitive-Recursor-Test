### Verdict
Yes, termination of the given TRS can be established from the rules alone (via a standard simplification order).

### Termination argument
The signature consists of the constant \(Z\), the unary symbol \(S\), the binary symbol \(G\), and the ternary symbol \(F\). Consider the lexicographic path order (LPO) induced by the precedence \(F > G > S > Z\) (with lexicographic status, left-to-right). This is a well-founded strict order on terms that is closed under contexts and substitutions. It orients both rules, as follows.

- Rule \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\): the left-hand side is rooted by \(F\), so it is strictly larger than each of its proper subterms (in particular the variable \(x\)).
- Rule \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\): the precedence gives \(F > G\), so it is enough to check that the left-hand side is strictly larger than each immediate subterm of the right-hand side.
  - \(F(x,y,S(n)) > y\) holds because \(y\) is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
  - \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) holds because the two sides have the same root and the arguments compare lexicographically: the first two arguments are identical, while the third arguments satisfy \(S(n) > n\) (again because \(n\) is a proper subterm).

Thus every rewrite step (in any context, under any substitution) strictly decreases the LPO measure, so there are no infinite rewrite sequences.

(The same orientation is obtained from the multiset path order with the same precedence, or from a suitable Knuth–Bendix order; the argument never adds rules, guards, or side-conditions to the original system, nor does it rely on any object-level axioms beyond the free term algebra generated by the signature.)
