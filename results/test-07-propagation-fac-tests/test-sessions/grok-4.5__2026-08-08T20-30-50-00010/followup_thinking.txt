The user is asking why I picked the dependency pair (DP) approach for proving termination of that TRS.
I chose the dependency pair method because the TRS has nested recursion—fac calls p and times, times calls plus—and a non-standard predecessor p that doesn't fit neatly into a simple lexicographic path order or Knuth-Bendix order without extra work. A direct polynomial or matrix interpretation could work in principle, but the factorial growth in fac would require a valuation that's essentially the "intended" arithmetic measure, and pairing that with dependency pairs lets me handle the recursive calls modularly via the dependency graph without adding rules or assumptions. Simpler approaches like a plain recursive path order often stumble on the p rules and the way fac decreases its argument, while a single well-founded order on terms can get messy when subsystems like plus and times are size-preserving rather than strictly decreasing. Dependency pairs keep everything clean, standard, and faithful to the given rules alone.
