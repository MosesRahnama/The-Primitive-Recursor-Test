### Verdict
Yes, termination can be established from the given rules and syntax alone (via a standard method: the dependency-pair (DP) approach, supported by a simple valuation on the term algebra that is fully determined by the signature).

### Termination argument
All reasoning below is purely on the free term algebra over the signature \(\{0, s, p, \mathit{plus}, \mathit{times}, \mathit{fac}\}\) (variables included). No rules are added, no side-conditions/guards are imposed, and no external object-level axioms (injectivity of \(s\), induction schemata, etc.) are imported. The only meta-level ingredients are the well-foundedness of \(\mathbb{N}\) (standard for any termination argument that ultimately maps into a numeric measure) and the DP theorem (a standard, syntax-directed proof method for first-order TRSs).

#### 1. The valuation \(v\)
Define a function \(v\) from terms to \(\mathbb{N}\) by structural recursion on the syntax (set \(v(x)=0\) for every variable \(x\)):
\[
\begin{align*}
v(0)&=0,\\
v(s(t))&=v(t)+1,\\
v(p(t))&=v(t)\ominus 1,\\
v(\mathit{plus}(t,u))&=v(t)+v(u),\\
v(\mathit{times}(t,u))&=v(t)\cdot v(u),\\
v(\mathit{fac}(t))&=v(t)!.
\end{align*}
\]
(This is well-defined on every term; factorial and monus are the ordinary meta-level operations on \(\mathbb{N}\).)

Direct inspection of each of the eight rules shows that \(v\) is preserved (i.e., \(v(\ell)=v(r)\)):
- \(\mathit{plus}(x,0)\to x\): both sides evaluate to \(v(x)\).
- \(\mathit{plus}(x,s(y))\to s(\mathit{plus}(x,y))\): both sides evaluate to \(v(x)+v(y)+1\).
- \(\mathit{times}(0,y)\to 0\): both sides evaluate to \(0\).
- \(\mathit{times}(x,0)\to 0\): both sides evaluate to \(0\).
- \(\mathit{times}(s(x),y)\to\mathit{plus}(\mathit{times}(x,y),y)\): both sides evaluate to \((v(x)+1)\cdot v(y)\).
- \(p(s(s(x)))\to s(p(s(x)))\): both sides evaluate to \(v(x)+1\).
- \(p(s(0))\to 0\): both sides evaluate to \(0\).
- \(\mathit{fac}(s(x))\to\mathit{times}(\mathit{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x))\): both sides evaluate to \((v(x)+1)!\)
  (because \(v(p(s(x)))=v(x)\)).

Consequently every rewrite step (and therefore every finite rewrite sequence) preserves \(v\). The same holds after arbitrary substitution.

#### 2. Dependency pairs
The defined symbols are \(\mathit{plus}\), \(\mathit{times}\), \(p\) and \(\mathit{fac}\). The dependency pairs of the system are exactly:
\begin{align*}
\mathit{plus}^\#(x,s(y))&\to\mathit{plus}^\#(x,y),\\
\mathit{times}^\#(s(x),y)&\to\mathit{times}^\#(x,y),\\
\mathit{times}^\#(s(x),y)&\to\mathit{plus}^\#(\mathit{times}(x,y),y),\\
p^\#(s(s(x)))&\to p^\#(s(x)),\\
\mathit{fac}^\#(s(x))&\to\mathit{fac}^\#(p(s(x))),\\
\mathit{fac}^\#(s(x))&\to\mathit{times}^\#(\mathit{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x)).
\end{align*}
(The pairs arise mechanically by marking the root of each left-hand side and collecting every marked subterm of a right-hand side whose root is defined; no extra rules are introduced.)

By the DP theorem it is sufficient to show that no infinite DP-chain exists. A DP-chain is a (possibly infinite) sequence of the pairs above connected by reverse rewrite steps of the original system after substitution. Because every original rule preserves \(v\), every connecting rewrite sequence also preserves \(v\) of the relevant arguments.

#### 3. The dependency graph has only trivial cyclic SCCs
The only possible edges among the six pairs are:
- the \(\mathit{plus}^\#\)-pair loops only to itself,
- the \(p^\#\)-pair loops only to itself,
- the two \(\mathit{times}^\#\)-pairs stay inside \(\{\mathit{times}^\#\}\) or exit permanently into \(\mathit{plus}^\#\),
- the two \(\mathit{fac}^\#\)-pairs stay inside \(\{\mathit{fac}^\#\}\) or exit permanently into \(\mathit{times}^\#\).

There are therefore four cyclic SCCs, each consisting of a single looping pair (the exiting pairs cannot participate in any cycle). It is enough to derive a contradiction from the assumption that any one of those four pairs occurs infinitely often in a chain.

#### 4. Infinite descent on each cyclic SCC
- Suppose the \(\mathit{plus}^\#\)-pair occurs infinitely often. Each connecting rewrite must turn the second argument \(y\) into a term headed by \(s\). Preservation of \(v\) immediately yields a strictly decreasing sequence of natural numbers \(v(y)>v(y')>\dots\), which is impossible.
- Suppose the \(p^\#\)-pair occurs infinitely often. Each connecting rewrite must turn the argument of the right-hand side into a term headed by \(s(s(\_\))\). Preservation of \(v\) again produces an infinite descent in \(\mathbb{N}\).
- Suppose the looping \(\mathit{times}^\#\)-pair occurs infinitely often. Each connecting rewrite must turn the first argument \(x\) into a term headed by \(s\). Preservation of \(v\) produces an infinite descent in \(\mathbb{N}\).
- Suppose the looping \(\mathit{fac}^\#\)-pair occurs infinitely often. Each connecting rewrite must turn \(p(s(x))\) into a term headed by \(s\). Preservation of \(v\) gives
  \[
  v(p(s(x)))=v(x)=v(z)+1
  \]
  (the case \(v(x)=0\) is impossible because the right-hand side would then have valuation at least \(1\)). This is again an infinite descent in \(\mathbb{N}\).

In every case a contradiction is reached. Hence no infinite DP-chain exists.

#### 5. Conclusion
By the DP theorem the original TRS is terminating. Every one of the eight rules has been used: the first seven generate the DPs that are examined for cycles, the eighth generates the two \(\mathit{fac}^\#\) pairs, and all eight are required to establish that \(v\) is invariant (the invariant that fuels the descents).

(The same argument specialises without change to ground terms; non-ground infinite sequences would instantiate to ground infinite sequences by substituting \(0\) for every variable, which is again impossible.)

Thus termination follows from the presented rules and syntax alone.
