### Why dependency pairs (DPs)

The TRS is a first-order system over a free algebra with nested defined symbols (`fac` calls `times` and `p`; `times` calls `plus`) and with rules that are not size-decreasing (e.g. the recursive `times` rule duplicates its second argument, and the `fac` rule builds a `times` node). A single global reduction order must therefore orient both the “administrative” rules (`plus`, `times`, `p`) and the genuinely recursive `fac` rule at once. Standard simplification orders run into immediate obstacles:

- Lexicographic / recursive path orders need a precedence and a status that simultaneously make  
  `times(s(x),y) ≻ plus(times(x,y),y)`  
  and  
  `fac(s(x)) ≻ times(fac(p(s(x))),s(x))`  
  while still orienting the non-strictly-decreasing `p`-rules. No such precedence exists without additional argument filtering or status tricks that effectively recreate the dependency-graph analysis.
- Polynomial (or matrix) interpretations are blocked by `fac`: any polynomial large enough to satisfy the `fac` inequality grows slower than factorial, so the interpretation cannot be strictly decreasing on that rule.

The DP method sidesteps the search for one monolithic order. It extracts, directly from the eight given rules, the six recursive calls that can appear in an infinite reduction (the dependency pairs). The original rules are afterwards used only as a rewrite relation that connects those pairs; they never have to be oriented strictly. The only semantic ingredient required is the valuation \(v\) already determined by the signature (the unique homomorphism that sends the constructors to the obvious arithmetic operations). Because every rule preserves \(v\), every connecting rewrite in a DP-chain preserves \(v\). Consequently each of the four cyclic SCCs of the dependency graph produces an infinite descending sequence in \(\mathbb{N}\), which is impossible.

All of the above stays inside the permitted boundary:

- no extra rewrite rules, guards or side-conditions are added;
- no object-level axioms (injectivity of \(s\), induction principles, \ldots) are imported;
- the only meta-level facts used are the DP theorem itself (a standard, purely syntactic result about first-order TRSs) and the well-foundedness of \(\mathbb{N}\).

Hence DPs give a complete, self-contained termination argument that inspects every rule exactly once (four rules generate the pairs that stay inside cycles; the remaining four are needed to prove that \(v\) is invariant) and that cannot be replaced by a simpler off-the-shelf order without leaving the fragment of methods allowed by the query.
