### Verdict
Yes, termination of the given TRS can be established from the rules alone (via the dependency-pair method equipped with a reduction pair induced by a suitable interpretation into the well-founded domain \(\mathbb{N}\)).

### Termination argument
The signature is \(\Sigma = \{0, s, \mathit{plus}, \mathit{times}, p, \mathit{fac}\}\), with \(0\) a constant and \(s\) unary (constructors) and the remaining symbols defined. The dependency pairs (DPs) of the TRS, obtained in the standard way by marking the root symbol of each left-hand side and extracting every defined-rooted subterm of the corresponding right-hand side, are exactly:

- \(\mathit{plus}^\sharp(x,s(y)) \to \mathit{plus}^\sharp(x,y)\)
- \(\mathit{times}^\sharp(s(x),y) \to \mathit{plus}^\sharp(\mathit{times}(x,y),y)\)
- \(\mathit{times}^\sharp(s(x),y) \to \mathit{times}^\sharp(x,y)\)
- \(p^\sharp(s(s(x))) \to p^\sharp(s(x))\)
- \(\mathit{fac}^\sharp(s(x)) \to \mathit{times}^\sharp(\mathit{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x))\)
- \(\mathit{fac}^\sharp(s(x)) \to \mathit{fac}^\sharp(p(s(x)))\)

(The original rules that contain no defined-rooted subterm on the right-hand side contribute no DPs.) By the dependency-pair theorem it is sufficient to exhibit a reduction pair \((\gtrsim,>)\) such that every original rule satisfies \(\mathit{lhs}\gtrsim\mathit{rhs}\) and every DP satisfies \(\mathit{lhs}>\mathit{rhs}\). (Here \(\gtrsim\) must be a rewrite preorder—i.e., a stable quasi-order closed under contexts—while \(>\) must be a well-founded stable order compatible with \(\gtrsim\).)

Such a pair is induced by the following interpretation into \(\mathbb{N}\) (with the usual order). Unmarked symbols receive the weakly monotonic functions
\[
\begin{align*}
[0] &= 0,\\
[s](t) &= [t]+1,\\
[\mathit{plus}](t,u) &= [t]+[u],\\
[\mathit{times}](t,u) &= [t]\cdot[u],\\
[p](t) &= \max([t]-1,0),\\
[\mathit{fac}](t) &= 0.
\end{align*}
\]
Marked symbols receive the (not necessarily monotonic) functions
\[
\begin{align*}
[\mathit{plus}^\sharp](t,u) &= [u],\\
[\mathit{times}^\sharp](t,u) &= [t]+[u]+1,\\
[p^\sharp](t) &= [t],\\
[\mathit{fac}^\sharp](t) &= 2\cdot[t]+1.
\end{align*}
\]
(The interpretation of a compound term is defined in the evident compositional way; variables are interpreted by arbitrary elements of \(\mathbb{N}\).) The relation \(t\gtrsim u\) holds when \([t]\ge[u]\), and a marked DP \(l^\sharp\to r^\sharp\) is regarded as strictly decreasing when \([l^\sharp]>[r^\sharp]\). Weak monotonicity of every unmarked function guarantees that \(\gtrsim\) is closed under contexts (and it is stable by construction). The order \(>\) on \(\mathbb{N}\) is well-founded, and the two relations are compatible by elementary arithmetic. It remains only to verify the required inequalities on rules and DPs (all of which are identities in \(\mathbb{N}\) and hold for every assignment of natural numbers to the variables).

**Original rules (weak decrease).**  
- \(\mathit{plus}(x,0)\): \([x]+0=[x]\).  
- \(\mathit{plus}(x,s(y))\): \([x]+([y]+1)=([x]+[y])+1\).  
- \(\mathit{times}(0,y)\): \(0\cdot[y]=0\le[y]\) (always \(\ge0\)).  
- \(\mathit{times}(x,0)\): \([x]\cdot0=0\).  
- \(\mathit{times}(s(x),y)\): \(([x]+1)\cdot[y]=[x]\cdot[y]+[y]\).  
- \(p(s(s(x)))\): \(\max(([x]+2)-1,0)=[x]+1\) and \(s(p(s(x)))\) receives the same value.  
- \(p(s(0))\): \(\max(1-1,0)=0\).  
- \(\mathit{fac}(s(x))\): both sides receive \(0\) (since \([\mathit{fac}]\) is constantly zero and therefore \([\mathit{times}](0,s(x))=0\)).

**Dependency pairs (strict decrease).**  
- \(\mathit{plus}^\sharp(x,s(y))\): \([y]+1>[y]\).  
- \(\mathit{times}^\sharp(s(x),y)\): \([x]+[y]+2>[y]\) (right-hand side projected by \([\mathit{plus}^\sharp]\)).  
- \(\mathit{times}^\sharp(s(x),y)\): \([x]+[y]+2>[x]+[y]+1\).  
- \(p^\sharp(s(s(x)))\): \([x]+2>[x]+1\).  
- \(\mathit{fac}^\sharp(s(x))\): \(2([x]+1)+1=[x]\cdot0+[x]+2\) simplifies to \(2[x]+3>[x]+2\).  
- \(\mathit{fac}^\sharp(s(x))\): \(2[x]+3>2\cdot\max(([x]+1)-1,0)+1=2[x]+1\).

All required inequalities hold, so the reduction pair orients the DP problem. Consequently the original TRS is terminating.

(Note that the interpretation uses only the language of arithmetic on \(\mathbb{N}\) at the meta-level; no object-level equations, guards, or extra rewrite rules are added to the given system, nor is any assumption made about the carriers of \(0\) and \(s\) beyond the free term algebra generated by the displayed signature.)
