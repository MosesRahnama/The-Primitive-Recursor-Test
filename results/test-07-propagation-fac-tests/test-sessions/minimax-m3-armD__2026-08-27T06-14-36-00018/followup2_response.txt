## Yes — the method complies

Let me walk through the constraints from the prompt one by one and show that my polynomial interpretation argument meets each of them.

### 1. "Established from the presented rules alone"

I used only the seven rules as written. I did not add new rules, did not modify existing ones, and did not add any conditional variants of them. My proof consists of a *meta‑level* claim ("every rewrite step decreases a certain natural number") about the system, not a change to the system.

### 2. "Without importing structural axioms"

I did not import axioms like commutativity, associativity, distributivity, $\mathsf{s}(x)\neq 0$, injectivity of $\mathsf{s}$, $x+0 = 0+x$, etc. The proof never uses any equation between distinct function symbols or any property of the constructors beyond their syntactic behaviour under the seven rules. The polynomials I assigned are *interpretations* — they are numbers I assign to terms — not statements I am postulating as true of the term algebra.

### 3. "Without … object‑level assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given syntax and rewrite rules"

I did not assume, e.g., that `times` distributes over `plus`, that `plus` is associative, or that the interpretation coincides with the standard arithmetic meaning of these symbols. The constants I picked
$$\mathcal{I}(0)=0,\ \mathcal{I}(\mathsf{s}(x))=x+1,\ \mathcal{I}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y))=x+2y+1,\ \mathcal{I}(\mathsf{times}(x,y))=(2x+1)(2y+1),\ \mathcal{I}(\mathsf{p}(x))=2x$$
are simply a function from terms to $\mathbb{N}$. Their *semantics* (whether they agree with the "real" arithmetic of $\mathbb{N}$) is irrelevant; what matters is the seven inequalities in the table, each of which is a polynomial identity in the variables $x,y$ over $\mathbb{N}$, and each is checked by straightforward expansion — no object‑level fact about the term algebra is invoked.

### 4. "You may use any standard proof method"

Polynomial interpretations are a textbook, standard termination method: introduced by Dershowitz (1979), covered in Terese's *Term Rewriting and All That*, the Termination Problems Database, and implemented in tools like AProVE, TTT2, and CiME. Using it is squarely within "any standard proof method."

### 5. "Provided it does not require strengthening the system with extra rewrite rules"

I introduced no new rewrite rules. In particular, I did *not* use the **dependency‑pair** method, which would have required me to add fresh symbols $F_{\mathsf{plus}}, F_{\mathsf{times}}, F_{\mathsf{p}}$ and new DP rules — that *would* be a strengthening, and I deliberately avoided it. A polynomial interpretation works directly on the original seven rules.

### 6. "Guards, side conditions, or hidden assumptions"

- No guards (no `if`-style preconditions on rule application).
- No side conditions (no extra variables, no `x\neq y`, no `x\neq 0`, no "applied to a normal form only").
- No hidden assumptions: the only ingredients are the seven rules (given) and the well‑foundedness of the strict order $>$ on $\mathbb{N}$ (a meta‑fact about the natural numbers, used as the target of the interpretation). This is not an "assumption about the rewrite system"; it is the well‑founded order that *every* termination proof for a first‑order TRS ultimately relies on, and the prompt's "standard proof method" clause is precisely what licenses its use.

## Caveat I want to be explicit about

The choice of the *specific* polynomials is a meta‑level act of proof‑search on my part; nothing in the rewrite system forces those polynomials. But that is true of *any* termination proof technique (RPO also needs a chosen precedence, a lexicographic combination needs a chosen tuple, etc.). The proof itself — once the polynomials are written down — is a finite, mechanical verification of seven polynomial inequalities and the invocation of the standard soundness theorem. No rule, axiom, or assumption about the system's semantics is smuggled in.
