## Verdict

**Yes, termination can be established from the rules alone**, using a polynomial interpretation (a.k.a. polynomial order) over the natural numbers. No extra rules, guards, or object‑level assumptions are required.

## Termination argument

Define a polynomial interpretation $\mathcal{I}:\mathcal{T}(\Sigma,\mathcal{X})\to\mathbb{N}$ by

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{I}(0) &= 0 \\
\mathcal{I}(\mathsf{s}(x)) &= x+1 \\
\mathcal{I}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y)) &= x+2y+1 \\
\mathcal{I}(\mathsf{times}(x,y)) &= (2x+1)(2y+1)=4xy+2x+2y+1 \\
\mathcal{I}(\mathsf{p}(x)) &= 2x
\end{aligned}
$$

Every right‑hand side is a polynomial in the variables of its left‑hand side with **non‑negative integer coefficients** and is **strictly monotone in every variable that occurs on a left‑hand side**. The standard soundness theorem for polynomial interpretations (cf. Dershowitz, Zantema) then says: if for every rule $l\to r$ and every ground instance $\sigma$ we have $\mathcal{I}(l\sigma)>\mathcal{I}(r\sigma)$, the system terminates. I check this rule by rule.

| # | Rule $l\to r$ | $\mathcal{I}(l)$ | $\mathcal{I}(r)$ | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | $\mathsf{plus}(x,0)\to x$ | $x+1$ | $x$ | $1$ |
| 2 | $\mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))\to \mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y))$ | $x+2(y+1)+1=x+2y+3$ | $(x+2y+1)+1=x+2y+2$ | $1$ |
| 3 | $\mathsf{times}(0,y)\to 0$ | $(1)(2y+1)=2y+1$ | $0$ | $2y+1\ge 1$ |
| 4 | $\mathsf{times}(x,0)\to 0$ | $(2x+1)(1)=2x+1$ | $0$ | $2x+1\ge 1$ |
| 5 | $\mathsf{times}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)\to \mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{times}(x,y),y)$ | $(2x+3)(2y+1)=4xy+2x+6y+3$ | $(2x+1)(2y+1)+2y+1=4xy+2x+4y+2$ | $2y+1\ge 1$ |
| 6 | $\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{s}(x)))\to \mathsf{s}(\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(x)))$ | $2(x+2)=2x+4$ | $1+2(x+1)=2x+3$ | $1$ |
| 7 | $\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(0))\to 0$ | $2\cdot 1=2$ | $0$ | $2$ |

In every row the left‑hand side strictly exceeds the right‑hand side, with a non‑negative difference that is itself strictly positive for every ground substitution $x,y\in\mathbb{N}$. Therefore every rewrite step strictly decreases $\mathcal{I}(\,\cdot\,)$, and since $\mathcal{I}$ maps into $\mathbb{N}$ (which is well‑founded), **no infinite rewrite sequence exists**.

## Scope check

The argument uses only the seven rules as written and the standard polynomial‑interpretation soundness theorem (a generic meta‑result about TRS termination, not an extra object‑level axiom about arithmetic). It introduces no extra rewrite rules, no side conditions, and no strengthening of the system. It addresses every rule. $\square$
