I chose the **Dependency Pair (DP) framework combined with a semantic ordering (like Polynomial Interpretations)** because the most common, simpler methods for proving termination mathematically *fail* on this specific system. 

Here is the step-by-step breakdown of why this specific approach is required:

### 1. Why simpler methods (Simplification Orderings) fail
The standard first-line approach to proving TRS termination is to use a **Simplification Ordering**, such as Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO) or Recursive Path Ordering (RPO). These methods rely heavily on the **subterm property**—the idea that a term is always strictly greater than its subterms (e.g., `s(x) > x`).

Almost all your rules work perfectly with LPO/RPO, because the recursive calls happen on direct subterms. However, this rule ruins it:
`fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`

In this rule, the recursive call applies `fac` to `p(s(x))`. Syntactically, `p(s(x))` is **not** a subterm of `s(x)`. In fact, structurally, `p(s(x))` looks *bigger* than `s(x)` because it introduces a brand new symbol (`p`). A purely structural simplification ordering cannot prove that `p(s(x))` is "smaller" than `s(x)`, so it gets stuck and fails to orient the rule.

### 2. Why the Dependency Pair (DP) Framework is needed
Because `fac` recurses on a term that first needs to be evaluated by another function (`p`), this is a case of recursion on a defined symbol. 

The DP framework is designed exactly for this. Instead of trying to prove that the entire right-hand side of the rule is smaller than the left, DP isolates only the recursive loops. It extracts the dependency pair:
`FAC(s(x)) -> FAC(p(s(x)))`

This strips away the `times` function and allows us to focus entirely on proving that the argument going into the next `FAC` call is strictly decreasing.

### 3. Why Polynomial/Matrix Interpretations complete the proof
Even with the DP extracted, we still have to prove that `p(s(x))` is strictly smaller than `s(x)`. Since we established that structural syntax can't do this, we need a **semantic ordering**.

Polynomial interpretations map terms to numbers (or matrices) in a way that respects the rules. We can easily define an interpretation where:
*   `s(x) = x + 1`
*   `p(x) = x - 1` (with a baseline to prevent negative numbers)

Under this mathematical interpretation, the system easily calculates that `p(s(x))` evaluates to `x`, which is strictly smaller than `s(x)` (which is `x + 1`). 

### Summary
I picked this method because your TRS contains a recursive call on a defined function (`fac(p(s(x)))`). This breaks purely syntactic path orderings. The Dependency Pair framework combined with a Polynomial Interpretation is the standard, most robust automated technique to bridge the gap between syntactic structure and evaluative decrease.
