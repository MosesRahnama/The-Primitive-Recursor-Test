### 2. A Strictly More Compliant Method: Multiset Path Ordering (MPO)
If we interpret "without importing structural axioms" in the strictest possible proof-theoretic sense—meaning **we must not leave the free term algebra $\mathcal{T}(\mathcal{F}, \mathcal{V})$** or assume the existence of arithmetic—then a **syntactic path ordering** is a superior choice. 

We can prove termination using **Multiset Path Ordering (MPO)**. This method is purely syntactic, requiring only a precedence (a partial order) on the function symbols themselves, and relies on no external algebras.

#### The Precedence:
We define a precedence relation on our signature: 
$$F \succ_{prec} G \quad \text{and} \quad F \succ_{prec} S$$

#### The Proof:
We show that for both rules, the LHS is strictly greater than the RHS ($L \succ_{mpo} R$) based purely on the syntactic rules of MPO.

##### **Rule 1: $F(x, y, Z) \rightarrow x$**
* By the **Subterm Property** of MPO: A term is always strictly greater than any of its proper subterms.
* Since $x$ is a subterm of $F(x, y, Z)$, we immediately have:
  $$F(x, y, Z) \succ_{mpo} x$$

##### **Rule 2: $F(x, y, S(n)) \rightarrow G(y, F(x, y, n))$**
We compare $L = F(x, y, S(n))$ and $R = G(y, F(x, y, n))$.
Since the root symbol of $L$ is $F$ and the root symbol of $R$ is $G$, and we have the precedence $F \succ_{prec} G$, the MPO definition requires us to prove two things:

1. **$L \succ_{mpo} t$ for every argument $t$ of $R$:**
   The arguments of $R$ are $y$ and $F(x, y, n)$.
   * **For $y$:** Since $y$ is a direct subterm of $L$, we have $F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{mpo} y$ by the subterm property.
   * **For $F(x, y, n)$:** We compare $F(x, y, S(n))$ with $F(x, y, n)$. Since they have the same root symbol ($F$), we compare their arguments as multisets:
     $$\{x, y, S(n)\} \succ_{mul} \{x, y, n\}$$
     To prove this multiset inequality, we remove the common elements $x$ and $y$, leaving us to compare $\{S(n)\}$ and $\{n\}$. Since $S(n) \succ_{mpo} n$ (by the subterm property), the multiset inequality holds. Thus:
     $$F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{mpo} F(x, y, n)$$

2. **$L$ is not a subterm of any argument of $R$:**
   This trivially holds by size inspection.

Therefore:
$$F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{mpo} G(y, F(x, y, n))$$
