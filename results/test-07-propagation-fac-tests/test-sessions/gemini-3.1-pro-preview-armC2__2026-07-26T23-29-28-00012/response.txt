**Assessment:** Yes, termination of this system can be established purely from the presented rewrite rules.

**Reasoning:**
To prove termination, we must show that there is no infinite rewrite sequence. The rules for `plus` and `times` are strictly decreasing in their first arguments and trivially terminate. 

The primary potential for non-termination lies in the recursive rule:
`fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`

For this to loop infinitely, the term `p(s(x))` would repeatedly have to evaluate to a term of the form `s(...)` so that the `fac(s(...))` rule could trigger again. 

Looking at the rules for `p`:
1. `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))`
2. `p(s(0)) -> 0`

These rules take a `p` and push it inward through consecutive `s` constructors. For any arbitrary term `x`, let `x = s^k(t)` where `t` is a term that does not have `s` at its root. 
- Evaluating `p(s(x))` translates to `p(s(s^k(t)))`, which safely rewrites to `s^k(p(s(t)))`. 
- Since `t` does not begin with `s` (and no rule allows it to arbitrarily become an `s`), `p(s(t))` cannot be evaluated further into an `s(...)` term. 

Because each application of the `fac` rule strictly strips away one leading `s` constructor from the top-level of its argument before replacing it with a `p`, the number of consecutive `s` constructors at the head of the argument strictly decreases with each recursive expansion. Once the leading `s` constructors run out, `fac` is presented with a term that starts with `p` (e.g., `fac(p(s(t)))`), matching no rules, and the evaluation halts. 

Although traditional simplification orderings (like LPO or KBO) fail here because `s(x)` is a subterm of `p(s(x))`, modern automated techniques such as the **Dependency Pair framework** can successfully orient and prove this system terminating without requiring outside assumptions, structural axioms, or object-level induction.
