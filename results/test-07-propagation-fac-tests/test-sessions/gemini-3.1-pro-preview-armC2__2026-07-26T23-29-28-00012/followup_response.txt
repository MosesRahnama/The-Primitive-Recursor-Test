I picked the **Dependency Pair (DP) framework** (and specifically noted the failure of traditional methods like LPO and KBO) because this specific rewrite system contains a classic syntactic trap that makes it mathematically impossible to prove termination using standard **simplification orderings**.

Here is the exact step-by-step reasoning for why older methods fail and why the Dependency Pair method is required:

### 1. Why Traditional Methods (LPO, KBO) Fail
Traditional termination methods rely on "simplification orderings." A fundamental rule of any simplification ordering is the **subterm property**: a term is always strictly greater than any of its proper subterms. 

Because `s(x)` is inside `p(s(x))`, the subterm property dictates that:
**`p(s(x)) > s(x)`**

Now, look at the factorial rule:
`fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`

To prove this rule terminates, the left side must be strictly greater than the right side. By the rules of simplification orderings, the whole right side must be greater than its subterms, meaning:
`times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x)) > fac(p(s(x)))`

Therefore, to orient the rule from left to right, we are forced to prove:
`fac(s(x)) > fac(p(s(x)))`

Because simplification orderings are monotonic (meaning you can strip away identical outer functions), this requires proving:
**`s(x) > p(s(x))`**

This creates a direct mathematical contradiction: the system demands `s(x) > p(s(x))` to evaluate `fac`, but the subterm property rigidly demands `p(s(x)) > s(x)`. Because of this contradiction, **no simplification ordering can ever prove this system terminates.**

### 2. Why the Dependency Pair (DP) Framework Succeeds
I chose the Dependency Pair framework because it was specifically invented by Arts and Giesl (1997) to bypass this exact limitation. 

Instead of forcing a single, rigid, monotonic ordering over the entire term, the DP framework isolates the recursive calls. For the factorial rule, it generates a "Dependency Pair":
`FAC(s(x)) -> FAC(p(s(x)))`

The genius of the DP framework is that it **drops the monotonicity requirement** for the strictly decreasing order. You are no longer bound by the strict syntactic subterm property. 

Instead, DP allows you to pair the isolated recursive calls with a semantic interpretation (like Polynomial Interpretations). You can simply map the terms to mathematical functions—for example, mapping `s(x)` to $x + 1$ and `p(x)` to $x - 1$. 
Under this interpretation, `FAC(x+1)` strictly decreases to `FAC((x+1)-1)`, which is just `FAC(x)`. 

Because the Dependency Pair framework frees us from the syntactic contradiction of the subterm property and allows us to look at the mathematical "meaning" of the decreasing arguments, it is the standard, necessary choice for orienting this specific type of system.
