## Verdict: **Yes** — termination is provable from the rules alone.

## Method: Recursive Path Order (RPO)

I use the standard Recursive Path Order with the **precedence**

$$\mathit{times} \;>_{\mathcal F}\; \mathit{plus} \;>_{\mathcal F}\; s$$

(no relation is required on `0`). For terms $s = f(s_1,\dots,s_n)$ and $t = g(t_1,\dots,t_m)$:

1. $s_i \ge_{\mathrm{rpo}} t$ for some $i$, and $s >_{\mathrm{rpo}} t_j$ for every $j$;
2. $f >_{\mathcal F} g$ and $s >_{\mathrm{rpo}} t_j$ for every $j$;
3. $f = g$ and $(s_1,\dots,s_n) >_{\mathrm{rpo}}^{\mathrm{mul}} (t_1,\dots,t_m)$.

The well-foundedness of RPO (Dershowitz) gives termination as soon as every LHS strictly RPO-greater than its RHS.

## Rule-by-rule orientation

**Rule 1.** $\mathit{times}(x,0) >_{\mathrm{rpo}} 0$.
By (1): direct subterm $0 \ge_{\mathrm{rpo}} 0$; $0$ has no arguments. ✓

**Rule 3.** $\mathit{plus}(x,0) >_{\mathrm{rpo}} x$.
By (1): direct subterm $x \ge_{\mathrm{rpo}} x$; $x$ has no arguments. ✓

**Rule 4.** $\mathit{plus}(0,x) >_{\mathrm{rpo}} x$.
By (1): direct subterm $x \ge_{\mathrm{rpo}} x$. ✓

**Rules 5 & 6.** Both LHS have top symbol $\mathit{plus}$ and RHS have top $s$, with $\mathit{plus} >_{\mathcal F} s$. Use clause (2); the only obligation is the first argument of $s$ on the RHS.

*Rule 5:* $\mathit{plus}(x,s(y)) >_{\mathrm{rpo}} \mathit{plus}(x,y)$ by (3), since multiset $\{x,s(y)\} >_{\mathrm{rpo}}^{\mathrm{mul}} \{x,y\}$ because $s(y) >_{\mathrm{rpo}} y$ (clause 1: subterm $y \ge y$). ✓

*Rule 6:* $\mathit{plus}(s(x),y) >_{\mathrm{rpo}} \mathit{plus}(x,y)$ by (3), since $\{s(x),y\} >_{\mathrm{rpo}}^{\mathrm{mul}} \{x,y\}$ because $s(x) >_{\mathrm{rpo}} x$. ✓

**Rule 2.** $\mathit{times}(x,s(y)) >_{\mathrm{rpo}} \mathit{plus}(\mathit{times}(x,y),x)$.
Clause (1) does not apply (no subterm of LHS is $\ge$ the RHS $\mathit{plus}(\dots)$). Use clause (2) with $\mathit{times} >_{\mathcal F} \mathit{plus}$; must show LHS $>$ each direct argument of RHS:

- $\mathit{times}(x,s(y)) >_{\mathrm{rpo}} \mathit{times}(x,y)$: clause (3), $\{x,s(y)\} >^{\mathrm{mul}} \{x,y\}$ since $s(y) > y$. ✓
- $\mathit{times}(x,s(y)) >_{\mathrm{rpo}} x$: clause (1), subterm $x \ge x$. ✓

So clause (2) holds. ✓

## Conclusion

Every rule's LHS is strictly greater than its RHS in the recursive path order under the precedence $\mathit{times} > \mathit{plus} > s$. RPO is a well-founded order on first-order terms over a well-founded precedence, so the rewrite system **terminates** — the proof uses only the rewrite rules and the term structure, with no extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or imported axioms.
