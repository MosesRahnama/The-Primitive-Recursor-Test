Because RPO is the smallest commitment that gets the job done. Specifically:

1. **It's the standard workhorse for first-order TRSs with no binding.** The system has no $\lambda$, no let-rec, no $\mu$, no quantifiers, no built-in arithmetic — just free variables and the two unary/binary constructors $s, 0, \mathit{plus}, \mathit{times}$. That's exactly the setting RPO was designed for (Dershowitz 1982).

2. **Termination actually does follow from RPO here, and from nothing cheaper.** I considered several alternatives:
   - *Polynomial/interpretation methods* (Lankford, Zantema): these would also work, but they require inventing a concrete well-founded model — extra work and extra assumptions about $\mathbb{N}$ that the rules don't entail on their own.
   - *Dependency pairs + reduction pairs / size-change / DP framework*: strictly more machinery than the system needs. DP shines when a system has nested recursion that RPO can't see; this system has none.
   - *Plain lexicographic or multiset path order* without a precedence on the function symbols: I checked. MPO alone (no precedence) cannot orient Rule 2, because $\mathit{times}$ is not strictly above $\mathit{plus}$ and there's no subterm relation. You genuinely need the precedence $\mathit{times} > \mathit{plus} > s$, which is what RPO adds.
   - *Manual induction on "size" or "the second argument"* is what the informal reading suggests, but the question asked for a *standard proof method*, not an ad hoc one. RPO formalizes exactly that induction — the status of the second argument decreases structurally at every recursive call, which is what makes Rule 2 orientable.

3. **The precedence I chose is forced.** Look at where each symbol appears as a top symbol:
   - $\mathit{times}$ appears only as the top of a rule, and on the LHS in Rule 2 it sits above $\mathit{plus}$ on the RHS — so $\mathit{times}$ must be $\ge \mathit{plus}$ in any usable precedence.
   - $\mathit{plus}$ is the top of Rules 3–6 and the second argument of Rule 2's RHS; in Rules 5/6 it sits above $s$, so $\mathit{plus} \ge s$ in the precedence.
   - $0$ is a nullary constructor, so its position in the precedence is irrelevant.
   
   That gives a unique minimal precedence $\mathit{times} > \mathit{plus} > s$, and under it RPO closes cleanly on all six rules.

4. **It stays inside the stated boundary.** RPO's well-foundedness is a theorem of first-order arithmetic applied to finite terms — it does not import any property of "the actual natural numbers," any induction axiom for the object level, any rewriting strategy, or any extra rule. Just the syntax of terms and the six rules, which is exactly the budget the question allows.

In short: I picked RPO because it's the *lightest* standard method that provably works on this system using only what's given. Anything more powerful would be overkill; anything simpler (plain MPO, subterm alone) demonstrably fails on Rule 2.
