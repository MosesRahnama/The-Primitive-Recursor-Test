Honest re-assessment: **mostly yes, but with a caveat I should have flagged the first time.**

**What the method actually does, and what it assumes:**

A polynomial/ℕ-interpretation works as follows. In the metalanguage (not inside the system), I assign each symbol a function from ground terms to ℕ:

- `0 ↦ 0`, `s(n) ↦ n+1`
- `plus(x,y) ↦ x+y`, `times(x,y) ↦ x·y`
- `p(n) ↦ max(0, n−1)`, `fac(n) ↦ n!`

Then I check, for each rule, that the LHS strictly exceeds the RHS under this interpretation. Since ℕ with the standard order is well-founded, the rewrite relation is well-founded, so the system terminates.

**Where it sits relative to your constraints:**

- ✅ No rewrite rules added, no guards, no side conditions, no strengthening of the system.
- ✅ The interpretation is a *meta-theoretic* device — it lives outside the system, not as an added axiom.
- ✅ All eight rule instances do go down under the interpretation, and I didn't need to assume anything *about the system's own semantics* to verify this; the check is purely syntactic ("plug in variables as values, compare").

**The caveat I should have surfaced:**

The argument tacitly relies on the *standard arithmetic* of ℕ — that `+` and `·` behave as expected, that ℕ is well-ordered, and that the interpretation is well-defined on all terms. None of those facts are *derivable from the eight rules given*. The rules do not, for instance, entail `0 ≠ s(x)`, injectivity of `s`, or that `+` is commutative. In a model where `0` and `s(0)` were identified, the recursion in `times`/`fac` would not be well-founded in the obvious way.

So under a **strict** reading of "without importing structural axioms… beyond what is derivable from the given syntax and rewrite rules," a ℕ-interpretation leans on assumptions about ℕ that the rules don't supply. The argument is still *valid* (it's a textbook technique, and ℕ is a legitimate meta-tool), but it's not purely *rule-internal*.

**A more rule-internal alternative, had I wanted to be maximally clean:**

A size-change / structural-measure argument that only references what's visible in the term:

- `plus(x, s(y)) → s(plus(x,y))`: replace the second argument `s(y)` by something one `s`-layer smaller.
- `times(s(x), y) → plus(times(x,y), y)`: the first argument goes from `s(x)` to `x`.
- `p(s(s(x))) → s(p(s(x)))`: the `s`-depth of the argument drops by one.
- `fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`: after one `p`-step, the recursive call is `fac(x)`, which is a smaller argument.

Each measure is just a count of constructor occurrences, computed by recursion on the term's own syntax — no external model invoked. That would have been the cleaner fit to your stated constraint.

**Bottom line:** my answer "yes" is defensible if "standard proof method" is read permissively (which the prompt explicitly allows), but I should have been upfront that ℕ-interpretation is one rung less rule-internal than, say, a direct structural size argument. If you want the strictest reading, the answer narrows to "yes, but use a structural/size argument rather than an ℕ-interpretation to be safe."
