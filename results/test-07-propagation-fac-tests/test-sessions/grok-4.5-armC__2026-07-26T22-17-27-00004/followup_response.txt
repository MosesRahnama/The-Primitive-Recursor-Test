The dependency-pair (DP) method (with a simple reduction order or argument filter) is a natural fit for this system because the only non-trivial recursion is the single self-dependent rule for \(\mathrm{fac}\), whose recursive call is guarded by the structurally smaller argument produced by \(\mathrm{p}\circ\mathrm{s}\). All other rules are ordinary primitive-recursive definitions of addition and multiplication (plus a trivial unrolling of \(\mathrm{p}\)) and generate no recursive DPs at all. Consequently a very weak order—e.g., a lexicographic combination of a polynomial interpretation that decreases the \(\mathrm{fac}\)-argument together with a simple linear norm on the remaining symbols—already orients every DP and every original rule, proving termination from the rewrite rules alone.

(The same reasoning can be phrased directly as a lexicographic/multiset measure on the arguments of \(\{\mathrm{fac},\mathrm{times},\mathrm{plus},\mathrm{p}\}\) without mentioning DPs; both presentations stay entirely inside the given signature and rules.)
