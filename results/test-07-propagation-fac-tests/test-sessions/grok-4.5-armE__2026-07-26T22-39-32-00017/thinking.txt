The user asks about a first-order term rewriting system and whether termination can be established from the presented rules alone.
Yes, termination can be established from the given rules alone.

The key is to observe that the only redexes are those headed by F, and every rule strictly decreases the size of the third argument (measured by the number of S-symbols in its normal form, or more precisely, by the depth of the constructor chain 0 or S(·)). The first rule replaces F(x,y,Z) by x, removing the entire third argument. The second rule replaces F(x,y,S(n)) by G(y,F(x,y,n)), which replaces the outer S(n) by n (a strictly smaller term in the same sense) while wrapping the 1-step smaller F(x,y,n) inside G.

To formalize this, consider the lexicographic path order (LPO) induced by the precedence F ≻ G ≻ S ≻ Z together with the lexicographic status on the arguments of F.
