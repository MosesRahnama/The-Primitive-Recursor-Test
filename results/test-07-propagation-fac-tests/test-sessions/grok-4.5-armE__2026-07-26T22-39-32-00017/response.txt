### Verdict
**Yes**, termination of this TRS can be established from the given rules alone (using a standard simplification order).

### Termination Argument
The signature consists of the function symbols \(\{F/3, G/2, S/1, Z/0\}\) together with the variables \(\{x, y, n\}\). The two rules are the only rewrite rules; there are no equations, no conditional rules, no extra-theory axioms, and no binders.

Consider the lexicographic path order (LPO) induced by the precedence
\[
F > G > S > Z
\]
and left-to-right lexicographic status for every symbol. LPO is a simplification order, hence a well-founded reduction order on ground terms; it is closed under contexts and substitutions. It therefore suffices to verify that both rules are strictly oriented.

- Rule 1: \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\).  
  The root symbol \(F\) is greater than the variable \(x\) in the precedence, so the LPO inequality holds immediately.

- Rule 2: \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\).  
  The root \(F\) is strictly greater than the root \(G\). It remains only to check that the left-hand side is strictly greater than each immediate argument of the right-hand side:  
  - \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} y\) holds because a term is always greater than a variable.  
  - \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} F(x,y,n)\) holds by the lexicographic comparison of argument tuples \((x,y,S(n))\) versus \((x,y,n)\): the first two components are identical and the third satisfies \(S(n) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} n\) (subterm property).  

Both rules are therefore strictly decreasing in LPO. Consequently every rewrite sequence is finite.

(The same orientation is obtained with the multiset path order or with a simple recursive path order under the same precedence; the argument never leaves the pure first-order syntax and the two given rules.)
