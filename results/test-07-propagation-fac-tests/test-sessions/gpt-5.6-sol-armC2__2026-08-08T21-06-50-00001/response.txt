**Assessment: Yes.** Termination can be established from the rewrite rules alone, for all first-order terms, without assuming externally that terms denote natural numbers or that `p` is an axiomatic predecessor.

A standard dependency-pair argument suffices:

- `plus` recursively removes one leading `s` from its second argument.
- `times` recursively removes one leading `s` from its first argument; its calls to `plus` do not lead back to `times`.
- `p(s(s(x)))` recursively changes the argument of `p` from `s(s(x))` to `s(x)`.
- The recursive `fac` call changes its argument from `s(x)` to `p(s(x))`. This descent can be certified syntactically using the well-founded interpretation
  \[
  |s(t)|=|t|+1,\qquad |p(t)|=\max(|t|-1,0).
  \]
  Thus \(|p(s(x))|=|x|<|s(x)|\). The `p` rules are compatible with this interpretation: the first preserves the value and `p(s(0)) -> 0` does as well.

The remaining dependency edges (`fac` to `times`, `times` to `plus`) are acyclic. Hence every recursive dependency cycle has a strict well-founded decrease. The interpretation is a metatheoretic termination measure, not an imported object-level axiom about `p`.
