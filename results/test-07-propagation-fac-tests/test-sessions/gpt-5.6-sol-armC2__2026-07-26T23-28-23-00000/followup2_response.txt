**Yes, in substance—though my first explanation compressed some technical details.**

The natural-number assignments are a **meta-level reduction interpretation**, not object-level axioms asserting that terms denote naturals or that `p` is predecessor. Variables may still range over arbitrary terms. To use the interpretation, one only verifies that every given rewrite rule is weakly decreasing and that the cyclic dependency pairs are strictly decreasing.

Likewise, dependency pairs are proof artifacts; they do not add rewrite rules, guards, or side conditions to the system.

In particular, the choice

\[
0=0,\quad s(n)=n+1,\quad p(n)=\max(n-1,0)
\]

does **not** assume an extra rule such as `p(s(x)) -> x`. It merely supplies a well-founded semantic measure satisfying the inequalities induced by the rules. Marked dependency symbols can then measure the relevant recursive argument:

- `PLUS#` by its second argument,
- `TIMES#` by its first,
- `P#` by its argument,
- `FAC#` by its argument.

Each recursive dependency-pair cycle strictly decreases, while all original rules are weakly decreasing. Cross-component calls do not create cycles.

Therefore, the method complies with the stated restriction: it proves termination of the presented TRS as-is, using a standard meta-level termination theorem, without strengthening the object-level system.
