**Yes.** Termination can be established for the unrestricted first-order rewrite system itself; no external structural axioms about natural numbers are needed.

One route is a dependency-pair proof. Interpret terms over \(\mathbb N\) by

- \(s(n)=n+1\)
- \(p(n)=\max(n-1,0)\)
- \(\mathrm{plus}(m,n)=m+n\)
- \(\mathrm{times}(m,n)=mn\)
- \(\mathrm{fac}(n)=0\)

All original rewrite rules are weakly decreasing under this interpretation. The recursive dependency-pair cycles are strictly decreasing:

- `plus` decreases its second argument;
- `times` decreases its first argument;
- `p` decreases from `s(s(x))` to `s(x)`;
- `fac` decreases from `s(x)` to `p(s(x))`, interpreted as \(x+1>x\).

The remaining calls go only downward between these components (`fac` to `times`/`p`, and `times` to `plus`), so they introduce no cycle. Thus the dependency-pair criterion establishes termination solely from the presented rules. The numerical interpretation is a proof ordering, not an imported object-level axiom.
