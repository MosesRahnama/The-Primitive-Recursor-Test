**Yes.** The LPO proof complies with those requirements.

The proof uses only:

- the syntactic tree structure of finite first-order terms;
- the two displayed rewrite rules;
- a standard well-founded reduction ordering;
- a chosen precedence \(F \succ G\).

That precedence is **not an added object-level axiom or rewrite rule**. It is a metatheoretic parameter used to construct a termination measure, just as choosing weights in a polynomial interpretation would be. It does not alter which terms rewrite or impose conditions on substitutions.

Likewise, the facts

\[
F(x,y,Z)>x,\qquad S(n)>n
\]

come from the strict-subterm property of LPO, hence from the given term syntax—not from assumptions such as “\(S\) is a successor operation” or “\(n\) is a natural number.”

The proof makes no assumptions that:

- every third argument is \(Z\) or \(S(n)\);
- \(S\) is injective;
- \(Z\) and \(S(n)\) are distinct semantically;
- \(F\) or \(G\) has any intended interpretation;
- terms are well-sorted;
- additional rules or guards exist.

Terms not matching either left-hand side simply cannot rewrite at their root, though rewriting may still occur in their subterms; context closure of LPO covers that case.

The only background fact used is the standard meaning of a first-order TRS as operating on **finite first-order terms**. That is part of the stipulated syntactic framework, not an imported object-level assumption. Thus the method stays within the boundary stated in the initial prompt.
