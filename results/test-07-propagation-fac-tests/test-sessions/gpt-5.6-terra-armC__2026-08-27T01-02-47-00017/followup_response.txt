I picked a syntactic rewrite-theoretic approach because your question explicitly forbade relying on an intended model such as the natural numbers or on unlisted axioms about `plus`, `times`, `p`, or `fac`.

In that setting, the relevant evidence is only:

- which symbols can occur at the root of a redex,
- how recursive calls are created by right-hand sides, and
- whether those calls can form an infinite dependency chain under the given rules.

So I focused on the apparent recursive structure:

- `plus` consumes a successor in its second argument;
- `times` consumes a successor in its first argument;
- `p` transforms a `p(s(s(...)))` call toward a smaller successor-prefix case;
- `fac` creates a recursive `fac` occurrence only below `p(s(...))`, whose ability to expose another `s(...)` is constrained by the `p` rules.

That is essentially an informal dependency-pair/recursive-call descent analysis, rather than a semantic arithmetic proof.

That said, my previous response was stronger than its displayed justification: saying that “a suitable well-founded ordering” exists without actually giving one is not itself a formal termination proof. A rigorous establishment would need an explicit well-founded measure, reduction ordering, or dependency-pair argument that covers arbitrary first-order terms—not merely numeral-shaped terms such as `s^n(0)`.
