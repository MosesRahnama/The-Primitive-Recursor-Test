Yes. Termination is a property of the rewrite relation generated by these rules, so it can be assessed syntactically; it does not require assuming that terms denote natural numbers or importing predecessor/arithmetic axioms.

The potentially delicate rule is

```text
fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))
```

because it introduces another `fac` call. But the only way that recursive call can again have an `s(...)` argument is through the `p` rules, which operationally consume the available leading `s` structure. For example, `p(s(s(t)))` becomes `s(p(s(t)))`; repeated use cannot create an unbounded supply of new leading successors. If no applicable `p` rule exposes an `s` at the needed position, the recursive `fac` call is stuck rather than recurring.

Similarly, `plus` and `times` recurse by consuming successor structure, and the `p` subsystem has no cycle. A formal termination proof would use a suitable well-founded ordering or dependency-pair-style argument rather than naïve term size, since some rules preserve or enlarge raw size. But no external object-level assumption—such as “all inputs are natural numerals” or algebraic facts about `p`—is needed for the termination claim.
