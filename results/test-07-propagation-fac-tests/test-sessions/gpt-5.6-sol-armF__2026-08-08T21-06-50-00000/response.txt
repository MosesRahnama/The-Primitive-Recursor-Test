## Verdict

**Yes.** The system is terminating on ordinary finite first-order terms. A recursive path ordering (RPO) proves this using only the displayed syntax and rules; no arithmetic, associativity, commutativity, constructor-disjointness, or other object-level assumptions are needed.

Choose an RPO with:

\[
\mathsf{times} \succ \mathsf{plus} \succ \mathsf{s} \succ 0
\]

and give the binary symbols multiset status. RPO is a well-founded reduction ordering, stable under substitutions and contexts.

Write \(>_{\mathrm{rpo}}\) for this ordering. It has the syntactic subterm property, so in particular:

\[
\mathsf{s}(t)>_{\mathrm{rpo}}t,\qquad
f(\ldots,t,\ldots)>_{\mathrm{rpo}}t.
\]

### Orientation of every rule

1. **\(\mathsf{times}(x,0)\to 0\)**

   \(0\) is an immediate subterm of the left side, hence

   \[
   \mathsf{times}(x,0)>_{\mathrm{rpo}}0.
   \]

2. **\(\mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
   \to \mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{times}(x,y),x)\)**

   Since \(\mathsf{s}(y)>_{\mathrm{rpo}}y\), multiset comparison gives

   \[
   \mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
   >_{\mathrm{rpo}}\mathsf{times}(x,y).
   \]

   The left side is also greater than \(x\) by the subterm property. Since
   \(\mathsf{times}\succ\mathsf{plus}\), it follows that

   \[
   \mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
   >_{\mathrm{rpo}}
   \mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{times}(x,y),x).
   \]

3. **\(\mathsf{plus}(x,0)\to x\)**

   \(x\) is an immediate subterm, so the rule strictly decreases.

4. **\(\mathsf{plus}(0,x)\to x\)**

   Again, \(x\) is an immediate subterm.

5. **\(\mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
   \to \mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y))\)**

   From \(\mathsf{s}(y)>_{\mathrm{rpo}}y\), multiset comparison gives

   \[
   \mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
   >_{\mathrm{rpo}}\mathsf{plus}(x,y).
   \]

   Because \(\mathsf{plus}\succ\mathsf{s}\), this implies

   \[
   \mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
   >_{\mathrm{rpo}}\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y)).
   \]

6. **\(\mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)
   \to \mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y))\)**

   Likewise, \(\mathsf{s}(x)>_{\mathrm{rpo}}x\), so

   \[
   \mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)
   >_{\mathrm{rpo}}\mathsf{plus}(x,y),
   \]

   and \(\mathsf{plus}\succ\mathsf{s}\) yields the required orientation.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded ordering. Since RPO is closed under substitutions and contexts, reductions at arbitrary positions also decrease it. Therefore no infinite rewrite sequence exists, and the TRS is terminating.
