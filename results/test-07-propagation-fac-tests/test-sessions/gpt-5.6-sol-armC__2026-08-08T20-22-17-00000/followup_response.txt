I chose a **dependency-pair / semantic-ordering style argument** because the `fac` rule is not structurally recursive in the ordinary syntactic sense:

```text
fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))
```

The new argument `p(s(x))` is not a proper subterm of `s(x)`, so a basic subterm ordering does not immediately prove descent. A simple recursive path ordering also faces conflicting requirements: orienting the `fac` recursion suggests treating `s` as larger than `p`, while

```text
p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))
```

naturally suggests the opposite precedence.

Dependency pairs isolate the potentially infinite call

```text
fac#(s(x)) -> fac#(p(s(x)))
```

from the terminating administrative work performed by `times`, `plus`, and `p`. One can then use a semantic measure that tracks how much finite `s`-structure `p` can expose or consume, rather than assuming terms represent natural numbers.

So I picked that method because it handles the **indirect, computed recursive argument** of `fac`, where a straightforward “recursive call on a subterm” proof does not. My previous response was a proof sketch rather than a complete dependency-pair certificate.
