**Assessment: yes.** Termination can be established syntactically from the given rules alone; no assumption that terms denote natural numbers is required.

The `plus` and `times` rules recurse on a proper `s`-spine, and the `p` rules terminate by shortening the relevant nested `s` structure. The only non-obvious rule is

```text
fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x)).
```

Although `p(s(x))` is not literally a subterm of `s(x)`, another `fac` step can continue only when `p(s(x))` rewrites to an `s`-headed term. By the given `p` rules, this requires exposing and consuming further finite `s` structure inside `x`. Thus an infinite chain of recursive `fac` calls cannot occur. A dependency-pair or suitable semantic-ordering proof can formalize this argument.

So the system is terminating on arbitrary finite first-order terms, not merely under an imported “natural-number” interpretation.
