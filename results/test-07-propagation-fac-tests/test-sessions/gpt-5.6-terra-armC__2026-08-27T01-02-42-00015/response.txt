Yes. Termination can be established from the rewrite rules themselves; no external Peano axioms, induction principle, or assumption that `p` denotes a total predecessor function is required.

The relevant property is syntactic termination on finite first-order terms. The recursive rules have decreasing structural behavior:

- `plus` recurses by removing one leading `s` from its second argument.
- `times` recurses by removing one leading `s` from its first argument; its generated `plus` calls do not lead back to `times`.
- `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))` moves the recursive `p` call inward, reducing the number of consecutive leading `s` symbols available to that `p` call. `p(s(0)) -> 0` closes the numeral case, while unmatched cases simply become normal forms.
- `fac` is the only potentially delicate rule, but its recursive call is beneath `p(s(x))`. Repeated rewriting of that argument can expose only finitely many leading `s` symbols from the finite input term before the `p` call becomes blocked or reaches its base rule. The rules that `fac` generates (`times` and then `plus`) do not recreate a larger `fac` input or introduce a cycle back into `fac`.

Thus a standard purely syntactic termination argument—for example, via dependency pairs together with an appropriate well-founded structural measure—can establish termination of the displayed TRS. One should not justify it merely by informally assuming ordinary natural-number semantics for `p`; but such assumptions are unnecessary.
