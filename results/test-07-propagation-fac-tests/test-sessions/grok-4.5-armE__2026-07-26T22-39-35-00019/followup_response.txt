### Why this method

The system is a pure first-order TRS with only two rules and a simple constructor hierarchy (Z/S for the third argument of F, plus the auxiliary G). For exactly this shape, a recursive path order (RPO) induced by a precedence on the finite signature is the most direct standard tool that

- is defined entirely from the syntax (no external well-founded relations, no arithmetic, no extra axioms),
- orients both rules in a single uniform check,
- relies only on the subterm property and the root-symbol comparison F > G,
- and immediately yields well-foundedness because every RPO is a simplification order on the set of finite terms.

A pure polynomial or matrix interpretation would also work, but would require choosing coefficients and proving non-negativity; a custom multiset measure on “S-depth of third arguments of F-subterms” works too, but is essentially a specialised instance of the same path-order idea. RPO is simply the lightest off-the-shelf device that already contains that reasoning, needs no side conditions or rule extensions, and discharges every rule by a two-line comparison. That is why it was chosen.
