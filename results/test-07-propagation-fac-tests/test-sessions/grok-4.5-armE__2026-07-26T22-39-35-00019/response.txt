### Verdict
Yes, termination can be established from the given rules and syntax alone.

### Termination Argument
The signature consists of the constant \(Z\), the unary symbol \(S\), and the binary symbols \(F\) and \(G\), with the two rules
\[
F(x,y,Z)\to x,
\]
\[
F(x,y,S(n))\to G(y,F(x,y,n)).
\]
(Terms are finite by the definition of first-order terms; no other symbols or axioms are present.)

Define a strict order \(\succ\) on terms by the recursive path order induced by the precedence \(F>G>S>Z\) (lexicographic status). This order is a well-founded simplification order on the set of all terms, and it is completely determined by the signature (no external well-founded relations or arithmetic axioms are required). It remains only to check that both rules are oriented by \(\succ\).

- For the first rule the left-hand side has root \(F\) while the right-hand side is a proper subterm, so \(F(x,y,Z)\succ x\) holds immediately by the subterm property of recursive path order.
- For the second rule the roots satisfy \(F>G\). It is therefore enough to verify the two strict inequalities
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n))\succ y
  \qquad\text{and}\qquad
  F(x,y,S(n))\succ F(x,y,n).
  \]
  The first holds by the subterm property. The second holds because the first two arguments are identical and the third arguments satisfy \(S(n)\succ n\) (again by the subterm property). Hence
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n))\succ G(y,F(x,y,n)).
  \]

Both rules decrease in the well-founded order \(\succ\). Consequently every rewrite sequence is finite.

(The same orientation is obtained with the lexicographic path order under the identical precedence, or by the multiset measure that sends each term to the multiset of the \(S\)-depths of the third arguments of all its \(F\)-subterms; each of those measures is likewise definable from the syntax alone.)
