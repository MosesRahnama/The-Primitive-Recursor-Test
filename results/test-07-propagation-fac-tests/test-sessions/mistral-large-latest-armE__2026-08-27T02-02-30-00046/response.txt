### Termination Argument:

We will use a **lexicographic path order (LPO)** with a carefully chosen precedence on the function symbols to establish termination. LPO is a standard method for proving termination of first-order term rewriting systems, and it does not require any additional axioms or assumptions beyond the given rules.

#### Function Symbols and Precedence:
The system has three function symbols: `F`, `G`, and `S`, along with a constant `Z`. We assign the following precedence (where `>` denotes "has higher precedence than"):

This means `F` is the highest, `G` is next, and `S` is the lowest. The constant `Z` has no precedence constraints.

#### Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) Rules:
LPO compares terms by recursively comparing their subterms. For two terms `s` and `t`, `s >_lpo t` if one of the following holds:
1. `s = f(s_1, ..., s_n)` and `t = g(t_1, ..., t_m)` and `f > g` and `s >_lpo t_i` for all `i`.
2. `s = f(s_1, ..., s_n)` and `t = f(t_1, ..., t_n)` and there exists `j` such that `s_i = t_i` for all `i < j`, `s_j >_lpo t_j`, and `s >_lpo t_k` for all `k > j`.
3. `s = f(s_1, ..., s_n)` and `t` is a subterm of some `s_i`.
