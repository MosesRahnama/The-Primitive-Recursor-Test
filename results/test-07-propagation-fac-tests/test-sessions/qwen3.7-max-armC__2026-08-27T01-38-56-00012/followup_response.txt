I chose this specific combination of methods—**the Dependency Pair (DP) framework** paired with a **natural number interpretation using `max(0, x-1)`**—through a process of elimination. Simpler, standard termination techniques fail on this specific Term Rewriting System (TRS) for distinct mathematical and syntactic reasons. 

Here is the step-by-step reasoning for why this specific method was necessary:

### 1. Why standard syntactic orderings (RPO, LPO, KBO) fail
Simplification orderings like the Recursive Path Ordering (RPO) or Knuth-Bendix Ordering (KBO) rely on the principle that the arguments of recursive calls must be *syntactic subterms* (or strictly smaller in a syntactic weight/status) of the original arguments. 
* Look at the `fac` rule: `fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`.
* The recursive call is on `p(s(x))`. 
* Syntactically, `p(s(x))` is **larger** than `s(x)` because it wraps `s(x)` inside another function symbol `p`. 
Therefore, no standard syntactic ordering can prove that `s(x) > p(s(x))`.

### 2. Why direct polynomial interpretations fail
When syntactic methods fail, the next standard step is to try a direct semantic interpretation (e.g., mapping terms to polynomials over $\mathbb{N}$). This also fails for two reasons:
* **The Predecessor Problem:** To make `s(x)` strictly greater than `p(s(x))`, the interpretation of `p` must strictly decrease the value. However, standard polynomials over $\mathbb{N}$ (like `[p(x)] = x + c` or `[p(x)] = c`) cannot strictly decrease a value while remaining non-negative and weakly monotone. 
* **The RHS Growth Problem:** Even if we solved the predecessor problem, direct interpretations require the *entire* right-hand side to be strictly smaller than the left-hand side. The RHS contains `times(...)`. Any valid interpretation of `times` (like multiplication or even addition) will cause the RHS to grow too fast relative to the LHS `fac(s(x))`, making it impossible to satisfy `[fac(s(x))] > [times(fac(...), s(x))]`.

### 3. Why the Dependency Pair (DP) Framework was chosen
The DP framework solves the **RHS Growth Problem**. 
* Instead of requiring the entire right-hand side of the `fac` rule to be strictly smaller, the DP framework isolates the *recursive calls* into Dependency Pairs. 
* The only DP generated for `fac` is: `FAC(s(x)) -> FAC(p(s(x)))`.
* This strips away the `times(..., s(x))` context. We no longer have to worry about the size explosion caused by `times`; we only have to prove that the argument passed to the next `FAC` call (`p(s(x))`) is strictly smaller than the current argument (`s(x)`).

### 4. Why the `max(0, x-1)` interpretation was chosen
With the DP framework isolating the problem, we still have to solve the **Predecessor Problem** to prove `[s(x)] > [p(s(x))]` while keeping the usable rules for `p` weakly decreasing.
* Standard polynomials still can't do this. 
* However, termination tools (like AProVE or TTT2) routinely use **polynomial interpretations extended with the `max` function** over $\mathbb{N}$. 
* By defining `[p(x)] = max(0, x - 1)`, we perfectly capture the mathematical semantics of a truncated predecessor without importing any object-level axioms. 
* This allows `p` to strictly decrease the value when $x > 0$ (satisfying the strict DP requirement: $x + 1 > \max(0, x) = x$), while gracefully bottoming out at $0$ to maintain weak monotonicity and satisfy the usable rules (e.g., $p(s(0)) \to 0$ evaluates to $\max(0, 1-1) \ge 0$, which is $0 \ge 0$).

### Summary
I picked this method because it is the minimal, standard automated termination pipeline required to bypass the specific traps in your TRS: **Dependency Pairs** bypass the syntactic size increase and RHS context growth, and the **`max`-extended natural number interpretation** bypasses the inability of standard polynomials to model a truncated predecessor.
