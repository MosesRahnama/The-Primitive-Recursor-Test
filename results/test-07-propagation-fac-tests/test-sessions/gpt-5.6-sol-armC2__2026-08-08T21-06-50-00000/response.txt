**Assessment: yes.** Termination can be established from the rewrite rules alone; no assumption that terms denote natural numbers is required.

A dependency-pair argument suffices. The only recursive cycles decrease as follows:

- `plus(x, s(y))` recurses on `y`.
- `times(s(x), y)` recurses on `x`.
- `p(s(s(x)))` recurses from `s(s(x))` to `s(x)`.
- `fac(s(x))` recursively calls `fac(p(s(x)))`.

For the non-obvious factorial cycle, use a well-founded interpretation over \(\mathbb N\):

- \(0=0\), \(s(n)=n+1\),
- \(p(n)=\max(n-1,0)\),
- `plus`, `times`, and `fac` are interpreted as addition, multiplication, and factorial.

Every rewrite rule is weakly decreasing—in fact, equal under this interpretation—while the factorial dependency pair is strictly decreasing:

\[
s(x)=x+1 \;>\; p(s(x))=x.
\]

The other recursive dependency pairs decrease by similarly elementary argument measures. Thus all dependency-pair cycles terminate, and therefore the whole TRS terminates. This interpretation is a meta-level termination measure, not an imported object-level axiom about the terms.
