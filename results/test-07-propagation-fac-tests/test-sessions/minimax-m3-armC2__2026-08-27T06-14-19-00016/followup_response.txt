Honest answer: I picked **polynomial interpretations** together with the **dependency-pair method** because they're the most general automated-style techniques I expected to apply uniformly to all the rules in one framework. Let me unpack the reasoning, including some caveats I glossed over the first time.

**Why these two together?**

- **Dependency pairs** decompose the question "does the whole TRS terminate?" into "does each cycle of recursive calls terminate?" For this system the cycles are essentially isolated: `plus` recurses on its second argument, `times` on its first, `fac` on `p(s(x))`. Once stated as DPs, each cycle can be checked independently with a reduction pair.
- **Polynomial interpretations** give a concrete well-founded order on ground terms (interpret into ℕ with polynomials and `+`, `×`, `max`) that is decidable to search for. They handle `plus` and `times` directly, and they're a canonical choice in the termination-proving literature.

**What I should have flagged more carefully**

The `p` rules are actually the subtle part:
- `p(s(0)) → 0` is fine.
- `p(s(s(x))) → s(p(s(x)))` is *not* size-decreasing: the LHS has two `s`s above `p`, the RHS still has two `s`s in total (one is now on the outside). A naive 1-dimensional polynomial interpretation like `s(x)=x+1, p(x)=0` gives `0 > 1`, which is the wrong direction.

So strictly speaking, "plug in one polynomial interpretation over all symbols" doesn't just work out of the box. The honest options are:

1. A **2-component (lexicographic / matrix) interpretation** where the first component tracks the "depth of `p`" — essentially the measure I described in the first answer.
2. A **manual proof that the `p`-subsystem terminates** (by induction on that depth), then treat `p(s(x))` as a "smaller term" in the reduction pair for the `fac` DP.
3. Use **RPO with an appropriate status/quasi-precedence** instead of polynomials, which can also handle the depth-movement of `p`.

**Why I still led with polynomial+DP rather than the alternatives**

- RPO/MPO works but the precedence/status needs to be justified carefully for the `fac` rule, and that justification is essentially the same as the manual measure argument.
- A pure manual induction is fine and arguably more direct, but it doesn't *generalize* — the question implicitly asks "can a standard method establish this from the rules alone," and polynomial+DP is the canonical such method in the rewriting literature.
- Tools like AProVE, NaTT, and CiME use exactly this combination, and the system is in their standard benchmark suites.

**What I would have said differently in hindsight**

The cleanest single sentence is: "Termination follows by the dependency-pair method using a lexicographic polynomial interpretation in which the first component decreases on the recursive argument of each function and a separate (or built-in) argument shows the `p` rules are well-founded, after which `fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))` is handled by the depth-decreasing measure on `p`." The point is the DP framework plus a *vector* interpretation, not a single scalar one — which is what I was implicitly relying on.
