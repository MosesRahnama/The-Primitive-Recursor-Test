**Short answer: no, not strictly as I described it.** The method I sketched is correct in substance, but at least one step in the version I wrote would smuggle in a structural axiom if taken literally. Let me pinpoint it.

**The problematic step**

For the `p` rules I said "use a 2-component interpretation / matrix interpretation / a separate induction." But the second component of such a vector interpretation, or the inductive argument for `p`, is essentially recognizing that:

> `p` removes the *innermost* `s`, and a term decreases in a well-founded measure once the call to `p` has stripped an `s` off.

That fact isn't a rule in the system — it's an external claim about the *shape* of the terms that arise as rewrites. I justified it as "manual induction on the depth of `p`," which is fine *if* I limit myself to terms that are reachable by rewriting from closed ground terms. In that setting the measure is internal to what the rules generate.

**Where the method does and doesn't comply**

- ✅ Dependency-pair framework: pure syntactic tool over the given rules, no extra axioms.
- ✅ Polynomial interpretations over ℕ with `+`, `×`, `max`: a standard semantic argument, no object-level arithmetic axioms about the *meaning* of `0`, `s`, `+` are needed — only that the polynomials define a monotone, well-founded algebra on the carrier, which is a meta-level fact, not an object-level one.
- ⚠️ The depth-decreasing argument for `p` (and the way it slots into the `fac` DP as "the call `fac(p(s(x)))` is made on a smaller term"): the smaller-than relation on terms I'm using is the **subterm relation on a derived measure**, not a direct rule. This is standard and is *not* the same as adding rules or guards, but it does require a small lemma (essentially: every term has a well-founded `p`-depth) that is proved by structural induction on the syntax. That induction is a derived proof, not an imported axiom — so on a strict reading it does comply.
- ❌ What would *not* comply: invoking a separate, pre-known termination theorem for `p` based on "p strips an s", or assuming Peano arithmetic facts (e.g., "every numeral has a unique predecessor"), or adding rules like `p(s(x)) → x` as a "structurally obvious" axiom. None of those are in the system, and I shouldn't have gestured at them.

**Revised, compliant version of the argument**

1. Apply the **dependency-pair method** to the rules as given. The resulting DPs are:
   - `PLUS(s(x), y) → PLUS(x, y')` with `y = s(y')`
   - `TIMES(s(x), y) → PLUS'(TIMES(x, y), y)` and `TIMES(s(x), y) → TIMES(x, y')` with `y = s(y')`
   - `FAC(s(x)) → TIMES'(FAC(p(s(x))), s(x))` and `FAC(s(x)) → FAC(p(s(x)))`
   The only cycle is the `FAC → FAC` self-loop through `p(s(·))`.
2. Prove the `p` rules terminate on their own. The DP for `p` is `P(s(s(x))) → P(s(x))`. A polynomial interpretation `[[s]](n)=n+1, [[p]](n)=0, [[0]]=0, [[x]]=x` does not directly work (gives `1 > 0` for `p(s(s(x))) → s(p(s(x)))`, which is the wrong direction). But a **matrix interpretation** or a **lexicographic / tuple interpretation** with first component counting the `s`-depth above `p` does:
   - e.g., `[[p(s^n(x))]] = (n, x)` in lex order, with `[[s]](a,b)=(a+1,b)`, `[[0]]=(0,0)`. Then `p(s(s(x))) → s(p(s(x)))` maps `(2,x) → (1+1, x) = (2, x)` — still equal. So you actually need a 3-component version tracking the *unmatched* leading `s` of `p` separately, e.g. `[[t]] = (unmatched_s_above_p, total_s_depth, x)`, and show each `p`-rule strictly decreases the first component. This is a routine but non-trivial construction.
3. Once `p` is shown to terminate, the `FAC` cycle `FAC(s(x)) → FAC(p(s(x)))` is covered by the **dependency-pair processor "reduction pair with usable rules"**, using the well-founded order on terms in which `p(s(·))` is below its argument — which is the same measure from step 2. No rule added; it's the same termination proof extended to the `FAC` context.
4. The `PLUS` and `TIMES` DPs are handled by standard interpretations (e.g., `[[plus]](x,y)=x+y`, `[[times]](x,y)=2xy` or a matrix interpretation with a triangle matrix) within the same framework.

Every step is now a syntactic / semantic operation over the given rules. No new rules, no guards, no side conditions, and no fact about the *meaning* of `0`, `s`, or `p` beyond what the rules force.

**Honest bottom line**

My first answer waved at this and got the conclusion right, but the description was loose enough that a strict reader could object I was importing "obvious" facts about `p`. The corrected version above stays inside the rules. The price of staying inside the rules is that the `p` step is genuinely the hardest part of the proof and cannot be waved at — you either do a multi-component interpretation or prove termination of `p` separately by induction on the term structure reachable from rewriting, and then carry that order into the `fac` cycle. That is all *standard and standardly accepted as not importing axioms*, but it is worth being explicit about, which I wasn't the first time.
