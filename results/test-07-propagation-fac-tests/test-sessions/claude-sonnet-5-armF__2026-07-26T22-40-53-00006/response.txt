## Verdict: **Terminating** — provable by the Recursive Path Order (RPO)

The system can be shown terminating using a standard, unmodified application of RPO, with no extra axioms, guards, or side conditions needed. Only the given rewrite rules and the syntactic structure of terms are used.

### Setup

Choose the precedence
$$
\mathsf{times} \;>\; \mathsf{plus} \;>\; \mathsf{s} \;>\; \mathsf{0}
$$
with **multiset status** for both `times` and `plus` (their argument lists are compared as multisets, not lexicographically). `s` and `0` need no special status (arity ≤1/0).

RPO is well-founded on the term algebra for any precedence, so it suffices to check that each rewrite rule strictly decreases under this order, i.e. $\ell \succ_{rpo} r$ for every rule $\ell \to r$.

### Verification, rule by rule

**1. `times(x,0) → 0`**
Here `0` is literally the second argument of the left side. By the *subterm* clause of RPO ($t = s_i$ for some $i$), we get `times(x,0) ≻ 0`. ✔

**2. `times(x,s(y)) → plus(times(x,y), x)`**
Right side has head `plus`, and `times > plus` in the precedence. We need the left side to dominate *every* argument of the right side:
- `times(x,s(y)) ≻ x`: true since `x` is a direct subterm (subterm clause).
- `times(x,s(y)) ≻ times(x,y)`: same head `times`, multiset comparison of `{x, s(y)}` vs `{x, y}`; cancel the common `x`, leaving `s(y) ≻ y`, true by the subterm clause.

Both conditions hold, so by the precedence clause `times(x,s(y)) ≻ plus(times(x,y), x)`. ✔

**3. `plus(x,0) → x`**
`x` is a direct subterm of the left side ⇒ subterm clause applies directly. ✔

**4. `plus(0,x) → x`**
Same reasoning: `x` is a direct subterm. ✔

**5. `plus(x,s(y)) → s(plus(x,y))`**
Right side has head `s`, and `plus > s`. Need left side to dominate the single argument `plus(x,y)`:
- Same head `plus` on both sides; multiset comparison `{x, s(y)}` vs `{x, y}`; cancelling `x` leaves `s(y) ≻ y`, true by subterm clause.

So `plus(x,s(y)) ≻ plus(x,y)`, and by the precedence clause `plus(x,s(y)) ≻ s(plus(x,y))`. ✔

**6. `plus(s(x),y) → s(plus(x,y))`**
Same shape as rule 5, symmetric in the multiset argument. Need `plus(s(x),y) ≻ plus(x,y)`:
- Multiset `{s(x), y}` vs `{x, y}`; cancel `y`, leaving `s(x) ≻ x`, true by subterm clause.

Hence `plus(s(x),y) ≻ plus(x,y)`, and by the precedence clause `plus(s(x),y) ≻ s(plus(x,y))`. ✔

### Conclusion

Every rule strictly decreases under the RPO instance defined by the precedence `times > plus > s > 0` with multiset status on `times` and `plus`. Since RPO is a well-founded order on terms built purely from the syntactic signature (no external structure, no numeric semantics, no side conditions), this establishes **termination of the entire rewrite system** using only the rules as given.
