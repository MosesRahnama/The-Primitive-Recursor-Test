**Why I chose the assessment and method I did:**

I picked **the standard structural-termination argument for the natural-number encoding** — a method well known in term rewriting for this exact shape of system — because the system is essentially the textbook TRS encoding of arithmetic with factorial, and the rules are individually well-founded in a way that does not require importing any object-level axioms.

**Specific reasoning for choosing this approach:**

1. **Shape of the rules.** Every recursive clause consumes one constructor from a recursive argument before recurring (`s(y) → y`, `s(x) → x`, `p(s(x)) → …`, `p(s(s(x))) → …` followed by `p(s(0)) → 0`). This is the classic "terminating under the natural-number interpretation" pattern, and proving termination from the rules alone is exactly the job of a TRS termination tool.

2. **No need for semantic / object-level assumptions.** Because the only functions are the constructors `s`, `0` and the defined ones `plus`, `times`, `p`, `fac`, well-foundedness follows from the syntactic form of the rules: each recursive call is on a *strict syntactic subterm* (modulo a constructor). That is a property of the rewrite system itself, not of a separate model.

3. **Why not use a heavy-weight proof technique?** Things like dependency pairs (Arts & Giesl) or polynomial/ matrix interpretations (Lankford, 1979) would also work, but for this system the structural argument is cleaner and the question is about whether *any* termination argument from the rules alone exists. I picked the simplest one that applies.

4. **Why I rejected alternative answers.** One could in principle raise doubts: e.g., is `p(s(x))` really well-defined? — but termination is not the same as totality. The question only asks whether the rewrite relation is well-founded, not whether every natural number has a factorial. Even if one worries about `p` on arguments not in the image of `s` (there are no closed ground terms of that form other than `0`, and `p(0)` is irreducible and contributes no cycles), no rule produces a cycle, so termination holds for *all* closed terms — and a fortiori for terms built from the constructors.

So: I chose the structural / rule-only termination argument because it is the natural one for this system, it uses nothing beyond the given rules, and it directly answers the question asked.
