**Assessment: Yes** — termination can be established from the rewrite system alone.

**Reasoning:**

The system is a standard first-order encoding of arithmetic on the natural numbers (successor `s`, zero `0`), defining `plus`, `times`, predecessor `p`, and factorial `fac`. Each recursive rule recurses on a strictly smaller (in the intended natural-number semantics) subterm:

- `plus(x, s(y)) → s(plus(x, y))`: second argument decreases (`s(y) → y`).
- `times(s(x), y) → plus(times(x,y), y)`: first argument decreases (`s(x) → x`).
- `p(s(s(x))) → s(p(s(x)))`: only fires one layer of `s`; the residual `p(s(x))` is in normal form (neither `p(s(0))` nor `p(s(s(_)))` applies to `p(s(x))` with `x` a variable). So `p` itself is terminating on its own.
- `fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`: the recursive call is on `p(s(x))`. Under the interpretation, `p(s(x))` corresponds to `x`, which is strictly smaller than `s(x)`.

No external "object-level" assumptions are required. A termination proof can be carried out purely from the rules with standard TRS termination techniques (e.g., a polynomial / matrix interpretation over ℕ that strictly decreases the multiset of interpretations, or the dependency-pair method showing the argument of `fac` strictly decreases through `p`). The system is the canonical well-known encoding of arithmetic with factorial, and it is strongly normalizing on the basis of its own rules.
