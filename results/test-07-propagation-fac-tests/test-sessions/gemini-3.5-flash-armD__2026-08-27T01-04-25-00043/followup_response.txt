I provided both **Lexicographic Path Order (LPO)** and **Polynomial Interpretations** because they represent the two main, complementary paradigms of termination analysis (syntactic vs. semantic), and each offers distinct advantages for this specific system:

### 1. Why Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) was the primary choice:
* **Natural Fit for Hierarchical Definitions:** This term rewriting system (TRS) is structured hierarchically: `times` is defined in terms of `plus`, which is defined in terms of `s` (successor). LPO is exceptionally good at exploiting this kind of hierarchy. By setting the precedence $\text{times} \succ \text{plus} \succ s \succ 0$, LPO handles the recursive "unfolding" of these operators automatically.
* **No Mathematical Guesswork (Purely Syntactic):** LPO does not require mapping terms to numbers or finding coefficients. It operates entirely on the syntax of the terms themselves. Once the precedence is declared, verifying the rules is a mechanical, finite-step process. 
* **Seamless Handling of Nested Rules:** For rules like $p(s(s(x))) \to s(p(s(x)))$, LPO's subterm property makes proving the reduction trivial (since $s(s(x))$ is structurally larger than $s(x)$), whereas finding working polynomials for such rules can sometimes require tedious algebraic tuning.

### 2. Why Polynomial Interpretations was provided as a backup:
* **Semantic Intuition:** The TRS defines standard arithmetic operations (addition, multiplication, and a predecessor-like function $p$). Polynomial interpretations allow us to assign a concrete mathematical "size" or "energy" to each term that directly aligns with our intuitive understanding of these operations.
* **First-Principles Rigor:** While LPO relies on trusting the mathematical theory of path orderings, a polynomial interpretation reduces the proof to simple high-school algebra over the positive integers ($\mathbb{N}_{\ge 1}$). Because $(\mathbb{N}_{\ge 1}, >)$ is universally recognized as well-founded, showing that $[l] > [r]$ for every rule is a mathematically transparent proof of termination that requires no knowledge of term rewriting theory to verify. 

Choosing these two standard methods ensured that the termination argument was both **computationally elegant** (via LPO) and **algebraically undeniable** (via Polynomials), meeting your requirement for a rigorous, self-contained proof.
