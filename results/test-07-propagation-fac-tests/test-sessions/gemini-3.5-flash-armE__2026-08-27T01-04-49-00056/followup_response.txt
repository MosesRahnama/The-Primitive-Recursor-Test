I chose the **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)** for several specific reasons tied to the structure of your term rewriting system and the constraints of your prompt:

### 1. It perfectly matches the "Primitive Recursive" structure
The rewrite rules define the function $F$ by induction (or primitive recursion) on its third argument:
* The base case is $F(x, y, Z)$.
* The step case is $F(x, y, S(n))$, which makes a recursive call to $F(x, y, n)$ but wraps it inside another function symbol, $G$.

LPO is designed precisely to handle this kind of recursion. By setting the precedence $F \succ G$, we allow the "defined" symbol $F$ to dominate the "auxiliary" symbol $G$. This makes it easy for the ordering to show that the outer $F$ on the left-hand side can "pay for" the creation of the $G$ on the right-hand side.

### 2. It effortlessly handles preserved variables
In the recursive call $F(x, y, n)$, the first two arguments ($x$ and $y$) do not change, while the third argument strictly decreases from $S(n)$ to $n$. 
LPO's **lexicographic comparison** compares arguments from left to right:
* It sees $x = x$ (no change).
* It sees $y = y$ (no change).
* It then looks at the third argument and sees $S(n) \succ_{lpo} n$ (a strict decrease).

Because LPO is defined lexicographically, it handles this "stable parameters with one decreasing parameter" pattern automatically without needing complex mathematical weight formulas.

### 3. It is purely syntactic (No external mathematical assumptions)
Your prompt requested a proof *“without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given syntax.”*

If I had chosen a method like **Polynomial Interpretations** (e.g., mapping terms to polynomials over natural numbers like $[[S(n)]] = n + 1$), the proof would rely on the algebraic properties of addition and multiplication over the natural numbers. Strictly speaking, that imports external mathematical theories. 

LPO, on the other hand, is **purely syntactic**. It operates entirely on:
* The syntax of the terms themselves.
* The subterm relation (which is purely structural).
* A simple user-defined preference order (precedence) on the finite set of symbols ($F \succ G$).

This makes the proof self-contained and mathematically rigorous within the strict boundaries of formal grammar and logic.
