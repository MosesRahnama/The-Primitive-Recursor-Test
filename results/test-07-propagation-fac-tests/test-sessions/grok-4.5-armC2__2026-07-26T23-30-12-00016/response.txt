### Assessment: Yes

The given TRS (over the signature containing at least the symbols appearing in the rules) is terminating, and this can be established by a purely syntactic/analysis-based argument that inspects only the rules themselves. No structural axioms (e.g., freeness/injectivity of \(s\), totality of a predecessor on a base sort, induction principles) and no object-level semantic assumptions beyond the existence of a well-founded carrier for a reduction pair are required.

A standard dependency-pair argument works. The dependency pairs are
\[
\begin{align*}
\operatorname{plus}^\sharp(x,s(y))&\to\operatorname{plus}^\sharp(x,y),\\
\operatorname{times}^\sharp(s(x),y)&\to\operatorname{times}^\sharp(x,y),\\
\operatorname{times}^\sharp(s(x),y)&\to\operatorname{plus}^\sharp(\operatorname{times}(x,y),y),\\
p^\sharp(s(s(x)))&\to p^\sharp(s(x)),\\
\operatorname{fac}^\sharp(s(x))&\to\operatorname{fac}^\sharp(p(s(x))),\\
\operatorname{fac}^\sharp(s(x))&\to\operatorname{times}^\sharp(\operatorname{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x)).
\end{align*}
\]
The estimated dependency graph decomposes into SCCs whose recursive pairs are essentially the “self-calls” of \(\operatorname{plus}^\sharp\), \(\operatorname{times}^\sharp\), \(p^\sharp\) and \(\operatorname{fac}^\sharp\). Each SCC is solved by a reduction pair generated from a simple polynomial interpretation over the non-negative integers (the same interpretation works uniformly):
- \([[0]]=0\), \([[s]](n)=n+1\),
- \([[p]](n)=\max(n-1,0)\),
- projection onto the first argument for \(\operatorname{plus}^\sharp\) and \(\operatorname{times}^\sharp\),
- the identity for \(p^\sharp\) and \(\operatorname{fac}^\sharp\).

All ordinary rules (and all usable rules of each SCC) are weakly oriented; each recursive dependency pair is strictly oriented. The base cases of \(\operatorname{plus}\), \(\operatorname{times}\) and \(p\) contribute no dependency pairs and are likewise weakly oriented by the same interpretation. Consequently every dependency chain is finite, so the original TRS terminates.

(The same conclusion can be reached by other standard processors—e.g., a suitable matrix interpretation or a multi-set path order after an argument filter—but the dependency-pair+polynomial proof already shows that nothing beyond the rewrite rules is required.)

Final answer: yes
