### Why dependency pairs + polynomials

I chose that method because it is a standard, purely syntactic, modular technique that applies directly to the given rules with no extra axioms, and it makes the termination argument easy to check by hand.

- The system is a typical first-order TRS with a mix of primitive-recursive definitions (plus, times, fac) and a slightly non-standard predecessor. Dependency pairs isolate exactly the recursive calls, so each function can be treated separately.
- The estimated dependency graph immediately breaks into tiny SCCs (one per recursive function). That keeps the proof obligations small.
- A single, simple polynomial interpretation (the usual “size” measure) orients every recursive dependency pair strictly and every ordinary/usable rule weakly. No elaborate orders, no semantic side-conditions, and no need to reason about the intended domain of the symbols.
- Other common methods (RPO/LPO with argument filters, matrix interpretations, etc.) also work, but they are heavier to write out by hand for this example; DPs + polynomials give the shortest fully rigorous certificate that stays entirely inside the presented rewrite system.

In short, it is the lightest tool that still yields a complete, self-contained termination proof from the rules alone.
