## Verdict

**Yes.** Termination follows from the rules alone. No arithmetic meaning for `0`, `s`, `plus`, or `times`, and no associativity, commutativity, induction, or constructor axioms are needed.

Use the **lexicographic path ordering (LPO)** with precedence

\[
\mathsf{times} \succ \mathsf{plus} \succ \mathsf{s} \succ 0,
\]

and lexicographic status for the binary symbols. LPO is a well-founded, substitution-stable, context-closed simplification ordering on finite first-order terms.

Let \(>\) denote this LPO.

### Base rules

By the strict subterm property:

\[
\mathsf{times}(x,0)>0,
\]

\[
\mathsf{plus}(x,0)>x,
\]

\[
\mathsf{plus}(0,x)>x.
\]

Thus the first, third, and fourth rules strictly decrease.

### Recursive `times` rule

Because \(\mathsf{s}(y)>y\), lexicographic comparison gives

\[
\mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
>
\mathsf{times}(x,y).
\]

Also,

\[
\mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))>x
\]

by the subterm property. Since \(\mathsf{times}\succ\mathsf{plus}\), the left-hand side dominates the root and both arguments of the right-hand side, so

\[
\mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
>
\mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{times}(x,y),x).
\]

### Recursive `plus` rules

Again, \(\mathsf{s}(y)>y\), hence lexicographically

\[
\mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
>
\mathsf{plus}(x,y).
\]

Since \(\mathsf{plus}\succ\mathsf{s}\),

\[
\mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
>
\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y)).
\]

Similarly, \(\mathsf{s}(x)>x\), so

\[
\mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)
>
\mathsf{plus}(x,y),
\]

and therefore

\[
\mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)
>
\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y)).
\]

Every rewrite rule is thus strictly decreasing under one well-founded reduction ordering. Consequently, no infinite rewrite sequence exists, and the TRS is terminating purely syntactically.
