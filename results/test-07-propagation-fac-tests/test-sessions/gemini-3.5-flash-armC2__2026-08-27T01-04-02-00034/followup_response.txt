I chose the **Dependency Pair (DP) Framework** combined with a **Polynomial Interpretation** for three primary reasons:

### 1. Syntactic path orders (like LPO/MPO) struggle with defined functions in recursive arguments
In simpler term rewriting systems, one might try to use standard syntactic relations like the **Lexicographic Path Order (LPO)** or **Multiset Path Order (MPO)**. However, these syntactic orders struggle with this system because of the factorial rule:
$$\text{fac}(s(x)) \to \text{times}(\text{fac}(p(s(x))), s(x))$$

To prove termination syntactically, we would need the argument of the recursive call to be strictly smaller than the input, meaning we must show $s(x) \succ p(s(x))$. 
Because $p$ is a *defined function symbol* (not a constructor) and does not appear in the left-hand side $s(x)$, standard LPO cannot prove this without complex argument filterings, because it cannot "know" that $p(s(x))$ evaluates to something smaller than $s(x)$.

### 2. The Dependency Pair Framework isolates the recursion
The DP Framework (developed by Arts and Giesl) is the modern standard for termination analysis. It mathematically isolates the actual "loops" (recursive calls) from the rest of the term structure. 

By focusing only on the transition:
$$\text{fac}^\sharp(s(x)) \to \text{fac}^\sharp(p(s(x)))$$
we no longer have to prove that the entire right-hand side (including the `times` multiplication) is smaller than the left-hand side. We only have to show that the control flow state decreases, which is a much simpler constraint to satisfy.

### 3. Polynomial interpretations naturally match arithmetic rewrite rules
Since the rewrite system is defining standard arithmetic operations over Peano numbers, mapping these terms to actual algebraic polynomials over the natural numbers ($\mathbb{N}$) is the most intuitive and direct method. 

By defining the interpretation of the predecessor function as $[p](x) = x$ and the successor as $[s](x) = x + 1$, the complex symbolic reduction:
$$s(x) \to p(s(x))$$
simplifies semantically to a trivial, strictly decreasing mathematical inequality:
$$x + 1 > x$$

This combination provides a highly robust, fully automatable, and easily verifiable proof that mirrors our mathematical intuition of why the system terminates.
