I picked Recursive Path Ordering (RPO) because the system exhibits a simple, structural descent that is perfectly captured by the purely syntactic nature of the order, without needing any external lemmas, arithmetic interpretations, or inductive hypotheses.

Specifically:

- **Structural recursion on the third argument**: The second rule reduces the argument `S(n)` to `n` in the recursive call. RPO can detect that `S(n) > n` straight from the subterm property—no external theory of natural numbers is needed.
- **Wrapper symbol `G`**: The result is wrapped in `G(y, ·)`, but the overall call on the left has root `F`. By giving `F` higher precedence than `G`, RPO directly accepts the left side as larger than the whole right side, provided each of its immediate subterms is smaller. This maps directly onto the rule’s structure.
- **No extra assumptions**: The proof requires only a total precedence on the symbols that appear (`F > G`, `S > Z`). That’s a finite piece of information derivable purely from the syntax of the rules, matching the constraint “no imported object-level axioms.”
- **Simplicity over other methods**: Alternatives like polynomial interpretations would require choosing a concrete monotone algebra and checking inequalities, which is heavier. Structural induction would involve an external principle about the shape of values. RPO is a slick, self-contained syntactic check that terminates these kinds of “primitive-recursive-looking” definitions immediately.

In short, RPO was chosen because the system’s termination proof reduces to recognizing a subterm decrease and a decrease in the root symbol—both of which RPO handles mechanically without any hidden strengthening.
