The dependency‑pair method with a polynomial interpretation was chosen because the system contains a recursion pattern that is not simply structural: the rule  

`fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`

reduces the argument `s(x)` to `p(s(x))`, which is not an immediate subterm (and `p(s(x))` is not reducible by the given rules when `x` is a variable). A direct syntactic path order (like a recursive path order) would therefore fail.  

The dependency‑pair framework isolates the recursive calls from the surrounding context, and a **reduction pair** that interprets all function symbols as arithmetic functions on ℕ (with `0`, `s`, `p`, `plus`, `times`, `fac` given their natural numerical values) neatly captures the decrease.  

This approach does **not** import any structural axioms or object‑level assumptions. The interpretation functions are defined externally on the domain ℕ, not as rewrite rules, and they are only used to establish a well‑founded order. The equalities needed to show that the rules are weakly decreasing hold **by the definition of the interpretation**, not by assuming any extra equations between terms. No additional rewrite rules, guards, or side conditions are added to the system—only a meta‑level ordering is employed, which is standard and fully self‑contained.
