Use the **lexicographic path ordering** (LPO) with lexicographic status for the binary symbols and precedence

\[
\mathsf{times} \succ \mathsf{plus} \succ \mathsf{s} \succ \mathsf{0}.
\]

This is a well-founded simplification order on first-order terms over the given finite signature. It is stable under substitutions and contexts, so if every left-hand side is strictly greater than its right-hand side, termination follows.

Write \(>_{\mathrm{LPO}}\) simply as \(>\).

1. **`times(x, 0) -> 0`**

   \[
   \mathsf{times}(x,0) > 0
   \]

   since the left side has root symbol \(\mathsf{times}\), which has higher precedence than the constant \(0\) (and, equivalently, the constant is a proper subterm argument position of the left side only in the trivial ordering sense required by LPO).

   \[
   \mathsf{times} \succ \mathsf{plus}.
   \]

   \[
   \mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y)) > \mathsf{times}(x,y)

   because the roots are the same and, lexicographically,

   \[
   \mathsf{s}(y) > y
   \]

   by the subterm property.

   Also,

   \[
   \mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y)) > x
   \]

   by the subterm property. Therefore

   \[
   \mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
   >
   \mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{times}(x,y),x).

4. **`plus(0, x) -> x`**

   \[
   \mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))

   \mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)
