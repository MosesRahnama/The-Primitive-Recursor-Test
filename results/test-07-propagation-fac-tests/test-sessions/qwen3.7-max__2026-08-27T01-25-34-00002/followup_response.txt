I chose the **Dependency Pair (DP) framework combined with monotone algebra interpretations** because simpler, classical termination methods provably fail on this specific term rewriting system. 

Here is the technical breakdown of why standard alternatives fail and why this specific combination was necessary:

### 1. Why Syntactic Orderings (KBO / RPO) Fail
*   **Knuth-Bendix Ordering (KBO):** KBO requires that no variable appears more times on the right-hand side (RHS) of a rule than on the left-hand side (LHS). The rule `times(s(x), y) -> plus(times(x, y), y)` duplicates the variable `y` (once on LHS, twice on RHS). KBO fails immediately.
*   **Recursive Path Ordering (RPO/LPO):** To orient the factorial rule `fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`, RPO requires the LHS to be strictly greater than the RHS. This requires `fac(s(x)) > fac(p(s(x)))`, which in turn requires the argument `s(x)` to be strictly greater than `p(s(x))`. In RPO, a term cannot be strictly greater than another term that contains it as a direct subterm (since `s(x)` is the argument to `p`). RPO fails.

### 2. Why Direct Polynomial Interpretations Fail
Standard polynomial interpretations over $\mathbb{N}$ require every function symbol to be interpreted as a polynomial with natural number coefficients. This creates a fatal conflict with the predecessor function `p`:
*   To make the recursive call in `fac` terminate, the interpretation of `p` must strictly decrease its argument: $[p](x+1) < x+1$. Over $\mathbb{N}$, the only polynomials that satisfy this are constants or sub-linear functions.
*   However, the rule `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))` requires weak decrease: $[p](x+2) \ge [p](x+1) + 1$. This forces $[p]$ to grow at least linearly (e.g., $[p](x) = x + c$).
*   You cannot satisfy both conditions simultaneously with standard polynomials. (Furthermore, $[p](x) = x - 1$ is invalid because it maps $0$ to $-1 \notin \mathbb{N}$).

### 3. Why the Chosen Method Succeeds
The DP framework with monotone algebras bypasses these exact roadblocks:
*   **The DP Framework** breaks the proof into independent Strongly Connected Components (SCCs). This allows us to demand *strict* decrease only for the recursive calls (the dependency pairs) and only *weak* decrease for the usable rules. This relaxation is what makes handling `p` mathematically possible.
*   **Monotone Algebras (Non-polynomial interpretations)** allow the use of well-founded, weakly monotonic functions that are not rigid polynomials. By using truncated subtraction (`max(0, x - 1)`) for `p` and the factorial function for `fac`, we perfectly capture the operational semantics of the system. These functions are weakly monotonic over $\mathbb{N}$ (satisfying the algebra requirements) but successfully model the strict decrease needed for the `fac` recursion.

In short, this method was chosen because it is the minimal standard technique capable of handling the specific combination of **variable duplication** (in `times`), **predecessor semantics** (in `p`), and **factorial recursion** (in `fac`) without requiring the addition of extra rules, guards, or structural axioms.
