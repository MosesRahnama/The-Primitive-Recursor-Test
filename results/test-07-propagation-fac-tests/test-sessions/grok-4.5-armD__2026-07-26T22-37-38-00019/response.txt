### Verdict
Yes, termination can be established from the given rules alone.

### Termination Argument
The system is a finite first-order TRS over the signature \(\{0, s, \mathsf{plus}, \mathsf{times}, p\}\) (with \(0\) a constant and the rest of the indicated arities). Termination follows by exhibiting a reduction order that orients every rule: the lexicographic path order (LPO) induced by the precedence
\[
\mathsf{times} \succ \mathsf{plus} \succ p \succ s \succ 0
\]
(with lexicographic status, left-to-right). LPO is a well-founded simplification order, hence stable under contexts and substitutions; it is therefore enough to check that each left-hand side is strictly larger than the corresponding right-hand side. The checks are as follows (using the standard recursive clauses of LPO).

- \(\mathsf{plus}(x,0) \to x\).  
  The right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.

- \(\mathsf{plus}(x,s(y)) \to s(\mathsf{plus}(x,y))\).  
  Root symbols satisfy \(\mathsf{plus} \succ s\), so it is enough to verify the single strict inequality \(\mathsf{plus}(x,s(y)) \succ_{\mathrm{lpo}} \mathsf{plus}(x,y)\). The roots are identical, the first arguments are identical, and the second arguments satisfy \(s(y) \succ_{\mathrm{lpo}} y\) (subterm). The side condition that the left-hand side dominates both arguments of the right-hand side holds by the subterm property.

- \(\mathsf{times}(0,y) \to 0\) and \(\mathsf{times}(x,0) \to 0\).  
  In both cases the root of the left-hand side is strictly above the constant \(0\) in the precedence, and the left-hand side dominates the (empty) tuple of arguments of the right-hand side.

- \(\mathsf{times}(s(x),y) \to \mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{times}(x,y),y)\).  
  Root symbols satisfy \(\mathsf{times} \succ \mathsf{plus}\), so it is enough to verify the two inequalities \(\mathsf{times}(s(x),y) \succ_{\mathrm{lpo}} \mathsf{times}(x,y)\) and \(\mathsf{times}(s(x),y) \succ_{\mathrm{lpo}} y\). The second is immediate (subterm). For the first the roots are identical, the second arguments are identical, and the first arguments satisfy \(s(x) \succ_{\mathrm{lpo}} x\) (subterm); the required side conditions again hold by the subterm property.

- \(p(s(s(x))) \to s(p(s(x)))\).  
  Root symbols satisfy \(p \succ s\), so it is enough to verify \(p(s(s(x))) \succ_{\mathrm{lpo}} p(s(x))\). The roots are identical and the arguments satisfy \(s(s(x)) \succ_{\mathrm{lpo}} s(x)\) (identical roots and \(s(x) \succ_{\mathrm{lpo}} x\)). The side condition holds by the subterm property.

- \(p(s(0)) \to 0\).  
  The root of the left-hand side is strictly above the constant \(0\) in the precedence.

Every rule is therefore strictly decreasing in LPO. Consequently every rewrite sequence is finite.

(Note that the argument nowhere relies on semantic interpretations of the symbols, on additional rewrite rules, on guards, or on any theory beyond the pure equational consequences of the seven given rules.)
