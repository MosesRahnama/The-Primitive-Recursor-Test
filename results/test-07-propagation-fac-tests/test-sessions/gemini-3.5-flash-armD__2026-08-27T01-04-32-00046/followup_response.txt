I chose the **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)** for this proof for several specific reasons that align with both the structure of your Term Rewriting System (TRS) and the constraints of your prompt:

### 1. Natural Fit for Hierarchical Definitions (Precedence)
Your TRS defines operations hierarchically, where more complex operations are defined in terms of simpler ones:
* `times` is defined using `plus` ($\text{times}(s(x), y) \rightarrow \text{plus}(\dots)$).
* `plus` is defined using successor `s` ($\text{plus}(x, s(y)) \rightarrow s(\dots)$).
* `p` (predecessor) is defined using `s` ($p(s(s(x))) \rightarrow s(p(s(x)))$).

LPO is designed precisely to exploit this hierarchical structure. By setting the precedence of the function symbols to mirror this hierarchy ($\text{times} \succ \text{plus} \succ p \succ s \succ 0$), the termination proof becomes highly intuitive and direct.

### 2. Purely Syntactic (No External Axioms Required)
Your prompt explicitly requested a proof **"without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions."** 
* Other popular termination proof methods, such as **Polynomial Interpretations**, map terms to mathematical structures (like the natural numbers $\mathbb{N}$). Proving termination with polynomials requires importing the axioms of arithmetic (e.g., proving that $x + y > x$ for all $x, y \in \mathbb{N}$).
* **LPO**, on the other hand, is a purely syntactic, structural order. It operates solely on the symbols and terms defined in your system. It does not require any external model, algebra, or mathematical domain to be well-defined.

### 3. Elegant Handling of Structural Recursion
The recursive steps in your rules reduce the "size" of the terms structurally (e.g., from $s(y)$ to $y$ in the second argument of `plus`, and from $s(x)$ to $x$ in the first argument of `times`).
* LPO’s **subterm property** ($f(\dots, t, \dots) \succ t$) and its recursive definition naturally capture this structural decrease. 
* For rule 6 ($p(s(s(x))) \rightarrow s(p(s(x)))$), LPO easily handles the fact that the root symbol changes from $p$ to $s$ because $p \succ s$, while proving that the inner argument structurally decreased from $s(s(x))$ to $s(x)$.

### 4. Avoiding "Overkill"
While more powerful frameworks like **Dependency Pairs (DP)** or **Matrix Interpretations** exist, they are designed for systems with complex, non-obvious recursion (such as nested or mutually recursive rules). Your system is primitive recursive and structurally simple, meaning the heavier machinery of DP frameworks is unnecessary. LPO provides the most direct, elegant, and self-contained proof.
