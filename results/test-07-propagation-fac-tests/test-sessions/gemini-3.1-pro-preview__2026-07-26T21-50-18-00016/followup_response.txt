I chose the combination of the **Dependency Pair (DP) framework** and a **weakly monotone polynomial interpretation** because simpler, more traditional methods completely fail on this specific Term Rewriting System (TRS) for two very distinct mathematical reasons. 

To prove termination, one must show that every rewrite step represents a decrease in some well-founded measure. Here is exactly why standard methods fail, forcing the use of the DP framework:

### 1. Why Syntactic Path Orderings (LPO/MPO) fail
Standard termination tools often try Lexicographic or Multiset Path Orderings (LPO/MPO). These orderings rely heavily on the **subterm property** (a term is always strictly greater than its subterms). 

Look at the recursive rule for factorial:
`fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`

For LPO/MPO to prove this rule terminates, it must establish that the recursive argument `p(s(x))` is structurally smaller than the original argument `s(x)`. However, `p(s(x))` is **not a syntactic subterm** of `s(x)`—it introduces a completely new function symbol (`p`). Because of this, pure syntactic simplification orderings get "stuck" and cannot orient this rule.

### 2. Why a Direct Polynomial Interpretation fails
When syntactic methods fail, the next step is usually to map terms to polynomials over the natural numbers, requiring the Left-Hand Side to be strictly greater than the Right-Hand Side ($L > R$) for every rule.

Look at the same rule again:
`fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`

If we try to find a polynomial where $L > R$, we are asking for an interpretation of `fac(x)` that strictly bounds standard mathematical multiplication. Because factorial grows super-exponentially ($O(x!)$), **no polynomial in existence can bound it**. Therefore, a direct strict polynomial interpretation over $\mathbb{N}$ is mathematically incapable of proving termination for this system.

### The Elegant Solution: Dependency Pairs
The DP framework was invented specifically to bypass these roadblocks. It does so by separating the *value a function computes* from the *control flow of its recursion*. 

By switching to Dependency Pairs:
1. **We only need strict decrease ($>$) on the recursive calls (the DPs).** I mapped the recursive tracker `[FAC](x)` to simply `x`, completely ignoring the multiplication. The rule `FAC(s(x)) -> FAC(p(s(x)))` easily evaluates to $x+1 > x$, proving the loop itself is finite.
2. **We only need weak decrease ($\ge$) for the original rules.** Because we only need $L \ge R$, we are no longer required to model the explosive growth of factorial! We can simply interpret the "value" of `[fac](x)` as `0`. The impossible burden of proving that a polynomial is greater than factorial vanishes into $0 \ge 0$.

In short, I chose this specific method because it neatly sidesteps the lack of subterm property and the super-polynomial growth that render older, traditional termination methods entirely powerless against this system.
