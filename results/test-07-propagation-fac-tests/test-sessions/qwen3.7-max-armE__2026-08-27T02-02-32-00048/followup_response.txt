I chose the **Polynomial Interpretation** method primarily for its **algebraic self-containment** and **ease of verification**, though I also noted the **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)** because it represents the true structural nature of the system. 

Here is a detailed breakdown of why I made this choice, contrasting the "algebraic" approach with the "structural" approach.

### 1. Avoidance of "Imported" Structural Machinery
The prompt strictly forbade importing "structural axioms or object-level assumptions." 
* If I were to use a path ordering like LPO or RPO as the primary proof, I would have to formally invoke the inductive definitions of those orderings. This requires importing the **subterm property**, defining a **precedence relation** ($F > G$), and defining an **argument status** (lexicographic left-to-right). 
* By contrast, a Polynomial Interpretation builds a well-founded ordering from scratch using the natural numbers ($\mathbb{N}$) and the standard strictly-greater-than relation ($>$). The reader only needs elementary algebra (addition and multiplication) to verify the proof. It relies on zero term-rewriting-specific structural axioms.

### 2. Handling the "Peano" Assumptions Naturally
In many term rewriting systems involving `Z` (zero) and `S` (successor), there is a temptation to import Peano axioms (e.g., $S(n) \neq Z$, or $S$ is injective). 
* The polynomial interpretation elegantly sidesteps this by mapping `Z` to `0` and `S(n)` to `n + 1`. 
* It proves termination purely based on the *size* of the terms decreasing, without needing to assume anything about the structural distinctness or injectivity of the constructors.

### 3. The "Heavy Hammer" vs. The "Scalpel"
In automated termination proving (using tools like AProVE or TTT2), polynomial and matrix interpretations are often used as "heavy hammers." They are highly robust, easily automated via SAT/SMT solvers, and can prove termination even when the recursion is deeply nested or non-structural. I provided it because it is a universally bulletproof method that leaves no room for ambiguity.

### 4. Acknowledging the True Nature of the TRS (Why LPO is the conceptual match)
While I used polynomials for the formal proof, I included the side-note about **LPO** because the TRS is a textbook example of **primitive recursion** on the third argument. 
* **Rule 1** is the base case: it collapses the function to a subterm (`x`).
* **Rule 2** is the recursive step: it structurally deconstructs the third argument (`S(n) -> n`), passes the first two arguments through unchanged, and wraps the recursive call in a "lower" function symbol (`G`).

Conceptually, the system terminates simply because **the third argument is structurally shrinking in Rule 2**, and Rule 1 stops the recursion. LPO is the "scalpel" designed exactly for this shape of recursion. However, because LPO requires defining the structural rules of the ordering itself, the polynomial interpretation remained the safer, more self-contained choice for the primary mathematical argument. 

**Summary:** I picked Polynomial Interpretation because it reduces the termination proof to basic, indisputable arithmetic, strictly honoring the prompt's boundary against importing external structural frameworks, while perfectly capturing the decreasing "size" of the terms.
