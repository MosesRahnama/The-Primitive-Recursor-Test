### Why LPO

I chose the lexicographic path order because it is a standard simplification order that applies directly and exclusively to the six given rules: one simply fixes a precedence on the four symbols that appear in those rules, checks that each left-hand side is strictly larger than the corresponding right-hand side, and concludes well-foundedness. No auxiliary rewrite rules, no semantic interpretation of the symbols, no induction hypotheses about natural numbers, and no side conditions beyond the purely syntactic definition of LPO are required.  

The system is a classic constructor-based TRS (constructors \(0,\texttt{s}\); defined symbols \(\texttt{plus},\texttt{times}\)). For exactly this shape, a single LPO with the natural precedence \(\texttt{times}>\texttt{plus}>\texttt{s}>0\) orients every rule in one pass, which is why the method is both sufficient and minimal for the constraints stated in the query. Other techniques (polynomial interpretations, Knuth–Bendix order, dependency pairs, \ldots) also work, but each either needs extra numeric coefficients or generates intermediate constraints that go beyond “the presented rules alone.” LPO stays inside that boundary.
