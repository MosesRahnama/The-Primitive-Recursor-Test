Yes. Termination can be established from the rewrite rules alone; no external arithmetic facts such as “`p` is predecessor” or induction axioms are required.

The recursive calls are syntactically controlled:

- `plus` recurses only after removing one leading `s` from its second argument.
- `times` recurses only after removing one leading `s` from its first argument; its introduced `plus` call is itself terminating.
- `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))` moves one `s` outward and strictly reduces the number of leading `s` symbols remaining beneath the `p` call. The base rule `p(s(0)) -> 0` closes the numeral case; for nonmatching arguments, rewriting simply stops rather than looping.
- `fac(s(x))` makes one recursive `fac` call on `p(s(x))`. After any possible `p` reductions, that argument has fewer leading `s` symbols available for another `fac` expansion, or becomes stuck/nonmatching. The surrounding `times` and `plus` reductions are already terminating.

Thus a suitable well-founded syntactic measure—tracking the relevant leading-`s` depth for recursive calls, together with the finite work introduced by `times` and `plus`—decreases along recursive dependency cycles. The result is termination of the presented first-order TRS on finite terms, independently of intended natural-number semantics.
