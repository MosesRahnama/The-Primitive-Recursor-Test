A standard **lexicographic path ordering** (LPO) proves termination.

Take the precedence

\[
\mathsf{times} \;>\; \mathsf{plus} \;>\; \mathsf{s} \;>\; \mathsf{0},
\]

with lexicographic status for the binary symbols `times` and `plus`. LPO with a finite precedence is well-founded, so it suffices to orient every rewrite rule strictly from left to right.

Let \(>\) denote this LPO.

1. **`times(x, 0) -> 0`**

   \[
   \mathsf{times}(x,0) > 0
   \]

   because `times` has higher precedence than the constant `0`.

2. **`times(x, s(y)) -> plus(times(x, y), x)`**

   Since \(\mathsf{times} > \mathsf{plus}\), it suffices that

   \[
   \mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y)) >
   \mathsf{times}(x,y)
   \quad\text{and}\quad
   \mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y)) > x.
   \]

   The latter holds by the subterm property. For the former, compare lexicographically:

   \[
   (x,\mathsf{s}(y)) >_{\mathrm{lex}} (x,y),
   \]

   since \(\mathsf{s}(y) > y\) by the subterm property. Hence

   \[
   \mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
   >
   \mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{times}(x,y),x).
   \]

3. **`plus(x, 0) -> x`**

   \[
   \mathsf{plus}(x,0) > x
   \]

   by the subterm property.

4. **`plus(0, x) -> x`**

   \[
   \mathsf{plus}(0,x) > x
   \]

   by the subterm property.

5. **`plus(x, s(y)) -> s(plus(x, y))`**

   Since \(\mathsf{plus} > \mathsf{s}\), it suffices to show

   \[
   \mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
   >
   \mathsf{plus}(x,y).
   \]

   This follows lexicographically because the first arguments are identical and

   \[
   \mathsf{s}(y) > y.
   \]

   Therefore,

   \[
   \mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
   >
   \mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y)).
   \]

6. **`plus(s(x), y) -> s(plus(x, y))`**

   Again \(\mathsf{plus} > \mathsf{s}\), so it suffices to show

   \[
   \mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)
   >
   \mathsf{plus}(x,y).
   \]

   Lexicographically this holds because

   \[
   \mathsf{s}(x) > x.
   \]

   Thus,

   \[
   \mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)
   >
   \mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y)).
   \]

Every rewrite rule is strictly decreasing under the same well-founded LPO. Therefore there can be no infinite rewrite sequence.

Hence the rewrite system is **terminating**, purely as a first-order term rewriting system and without importing any additional structural or object-level assumptions.
