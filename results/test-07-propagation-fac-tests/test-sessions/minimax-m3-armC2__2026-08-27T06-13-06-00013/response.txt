**Assessment: Yes, termination can be established from the rewrite system alone.**

**Reasoning:**

The system is a standard first-order encoding of Peano arithmetic operations (plus, times, predecessor p) together with a recursive definition of factorial (fac). None of the rules introduce unbounded growth on their own, and the critical rule

> fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))

is well-founded because the argument p(s(x)) to the recursive call is *strictly smaller* than s(x) in a derivable, well-founded sense:

1. The p-rules alone form a terminating sub-system: p(s(0)) → 0 removes the constructor entirely, and p(s(s(x))) → s(p(s(x))) peels off an outer s while leaving a strictly smaller p-redex. By induction on the p-rules, for any ground term t, the p-normal form of s(t) is strictly smaller (in the number of leading s's) than s(t).

2. Therefore each application of the fac rule consumes one outer layer of s on the recursion argument, and the inner p(s(x)) computation terminates independently.

3. The remaining plus/times rules are the classical right-/left-recursive definitions; their termination is the textbook case (provable, e.g., by a lexicographic path ordering with the precedence fac > times > plus > p > s > 0, or by a polynomial/matrix interpretation, or by a lexicographic combination measure on the first argument of times/s and the second argument of plus).

A termination proof can be carried out purely with the rule apparatus (e.g., via the recursive path ordering, polynomial interpretation, or the dependency-pair framework) without importing any external structural axioms such as injectivity/disjointness of 0 and s or induction over N. The well-foundedness used is only the meta-level well-foundedness of the natural numbers (or of the chosen interpretation), not an object-level property of the signature.

So: termination **is** provable from the rules as presented.
