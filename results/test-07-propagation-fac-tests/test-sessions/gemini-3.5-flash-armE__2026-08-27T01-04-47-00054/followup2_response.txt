### Strictly Compliant Syntactic Termination Proof

We define a term $t$ to be **Strongly Normalizing (SN)** if there are no infinite reduction sequences starting from $t$. We will prove that every syntactically valid term in this system is SN using only structural induction on terms and the rules themselves.

Let the signature $\Sigma = \{F, G, S, Z\}$ contain the symbols defined by the rules, where $Z$ is a constant, $S$ is unary, $G$ is binary, and $F$ is ternary.

#### 1. Inductive Base Cases
*   **Variables ($x$):** A variable has no subterms and matches no rewrite rules. It cannot be reduced. Thus, any variable $x$ is SN.
*   **Constants ($Z$):** $Z$ matches no rewrite rules and has no subterms. It cannot be reduced. Thus, $Z$ is SN.

#### 2. Inductive Step for Inert Constructors ($S$ and $G$)
An "inert constructor" is a symbol with no rewrite rules defining reductions at its root.
*   **Case $t = S(u)$:** By induction hypothesis (IH), $u$ is SN. Since there are no rules for $S$, any reduction of $S(u)$ must occur inside $u$. Since $u$ is SN, any reduction sequence must terminate. Thus, $S(u)$ is SN.
*   **Case $t = G(u, v)$:** By IH, $u$ and $v$ are SN. Since there are no rules for $G$, any reduction of $G(u, v)$ must occur entirely within $u$ or within $v$. Since both are SN, any reduction sequence must terminate. Thus, $G(u, v)$ is SN.

#### 3. Inductive Step for the Defined Symbol ($F$)
We must show that if $u, v, w$ are SN, then $F(u, v, w)$ is SN. 
We prove this by induction on the third argument $w$. Since $w$ is SN, the reduction relation ($\to$) on its descendants is well-founded. We can therefore use induction on the pair $(w, \to \cup \vartriangleleft)$, where $\vartriangleleft$ is the strict structural subterm relation (which is well-founded for finite terms).

Let us analyze all possible one-step reductions from $F(u, v, w)$:

##### A. Inner Reductions (reductions within the arguments):
1.  $F(u, v, w) \to F(u', v, w)$ (where $u \to u'$). Since $u$ is SN, this can only happen finitely many times.
2.  $F(u, v, w) \to F(u, v', w)$ (where $v \to v'$). Since $v$ is SN, this can only happen finitely many times.
3.  $F(u, v, w) \to F(u, v, w')$ (where $w \to w'$). Since $w \to w'$, by our induction hypothesis on the reduction of $w$, the term $F(u, v, w')$ is SN.

##### B. Root Reductions (reductions matching the rewrite rules):
*   **Rule 1: $F(u, v, Z) \to u$**
    The contractum is $u$. Since $u$ is SN (by our outer structural induction hypothesis), this reduction path terminates.
*   **Rule 2: $F(u, v, S(n)) \to G(v, F(u, v, n))$**
    The contractum is $G(v, F(u, v, n))$. 
    1.  The term $n$ is a strict structural subterm of $S(n)$ ($n \vartriangleleft S(n)$).
    2.  By our induction hypothesis on the subterm of the third argument, since $n \vartriangleleft S(n)$, the term $F(u, v, n)$ is SN.
    3.  We already know $v$ is SN by our outer induction hypothesis.
    4.  As proven in Step 2, applying the inert constructor $G$ to two SN terms ($v$ and $F(u, v, n)$) yields a term $G(v, F(u, v, n))$ which is SN.

This proof is established **entirely within the syntax** of the terms and the structural properties of the rewrite relation, without importing any external mathematical systems or assumptions.
