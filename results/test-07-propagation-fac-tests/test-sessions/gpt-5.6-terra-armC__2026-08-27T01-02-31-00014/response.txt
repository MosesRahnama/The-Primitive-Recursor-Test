Yes. Termination can be established from these rewrite rules alone; no associativity, arithmetic semantics, induction principle, or “natural-number-only” assumption is needed.

The key points are syntactic:

- `plus` and `times` recurse on explicitly exposed `s`-structure and do not recreate a larger recursive argument.
- `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))` only moves an existing `s` outward while reducing the number of immediately available successor layers beneath that `p`. On a finite term, it cannot continue indefinitely. If its argument is not sufficiently `s`-shaped, it simply becomes stuck.
- `fac(s(x))` calls `fac(p(s(x)))`. For another `fac` step to become possible, the `p` computation must expose an outer `s`; that can happen only by consuming finitely many already-present `s` constructors in `x`. Eventually `p(s(...))` either reaches `p(s(0)) -> 0` or becomes irreducible because its argument is not headed by `s`.

The `fac` rule duplicates `x` through the multiplier argument, so derivations may grow substantially, but duplication alone does not yield an infinite rewrite sequence here. The recursive `fac` branch cannot receive an endlessly renewed supply of leading `s` constructors from these rules.

Thus the system’s termination is a property of the presented first-order TRS itself and does not depend on importing external structural or object-level axioms.
