The intended method was compliant in the sense that it used only the TRS syntax and rewrite relation: no arithmetic interpretation, no extra equations, no added guards, and no restriction of variables to numerals.

However, my presentation was not a fully adequate termination proof. It was an informal proof sketch with an important gap.

In particular, I treated the visible leading `s`-prefix of a `p` argument as if it were fixed. But rewriting inside that argument can expose new leading `s` constructors. For example, an argument such as

```text
plus(0, s(0))
```

is not initially headed by `s`, yet it rewrites to `s(0)`. Thus a term of the form

```text
p(s(plus(0, s(0))))
```

can later acquire further applicable `p` reductions even though the relevant `s` structure was not initially visible.

That does not import an external assumption or necessarily make the system nonterminating; it means that the claimed “prefix decreases” argument must be strengthened into a genuine global well-founded measure or a complete standard proof procedure, one that accounts for rewriting below `p` and for duplication by `times` and `fac`.

So the accurate assessment is:

- **The proposed style of proof is permitted by the requirements.**
- **My stated sketch did not fully establish termination under those requirements**, because it did not supply or verify a global descent argument for arbitrary first-order terms and arbitrary rewrite sequences.
- A proper proof would need to formalize that descent using, for example, an appropriate reduction ordering, dependency-pair analysis with justified processors, or an explicitly defined well-founded measure derived solely from the rules.
