The given system is a pure first-order TRS (no binders, no extra equations). Its rewrite relation on finite terms is in fact well-founded, but that fact cannot be obtained from the rules alone.

The only non-trivial recursive rule is
```
fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x)).
```
The recursive argument `p(s(x))` is not a subterm of the left-hand side. Consequently every standard syntactic termination technique that relies solely on the term algebra (recursive/path orders, Knuth-Bendix orders, polynomial interpretations over \(\mathbb{N}\), \ldots) fails: the necessary orientation of the dependency pair
```
FAC(s(x)) ▹ FAC(p(s(x)))
```
conflicts with the orientation demanded by the rules for `p`.  

Any successful termination argument must therefore switch to a semantic measure—classically the multiset of the natural-number values of all arguments of `fac`. Justifying that this measure is well-founded and decreases requires exactly the structural properties of the natural numbers (the constructor discipline for `0`/`s`, the defining equations of the predecessor, and the well-foundedness of \(<\) on \(\mathbb{N}\)). None of those properties is an equational consequence of the eight rewrite rules; they must be imported from outside.

Hence termination cannot be established from the presented rewrite system alone.
