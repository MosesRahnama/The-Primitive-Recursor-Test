**Verdict: yes.** The TRS is terminating on all finite first-order terms. No additional rewrite rules, guards, or object-level assumptions are needed.

A dependency-pair proof works, using a monotone semantic interpretation only as a meta-level termination measure.

### 1. Weak interpretation of all rewrite rules

Interpret terms over \(\mathbb N\) by

\[
\begin{aligned}
[0]&=0,\\
[s](n)&=n+1,\\
[p](n)&=\max(n-1,0),\\
[plus](n,m)&=n+m,\\
[times](n,m)&=nm,\\
[fac](n)&=n!.
\end{aligned}
\]

All these functions are monotone. Every rewrite rule is weakly decreasing—in fact, both sides have equal value:

- \(plus(x,0)\to x\):
  \[
  n+0=n.
  \]

- \(plus(x,s(y))\to s(plus(x,y))\):
  \[
  n+(m+1)=(n+m)+1.
  \]

- \(times(0,y)\to0\) and \(times(x,0)\to0\):
  \[
  0m=0,\qquad n0=0.
  \]

- \(times(s(x),y)\to plus(times(x,y),y)\):
  \[
  (n+1)m=nm+m.
  \]

- \(p(s(s(x)))\to s(p(s(x)))\):
  \[
  \max(n+2-1,0)=n+1
  \]
  and
  \[
  \max(n+1-1,0)+1=n+1.
  \]

- \(p(s(0))\to0\):
  \[
  \max(1-1,0)=0.
  \]

- \(fac(s(x))\to times(fac(p(s(x))),s(x))\):
  \[
  (n+1)! = n!(n+1),
  \]
  because \([p(s(x))]=n\).

Thus intervening rewrite steps cannot increase this interpretation.

### 2. Dependency pairs

Writing defined symbols in dependency pairs with capital letters, the relevant pairs are

\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathrm{PLUS}(x,s(y))&\to\mathrm{PLUS}(x,y),\\
\mathrm{TIMES}(s(x),y)&\to\mathrm{PLUS}(times(x,y),y),\\
\mathrm{TIMES}(s(x),y)&\to\mathrm{TIMES}(x,y),\\
\mathrm{P}(s(s(x)))&\to\mathrm{P}(s(x)),\\
\mathrm{FAC}(s(x))&\to\mathrm{TIMES}(fac(p(s(x))),s(x)),\\
\mathrm{FAC}(s(x))&\to\mathrm{FAC}(p(s(x))),\\
\mathrm{FAC}(s(x))&\to\mathrm{P}(s(x)).
\end{aligned}
\]

Pairs whose marked root changes only lead downward in the dependency graph:

\[
\mathrm{FAC}\to\mathrm{TIMES},\quad
\mathrm{FAC}\to\mathrm{P},\quad
\mathrm{TIMES}\to\mathrm{PLUS}.
\]

There are no edges back. Hence the only cyclic dependency-pair components are the self-recursive pairs for `PLUS`, `TIMES`, `P`, and `FAC`.

Each is strictly decreasing under a marked-symbol projection:

1. **PLUS component:** interpret
   \[
   [\mathrm{PLUS}](n,m)=m.
   \]
   Then
   \[
   [\mathrm{PLUS}(x,s(y))]=m+1>m
   =[\mathrm{PLUS}(x,y)].
   \]

2. **TIMES component:** interpret
   \[
   [\mathrm{TIMES}](n,m)=n.
   \]
   Then
   \[
   [\mathrm{TIMES}(s(x),y)]=n+1>n
   =[\mathrm{TIMES}(x,y)].
   \]

3. **P component:** interpret
   \[
   [\mathrm P](n)=n.
   \]
   Then
   \[
   [\mathrm P(s(s(x)))]=n+2>n+1
   =[\mathrm P(s(x))].
   \]

4. **FAC component:** interpret
   \[
   [\mathrm{FAC}](n)=n.
   \]
   Since \([p(s(x))]=n\),
   \[
   [\mathrm{FAC}(s(x))]=n+1>n
   =[\mathrm{FAC}(p(s(x)))].
   \]

All ordinary rewrite steps are weakly decreasing under the common interpretation from step 1, while every recursive dependency-pair transition in a cyclic component strictly decreases a natural number. Therefore no cyclic component admits an infinite dependency chain. The acyclic cross-component pairs cannot occur infinitely without eventually entering one of those components.

By the standard dependency-pair termination theorem, the original rewrite system is terminating.

The factorial and predecessor functions used above are only proof interpretations; they do not add equations or rewrite behavior to the presented system.
