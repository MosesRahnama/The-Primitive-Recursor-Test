I picked dependency pairs because the system’s termination structure is **call-based**, while several rules are not directly decreasing as whole terms.

In particular:

- `plus` recurses on its second argument:
  \[
  plus(x,s(y))\to s(plus(x,y)).
  \]
- `times` recurses on its first argument, but wraps that recursive result in `plus`:
  \[
  times(s(x),y)\to plus(times(x,y),y).
  \]
- `fac` recurses through the non-constructor expression `p(s(x))` and wraps the result in `times`:
  \[
  fac(s(x))\to times(fac(p(s(x))),s(x)).
  \]

That last rule makes a simple syntactic subterm argument unsuitable: \(p(s(x))\) is not literally a subterm of \(s(x)\), and the rules do not include a general rule \(p(s(x))\to x\). So I did not want to smuggle in that equation as an object-level assumption.

Dependency pairs solve exactly this issue: they extract only the potentially infinite sequence of recursive calls. The recursive calls then have clear semantic decreases:

\[
\begin{aligned}
PLUS(x,s(y))&\to PLUS(x,y),\\
TIMES(s(x),y)&\to TIMES(x,y),\\
P(s(s(x)))&\to P(s(x)),\\
FAC(s(x))&\to FAC(p(s(x))).
\end{aligned}
\]

The interpretation

\[
[s](n)=n+1,\qquad [p](n)=\max(n-1,0)
\]

proves, at the meta-level, that

\[
[p(s(x))]=[x],
\]

without asserting or adding the rewrite rule \(p(s(x))\to x\). Thus the `FAC` dependency pair strictly decreases from \(n+1\) to \(n\).

A second requirement is that ordinary rewriting between recursive calls must not increase the measure. That motivated the interpretation

\[
[plus](n,m)=n+m,\quad [times](n,m)=nm,\quad [fac](n)=n!,
\]

under which every original rule preserves value. Factorial was chosen because the `fac` rule has exactly the numerical shape

\[
(n+1)! = n!(n+1).
\]

So the method was chosen because it cleanly separates two jobs:

1. **Weak compatibility with every full rewrite rule**, including the expanding `times` and `fac` rules.
2. **Strict decrease of each genuinely recursive call**, including the non-syntactic `fac` call through `p`.

A direct global simplification order or elementary size measure would be considerably less natural here: several right-hand sides are larger than their left-hand sides, and the crucial factorial decrease is semantic rather than an immediate syntactic subterm decrease. Dependency pairs expose precisely the decreases needed without strengthening the TRS.
