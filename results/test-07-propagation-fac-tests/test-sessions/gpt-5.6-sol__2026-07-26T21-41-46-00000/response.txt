## Verdict

**Yes.** Termination follows from the presented rewrite rules alone. A standard dependency-pair proof with a well-founded interpretation over \(\mathbb N\) establishes termination for all terms, not merely constructor-ground terms. No additional rewrite rules, guards, or assumptions that variables denote numerals are needed.

## 1. Weak interpretation of all rewrite rules

Interpret unmarked symbols over \(\mathbb N\) by

\[
\begin{aligned}
[0] &= 0,\\
[s](a) &= a+1,\\
[plus](a,b) &= a+b,\\
[times](a,b) &= ab,\\
[p](a) &= \max(a-1,0),\\
[fac](a) &= a!,
\end{aligned}
\]

with \(0!=1\). These functions are weakly monotone.

Every rewrite rule preserves its interpretation exactly:

\[
[plus(x,0)] = x = [x],
\]

\[
[plus(x,s(y))] = x+y+1
               = [s(plus(x,y))],
\]

\[
[times(0,y)] = 0,\qquad [times(x,0)]=0,
\]

\[
[times(s(x),y)] = (x+1)y
                = xy+y
                = [plus(times(x,y),y)],
\]

\[
[p(s(s(x)))] = x+1
              = [s(p(s(x)))],
\]

\[
[p(s(0))]=0,
\]

and

\[
[fac(s(x))]=(x+1)!
\]

while

\[
[times(fac(p(s(x))),s(x))]
   =[fac(x)](x+1)
   =x!(x+1)
   =(x+1)!.
\]

Thus every rewrite step is weakly decreasing—in fact interpretation-preserving—even when performed inside a context.

This interpretation is only a meta-level termination measure. It does not assume that object-level terms are natural-number representations.

## 2. Dependency pairs

The relevant dependency pairs are:

\[
PLUS^\#(x,s(y)) \to PLUS^\#(x,y),
\]

\[
TIMES^\#(s(x),y) \to TIMES^\#(x,y),
\]

\[
TIMES^\#(s(x),y)
 \to PLUS^\#(times(x,y),y),
\]

\[
P^\#(s(s(x))) \to P^\#(s(x)),
\]

and from the factorial rule:

\[
FAC^\#(s(x))
 \to TIMES^\#(fac(p(s(x))),s(x)),
\]

\[
FAC^\#(s(x))
 \to FAC^\#(p(s(x))),
\]

\[
FAC^\#(s(x))
 \to P^\#(s(x)).
\]

The only cyclic strongly connected components are the self-recursive pairs for \(PLUS^\#\), \(TIMES^\#\), \(P^\#\), and \(FAC^\#\). The other pairs only lead from one defined symbol to another without a path back.

Interpret the marked symbols in their respective cyclic components by projection:

\[
[PLUS^\#](a,b)=b,
\qquad
[TIMES^\#](a,b)=a,
\qquad
[P^\#](a)=a,
\qquad
[FAC^\#](a)=a.
\]

Then every cyclic dependency pair is strictly decreasing:

\[
[PLUS^\#(x,s(y))]=y+1>y=[PLUS^\#(x,y)],
\]

\[
[TIMES^\#(s(x),y)]=x+1>x=[TIMES^\#(x,y)],
\]

\[
[P^\#(s(s(x)))]=x+2>x+1=[P^\#(s(x))],
\]

and, crucially,

\[
[FAC^\#(s(x))]=x+1
   >x
   =[FAC^\#(p(s(x)))].
\]

Here the last equality uses

\[
[p(s(x))]=\max((x+1)-1,0)=x.
\]

All intervening ordinary rewrite steps are weakly decreasing under the unmarked interpretation. Therefore an infinite dependency chain in any cyclic component would induce an infinite strictly descending sequence in \(\mathbb N\), which is impossible.

## Conclusion

By the dependency-pair termination theorem, the TRS is terminating. The proof covers every rule, applies to arbitrary first-order terms, and uses no structural or object-level assumptions beyond the given syntax and rules.
