The method complied because it did **not** add or assume any object-level facts such as:

- every term is a numeral;
- \(p(s(x))=x\) as an additional rewrite rule;
- induction principles internal to the TRS;
- constructor freeness, injectivity, or disjointness axioms;
- guards restricting variables to natural-number terms;
- extra equations for arithmetic or factorial.

The interpretation over \(\mathbb N\) is a **meta-level termination measure**, as routinely used in semantic interpretations and dependency-pair proofs. It does not claim that the terms themselves form the standard natural numbers. Variables are simply assigned numbers by arbitrary valuations, and each displayed rewrite rule is checked under every such valuation.

In particular, the equality

\[
[p(s(x))]=[x]
\]

comes from the chosen interpretation

\[
[s](a)=a+1,\qquad [p](a)=\max(a-1,0),
\]

not from importing the object-level equation \(p(s(x))\to x\). Likewise, interpreting `fac` by factorial is a choice of measure, not the addition of factorial axioms to the rewrite system.

The only external facts used are meta-theoretic facts inherent in the standard proof method:

- \(\mathbb N\) with \(>\) is well-founded;
- dependency-pair termination implies termination of a finite first-order TRS;
- the usual properties of the chosen numerical functions.

Those are not structural or object-level assumptions about the presented TRS.
