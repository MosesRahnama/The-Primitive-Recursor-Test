### Why dependency pairs (with a simple reduction pair / path order)

I chose that route for three tightly related reasons that are internal to the given TRS:

1. **The system is almost orthogonal and constructor-based, but not simply terminating by a plain RPO/LPO.**  
   A direct lexicographic or recursive path order with any precedence that makes the `fac` rule decrease (e.g. `fac > times > plus > p > s`) fails on the recursive call `fac(p(s(x)))`, because `p(s(x))` is not structurally smaller than `s(x)` under the usual multiset/status extensions once `p` is smaller than `s`. Polynomial interpretations run into the same obstacle: the predecessor rules force a weakly decreasing interpretation for `p`, which then makes it hard to obtain a strict decrease for `fac`. Hence a plain simplification order on the original rules is inconvenient, while the dependency-pair transformation isolates exactly the one problematic recursive call.

2. **Only one non-trivial dependency pair appears.**  
   After the DP transformation the only pair that is not immediately decreasing is  
   `fac♯(s(x)) → fac♯(p(s(x)))`  
   (all other DPs are headed by `plus` or `times` and are oriented by the same order that already orients those defining rules). The usable rules for that pair are precisely the two `p`-rules. This reduces the whole termination question to a trivial two-rule argument: show that repeated application of the `p`-rules to a term of the form `p(s(t))` cannot produce an infinite chain and eventually yields a constructor term strictly smaller than the original argument of `fac`. That is immediate by a simple polynomial interpretation (`p(x) = x`, `s(x) = x+1`) or by a multiset path order on the DPs, both of which stay entirely inside the given signature and rules.

3. **The method is self-contained and does not import external induction or freeness axioms.**  
   Everything needed—generation of dependency pairs, computation of usable rules, and orientation by a reduction pair—is a purely syntactic calculation on the eight rules that appear in the prompt. No semantic notion of “natural number,” no assumption that `s` is injective, and no imported lemmas about `plus`/`times` are required; the well-foundedness of the induced DP relation already implies termination of the original TRS.

In short, dependency pairs are the lightest standard technique that turns an otherwise awkward recursive call through `p` into an obviously well-founded comparison, while still working exclusively with the concrete rewrite rules that were supplied.
