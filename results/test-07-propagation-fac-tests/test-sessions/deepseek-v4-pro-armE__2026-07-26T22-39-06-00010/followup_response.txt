I chose **Recursive Path Ordering (RPO)** because it is a purely syntactic, well-founded monotone order that works directly on the given rules without requiring any external semantic interpretations, extra axioms, or strengthening of the system.  

The termination structure here is very simple:

- Rule 1 (`F(x, y, Z) → x`) is a base case that projects to a subterm, immediately oriented by RPO’s subterm property.
- Rule 2 (`F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))`) makes a recursive call on a smaller third argument (`S(n) → n`) and uses a helper symbol `G` of lower precedence. RPO handles this by:
  - a precedence `F > G` to drop the outer constructor, and
  - a lexicographic comparison on arguments that respects the decreasing `S(n) > n` via the subterm property.

Thus RPO is the most direct and minimal method: a single precedence and the built‑in subterm relation suffice to orient both rules strictly, giving an immediate proof of strong normalization. No polynomial interpretations, dependency pairs, or other machinery was needed.
