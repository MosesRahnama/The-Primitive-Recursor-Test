## Why Polynomial Interpretation?

I chose it for three concrete reasons specific to this system:

**1. The system is small, linear, and constructor-shaped — a textbook fit.**

The signature has the classical "decrement-one-constructor" pattern `Z`/`S(n)` and a single recursive function `F` over the third argument. Polynomial interpretations (Lankford's method) are the standard, automatic-feeling hammer for exactly this shape: you write down linear/quadratic templates for each symbol, check finitely many inequalities, and you're done. No induction, no semantic reasoning, no need to inspect the rewrite graph globally.

**2. G has no rules, and that is the key observation the method lets you exploit cleanly.**

Looking at the rules, the only way a rewrite step "discards structure" is Rule 2: `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))`. The first argument `x` vanishes, the second argument `y` is wrapped in the nullary-with-respect-to-measure `G`, and only the third argument actually shrinks. The natural, parsimonious way to encode this is to give `G` interpretation `0` — it "costs nothing" in the measure, so the y-subterm can be safely dropped from the LHS bound without requiring extra equations, side conditions, or auxiliary lemmas about context. With the interpretation `F(x,y,z) = x + y + z + 1`, both rules have LHS that strictly dominates RHS for *any* natural numbers substituted. No per-instance case analysis needed.

**3. It respects the stated constraints.**

The prompt forbids adding rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, or hidden assumptions. Polynomial interpretation is a pure *syntactic* well-foundedness certificate: I assign a fixed polynomial to each symbol, and the check `⟦l⟧ > ⟦r⟧` is a first-order inequality in the variables. Nothing is added to the system; the measure is external to the rewrite relation. By contrast, other valid methods would have been awkward here:
- A **recursive path order / lexicographic combination** would work (compare the third argument first, since it is structurally smaller on the RHS of Rule 2) but requires more explicit argumentation about the tuple order.
- A **semantic interpretation into ℕ** (a "natural semantics" or computed function argument) works — and is essentially what the polynomial interpretation *is* — but the polynomial version gives a closed-form witness that is easy to verify by hand.
- **Dependency pairs** are designed for non-termination/certifiability frameworks and would be overkill for two rules.

So: minimal, automatic for this shape, exploits the inertness of `G` without extra machinery, and produces a checkable certificate. That is why I reached for it.
