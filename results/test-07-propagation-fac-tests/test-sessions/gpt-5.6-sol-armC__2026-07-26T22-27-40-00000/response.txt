**Assessment: yes.** Termination can be established purely from the given syntactic rewrite rules; no assumption that every term is a numeral, nor an external predecessor axiom such as \(p(s(x))=x\), is required.

A suitable semantic-labeling or dependency-pair proof can interpret

- \(s(n)=n+1\),
- \(p(n)=\max(n-1,0)\),
- `plus` and `times` as addition and multiplication,
- `fac` as factorial.

All displayed rules are valid in this interpretation. Labels then expose the relevant descent: `plus` decreases its second recursive argument, `times` its first, `p` moves through a finite successor spine, and the recursive `fac` call has predecessor label \(n\) instead of \(n+1\). Stuck non-numeral-shaped terms do not create recursion and therefore do not threaten termination.

This interpretation is a meta-level termination proof device, not an imported object-level axiom.
