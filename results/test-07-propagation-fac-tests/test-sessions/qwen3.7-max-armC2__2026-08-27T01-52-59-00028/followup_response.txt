I chose the **structural/semantic measure based on "s-depth"** (effectively a form of Size-Change Termination or a custom semantic interpretation) because it is the most direct and robust way to handle the specific interaction between constructors and destructors in this system. 

Here is the detailed reasoning for why I picked this method over standard automated termination techniques:

### 1. The Failure of Simple Syntactic Orderings (LPO/RPO/KBO)
Standard path orderings like Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO) or Knuth-Bendix Ordering (KBO) rely on a strict precedence of function symbols. This system creates a **precedence clash** that breaks these methods out-of-the-box:

* **The `p` rule requires `p > s`:** To orient `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))`, the root symbol changes from `p` to `s`. In LPO, this strictly requires `p` to have a higher precedence than `s` (`p > s`).
* **The `fac` rule requires `s > p`:** To orient the recursive call in `fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`, we must show that the argument to `fac` strictly decreases: `s(x) > p(s(x))`. Because the root symbols differ, LPO requires `s` to have a higher precedence than `p` (`s > p`).

This creates an impossible cycle (`p > s` and `s > p`). Furthermore, even if we ignore precedence, standard LPO requires the left-hand side to be strictly greater than all arguments of the right-hand side. To prove `s(x) > p(s(x))`, LPO would require `s(x) > s(x)`, which is false (they are only equal). Thus, simple syntactic orderings fundamentally fail on this system.

### 2. The Awkwardness of Standard Polynomial Interpretations
Polynomial interpretations map terms to polynomials over the natural numbers. While they can handle addition and multiplication easily, they struggle with the predecessor function `p`. 
* To model `p(s(x)) -> x`, we would need `[p](x+1) = x`, which implies `[p](x) = x - 1`. 
* However, standard polynomial interpretations require mappings to be strictly non-negative for all inputs in the domain. `x - 1` yields negative numbers for `x = 0`. 
* While one could use weakly monotonic algebras with $\max(0, x-1)$, this steps outside standard polynomial interpretations and requires more complex, non-standard semantic frameworks.

### 3. The Elegance of the "s-depth" (Size-Change) Method
Because the system is essentially defining primitive recursive functions over Peano naturals, the most natural well-founded measure is the **actual semantic size** of the terms—specifically, the number of `s` (successor) constructors.

By tracking the "s-depth" of the arguments, the method bypasses the syntactic brittleness of path orderings:
* It easily recognizes that `p` acts as a destructor that strictly reduces the `s`-depth of its argument.
* It effortlessly handles the `fac` recursion: it sees that `p(s(x))` inherently has a strictly smaller `s`-depth than `s(x)`, satisfying the well-foundedness requirement without needing to compare the root symbols `s` and `p`.

### Summary
I picked the "s-depth" semantic measure because it aligns perfectly with the operational intent of the rewrite system (Peano arithmetic). It avoids the known theoretical roadblocks of syntactic orderings (the constructor/destructor precedence clash) and standard polynomials (the predecessor non-negativity issue), providing a clean, human-readable, and mathematically sound proof of termination.
