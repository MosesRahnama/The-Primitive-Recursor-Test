I picked that combination for a few concrete reasons tied to the specific shape of these rules, not just because it's a generic "safe" answer:

1. **First I checked for the easy case.** Most of the rules (`plus`, `times`, `p`) are simple recursive definitions where the recursive call is on an immediate subterm (`s(y)→y`, `s(x)→x`, `s(s(x))→s(x)`). That pattern is exactly what a **simplification ordering** (subterm property, e.g. LPO/RPO) or an embedding argument handles trivially. So termination of the "arithmetic core" alone is uncontroversial and I didn't need heavy machinery for that part.

2. **Then I looked for the obstruction.** The rule
   `fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`
   is the one that matters. Here the recursive call `fac(p(s(x)))` is **not** a subterm of the left-hand side `fac(s(x)))` — `p(s(x))` is a term that only *reduces to* `x` after applying the `p`-rules; syntactically `p(s(x))` is a bigger term than `x`. This is precisely the kind of pattern (recursion "under" an auxiliary defined symbol whose value must first be computed) for which **plain simplification orderings fail**, because they only compare term shapes, not reduced/semantic values.

3. **This pushed me toward semantic/interpretation-based methods.** The natural fix is to give `p`, `s`, `fac`, etc. **numeric (polynomial or matrix) interpretations** that reflect their intended semantics — in particular an interpretation where `[p](n)` behaves like decrement, so that `[p(s(x))] < [s(x)]` numerically even though `p(s(x))` is not a subterm of `s(x)`. That's exactly the class of technique (polynomial/matrix interpretations) designed to prove decrease based on *value*, not *syntax*.

4. **I invoked dependency pairs because of the mixed structure.** The system isn't uniformly polynomial-orderable in one global interpretation without care, since `fac`'s growth (factorial) needs a different scale than `plus`/`times`/`p`. The **dependency pair framework** is the standard modular way to isolate the recursive call chains (e.g. `FAC(s(x)) -> FAC(p(s(x)))`) and discharge them using "usable rules" (the `p`, `plus`, `times` definitions) plus a reduction pair (polynomial/matrix interpretation) tailored just to what's needed for that chain. This modularity is why it's the standard tool for handling exactly this "recursion through an auxiliary decreasing function" pattern.

5. **I also recognized the example itself.** This `fac`/`p`/`plus`/`times` system is a classical benchmark in the termination literature (used to illustrate that simplification orderings are insufficient and that semantic interpretations or dependency pairs are needed for predecessor-style definitions). Recognizing that pattern told me directly which proof technique is known to succeed here, rather than needing to invent one from scratch.

So the choice wasn't arbitrary: I diagnosed the specific rule where naive subterm-based reasoning breaks down, identified the semantic (not syntactic) nature of the decrease required, and named the standard mechanized methodology (dependency pairs + polynomial/matrix interpretations) that is built precisely to handle that gap.
