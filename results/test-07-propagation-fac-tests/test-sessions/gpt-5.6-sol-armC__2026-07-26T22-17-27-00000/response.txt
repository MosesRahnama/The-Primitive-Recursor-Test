**Assessment: yes.** Termination can be established from the rewrite rules alone, for arbitrary first-order terms—not merely numeral-shaped terms.

The nontrivial rule is:

```text
fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))
```

Although it increases term size, another `fac` step requires `p(s(x))` eventually to expose an outer `s`. By the given `p` rules, that can happen only when `x` itself exposes another `s`; each such recurrence consumes one successive `s` layer. Non-numeral or otherwise malformed arguments simply become stuck rather than creating a loop.

The remaining recursive rules descend syntactically: `plus` through its second argument, `times` through its first argument, and `p` through the `s`-spine beneath it. A minimal-nonterminating-term argument can combine these observations to exclude an infinite reduction. No equation such as an assumed predecessor law, induction axiom, or restriction to natural-number terms is needed.
