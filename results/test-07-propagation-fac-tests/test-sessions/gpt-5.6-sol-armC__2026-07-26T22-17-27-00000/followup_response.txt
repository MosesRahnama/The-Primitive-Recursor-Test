I chose a **minimal-nonterminating-term / dependency-chain argument** because the usual simple measures are awkward here:

- Several rules increase term size or duplicate arguments, especially `times` and `fac`, so raw term size does not decrease.
- The recursive `fac` argument, `p(s(x))`, is not a syntactic subterm of `s(x)`.
- A straightforward precedence-based order also faces tension: orienting the recursive `p` rule naturally favors `p` over `s`, while showing the `fac` call decreases would naturally favor `s(x)` over `p(s(x))`.

Taking a minimal hypothetical nonterminating term makes all its proper subterms terminating. That lets one ignore infinite activity inside duplicated arguments and focus on possible root-level recursive chains. The `plus`, `times`, and `p` chains descend directly in designated arguments. The only remaining candidate is the `fac` chain, and continuing that chain requires `p(s(x))` to expose an `s`; the given `p` rules can do this only by consuming an existing successive `s` layer. Thus no infinite `fac` dependency chain is possible.

This method is also appropriate because it works for arbitrary first-order terms, including malformed or open terms, without assuming that terms denote natural numbers or importing predecessor equations.
