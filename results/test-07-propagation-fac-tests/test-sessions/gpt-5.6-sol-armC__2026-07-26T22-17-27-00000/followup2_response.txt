**Yes, the method itself complies.** A minimal-nonterminating-term argument is a standard meta-level termination proof for finite first-order terms. It does not add rewrite rules, guards, equations, or assumptions about terms denoting natural numbers.

The key claim is derived directly from the rules:

- For `p(s(x))` to rewrite to something headed by `s`, the applicable root rule must eventually be  
  `p(s(s(u))) -> s(p(s(u)))`.
- Therefore `x` must first reduce to `s(u)`.
- Repeating the `fac` recursion requires exposing and consuming successive `s` layers in this way.
- The alternative rule `p(s(0)) -> 0` stops the recursion, and if neither pattern is exposed, the term is stuck.

In a minimal-nonterminating-term proof, proper subterms such as `x` are already terminating. Since rewriting is finitely branching, a terminating finite term has only finitely many reducts and hence a finite bound on the number of leading `s` constructors it can expose. Thus an infinite `fac` chain cannot be obtained by assuming an unbounded hidden `s` structure.

The phrase “consumes an existing successive `s` layer” needed the bounded-reduct argument above to avoid sounding like an unstated constructor or numeral assumption. With that made explicit, the method uses only standard meta-theory of first-order rewriting and the presented rules.
