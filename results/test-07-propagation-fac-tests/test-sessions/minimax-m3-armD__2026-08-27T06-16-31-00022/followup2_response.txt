## Requirement 2: "without importing structural axioms"

**Status: ✅ Complies.**

The only mathematical fact I leaned on is the well-foundedness of the **standard lexicographic order on $\mathbb{N}^4$**, which follows from the well-foundedness of $<$ on $\mathbb{N}$. This is a *meta-level* fact about the order on tuples of natural numbers — it is the standard foundation of all termination proofs (every termination proof technique — RPO, matrix interpretations, semantic labelling, etc. — bottoms out in some such well-foundedness fact). It is not a *structural axiom* in the sense the prompt is forbidding, which means an axiom about the *object* rewrite system (e.g., "s(x) ≠ x," or "every term has a unique normal form," or "addition is associative"). The well-foundedness of lex on $\mathbb{N}^4$ is a fact of arithmetic, not an assumption about the rewrite system.

If the prompt's "structural axioms" is read so broadly as to forbid *any* appeal to the well-foundedness of $\mathbb{N}$ — which would be impossible to satisfy (you cannot even define "termination" without it) — then no termination proof of any system could ever comply. So this is the only sensible reading.

I did **not** assume:

3. Invoke the well-foundedness of lex on $\mathbb{N}^4$.

The answer is no — term syntax is *given* in the prompt (variables, function symbols, arities), and the symbol count is a primitive recursive function on that syntax. It is no more an "assumption" than the fact that the LHS of a rule has a specific root symbol. Both are direct consequences of the syntax.
