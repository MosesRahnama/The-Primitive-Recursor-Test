## Termination Argument

I construct an explicit well-founded measure $M(t) = (A(t),\, |t|)$ on terms, ordered lexicographically.

**Definitions.**

- $|t|$ = the number of symbol occurrences in $t$ (term size).
- For each occurrence of the constructor $\mathtt{s}(\cdot)$ inside $t$, its **label** is the closest defined-function symbol (i.e., one of $\mathtt{fac}, \mathtt{times}, \mathtt{plus}, \mathtt{p}$) on the path from the root of $t$ down to (but not including) that $\mathtt{s}$. If no defined-function symbol lies on that path, the label is $\bot$.
- $A(t)$ = the multiset of labels of all $\mathtt{s}$-occurrences in $t$.
- The labels are compared by the strict order $\bot < \mathtt{p} < \mathtt{plus} < \mathtt{times} < \mathtt{fac}$, and $A(t)$ is compared in the multiset extension of this order.

Both components live in well-founded domains (a finite ordered set, and $\mathbb{N}$), so their lex combination is well-founded.

**Rule-by-rule verification that $M$ strictly decreases.**

For each rule, I exhibit the structural change that drives the decrease.

| Rule | Change in $A$ | Change in $|t|$ | Verdict |
|------|---------------|------------------|---------|
| 1. $\mathtt{plus}(x,0)\to x$ | No $\mathtt{s}$ in $\mathtt{plus}$ or $0$, so $A$ unchanged | decreases by $2$ | ✓ via $\|t\|$ |
| 2. $\mathtt{plus}(x,\mathtt{s}(y))\to \mathtt{s}(\mathtt{plus}(x,y))$ | The inner $\mathtt{s}$ had label $\mathtt{plus}$; it becomes the outer $\mathtt{s}$ with label $\bot$. So $\{…, \mathtt{plus}, …\} \to \{…, \bot, …\}$. Since $\mathtt{plus}>\bot$, $A$ strictly decreases. | — | ✓ via $A$ |
| 3. $\mathtt{times}(0,y)\to 0$ | No $\mathtt{s}$ introduced or relabeled | decreases | ✓ via $\|t\|$ |

But the second copy of $y$ now sits under $\mathtt{plus}$, so any $\mathtt{s}$ in it is relabeled from $\mathtt{times}$ down to $\mathtt{plus}$. After the rewrite, the $\mathtt{s}$ inside $\mathtt{p}(\mathtt{s}(x))$ has closest defined ancestor $\mathtt{p}$, and the $\mathtt{s}$ as the second argument of $\mathtt{times}$ has label $\mathtt{times}$. So $\{\mathtt{fac}\}\to\{\mathtt{p},\mathtt{times}\}$. Since $\mathtt{fac}$ is greater than both $\mathtt{p}$ and $\mathtt{times}$, $A$ strictly decreases. | — | ✓ via $A$ |

**Conclusion.** Every rule strictly decreases $M(t)$ in the well-founded lex order. No external structure (induction scheme, semantic interpretation, extra rules) is invoked — only the syntactic notion of "closest defined-function ancestor of an $\mathtt{s}$" computed from the given term structure, and term size. Hence the system is terminating from the rules alone. ∎
