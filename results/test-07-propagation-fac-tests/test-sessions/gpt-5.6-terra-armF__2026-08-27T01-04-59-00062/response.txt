**Verdict: yes.** The system is terminating from the displayed rewrite rules alone. No equations such as associativity, commutativity, induction principles, or semantic assumptions about natural numbers are needed.

A standard proof uses the **lexicographic path ordering** (LPO) with lexicographic status for `times` and `plus`, and precedence

\[
\mathsf{times} \;>\; \mathsf{plus} \;>\; \mathsf{s} \;>\; \mathsf{0}.
\]

LPO with a well-founded precedence is itself well-founded. Thus it suffices to show that every left-hand side is strictly greater than its right-hand side in this ordering.

Use the usual LPO facts:

- A term is greater than each of its proper subterms.
- If \(f > g\), then \(f(\ldots)\) is greater than \(g(t_1,\ldots,t_n)\) provided it is greater than every \(t_i\).
- For equal root symbols with lexicographic status, compare arguments left to right; the first strictly decreasing argument establishes the strict decrease.

For each rule:

1.  
\[
\mathsf{times}(x,0)\to 0.
\]

The right-hand side `0` is a proper subterm of the left-hand side, so

\[
\mathsf{times}(x,0) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} 0.
\]

2.  
\[
\mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
 \to
\mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{times}(x,y),x).
\]

Since \(\mathsf{times}>\mathsf{plus}\), it is enough to show

\[
\mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
>
\mathsf{times}(x,y)
\quad\text{and}\quad
\mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))>x.
\]

The second comparison holds because \(x\) is a subterm of the left-hand side.

For the first, both terms have root `times`. Their first arguments agree, and their second arguments satisfy

\[
\mathsf{s}(y)>y
\]

because \(y\) is a proper subterm of \(\mathsf{s}(y)\). Therefore, by lexicographic comparison,

\[
\mathsf{times}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
>
\mathsf{times}(x,y).
\]

Hence the whole multiplication rule decreases.

3.  
\[
\mathsf{plus}(x,0)\to x.
\]

The variable \(x\) is a subterm of the left-hand side, so this decreases.

4.  
\[
\mathsf{plus}(0,x)\to x.
\]

Again, \(x\) is a subterm of the left-hand side, so this decreases.

5.  
\[
\mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
\to
\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y)).
\]

Since \(\mathsf{plus}>\mathsf{s}\), it suffices to show

\[
\mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
>
\mathsf{plus}(x,y).
\]

The roots are both `plus`, their first arguments agree, and

\[
\mathsf{s}(y)>y.
\]

Thus lexicographic comparison gives

\[
\mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
>
\mathsf{plus}(x,y),
\]

and consequently

\[
\mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
>
\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y)).
\]

6.  
\[
\mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)
\to
\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y)).
\]

Again \(\mathsf{plus}>\mathsf{s}\), so it is enough to establish

\[
\mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)
>
\mathsf{plus}(x,y).
\]

Both sides have root `plus`. At the first argument,

\[
\mathsf{s}(x)>x
\]

by the subterm property. Therefore the left-hand side is lexicographically greater than \(\mathsf{plus}(x,y)\), hence greater than its `s`-wrapped right-hand side.

Since every rewrite rule strictly decreases under a well-founded LPO, there cannot be an infinite rewrite sequence. Therefore the presented first-order TRS is terminating.
