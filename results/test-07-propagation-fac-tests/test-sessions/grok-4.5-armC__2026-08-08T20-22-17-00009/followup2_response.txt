### Yes, it complies

The dependency-pair + reduction-pair argument I gave is a standard, purely syntactic termination technique for first-order TRSs. It operates exclusively on the eight rules as written:

- It constructs the (finite) set of dependency pairs directly from the left- and right-hand sides of those rules.
- It builds the dependency graph from the usual syntactic unification/estimation checks on those pairs.
- It exhibits an explicit reduction pair (the weakly monotone interpretation into \(\mathbb{N}\) together with the standard order \((\ge,>)\)) that orients every rule weakly and every cyclic dependency pair strictly.

No extra rewrite rules are added, no guards or side-conditions are attached to existing rules, and no object-level equations (e.g., the missing full predecessor law \(p(s(x))=x\), freeness/injectivity of \(s\), or any arithmetic identities) are assumed or imported. The only well-foundedness used is the meta-level fact that \((\mathbb{N},>)\) is well-founded—the same meta-level ingredient present in every termination proof (RPO, KBO, matrix interpretations, etc.). Everything else is read off from the bare syntax of the given system.

Thus the proof establishes termination of precisely the rewrite relation generated by the presented rules and nothing stronger.
