### Why dependency pairs + reduction pairs?

I chose it because it is a **purely syntactic, fully automatable method that works directly on the given first-order rules** and needs nothing beyond them.

- The TRS is orthogonal (left-linear, no critical pairs) and the recursion is mild, so the dependency graph is tiny and acyclic except for the obvious self-loops of each defined symbol.
- A simple polynomial/algebraic interpretation (the one I wrote down) already orients every cycle strictly and every rule weakly. That is a complete, checkable certificate of termination.
- No semantic appeal to “\(\mathbb{N}\) is well-founded,” no extra equations such as \(p(s(x))=x\), and no induction axioms are required; the well-foundedness of the reduction pair is a meta-level fact about the chosen carrier (here \(\mathbb{N}\)).

Other classical techniques (lexicographic path order, Knuth-Bendix order, recursive path order, \ldots) also succeed, but they are less uniform and sometimes need a bit of manual precedence/status tuning. Dependency pairs + a reduction pair is the most systematic “off-the-shelf” route that stays strictly inside the presented rewrite system, which is exactly what the question asked for.
