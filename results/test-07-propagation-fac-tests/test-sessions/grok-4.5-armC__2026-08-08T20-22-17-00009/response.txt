### Assessment: Yes

The given system is a pure orthogonal first-order TRS (left-linear, no critical pairs). Its termination is a purely syntactic property of the rewrite relation generated by the eight rules and can be established directly from those rules, with no additional object-level equations, freeness/injectivity axioms for the constructors, induction principles, or semantic assumptions about \(\mathbb{N}\) beyond the ordinary meta-level well-foundedness arguments that appear in any termination proof.

A concrete witness is the dependency-pair + reduction-pair method. The defined symbols are \(\mathsf{plus}\), \(\mathsf{times}\), \(\mathsf{p}\) and \(\mathsf{fac}\). The resulting dependency graph contains only a handful of cycles (the recursive calls of each defined symbol). These cycles, together with all original rules, are oriented by the following weakly monotone \(\mathbb{N}\)-interpretation (extended homomorphically to terms):

- \([0]=0\),
- \([s](n)=n+1\),
- \([p](n)=\max(0,n-1)\),
- \([plus](m,n)=m+n\),
- \([times](m,n)=m\cdot(n+1)\),
- \([fac](n)=(n+1)!\).

Under the interpretation every original rule is weakly decreasing, while every dependency pair belonging to a cycle is strictly decreasing. The pair \((\ge,>)\) induced by the interpretation is therefore a reduction pair that proves the absence of infinite chains. (Many other reduction pairs also work; the one above is merely an existence proof.)

All of the above is read off from the rewrite rules alone; no equation that is not already a consequence of the rules (e.g., the “full” predecessor law \(p(s(x))=x\)) is required.
