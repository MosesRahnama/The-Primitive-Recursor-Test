## Termination Argument: Recursive Path Ordering (RPO)

All six rules can be strictly oriented left-to-right using the **Recursive Path Ordering** with the simple precedence on function symbols:

> **times > plus > s**

(0 is a constant and its position in the precedence is irrelevant; it is always handled by the subterm property.)

### RPO Definition (recap)

Given a well-founded strict precedence ≻ on function symbols, *s* >_rpo *t* iff one of:
1. **Subterm:** *s* = f(s₁,…,sₙ) and ∃i such that sᵢ ≥_rpo *t* (≥ means > or syntactic identity).
2. **Precedence:** *s* = f(s₁,…,sₙ), *t* = g(t₁,…,tₘ), f ≻ g, and *s* >_rpo tⱼ for all j.
3. **Same head / lexicographic:** *s* = f(s₁,…,sₙ), *t* = f(t₁,…,tₙ), (s₁,…,sₙ) >_lex (t₁,…,tₙ) w.r.t. >_rpo, and *s* >_rpo tⱼ for all j.

**Rule 3: plus(x, 0) → x**

**Rule 4: plus(0, x) → x**

**Rule 5: plus(x, s(y)) → s(plus(x, y))**
Head of LHS is `plus`, head of RHS is `s`, and plus ≻ s. By condition 2 we must show LHS >_rpo the sole argument of `s`, namely `plus(x, y)`.

- **plus(x, s(y)) >_rpo plus(x, y):** Same head symbol, so use condition 3.
  - Lexicographic comparison of arguments: (x, s(y)) vs (x, y). First pair x, x are identical; second pair s(y) vs y — since y is a subterm of s(y), we have s(y) >_rpo y by condition 1. So (x, s(y)) >_lex (x, y). ✓

**Rule 6: plus(s(x), y) → s(plus(x, y))**
Head of LHS is `plus`, head of RHS is `s`, and plus ≻ s. By condition 2, show LHS >_rpo `plus(x, y)`.

- **plus(s(x), y) >_rpo plus(x, y):** Same head, condition 3.

  - LHS >_rpo x (s(x) is subterm of LHS, s(x) >_rpo x, condition 1) ✓; LHS >_rpo y (y is subterm of LHS, condition 1) ✓.

Therefore plus(s(x), y) >_rpo s(plus(x, y)). ✓

**Rule 2: times(x, s(y)) → plus(times(x, y), x)**
Head of LHS is `times`, head of RHS is `plus`, and times ≻ plus. By condition 2, show LHS >_rpo both arguments of `plus`.

- **LHS >_rpo x** (second argument): x is a direct subterm of LHS. Condition 1. ✓

Therefore times(x, s(y)) >_rpo plus(times(x, y), x). ✓
