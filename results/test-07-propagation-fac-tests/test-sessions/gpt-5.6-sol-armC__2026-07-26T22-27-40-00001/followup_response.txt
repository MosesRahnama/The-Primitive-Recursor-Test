I chose a **dependency/call analysis combined with a natural-number interpretation** because ordinary term size does not decrease for several rules:

- `times(s(x),y)` expands into a larger `plus(...)` term.
- `fac(s(x))` also substantially enlarges the term.
- Several rules preserve, rather than strictly decrease, the semantic valuation.

So a single global “every rewrite makes the term smaller” size argument would be awkward or false for obvious size measures.

The important issue is instead whether **recursive calls** decrease:

- `plus` recurses on `y`;
- `times` recurses on `x`;
- `p` removes one surrounding `s` from its recursive argument;
- `fac` changes its recursive argument from `s(x)` to `p(s(x))`.

The interpretation
\[
|s(t)|=|t|+1,\qquad |p(t)|=\max(|t|-1,0)
\]
makes the last decrease precise for every substituted term:
\[
|p(s(x))|=|x|<|s(x)|=|x|+1.
\]
Likewise, it directly measures the decreases in the other recursive calls. Calls between different symbols form an acyclic hierarchy, such as `fac → times → plus`, so only the self-recursive calls need strict descent.

I picked this method especially because it does **not** assume that terms are natural numbers or that `p` is an externally specified predecessor. The numerical interpretation is only a meta-level termination measure assigned to syntax, and its required properties are checked directly against the displayed rules.
