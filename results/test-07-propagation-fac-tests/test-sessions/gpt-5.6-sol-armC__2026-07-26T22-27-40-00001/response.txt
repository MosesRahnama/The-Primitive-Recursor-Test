**Assessment: Yes.** Termination can be established from the rewrite rules alone over finite first-order terms; no arithmetic or structural object-level axioms are needed.

A useful meta-level valuation is:

- \(|0|=0\)
- \(|s(t)|=|t|+1\)
- \(|p(t)|=\max(|t|-1,0)\)
- \(|plus(a,b)|=|a|+|b|\)
- \(|times(a,b)|=|a|\cdot|b|\)
- \(|fac(t)|=0\)

Every rewrite rule preserves this valuation. Meanwhile, each recursive dependency strictly decreases the relevant argument valuation:

- `plus(x,s(y))` calls `plus(x,y)`;
- `times(s(x),y)` calls `times(x,y)`;
- `p(s(s(x)))` calls `p(s(x))`;
- `fac(s(x))` calls `fac(p(s(x)))`, whose argument has value \(|x|\), versus \(|x|+1\) initially.

The remaining calls are acyclic (`fac → times → plus`, and `fac → p`). Thus there can be no infinite dependency chain, giving termination. Terms not matching a rule may simply become stuck; no assumption that every term represents a natural number is required.
