### Why RPO

I chose the recursive path order because it is a purely syntactic, well-founded simplification order that can be read off the given signature and rules with no extra apparatus.  

- The system is a classic first-order TRS over a free algebra; RPO is the textbook tool designed exactly for that setting.  
- A single, obvious precedence (\(\texttt{times}\succ\texttt{plus}\succ\texttt{s}\succ 0\)) orients every rule by the plain definition of RPO (lex status, subterm property). No interpretation of the symbols, no polynomial or matrix weights, no dependency pairs, no semantic labelling, and no added equations or sorts are required.  
- The method therefore stays strictly inside the boundary you set: only the syntax and the six rewrite rules are used.  

Other standard techniques (e.g., a simple polynomial interpretation or a Knuth–Bendix order) also succeed, but they introduce numeric coefficients or term weights that are unnecessary once a path order already works. RPO is consequently the most economical choice that still gives a complete, self-contained termination argument.
