### Verdict
Yes, termination is established from the given rules alone.

### Termination Argument
We exhibit an explicit reduction order—the recursive path order (RPO)—that orients every rule and is known to be well-founded on the set of ground terms (hence also on open terms). No extra rewrite rules, guards, side-conditions, equational theories, or semantic interpretations of the function symbols are required; the order is defined purely from the signature and a precedence on that signature.

**Precedence.**  
\[
\texttt{times} \succ \texttt{plus} \succ \texttt{s} \succ 0
\]
All symbols are given lexicographic status (left-to-right).

**RPO (definition used).**  
For terms \(s=f(s_1,\dots,s_m)\) and \(t=g(t_1,\dots,t_n)\),
- \(s\succ_{\mathrm{rpo}}t\) if
  - \(f\succ g\) and \(s\succ_{\mathrm{rpo}}t_i\) for every \(i\), or
  - \(f=g\) and \((s_1,\dots,s_m)\succ_{\mathrm{rpo}}^{\mathrm{lex}}(t_1,\dots,t_n)\), or
  - \(s_i\succeq_{\mathrm{rpo}}t\) for some \(i\) (subterm property).
The relation is extended to non-ground terms by treating variables as minimal constants. RPO is a simplification order and therefore well-founded; any rewrite system compatible with it is terminating.

**Orientation of each rule.**

1. \(\texttt{times}(x,0)\to 0\)  
   Root symbol \(\texttt{times}\succ 0\), and the right-hand side has no arguments, so the first clause of RPO applies immediately.

2. \(\texttt{times}(x,\texttt{s}(y))\to\texttt{plus}(\texttt{times}(x,y),x)\)  
   - \(\texttt{times}\succ\texttt{plus}\), therefore it is enough to show  
     \(\texttt{times}(x,\texttt{s}(y))\succ_{\mathrm{rpo}}\texttt{times}(x,y)\)  
     and  
     \(\texttt{times}(x,\texttt{s}(y))\succ_{\mathrm{rpo}}x\).  
   - The second inequality holds by the subterm property.  
   - The first inequality holds by the lexicographic clause: the first arguments are identical and the second arguments satisfy \(\texttt{s}(y)\succ_{\mathrm{rpo}}y\) (again by the subterm property).

3. \(\texttt{plus}(x,0)\to x\)  
   Immediate by the subterm property.

4. \(\texttt{plus}(0,x)\to x\)  
   Immediate by the subterm property.

5. \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y))\to\texttt{s}(\texttt{plus}(x,y))\)  
   - \(\texttt{plus}\succ\texttt{s}\), therefore it is enough to show  
     \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y))\succ_{\mathrm{rpo}}\texttt{plus}(x,y)\).  
   - The latter holds by the lexicographic clause (first arguments identical, second arguments \(\texttt{s}(y)\succ_{\mathrm{rpo}}y\)).

6. \(\texttt{plus}(\texttt{s}(x),y)\to\texttt{s}(\texttt{plus}(x,y))\)  
   - \(\texttt{plus}\succ\texttt{s}\), therefore it is enough to show  
     \(\texttt{plus}(\texttt{s}(x),y)\succ_{\mathrm{rpo}}\texttt{plus}(x,y)\).  
   - The latter holds by the lexicographic clause (already the first arguments satisfy \(\texttt{s}(x)\succ_{\mathrm{rpo}}x\)).

Every left-hand side is strictly greater than the corresponding right-hand side in \(\succ_{\mathrm{rpo}}\). Consequently every rewrite step decreases a well-founded order, so no infinite rewrite sequence exists.

(The same orientation can be obtained with the multiset path order or with a suitable Knuth–Bendix order; the RPO argument above is merely the most economical.)
