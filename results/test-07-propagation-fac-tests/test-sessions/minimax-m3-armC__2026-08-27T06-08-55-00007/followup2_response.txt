# Short answer: Yes, but with one caveat I should flag.

## What I proposed, mapped to the requirements

| Requirement | Compliance |
|---|---|
| Use only the given rules | ✅ LPO, DP-framework, and polynomial/matrix interpretations all operate on the rules as formal objects. They don't add rules or guards. |
| No strengthening with extra rewrite rules | ✅ None added. |
| No side conditions | ✅ All three methods are unconditional on the rhs of the given rules. |
| No hidden assumptions | ✅ Interpretation-based methods require picking a well-founded carrier and monotone/algebraic interpretation. I picked ℕ, but that's a *meta-level* well-founded set used to witness termination — it's not an object-level axiom imported into the TRS. |

## The caveat (the part I should have been more careful about)

There's a subtle distinction worth being explicit about:

- **LPO and DP-framework** are *purely syntactic*. They prove termination by reasoning about term structure and rule shape alone. These are bulletproof under your stated constraints.

- **Polynomial / matrix interpretations** require a *semantic* witness: you define a monotone map `⟦·⟧: Terms → (ℕ → ℕ)` (or to a well-founded quasi-ordered set) such that for every rule `l → r`, `⟦l⟧(x⃗) > ⟦r⟧(x⃗)`. The well-foundedness of ℕ is what's smuggled in — but **this is meta-level machinery, not an object-level axiom about the TRS**. The TRS itself is never told "ℕ is your model" or given any rule involving it. This is the standard, accepted interpretation of "derivable from the given syntax and rewrite rules" in the termination-proving literature.

So strictly: if you meant *"no semantic interpretations either, just syntactic reasoning"*, then only the **LPO + precedence** part of my answer qualifies. The polynomial-interpretation part uses a semantic witness, even if it doesn't add rules to the TRS.

## Honest self-correction

In my previous answer I somewhat blurred this line by jumping to "sufficiently fast-growing polynomial interpretation such as `⟦fac⟧(n) = ω^(ω^n)`" — that example was actually an *ordinal* interpretation, which is more than you need and risks looking like I'm importing heavy set-theoretic structure. The simple integer polynomial `⟦s⟧(n)=n+1, ⟦p⟧(n)=n, ⟦0⟧(n)=0, ⟦plus⟧(n,m)=n+m+1, ⟦times⟧(n,m)=2·n·m, ⟦fac⟧(n)=2^n` is plenty and only uses ℕ.

## Bottom line

- **Syntactic methods (LPO):** fully compliant, no caveats.
- **Semantic methods (polynomial interpretations):** compliant under the standard reading of "from the rules alone" in the termination literature, because the well-founded carrier is meta-level witness, not an imported object-level axiom. But if your reading is stricter — "purely syntactic derivation" — then only the LPO part stands.

The strongest single sentence I can give you: **termination of this system can be established by a single LPO with precedence `times > plus > p > fac > s > 0`, which is a purely syntactic check on the rules as written, with no axioms, rules, or semantic assumptions added.**
