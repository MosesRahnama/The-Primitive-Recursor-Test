### Verdict
Yes, termination can be established from the given rules alone via the dependency pair (DP) method of Arts and Giesl, combined with a (weakly monotonic) interpretation into the well-founded domain of natural numbers. No rules are added, no side conditions or guards are imposed, and no properties are assumed of the symbols beyond the rewrite relation they generate. The argument is purely by exhibiting a reduction pair that weakly orients every rule and strictly orients every dependency pair.

### Dependency pairs
The defined symbols are \(\mathrm{plus}\), \(\mathrm{times}\), \(\mathrm{p}\) and \(\mathrm{fac}\). The dependency pairs extracted from the eight rules are exactly:

- \(\mathrm{plus}^\sharp(x,s(y)) \to \mathrm{plus}^\sharp(x,y)\)
- \(\mathrm{times}^\sharp(s(x),y) \to \mathrm{times}^\sharp(x,y)\)
- \(\mathrm{times}^\sharp(s(x),y) \to \mathrm{plus}^\sharp(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y)\)
- \(\mathrm{p}^\sharp(s(s(x))) \to \mathrm{p}^\sharp(s(x))\)
- \(\mathrm{fac}^\sharp(s(x)) \to \mathrm{fac}^\sharp(\mathrm{p}(s(x)))\)
- \(\mathrm{fac}^\sharp(s(x)) \to \mathrm{times}^\sharp(\mathrm{fac}(\mathrm{p}(s(x))),s(x))\)

(The remaining rules contribute no DPs.)

### Interpretation
Interpret every term in the carrier \(\mathbb{N}\) (ordered by the usual well-founded relation \(>\)) by the following weakly monotonic functions:

\[
\begin{align*}
[[0]] &= 0,\\
[[s]](n) &= n+1,\\
[[p]](n) &= n \mathbin{\dot{-}} 1,\\
[[\mathrm{plus}]](m,n) &= m+n,\\
[[\mathrm{times}]](m,n) &= m\cdot n,\\
[[\mathrm{fac}]](n) &= n!,\\
[[\mathrm{plus}^\sharp]](m,n) &= m+n,\\
[[\mathrm{times}^\sharp]](m,n) &= (m+1)\cdot(n+1),\\
[[\mathrm{p}^\sharp]](n) &= n,\\
[[\mathrm{fac}^\sharp]](n) &= 3\cdot(n+1)!.
\end{align*}
\]

(The factorial on the meta-level is the ordinary primitive-recursive function \(0!=1\), \((n+1)!=(n+1)\cdot n!\); the only properties required below are the elementary arithmetic identities that follow directly from this definition.)

### Orientation of the original rules
For an arbitrary assignment of natural numbers to the variables one has equality of interpretations on every rule (hence the required weak decrease \([[l]]\ge[[r]]\)):

- \([[\mathrm{plus}(x,0)]]=[[x]]\)
- \([[\mathrm{plus}(x,s(y))]]=[[s(\mathrm{plus}(x,y))]]\)
- \([[\mathrm{times}(0,y)]]=[[0]]\)
- \([[\mathrm{times}(x,0)]]=[[0]]\)
- \([[\mathrm{times}(s(x),y)]]=[[\mathrm{plus}(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y)]]\)
- \([[\mathrm{p}(s(s(x)))]]=[[s(\mathrm{p}(s(x)))]]\)
- \([[\mathrm{p}(s(0))]]=[[0]]\)
- \([[\mathrm{fac}(s(x))]]=[[\mathrm{times}(\mathrm{fac}(\mathrm{p}(s(x))),s(x))]]\).

(The equalities are the usual arithmetic facts \(m+0=m\), \(m+(n+1)=(m+n)+1\), \(0\cdot n=0\), \(m\cdot0=0\), \((m+1)\cdot n=m\cdot n+n\), \((n+2)\mathbin{\dot{-}}1=(n\mathbin{\dot{-}}1)+1\), \(1\mathbin{\dot{-}}1=0\) and \((n+1)!=(n\mathbin{\dot{-}}1+1)!\cdot(n+1)\).) Weak monotonicity of every function symbol guarantees that the induced relation is a rewrite quasi-order.

### Orientation of the dependency pairs
For an arbitrary assignment of natural numbers to the variables each dependency pair is mapped to a strict decrease:

- \([[\mathrm{plus}^\sharp(x,s(y))]]=m+(n+1)>m+n=[[\mathrm{plus}^\sharp(x,y)]]\)
- \([[\mathrm{times}^\sharp(s(x),y)]]=(m+2)(n+1)>(m+1)(n+1)=[[\mathrm{times}^\sharp(x,y)]]\)
- \([[\mathrm{times}^\sharp(s(x),y)]]=(m+2)(n+1)>m\cdot n+n=[[\mathrm{plus}^\sharp(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y)]]\)
  (expansion: \(mn+m+2n+2>mn+n\))
- \([[\mathrm{p}^\sharp(s(s(x)))]]=n+2>n+1=[[\mathrm{p}^\sharp(s(x))]]\)
- \([[\mathrm{fac}^\sharp(s(x))]]=3\cdot(n+2)!>3\cdot(n+1)!=[[\mathrm{fac}^\sharp(\mathrm{p}(s(x)))]]\)
- \([[\mathrm{fac}^\sharp(s(x))]]=3\cdot(n+2)!>(n!+1)\cdot(n+2)=[[\mathrm{times}^\sharp(\mathrm{fac}(\mathrm{p}(s(x))),s(x))]]\)
  (division by the positive integer \(n+2\) reduces the claim to \(3\cdot(n+1)!>n!+1\), i.e. \(n!\cdot(3n+2)>1\), which holds for every \(n\in\mathbb{N}\)).

Thus every DP is strictly decreasing. Weak monotonicity of the sharp symbols guarantees that intermediate ordinary rewrite steps (which can only decrease or preserve the interpretation of arguments) cannot increase the measure of a DP term.

### Conclusion
The pair \((\ge,>)\) induced by the interpretation is a reduction pair that weakly orients the whole rewrite system and strictly orients every dependency pair. By the fundamental theorem of the dependency-pair method the system is therefore terminating.

(Note that the same argument also shows that every proper subsystem is terminating, so each individual rule has been accounted for.)
