### Why dependency pairs + interpretation?

The system mixes ordinary primitive recursion (plus/times) with a non-structural “predecessor” p and a factorial whose recursive call is under p.  That combination makes purely syntactic orders (RPO/LPO/KBO with a simple precedence) awkward: one must carefully choose statuses and prove that p(s(x)) is still “smaller” than s(x) in the recursive path order while simultaneously making the nested times/plus calls decrease, all without extra side conditions.  A direct global interpretation on the original signature is likewise painful, because a single measure has to decrease on every rule at once, including the slow decrease of p and the explosive growth of fac.

The dependency-pair method isolates exactly the recursive calls (the six DPs listed earlier).  Once those are separated, a weakly monotonic interpretation into \(\mathbb{N}\) becomes straightforward:

- ordinary plus/times/p receive their natural arithmetic meanings (so every original rule is a numeric identity and therefore weakly oriented);
- the sharp symbols receive slightly inflated measures (the “+1” offsets and the factor \(3\cdot(-)!\) for fac♯) that turn each DP into a strict numeric decrease.

All of this stays inside the pure rewrite system: no extra rules, no guards, no semantic assumptions beyond the inductive definition of the interpretation functions themselves.  The fundamental theorem of Arts & Giesl then yields termination at once.

In short, DPs give the necessary modularity and the interpretation gives an elementary well-founded order; together they discharge every rule with a fully explicit, self-contained argument.  Other standard techniques can be made to work, but they require more delicate bookkeeping or hidden strengthenings that the problem statement asked us to avoid.
