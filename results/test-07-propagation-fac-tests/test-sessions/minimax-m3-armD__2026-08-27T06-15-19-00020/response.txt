## Termination Argument

The key observation is that the three defined functions are **mutually non-cyclic** in a way that admits a straightforward structural termination proof. I verify each function and every rule:

### 1. The function `p` terminates (independent module)

Rules involved:
- `p(s(0)) → 0`
- `p(s(s(x))) → s(p(s(x)))`

Neither rule ever produces a `p`-redex on the RHS that is larger than the one reduced. Concretely, the argument of `p` strictly decreases:
- `s(s(x))` rewrites to a context around `p(s(x))`, where `s(x)` is a proper subterm of `s(s(x))`.
- `s(0)` rewrites to the constant `0`.

By structural induction on the argument of `p`, every `p`-redex terminates. Moreover, `p` does **not appear** in the RHS of any `plus` or `times` rule, so it cannot feed back into the other functions.

### 2. The function `plus` terminates

Rules involved:

- `plus(x, s(y)) → s(plus(x, y))`

The first rule strictly decreases term size (`|plus(x,0)| = |x|+2 > |x|`). By structural induction on the second argument of `plus` (a well-founded order derivable from the rule syntax), `plus` terminates. Note that `plus` does **not** introduce any `times`- or `p`-redex on its RHS, so it cannot create new cyclic dependencies.

### 3. The function `times` terminates

Rules involved:
- `times(0, y) → 0`
- `times(x, 0) → 0`

The first two rules immediately yield a normal form. In the third rule, the first argument strictly decreases from `s(x)` to `x` (a proper subterm), and the RHS is a `plus`-redex whose two arguments are:
- `times(x, y)` — a smaller `times`-redex (smaller first argument),
- `y` — unchanged.

By induction on the size of the first argument of `times`:
- The base cases `times(0, y)` and `times(x, 0)` reduce in one step.
- For `times(s(x), y)`, the inner `times(x, y)` terminates by the induction hypothesis; then the outer `plus(...)` terminates by Point 2.

Because the only recursive call goes through `plus` (which is terminating) and the first argument of `times` decreases at every recursive step, `times` terminates.
