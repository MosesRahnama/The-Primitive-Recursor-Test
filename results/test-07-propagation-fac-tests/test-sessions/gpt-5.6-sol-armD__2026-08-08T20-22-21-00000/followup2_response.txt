It uses only:

- the listed rewrite rules;
- a standard meta-level termination theorem: if every rule decreases under a well-founded ordering stable under substitutions and contexts, then the TRS terminates.

The chosen symbol precedence and lexicographic statuses are **proof parameters**, not additions to the rewrite system. They introduce no new rules, equations, guards, or restrictions on when rewriting may occur. Likewise, LPO’s subterm property is a property of the syntactic ordering, not an object-level axiom such as an induction principle or an arithmetic fact.

- \(0\) and \(s\) denote natural numbers;
- `plus`, `times`, or `p` have their customary arithmetic meanings;
- terms satisfy any unlisted equations;
- variables range only over constructor terms or normal forms;
- rewriting follows a particular evaluation strategy.

LPO is stable under arbitrary substitutions, so the argument applies when variables are replaced by any finite terms over the signature, including terms containing defined symbols.

The only background fact being used is that ordinary first-order terms are finite syntactic trees. That is part of the stated first-order term-rewriting framework, not an imported object-level assumption. If infinite terms were intended, that would be a different rewriting setting, but nothing in the prompt indicates that.

Thus the ordering is an external proof instrument that certifies the original rules exactly as given; it does not strengthen or alter the system.
