**Verdict: Yes.** Termination follows from the rules alone by a standard lexicographic path ordering (LPO), using only the syntactic structure of finite first-order terms.

Choose a precedence extending

\[
\mathsf{times} \succ \mathsf{plus} \succ \mathsf{p} \succ \mathsf{s} \succ 0
\]

and give every nonconstant symbol lexicographic status. LPO is well-founded, closed under contexts and substitutions, and has the proper-subterm property.

Each rule strictly decreases:

1. \(\mathsf{plus}(x,0)\to x\)

   \(x\) is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.

2. \(\mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))\to
   \mathsf{s}(\mathsf{plus}(x,y))\)

   Lexicographically,
   \[
   \mathsf{plus}(x,\mathsf{s}(y))
   \succ \mathsf{plus}(x,y)
   \]
   because \(\mathsf{s}(y)\succ y\). Since \(\mathsf{plus}\succ\mathsf{s}\), the left-hand side therefore exceeds the entire right-hand side.

3. \(\mathsf{times}(0,y)\to 0\)

   \(0\) is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.

4. \(\mathsf{times}(x,0)\to 0\)

   Again, \(0\) is a proper subterm.

5. \(\mathsf{times}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)\to
   \mathsf{plus}(\mathsf{times}(x,y),y)\)

   Lexicographically,
   \[
   \mathsf{times}(\mathsf{s}(x),y)
   \succ \mathsf{times}(x,y)
   \]
   because \(\mathsf{s}(x)\succ x\). It also exceeds \(y\) by the subterm property. Since \(\mathsf{times}\succ\mathsf{plus}\), it exceeds the whole right-hand side. Duplication of \(y\) is harmless for LPO.

6. \(\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{s}(x)))\to
   \mathsf{s}(\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(x)))\)

   Since \(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{s}(x))\succ\mathsf{s}(x)\),
   \[
   \mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{s}(x)))
   \succ \mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(x)).
   \]
   Together with \(\mathsf{p}\succ\mathsf{s}\), this orients the full rule.

7. \(\mathsf{p}(\mathsf{s}(0))\to 0\)

   \(0\) is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded simplification ordering. Hence no infinite rewrite sequence exists: the TRS is terminating on finite first-order terms. No structural equations, semantic assumptions about natural numbers, extra guards, or additional rewrite rules are needed.
