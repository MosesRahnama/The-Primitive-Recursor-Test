**Verdict: yes.** The TRS is terminating on all finite first-order terms, including non-ground and non-constructor terms. No additional rewrite rules, guards, or assumptions about terms being natural-number encodings are needed.

Use the dependency-pair method together with a weakly monotone interpretation.

### 1. A weak interpretation of every rewrite rule

Interpret terms over \(\mathbb N\) by

\[
\begin{aligned}
[0]&=0,\\
[s(a)]&=a+1,\\
[p(a)]&=\max(a-1,0),\\
[\operatorname{plus}(a,b)]&=a+b,\\
[\operatorname{times}(a,b)]&=ab,\\
[\operatorname{fac}(a)]&=a!.
\end{aligned}
\]

All these operations are weakly monotone. Every rewrite rule is non-increasing—in fact, each side has the same value:

\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
[\operatorname{plus}(x,0)] &=& x,\\
[\operatorname{plus}(x,s(y))] &=& x+y+1
 = [s(\operatorname{plus}(x,y))],\\[1mm]
[\operatorname{times}(0,y)] &=& 0,\\
[\operatorname{times}(x,0)] &=& 0,\\
[\operatorname{times}(s(x),y)] &=& (x+1)y
 = xy+y
 = [\operatorname{plus}(\operatorname{times}(x,y),y)],\\[1mm]
[p(s(s(x)))] &=& x+1
 = [s(p(s(x)))],\\
[p(s(0))] &=& 0,\\[1mm]
[\operatorname{fac}(s(x))] &=& (x+1)!\\
&=&x!(x+1)\\
&=&[\operatorname{times}(\operatorname{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x))].
\end{array}
\]

Thus arbitrary intervening rewrite steps cannot increase this interpretation. This calculation covers every presented rule.

### 2. Dependency pairs

The recursive dependency pairs are:

\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{plus}^{\#}(x,s(y))
  &\to \operatorname{plus}^{\#}(x,y),\\
\operatorname{times}^{\#}(s(x),y)
  &\to \operatorname{times}^{\#}(x,y),\\
\operatorname{times}^{\#}(s(x),y)
  &\to \operatorname{plus}^{\#}(\operatorname{times}(x,y),y),\\
p^{\#}(s(s(x)))
  &\to p^{\#}(s(x)),\\
\operatorname{fac}^{\#}(s(x))
  &\to \operatorname{times}^{\#}(\operatorname{fac}(p(s(x))),s(x)),\\
\operatorname{fac}^{\#}(s(x))
  &\to \operatorname{fac}^{\#}(p(s(x))),\\
\operatorname{fac}^{\#}(s(x))
  &\to p^{\#}(s(x)).
\end{aligned}
\]

Their dependency graph has four recursive strongly connected components:

- `plus#` by itself,
- `times#` by itself,
- `p#` by itself,
- `fac#` by itself.

The other edges go only downward:

\[
\operatorname{fac}^{\#}\to\operatorname{times}^{\#},p^{\#},
\qquad
\operatorname{times}^{\#}\to\operatorname{plus}^{\#},
\]

with no path back.

Each recursive component strictly decreases a natural-number measure:

- For `plus#`, measure the second argument:
  \[
  [s(y)]=y+1>y.
  \]

- For `times#`, measure the first argument:
  \[
  [s(x)]=x+1>x.
  \]

- For `p#`, measure its argument:
  \[
  [s(s(x))]=x+2>x+1=[s(x)].
  \]

- For `fac#`, measure its argument:
  \[
  [s(x)]=x+1>x=[p(s(x))].
  \]

Intervening ordinary rewrites are non-increasing under the interpretation established above. Hence an infinite dependency chain within any component would produce an infinite strictly descending sequence of natural numbers, which is impossible. Since the component graph has no cycles between distinct components, an infinite dependency chain would eventually have to remain in one component; none permits one.

Therefore the dependency-pair criterion establishes termination of the entire rewrite system from the given rules alone.
