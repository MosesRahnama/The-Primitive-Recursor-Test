I selected the **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)** for three key reasons:

### 1. It is Purely Syntactic (No External Mathematical Domains)
The prompt strictly prohibited importing external axioms, object-level assumptions, or structural properties. 
* If I had chosen **Polynomial Interpretations** (a common alternative), I would have had to map the terms to an external algebraic structure, such as the natural numbers ($\mathbb{N}$) or real numbers ($\mathbb{R}$), and rely on the well-foundedness and monotonicity of standard arithmetic over those domains.
* **LPO**, by contrast, is a **purely syntactic framework**. It is defined entirely as an inductive relation over the algebra of terms themselves. It requires nothing more than a strict partial order (precedence) on the finite set of your system's function symbols ($\text{times} \succ \text{plus} \succ s \succ 0$). This keeps the proof entirely self-contained within the syntax of your rules.

### 2. It Perfectly Mirrors the Hierarchical Structure of the Rules
Your rewriting system defines arithmetic operations via primitive recursion, where higher-level operations are systematically defined in terms of lower-level ones:
* `times` is defined using `plus`.
* `plus` is defined using the successor `s`.
* `s` operates down to the base case `0`.

LPO is designed specifically to capture this kind of hierarchical, recursive definition. By setting the precedence to match this hierarchy ($\text{times} \succ \text{plus} \succ s \succ 0$), the termination of the nested recursive steps (such as `times` unfolding into `plus`, and `plus` unfolding into `s`) is resolved automatically by the path ordering rules.

### 3. Minimal Complexity (Occam's Razor)
Other syntactic methods, such as the **Dependency Pairs (DP) framework**, are highly powerful but introduce a massive amount of overhead (generating dependency pairs, constructing DP graphs, finding usable rules, and applying reduction pairs). 

Because your system contains no mutually recursive loops or complex nested patterns that defeat a standard simplification order, LPO acts as a "one-shot" proof tool. It is the simplest, most direct, and most mathematically elegant tool capable of proving your specific system's termination without any unnecessary machinery.
