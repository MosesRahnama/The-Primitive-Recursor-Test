Yes. Termination can be established from these rewrite rules alone; no associativity, commutativity, arithmetic semantics, or assumption that terms denote natural numbers is needed.

The key point is that every recursive-looking rule makes syntactic progress:

- `plus` consumes one outer `s` from its second argument.
- `times` consumes one outer `s` from its first argument; the resulting `plus` computation is itself terminating.
- `p` moves through a finite `s`-prefix toward its argument. If its argument is not of the required shape, it simply becomes stuck rather than looping.
- `fac(s(x))` recurses on `p(s(x))`. When that argument can reduce, the `p` rules expose a predecessor-like smaller successor structure; when it cannot, the recursive `fac` call is blocked/stuck. It does not recreate an unbounded fresh `fac(s(...))` chain.

The duplication of `y` in the multiplication rule can enlarge terms, but it does not create a rewrite cycle or an endlessly renewable recursive argument. A formal proof can be given entirely syntactically, for example with a suitable dependency-pair/lexicographic structural measure argument.

Missing cases such as `fac(0)` or `p(0)` do not threaten termination: they only leave additional normal forms.
