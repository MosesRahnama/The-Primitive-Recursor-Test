Yes—the proposed method, if carried out properly, complies.

Dependency pairs, argument filtering, recursive path orderings, polynomial/semantic interpretations, and related termination techniques are meta-level proof methods for the rewrite relation generated by the listed rules. They do not add object-level equations such as associativity/commutativity, do not assume that `0` and `s` mean natural numbers, and do not add rewrite rules, guards, or typing restrictions.

In particular, such a proof would reason about arbitrary first-order terms, including terms not built solely from `0` and `s`. Terms for which no left-hand side matches are simply irreducible; they need not be interpreted as malformed natural numbers or excluded by a hidden domain assumption.

My earlier informal language about a “predecessor-like” effect was potentially misleading, because it sounds semantic. That was only an intuition for the syntactic behavior of the `p` rules, not an assumption that `p` is a predecessor operation in a model of arithmetic. A compliant proof would need to state the relevant syntactic decrease or show the absence of infinite dependency-pair chains explicitly.
