**In intended method, yes:** it used only the given syntax, finite first-order terms, and the displayed rewrite rules. It did not add equations such as arithmetic predecessor laws, did not assume that `p` is a semantic predecessor on natural numbers, and did not add guards such as “`x` is a numeral.”

However, my presentation was somewhat too informal at one point. In particular, saying that a “successor spine ends” can sound as though `x` were assumed to be a numeral. That is **not** an allowed assumption here: `x` may be instantiated by any term, including reducible non-numeral terms.

The argument needs to be read, or better stated, purely syntactically:

- `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))` is the only rule that can expose an outer `s` from an active term headed by `p`.
- Each such step moves from the argument `s(s(x))` to its proper subterm `s(x)`.
- If the remaining argument is not syntactically headed by `s`, no root `p` rule applies.
- If reductions inside that argument later make it headed by `s`, those reductions must themselves be finite. This is handled by induction on nested `fac` occurrences together with termination of the `plus`/`times`/`p` subsystem.

That is a syntactic induction, not an appeal to the intended arithmetic meaning of the symbols.

So the method did comply with the stated boundary, but the earlier phrasing should have made the arbitrary-term case explicit. It should not be interpreted as relying on the unstated assumption that every argument of `fac` or `p` is a natural-number numeral of the form `s(...s(0)...)`.
