**Termination argument (using the recursive path ordering, RPO)**

We consider the signature \(\{\text{F}/3,\; \text{G}/2,\; \text{S}/1,\; \text{Z}/0\}\). Variables are treated as minimal elements. Choose a well-founded precedence \(>\) on function symbols:  
\(\text{F} > \text{G}\)   (and we can leave \(\text{S}, \text{Z}\) unrelated to the others, or put them below \(\text{F}\)).  
For the multiset/lexicographic status we use the standard lexicographic extension.

Now check each rule:

1. **Rule:** \(\text{F}(x, y, \text{Z}) \to x\)  
   The left-hand side is \(\text{F}(x,y,\text{Z})\); its right-hand side \(x\) is a proper subterm. In RPO a term is strictly greater than any of its proper subterms, so \(\text{F}(x,y,\text{Z}) >_{\text{rpo}} x\) holds trivially.

2. **Rule:** \(\text{F}(x, y, \text{S}(n)) \to \text{G}(y, \text{F}(x, y, n))\)  
   The top symbol on the left is \(\text{F}\), on the right it is \(\text{G}\). Since \(\text{F} > \text{G}\) in the precedence, the left side is greater than the right side if the left side is greater than every argument of \(\text{G}\) on the right. The arguments are:
   - \(y\): a proper subterm of the left side, so left side \(> y\).
   - \(\text{F}(x, y, n)\): the top symbols are equal (\(\text{F} = \text{F}\)). We compare the arguments lexicographically. The first two arguments are identical (\(x\) and \(y\)). The third argument on the left is \(\text{S}(n)\), on the right it is \(n\). Since \(\text{S}(n)\) has \(n\) as a proper subterm, we have \(\text{S}(n) >_{\text{rpo}} n\). Therefore \(\text{F}(x, y, \text{S}(n)) >_{\text{rpo}} \text{F}(x, y, n)\).

   Both conditions are satisfied, so the left side is strictly greater than the whole right side.

Since every rewrite step strictly decreases the term in a well-founded ordering (RPO), there can be no infinite rewrite sequence. The system is terminating.
