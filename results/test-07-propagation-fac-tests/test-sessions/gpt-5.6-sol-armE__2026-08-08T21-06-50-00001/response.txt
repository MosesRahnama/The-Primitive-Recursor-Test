**Verdict: Yes.** The system is terminating on all first-order terms, not merely on terms where the third argument has a particular “natural-number” shape.

Use a polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
[Z] &= 0,\\
[S(t)] &= [t]+1,\\
[G(a,b)] &= [a]+[b],\\
[F(a,b,c)] &= [a]+([c]+1)([b]+1).
\end{aligned}
\]

Every interpretation is strictly monotone in each argument, so replacing a subterm by one with smaller interpretation strictly decreases the interpretation of the entire surrounding term.

For the first rule:

\[
[F(x,y,Z)]
= [x]+(0+1)([y]+1)
= [x]+[y]+1
> [x].
\]

Thus

\[
F(x,y,Z) \to x
\]

strictly decreases the interpretation.

For the second rule, writing \(X=[x]\), \(Y=[y]\), and \(N=[n]\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
[F(x,y,S(n))]
 &= X+(N+2)(Y+1),\\
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
 &=Y+\bigl(X+(N+1)(Y+1)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

Their difference is

\[
X+(N+2)(Y+1)
-\left(Y+X+(N+1)(Y+1)\right)=1.
\]

Hence

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))
\]

also strictly decreases the interpretation.

Therefore every rewrite step, including one inside an arbitrary context and under an arbitrary substitution, strictly decreases a natural number. Since \(\mathbb N\) admits no infinite strictly descending sequence, the rewrite system is terminating. No structural axioms, typing assumptions, guards, or additional rewrite rules are needed.
