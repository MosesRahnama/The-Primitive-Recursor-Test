I chose the **Multiset Path Ordering (MPO)** because it is the most natural, elegant, and purely syntactic tool for the specific structural features of this rewrite system. 

Here is a breakdown of the specific reasons why MPO was the ideal choice over other standard methods (like Polynomial Interpretations or Lexicographic Path Ordering):

### 1. Handling the Symmetric Rules of `plus` (The "Multiset" Advantage)
The system contains symmetric rules for addition:
*   `plus(x, s(y)) -> s(plus(x, y))` (Reduces the *second* argument)
*   `plus(s(x), y) -> s(plus(x, y))` (Reduces the *first* argument)

MPO compares the arguments of a function as an unordered "bag" or multiset. When we compare `plus(x, s(y))` to `plus(x, y)`, MPO simply looks at the multisets `{x, s(y)}` and `{x, y}`. By canceling out the common `x`, we are left comparing `s(y)` to `y`, which is trivially true by the subterm property. The exact same trivial cancellation works for `{s(x), y}` and `{x, y}`. 

If I had used a Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO), I would have had to formally assign and track a strict directional status (left-to-right or right-to-left) to the arguments of `plus`. Because MPO is permutation-independent, it handles functions that recurse symmetrically on different arguments with zero extra friction.

### 2. Perfect Alignment with the System's Hierarchy
The rewrite rules exhibit a strict, primitive recursive hierarchy:
*   `times` is defined using `plus`
*   `plus` is defined using `s`
*   `s` wraps around variables or `0`

Path orderings (like MPO) were explicitly designed to exploit this exact kind of structural hierarchy. By assigning a precedence that perfectly matches this dependency chain (`times > plus > s > 0`), MPO allows us to essentially say: *"Replacing a higher-order operation with any number of lower-order operations guarantees termination."* This instantly takes care of the complex-looking rule `times(x, s(y)) -> plus(times(x, y), x)` without breaking a sweat.

### 3. Purely Syntactic (No "Magic Numbers")
An alternative way to prove termination would be **Polynomial Interpretations**, where we map every term to a mathematical polynomial over the natural numbers (e.g., mapping `plus(x,y)` to $2x + 2y + 1$). 

While polynomial interpretations would work here, they require:
1. "Guessing" the right coefficients.
2. Stepping outside the syntax of the system to do algebraic inequality proofs.

MPO avoids this entirely. It operates **strictly within the boundary of the presented rules and syntax**. It relies solely on the subterm property (the fact that `x` is smaller than `s(x)`) and the precedence of the symbols, perfectly satisfying your prompt's constraint to establish termination "without imported object-level axioms beyond the rules shown there." 

### Summary
I picked MPO because the system is a classic primitive recursive hierarchy with symmetric recursive branches. MPO evaluates exactly this class of systems algorithmically, syntactically, and reliably, making it the most robust and "textbook" choice for the proof.
