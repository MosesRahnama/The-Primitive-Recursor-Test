I chose the dependency pair method paired with a natural‑number algebraic interpretation because it directly captures the arithmetic semantics that are *implicit* in the rewrite rules, and it cleanly avoids the precedence conflicts a naïve recursive path ordering would create.

**Key obstacles a simpler method would face:**
- The rule `p(s(s(x))) → s(p(s(x)))` is not *structurally* decreasing (the argument of `p` decreases only after applying the definition of `p` itself).  
- The factorial rule `fac(s(x)) → times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))` calls `fac` on `p(s(x))`, which is only *semantically* smaller than `s(x)` once we know that `p` is a predecessor.  
- A lexicographic/recursive path order would force a single precedence on symbols, but `fac` must compare its argument `s(x)` with `p(s(x))`, and the `p` rule needs a conflicting precedence. The DP method separates these concerns by projecting each recursive call onto a monotone interpretation over ℕ, where `p` is interpreted as the true cut‑off predecessor, `s` as “plus 1”, and so on. All decreases become strict numeric inequalities (e.g. `x+1 > x`), which are trivially well‑founded. This approach needs no strengthening of the system, no conditional guards, and no external axioms—just the ordinary termination theorem for dependency pairs with a reduction pair.
