### Verdict
Yes, termination of the given TRS can be established from the rules alone (via a standard well-founded order on terms induced by the signature and rules, with no extra rewrite rules, guards, side-conditions, structural axioms, or object-level assumptions).

### Termination Argument
We use the lexicographic path order (LPO) induced by the precedence
\[
\texttt{times} \succ \texttt{plus} \succ \texttt{p} \succ \texttt{s} \succ \texttt{0}
\]
on the ranked signature \(\{\texttt{0}/0,\texttt{s}/1,\texttt{plus}/2,\texttt{times}/2,\texttt{p}/1\}\). (LPO is well-founded on ground terms whenever the precedence is well-founded, which it is here; the variable case is immediate from the definition of LPO. We recall the relevant clauses of the definition only as needed below: \(s\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}t\) holds if (among other cases) a proper subterm of \(s\) is \(\succeq_{\mathrm{lpo}}t\), or the roots satisfy \(f\succ g\) and \(s\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}\) every immediate argument of \(t\), or the roots are identical, a leftmost argument decreases, and \(s\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}\) every remaining argument of \(t\).)

It is enough to check that each rule is a strict LPO decrease. We discharge the seven rules in turn.

- \(\texttt{plus}(x,0)\to x\).  
  The right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left-hand side, so the claim is immediate.

- \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y))\to\texttt{s}(\texttt{plus}(x,y))\).  
  The precedence gives \(\texttt{plus}\succ\texttt{s}\), so it is enough to show the single inequality \(\texttt{plus}(x,\texttt{s}(y))\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}\texttt{plus}(x,y)\). The roots are identical and the first arguments are identical; the second arguments satisfy \(\texttt{s}(y)\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}y\) (subterm), and there are no further arguments. Hence the claim holds.

- \(\texttt{times}(0,y)\to 0\).  
  The precedence gives \(\texttt{times}\succ\texttt{0}\), and the left-hand side is therefore larger than the constant on the right-hand side.

- \(\texttt{times}(x,0)\to 0\).  
  Identical to the previous case.

- \(\texttt{times}(\texttt{s}(x),y)\to\texttt{plus}(\texttt{times}(x,y),y)\).  
  The precedence gives \(\texttt{times}\succ\texttt{plus}\), so it is enough to show the two inequalities \(\texttt{times}(\texttt{s}(x),y)\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}\texttt{times}(x,y)\) and \(\texttt{times}(\texttt{s}(x),y)\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}y\). The second is immediate (the right-hand side is an argument of the left-hand side). For the first the roots are identical; the first arguments satisfy \(\texttt{s}(x)\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}x\) (subterm) while the left-hand side is already known to dominate the remaining argument \(y\). Hence the claim holds.

- \(\texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(\texttt{s}(x)))\to\texttt{s}(\texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(x)))\).  
  The precedence gives \(\texttt{p}\succ\texttt{s}\), so it is enough to show the single inequality \(\texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(\texttt{s}(x)))\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}\texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(x))\). The roots are identical and the arguments satisfy \(\texttt{s}(\texttt{s}(x))\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}\texttt{s}(x)\) (because \(\texttt{s}(\texttt{s}(x))\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}x\) by two applications of the subterm rule, or directly by the recursive clause for identical roots). Hence the claim holds.

- \(\texttt{p}(\texttt{s}(0))\to 0\).  
  The precedence gives \(\texttt{p}\succ\texttt{0}\), and the left-hand side is therefore larger than the constant on the right-hand side.

Every rule is therefore a strict decrease in a well-founded order on terms. Consequently every rewrite sequence is finite, i.e., the TRS terminates.

(Note that the argument never leaves the pure syntax of the signature and the seven rules; it does not rely on any interpretation of \(\texttt{s}\) as a constructor, any injectivity or acyclicity axiom, any induction principle, any typing discipline, or any auxiliary rewrite rules.)
