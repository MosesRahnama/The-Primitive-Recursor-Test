### Why LPO

I chose the lexicographic path order (LPO) for a combination of technical fit and methodological hygiene that matches the constraints you set.

- The TRS is a pure first-order orthogonal system whose rules are classic primitive-recursive schemes on a free constructor signature. LPO is purpose-built for exactly this shape: it turns a simple precedence on the function symbols into a well-founded order on terms that orients recursive calls by “peeling off” constructors and by ranking defined symbols above the symbols that appear in their right-hand sides. No semantic interpretation, no polynomial or matrix ranking functions, and no dependency-pair machinery are required.

- The method stays strictly inside the given syntax. Once the precedence
  ```
  times ≻ plus ≻ p ≻ s ≻ 0
  ```
  is fixed, every rule is checked by a purely syntactic comparison; nothing is added to the rewrite relation, no side conditions are imposed, and no equational theory or typing discipline is assumed. That satisfies the “rules alone” boundary you imposed.

- Manual verification is immediate and exhaustive. Each of the seven rules falls into one of the three elementary LPO clauses (subterm, precedence, or lexicographic descent on arguments). The checks are short, local, and cover every rule without gaps or hidden lemmas—precisely what the question asked for.

- Alternatives exist (recursive path orders with different statuses, Knuth–Bendix orders, polynomial interpretations over the naturals, \ldots) but they are either more cumbersome to discharge by hand or require choosing coefficients/interpretations that are not uniquely determined by the syntax. LPO needed only a total precedence, which is read off the call hierarchy in a few seconds, so it was the most economical standard method that still gives a complete, self-contained proof.

In short, LPO is the lightest well-founded order that is still strong enough for the system, that can be justified from the signature and the rules alone, and that lets every single rule be inspected directly.
