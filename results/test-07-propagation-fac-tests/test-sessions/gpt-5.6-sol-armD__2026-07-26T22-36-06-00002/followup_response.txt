I chose a **polynomial interpretation** because the rules have competing structural effects that make simpler syntactic measures awkward:

- Some rules remove constructors, such as  
  \(\mathrm{plus}(x,0)\to x\).
- Some move constructors inward without obviously reducing term size, such as  
  \(\mathrm{plus}(x,s(y))\to s(\mathrm{plus}(x,y))\).
- The multiplication rule duplicates \(y\):
  \[
  \mathrm{times}(s(x),y)\to\mathrm{plus}(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y),
  \]
  so ordinary symbol count or term size can increase.
- The \(p\)-rule
  \[
  p(s(s(x)))\to s(p(s(x)))
  \]
  needs \(p\) to weigh its argument strongly enough to compensate for the new outer \(s\).

A polynomial interpretation handles all of these in one compositional, context-compatible ordering. The coefficients were chosen to match the critical constraints:

- \(s(x)=x+1\) records one constructor.
- \(p(x)=2x\) makes removing one \(s\) from beneath \(p\) worth \(2\), while adding an outer \(s\) costs only \(1\).
- \(\mathrm{plus}(x,y)=x+2y+1\) gives the recursive second argument enough weight.
- \(\mathrm{times}(x,y)=(x+1)(2y+2)\) makes decreasing the first argument of `times` release enough weight to pay for the additional occurrence of \(y\) on the right. In fact, the multiplication rule decreases by exactly \(1\).

This method was also a good fit for the requested boundary: it uses only a semantic measure assigned to the existing syntax. It does not add rules, assume that terms denote natural-number arithmetic, or require any structural/object-level axioms. The natural numbers are used only as the well-founded codomain of the termination measure.
