**Verdict: Yes.** Termination follows from the rules alone via a polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\). No additional rewrite rules, guards, or object-level axioms are needed.

Interpret the symbols as:

\[
\begin{aligned}
[0] &= 0,\\
[s](x) &= x+1,\\
[\mathrm{plus}](x,y) &= x+2y+1,\\
[\mathrm{times}](x,y) &= (x+1)(2y+2),\\
[p](x) &= 2x.
\end{aligned}
\]

These interpretations are strictly monotone in every argument. Each rule strictly decreases:

1. \(\mathrm{plus}(x,0)\to x\):
   \[
   x+1>x.
   \]

2. \(\mathrm{plus}(x,s(y))\to s(\mathrm{plus}(x,y))\):
   \[
   x+2(y+1)+1=x+2y+3>x+2y+2.
   \]

3. \(\mathrm{times}(0,y)\to0\):
   \[
   2y+2>0.
   \]

4. \(\mathrm{times}(x,0)\to0\):
   \[
   2x+2>0.
   \]

5. \(\mathrm{times}(s(x),y)\to\mathrm{plus}(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y)\):
   \[
   \begin{aligned}
   [\mathrm{times}(s(x),y)]
      &= (x+2)(2y+2),\\
   [\mathrm{plus}(\mathrm{times}(x,y),y)]
      &= (x+1)(2y+2)+2y+1.
   \end{aligned}
   \]
   Their difference is \(1\), so the left side is strictly larger.

6. \(p(s(s(x)))\to s(p(s(x)))\):
   \[
   2(x+2)=2x+4>2x+3.
   \]

7. \(p(s(0))\to0\):
   \[
   2>0.
   \]

Thus every rewrite step, including steps under arbitrary contexts and substitutions, strictly decreases a natural-number measure. Since \(\mathbb N\) admits no infinite strictly descending chain, the rewrite system is terminating on all terms.
