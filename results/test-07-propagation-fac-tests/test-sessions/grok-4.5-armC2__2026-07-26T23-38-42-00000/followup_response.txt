The method follows directly from the question’s constraints: we are given a pure first-order TRS and asked whether termination is already a consequence of those eight rules alone.

- First check the easy rules (plus, times, p). Each is oriented by any standard simplification order (LPO/RPO/KBO) or by a simple polynomial interpretation; no external facts are required.  
- The single remaining rule is the fac-equation. Its right-hand side is not a subterm of the left-hand side, so every purely syntactic order fails to decrease.  
- The only remaining technique that could still succeed is a semantic interpretation (or a dependency-pair argument that ultimately relies on one). Any such interpretation must prove the strict inequality  
  \[
  [[s(x)]] > [[p(s(x))]]
  \]
  for the chosen carrier. Establishing that inequality needs the constructor properties of 0/s and the well-foundedness of the naturals—precisely the structural axioms that are not among, and not derivable from, the given rewrite rules.

Thus the method is forced: exhaust the rule-internal techniques, observe that they stop short exactly at fac, and conclude that termination is not intrinsic to the presented system.
