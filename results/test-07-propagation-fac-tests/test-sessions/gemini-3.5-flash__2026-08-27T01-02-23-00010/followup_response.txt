I chose the **Dependency Pair (DP) Framework** combined with **semantic interpretations** because this specific term rewriting system contains features that make simpler, purely syntactic termination methods fail. 

Here is the detailed breakdown of why this method was selected:

### 1. Failure of Standard Syntactic Orders (like LPO or MPO)
Standard syntactic termination orderings, such as the **Lexicographic Path Order (LPO)** or **Multiset Path Order (MPO)**, cannot easily prove the termination of this system.

* **The Problem:** Look at the recursive step for the factorial function:
  $$\text{fac}(s(x)) \to \text{times}(\text{fac}(\mathbf{p(s(x))}), s(x))$$
* **Why LPO fails:** For LPO to orient this rule, the argument of the recursive call on the right-hand side, $p(s(x))$, must be strictly smaller than the argument on the left-hand side, $s(x)$. 
* Under any standard precedence, $p(s(x))$ is *not* syntactically smaller than $s(x)$ because of the defined symbol $p$ at the root. In fact, $p$ is defined elsewhere as a function, and syntactically, $p(s(x))$ is a larger, more complex term than $s(x)$. 

Thus, purely syntactic methods struggle because the recursion is **non-structural** (it relies on the semantic behavior of the predecessor function $p$).

---

### 2. The Modularity of the DP Framework
The DP framework is the modern standard for termination proofs because it allows us to **modularize** the problem. 

Instead of finding one global ordering that satisfies all 8 rules simultaneously, the DP framework:
1. **Isolates the loops:** It identifies the actual recursive loops (Strongly Connected Components, or SCCs) in the system.
2. **Deconstructs the system:** It allowed us to split the proof into 4 independent, much simpler sub-proofs (one for `plus`, one for `times`, one for `p`, and one for `fac`).
3. **Ignores irrelevant rules:** For instance, when proving that the `times` loop terminates, we do not need to worry about how `fac` or `p` behave.

---

### 3. Handling Semantics with Mathematical Interpretations
Once the system was split into SCCs, the only remaining challenge was SCC 4:
$$\text{FAC}(s(x)) \to \text{FAC}(p(s(x)))$$
To prove this strictly decreases, we must show that wrapping a term in $p$ decreases its "size." 

By choosing a **polynomial/algebraic interpretation** over the domain of non-negative reals ($\mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$) with the function $[p](X) = \max(0, X - 1)$, we perfectly captured the mathematical semantics of the predecessor function:
* The size of $s(x)$ is $x + 1$.
* The size of $p(s(x))$ is $\max(0, (x + 1) - 1) = x$.
* Since $x + 1 > x$, we mathematically prove that the argument of `FAC` strictly decreases on every iteration.

### Summary
The DP framework was chosen because it **bypasses the limitations of syntactic subterm comparison** by allowing us to use local, semantic interpretations to model the mathematical behavior of the auxiliary functions (like predecessor $p$), proving termination rigorously and cleanly.
