**Reasoning:**
Termination of a Term Rewriting System (TRS) is a syntactic property of the rules. To determine if the system terminates, we need to show that no infinite sequence of rewrites is possible for any finite starting term. 

1. **`plus` and `times`:** The rules for these functions are standard and strictly decrease the structural size of their first argument (stripping an `s`), guaranteeing they terminate.
2. **`p` (predecessor):** The rules for `p` are `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))` and `p(s(0)) -> 0`. If `p` is applied to a term with multiple `s` constructors, it shifts the `p` inward. Crucially, doing so consumes an `s` constructor from the inner term to produce an `s` on the outside. 
3. **`fac` (factorial):** The rule `fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))` makes a recursive call to `fac` with the argument `p(s(x))`. For this to cause an infinite loop, `p(s(x))` would have to repeatedly rewrite into a term headed by `s` (so it can match `fac(s(x))` again). 

However, because the rules for `p` consume an `s` from the inner structure of the term during each application, `p(s(x))` can only evaluate to a term headed by `s` as many times as there are `s` constructors initially present in the finite term `x`. 

Once those `s` constructors are exhausted, the argument will either evaluate to `0` (and stop, because there is no rewrite rule for `fac(0)`) or get stuck as an irreducible normal form like `p(s(y))` where `y` is a variable. Consequently, there are no infinite rewrite sequences.
