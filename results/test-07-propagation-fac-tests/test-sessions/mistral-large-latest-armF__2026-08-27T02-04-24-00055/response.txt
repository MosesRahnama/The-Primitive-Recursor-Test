### Termination Argument:

We will use a **lexicographic path order (LPO)** with a carefully chosen precedence on the function symbols to establish termination. LPO is a standard method for proving termination of first-order term rewriting systems, and it does not require any additional assumptions beyond the given rules.

#### Function Symbols and Precedence:

```
times > plus > s > 0
```

This means:

- `plus` is next,
- `s` is next,
- `0` is the lowest.

#### Status:
We assign **left-to-right status** to `plus` and `times` (i.e., these symbols are left-to-right lexicographic in their arguments). This is a common choice for arithmetic operations where the left argument is often "simpler" in some sense.

#### LPO Definition:
For two terms `s` and `t`, `s >_lpo t` if one of the following holds:
1. `s = f(s_1, ..., s_n)` and `t = f(t_1, ..., t_n)` and `(s_1, ..., s_n) >_lpo (t_1, ..., t_n)` lexicographically, or
2. `s = f(s_1, ..., s_n)` and `t` is a subterm of some `s_i`, or
3. `s = f(s_1, ..., s_n)` and `t = g(t_1, ..., t_m)` with `f > g` and `s >_lpo t_i` for all `i`, or
4. `s = f(s_1, ..., s_n)` and `t = g(t_1, ..., t_m)` with `f = g` and `f` has lexicographic status, and `(s_1, ..., s_n) >_lpo (t_1, ..., t_m)` lexicographically.
