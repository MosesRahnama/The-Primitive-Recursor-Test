I chose the **Dependency Pair (DP) Framework** combined with **Polynomial Interpretations** because this system contains a classic structural conflict that makes simpler methods fail. 

Here is the step-by-step reasoning for why this specific method is the most appropriate:

### 1. Simple Path Orderings (like LPO or RPO) Fail
In a standard Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO) or Recursive Path Ordering (RPO), we try to find a global precedence of function symbols (e.g., $f \succ g$) to prove that the left-hand side (LHS) of every rule is strictly greater than the right-hand side (RHS).

In this system, we run into an immediate conflict:
* To prove that `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))` terminates, we must have $p \succ s$ because the symbol $s$ is introduced at the root of the RHS.
* But in the factorial rule `fac(s(x)) -> times(fac(p(s(x))), s(x))`, we are passing $p(s(x))$ into $fac$. To show that the recursive argument is smaller, we essentially need $s(x) \succ p(s(x))$, which implies $s \succ p$.

Since we cannot have both $p \succ s$ and $s \succ p$, **simple simplification orderings cannot prove termination here.**

### 2. How the Dependency Pair Framework Resolves the Conflict
The Dependency Pair framework (pioneered by Arts and Giesl) is the standard state-of-the-art technique for handling such systems. It works by:
1. **Decoupling the recursive calls from the structure of the rules.** Instead of proving that the entire RHS of a rule is smaller than the LHS, we only prove that the *arguments of the recursive calls* decrease.
2. **Weak vs. Strict Constraints:** It allows us to split the rewrite rules into two categories:
   * The **Rules** (like `p(s(s(x))) -> s(p(s(x)))`) only need to be *weakly decreasing* ($\ge$). This means we don't need $p \succ s$; we just need to ensure that applying `p` does not make a term grow infinitely.
   * The **Dependency Pairs** (which represent the actual loops, like $\text{FAC}(s(x)) \to \text{FAC}(p(s(x)))$) must be *strictly decreasing* ($>$).

### 3. Polynomial Interpretations are Ideal for "Predecessor" Semantics
Once the DP framework isolates the loop $\text{FAC}(s(x)) \to \text{FAC}(p(s(x)))$, we need a way to show that the term $p(s(x))$ is strictly smaller than $s(x)$. 

A polynomial interpretation is perfect here because it allows us to map the symbolic terms to algebraic expressions over natural numbers:
* We can map $s(x)$ to $x + 1$ (or $x + 2$).
* We can map $p(x)$ to $x - 1$ (for $x \ge 1$).
* Consequently, the valuation of $p(s(x))$ becomes $(x + 1) - 1 = x$.
* Since $x < x + 1$, we get a strict mathematical decrease ($[s(x)] > [p(s(x))]$) that perfectly models the behavior of the predecessor function.

### Summary
I picked this method because it is the **most robust, standard mathematical tool** capable of resolving the mutual dependency between the successor $s$ and predecessor $p$ functions, allowing us to prove termination purely from the syntactic rules without relying on human intuition about what "factorial" or "predecessor" mean.
