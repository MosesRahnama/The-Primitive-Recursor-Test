**Yes.** The LPO proof complies with those requirements.

The precedence

\[
\mathsf{times}\succ\mathsf{plus}\succ\mathsf p\succ\mathsf s\succ 0
\]

is not an added rewrite rule or an object-level assumption about what these symbols mean. It is a **metatheoretic ordering chosen for the termination proof**. It does not alter which terms rewrite or under what conditions.

The proof uses only:

- the finite syntactic structure of ordinary first-order terms;
- the left- and right-hand sides of the given rules;
- the standard theorem that LPO is well-founded and stable under substitutions and contexts;
- direct verification that each given rule decreases under that ordering.

It does **not** assume that:

- `0` and `s` denote natural numbers;
- `plus` or `times` have their usual arithmetic meanings;
- constructors are injective or disjoint;
- terms satisfy induction or any external equations;
- rewriting has additional guards, strategies, or side conditions.

The lexicographic statuses and symbol precedence are parameters of the proof ordering, not modifications to the TRS. Likewise, the ordering conditions checked while orienting a rule are not guards imposed on applications of that rule: every substitution instance in every context decreases automatically because LPO is substitution- and context-stable.

The only background convention is the standard one already inherent in “first-order term rewriting system”: terms are finite first-order terms. Under that standard meaning, the argument remains entirely within the requested boundary.
