Yes. Termination can be established from the rewrite rules themselves; no external arithmetic, induction principle, or structural equations are needed.

Informally:

- `plus` recursively reduces the second argument from `s(y)` to `y`.
- `times` either immediately reaches `0` or recursively reduces its first `s(x)` argument to `x`; the resulting `plus` call is itself terminating.
- `p` moves from `p(s(s(x)))` to `p(s(x))` under one fewer leading `s` inside the recursive `p` call, eventually reaching `p(s(0))` or becoming irreducible.
- `fac` does introduce a recursive `fac` occurrence, but its argument is `p(s(x))`. That cannot directly match the `fac(s(_))` left-hand side without reductions of `p`; those `p` reductions consume the available leading `s` structure. If the argument is not suitably `s`-structured, the recursive `fac` simply becomes stuck rather than generating an infinite sequence.

Thus there is no rewrite rule or combination of rules that restores a previously larger recursive configuration indefinitely. The system is terminating on all finite first-order terms under ordinary unrestricted rewriting.
