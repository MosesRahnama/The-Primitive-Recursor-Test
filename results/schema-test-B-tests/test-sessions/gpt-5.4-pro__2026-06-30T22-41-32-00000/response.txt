Here is the classification of the five methods, **as stated**.

| Method | Proves termination? | Stays within benchmark boundary? | Why |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **(A) LPO with precedence F > G > S > Z** | **Yes** | **Yes** | `F(x,y,Z) -> x` is oriented by the subterm property. For `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y,F(x,y,n))`, the root symbol drops from `F` to `G`, and the recursive call is smaller since `S(n) > n`. Purely syntactic. |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation** `F=x+n+1, S=n+1, G=b, Z=0` | **No** | **Yes** | At the rule roots, both rules decrease numerically. But as a termination proof for the full TRS, it fails because the interpretation is **not monotone** in all arguments: `G(a,b)=b` ignores its first argument. So a step inside the first argument of `G` is invisible, e.g. `G(F(u,v,Z),c) -> G(u,c)` has the same interpretation on both sides. |
| **(C) KBO with uniform weights 1** | **No** | **Yes** | The second rule cannot be oriented by KBO. The RHS duplicates `y` (`lhs` has one `y`, `rhs` has two), violating the KBO variable condition. Also with unit weights the RHS is heavier anyway. So this fails regardless of precedence. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion, project 3rd arg of F** | **Yes** | **Yes** | The only dependency pair is `F#(x,y,S(n)) -> F#(x,y,n)`. Projecting to the 3rd argument gives `S(n) ▹ n`, a strict subterm decrease. No extra assumptions are used. |
| **(E) Direct measure** `mu(F)=depth_S(n)+1, mu(G)=mu(b), mu(x)=0` | **No** | **Yes** | As stated, it is not a valid global descending measure. Two problems: (i) it is not stable under substitution, e.g. `F(F(a,b,S(Z)),u,Z) -> F(a,b,S(Z))` gives `1 -> 2`; (ii) it ignores rewrites in the first argument of `G`, e.g. `G(F(a,b,Z),c) -> G(a,c)` leaves `mu` unchanged. So it does not prove strong normalization. |

**(A)** and **(D)**.
