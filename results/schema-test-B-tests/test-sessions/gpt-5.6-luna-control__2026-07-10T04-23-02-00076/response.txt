| Method | Terminates? | Within boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **(A) LPO, \(F>G>S>Z\)** | **Yes** | **No** | The second rule is oriented because \(F(x,y,S(n))\) is greater than \(G(y,F(x,y,n))\): \(F>G\), and the recursive \(F\)-term is smaller by the lexicographic comparison of the third arguments \(S(n)>n\). However, the precedence is an imported global ordering choice not fixed by the rewrite rules. |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation** | **Yes** | **Yes** | For the first rule, \(x+n+1>x\). For the second, \([F(x,y,S(n))]=x+n+2\), while \([G(y,F(x,y,n))]=x+n+1\). Thus every rule strictly decreases over \(\mathbb N\). The use of the standard well-foundedness/soundness of \(\mathbb N\) is ordinary metatheoretic reasoning, not an extra object-level assumption. |
| **(C) KBO with all weights \(1\)** | **No** | **No** | The second rule cannot be oriented: \(y\) occurs once on the left but twice on the right, violating KBO’s variable-condition. The uniform weight assignment is also an imported global choice. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs, projection to argument 3** | **Yes** | **No** | The relevant dependency pair is essentially \(F^\#(x,y,S(n))\to F^\#(x,y,n)\), and projection to the third argument gives \(S(n)\succ n\). Thus the subterm criterion succeeds. But the chosen projection is an imported global structural choice not fixed by the rules themselves. |
| **(E) Direct measure \(\mu\)** | **No, as stated** | **Yes** | It decreases for root instances of the displayed rules, but it does not strictly decrease for every rewrite step in arbitrary contexts. For example, an inner rewrite can replace a non-\(S\)-headed third argument by a term beginning with many \(S\)'s, increasing the outer term’s stated measure. Hence the claimed global descent argument is invalid as stated. The intended third-argument descent is nevertheless directly motivated by the rules and introduces no arbitrary global structural choice. |

Therefore, **only method (B)** satisfies **both** conditions.
