Here is the evaluation of the five methods **as stated**.

| Method | Proves termination? | Stays within benchmark boundary? | Why |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **(A) LPO with precedence \(F > G > S > Z\)** | **Yes** | **Yes** | Rule 1: \(F(x,y,Z) > x\) by subterm. Rule 2: \(F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))\): since \(F>G\), it suffices to show the lhs is bigger than both rhs arguments; \(y\) is a subterm, and \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) because the third argument drops from \(S(n)\) to \(n\). Purely syntactic, no extra assumptions. |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\)** | **No** | **Yes** | At the rule level, both rules decrease numerically. But as stated this is **not** a valid global termination proof for the full TRS, because the interpretation ignores rewrite-active positions: \(G(a,b)=b\) ignores the first argument, and \(F(x,y,n)=x+n+1\) ignores the second. So it is not strictly monotone in all positions, hence not context-closed as a reduction order. Example: \(G(F(u,v,Z),w)\to G(u,w)\), but both sides have the same interpretation. No extra object-level assumptions are imported; it just fails as a proof. |
| **(C) KBO with uniform weights 1** | **No** | **Yes** | Rule 2 cannot be oriented by standard KBO. The variable \(y\) occurs once on the lhs and **twice** on the rhs, violating the KBO variable-occurrence condition. Also, with uniform symbol weights 1, the rhs is heavier anyway. This stays within the benchmark boundary; it is simply insufficient. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion, project 3rd arg of \(F\)** | **Yes** | **Yes** | The only recursive call gives the DP \(F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n)\). Projecting to the 3rd argument yields \(S(n)\) on the lhs and \(n\) on the rhs, and \(S(n)\) is a proper superterm of \(n\). So the SCC is chain-free by the subterm criterion. No extra structural/object assumptions are used. |
| **(E) Direct measure \(\mu\) on the 3rd argument** | **No** | **No** | As stated, the claim “each rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\)” is false. It is not substitution-stable and not context-monotone. Root-step counterexample: \(F(F(a,b,S(Z)),c,Z)\to F(a,b,S(Z))\), but \(\mu\) goes from \(1\) to \(2\). Context counterexample: rewriting inside the first argument of \(G\) leaves \(\mu\) unchanged because \(\mu(G(a,b))=\mu(b)\). To make this work, one would need extra assumptions such as restricting variables/positions/strategies, which goes beyond the benchmark boundary. |
