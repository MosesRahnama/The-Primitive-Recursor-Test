| Method | Proves termination? | Within benchmark boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **A. LPO** | **Yes** | **No** | For the recursive rule, \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} F(x,y,n)\) because \(S(n)>n\), and then \(F>G\) orients the entire right-hand side. The base rule follows from the subterm property. However, the externally chosen global precedence \(F>G>S>Z\) is not fixed by the rewrite rules. |
| **B. Polynomial interpretation** | **No** | **No** | Both rules decrease at the root, but the interpretation is not strictly monotone in every argument: \(G\) ignores its first argument and \(F\) ignores its second. Thus rewriting inside such an ignored argument need not decrease the interpretation, so this does not yield a context-closed reduction order. It also imports an arbitrary global interpretation over \(\mathbb N\). |
| **C. KBO** | **No** | **No** | In the recursive rule, \(y\) occurs once on the left but twice on the right, violating KBO’s variable condition. With positive variable weight, the right side is also heavier under the stated uniform symbol weights. The weight assignment is additionally an imported global structural choice. |
| **D. Dependency pairs** | **Yes** | **Yes** | The only dependency pair is \(F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n)\). Projecting to argument 3 gives \(S(n)\) versus \(n\), a strict proper-subterm decrease. This uses the recursive-call structure visible directly in the rule and ordinary well-foundedness of proper subterms, not an external object-level assumption. |
| **E. Direct \(\mu\)-measure** | **No** | **Yes, as stated** | The proposed measure is not strictly decreasing for arbitrary rewrite steps. For example, \(F(t,u,Z)\to t\) has left measure \(1\), while \(\mu(t)\) can be arbitrarily large. Rewriting inside an argument ignored by \(\mu\), such as the first argument of \(G\), also leaves the measure unchanged. As a syntactic measure attempt it is within the boundary; repairing it by assuming arguments are only \(S/Z\)-numerals would import an assumption not present in the system. |

Therefore, **only method D satisfies both conditions**.
