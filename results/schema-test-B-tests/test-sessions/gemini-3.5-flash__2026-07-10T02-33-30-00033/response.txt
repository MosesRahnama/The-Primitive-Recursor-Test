Here is the analysis of each proposed proof method:

---

### **(A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**

1. **Does the method succeed?**  
   **Yes.**  
   * For the first rule: $F(x, y, Z) \to_{LPO} x$ holds by the subterm property of LPO, since $x$ is a subterm of the left-hand side (LHS).
   * For the second rule: $F(x, y, S(n)) \to_{LPO} G(y, F(x, y, n))$ holds because the precedence is $F > G$, which requires showing:
     1. $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{LPO} y$ (holds by subterm property).
     2. $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{LPO} F(x, y, n)$ (since the root symbols are both $F$, we compare arguments lexicographically: $x$ is identical to $x$, $y$ is identical to $y$, and the third arguments satisfy $S(n) >_{LPO} n$ by the subterm property).
   Thus, LPO successfully proves termination.

2. **Does it stay within the original benchmark boundary?**  
   **Yes.** LPO is a standard, purely syntactic, self-contained termination method for term rewriting systems.

---

### **(B) Polynomial interpretation over $\mathbb{N}$**

1. **Does the method succeed?**  
   **No.**  
   A valid polynomial interpretation used to prove termination must be strictly monotonic in all arguments of every function symbol. Under the proposed interpretation, $[G(a, b)] = b$ is not strictly monotonic in its first argument $a$ (since its coefficient is $0$). Consequently, rewrite steps occurring inside the first argument of $G$ would not be guaranteed to strictly decrease the overall term's interpretation.

2. **Does it stay within the original benchmark boundary?**  
   **Yes.** Polynomial interpretations are standard termination methods, but this specific one fails.

---

### **(C) Knuth-Bendix Order (KBO) with uniform symbol weights**

1. **Does the method succeed?**  
   **No.**  
   KBO requires that for any rule $l \to r$, the number of occurrences of any variable $x$ in $l$ must be greater than or equal to its occurrences in $r$ ($|l|_x \ge |r|_x$).  
   * In the second rule, $F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n))$, the variable $y$ appears once on the LHS but twice on the RHS, violating this condition.
   * Additionally, with uniform weights $w = 1$ and variable weight $w_0 = 1$, the LHS weight is $5$, whereas the RHS weight is $6$, violating the KBO weight condition ($w(l) \ge w(r)$).

2. **Does it stay within the original benchmark boundary?**  
   **Yes.** KBO is a standard syntactic method, but it fails here.

---

### **(D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F**

1. **Does the method succeed?**  
   **Yes.**  
   * The only defined symbol in the system is $F$. Thus, the only dependency pair generated is:
     $$F^\sharp(x, y, S(n)) \to F^\sharp(x, y, n)$$
   * Applying the subterm criterion with projection $\pi(F^\sharp) = 3$ yields:
     $$\pi(F^\sharp(x, y, S(n))) = S(n) \quad \text{and} \quad \pi(F^\sharp(x, y, n)) = n$$
   * Since $S(n) \triangleright n$ (strict subterm), the dependency pair is strictly decreased and eliminated, leaving an empty set of dependency pairs, which proves termination.

2. **Does it stay within the original benchmark boundary?**  
   **Yes.** Dependency pairs with the subterm criterion is a purely syntactic, standard termination method.

---

### **(E) Direct descent measure on the third argument**

1. **Does the method succeed?**  
   **No.**  
   For a direct measure to prove termination, *every* valid rewrite step must strictly decrease the measure. However, if a rewrite step occurs inside the first argument of $F$ (for example, rewriting $F(F(x,y,S(z)), y, Z) \to F(G(y,F(x,y,z)), y, Z)$), the value of $\mu$ for both the LHS and the RHS remains exactly $depth_S(Z) + 1 = 1$. Because there is no strict decrease for this step, the method fails.

2. **Does it stay within the original benchmark boundary?**  
   **No.** It imports an external non-standard helper function ("$depth_S$") and semantical assumptions not derivable from the rules of the TRS alone.

### **Conclusion**

The methods that satisfy **both** conditions are:
* **(A)** Lexicographic Path Order (LPO)
* **(D)** Dependency pairs with subterm criterion
