**Analysis of each method:**

**(A)**  
1. Yes: LPO is a simplification order (hence well-founded). Both rules strictly decrease. For the first rule, the RHS is a proper subterm of the LHS, so it decreases. For the second, F > G implies the LHS is greater than both arguments of the RHS (by subterm property and recursion on arguments); at equal head F the third arguments compare as S(n) > n (subterm property) so the lexicographic comparison holds.  
2. Yes: LPO is a purely syntactic term order whose parameters (here, the given precedence on symbols already present in the TRS) are fixed by the rewrite rules. No external domain, no structural axioms about S/Z, and no object-level assumptions are used.

**(B)**  
1. Yes: Every ground instance of each rule strictly decreases under the interpretation (first rule: x+1 > x; second rule: x+(n+1)+1 > x+n+1). The carrier (N, >) is well-founded, so termination follows by the standard interpretation theorem.  
2. No: The method imports an external algebraic structure (polynomials over N with the given mapping that treats S/Z numerically) not fixed by the rules. This is an arbitrary global structural choice on an imported carrier.

**(C)**  
1. No: With uniform weights 1 (standard variable weight 1), the second rule increases weight (LHS weight 5, RHS weight 6). KBO therefore cannot orient the rule left-to-right regardless of precedence.  
2. Yes: KBO is a purely syntactic term order; uniform weights introduce no external domain or structural axioms beyond the TRS itself.

**(D)**  
1. Yes: The dependency pairs are F#(x,y,S(n)) → G#(y,F(x,y,n)) and F#(x,y,S(n)) → F#(x,y,n). Projecting every marked symbol to the third argument position of F (second argument position of G#) sends every DP to a strict subterm decrease (S(n) > n). The subterm relation is well-founded, so the subterm criterion eliminates all DPs and termination follows.  
2. Yes: Both dependency pairs and the subterm criterion operate directly on the given rules and their syntactic structure. The projection to the third argument is the unique position fixed by the recursive rule itself; no external domain, no structural axioms about S/Z, and no object-level assumptions are imported.

**(E)**  
1. No: The measure is only partially defined (undefined on terms headed by S or Z). Even if extended by setting depth_S(t)=0 for non-S/Z heads, rewrite steps need not decrease: e.g., the inner redex F(u,v,Z) → u with u = S(S(Z)) sends a term whose outer measure is 1 to a term whose outer measure is 3. The claimed "each rewrite step strictly decreases μ" therefore does not hold.  
2. No: Defining and reasoning about depth_S requires structural case analysis or induction on the third argument (or on term structure), importing structural axioms about constructors that are not derivable from the given rules alone.
