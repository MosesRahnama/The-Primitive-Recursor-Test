| Method | Terminates the TRS as stated? | Within the benchmark boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **(A) LPO** | **Yes** | **Yes** | With \(F>G>S>Z\), the first rule is oriented because a nonvariable term is greater than \(x\). For the second rule, \(F(x,y,S(n))\) is greater than \(G(y,F(x,y,n))\): \(F>G\), and \(F(x,y,S(n))>F(x,y,n)\) because \(S(n)>n\). |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation** | **No, as stated** | **Yes** | At the root, both rules have strict numerical decrease: \(x+1>x\) and \(x+n+2>x+n+1\). However, the interpretation is not strictly monotone in all arguments: \(F\) ignores \(y\), and \(G\) ignores its first argument. Thus a rewrite inside an ignored argument need not decrease the interpretation. For example, rewriting inside the first argument of \(G\) leaves \([G(a,b)]=b\) unchanged. Hence the displayed interpretation alone is not a global termination proof. |
| **(C) KBO** | **No** | **Yes** | The second rule violates KBO’s variable condition: \(y\) occurs once on the left but twice on the right. Also, with all weights \(1\), the right-hand side has greater total weight. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion** | **Yes** | **Yes** | The relevant dependency pair is essentially \(F^\#(x,y,S(n))\to F^\#(x,y,n)\). Projecting to argument \(3\) gives \(S(n)\to n\), a strict subterm decrease. There are no other recursive dependency pairs of concern. |
| **(E) Direct measure \(\mu\)** | **No, as stated** | **Yes** | The measure decreases for root applications of the two rules, but not for arbitrary rewrite steps in contexts. For example, a rewrite inside the first argument of \(G\) leaves \(\mu(G(a,b))=\mu(b)\) unchanged. Likewise, rewrites in ignored arguments of \(F\) can leave the measure unchanged. Therefore the claim that *each* rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\) is false for the full rewrite relation. |

Thus, restricting attention to the five methods exactly as given, the methods satisfying **both** conditions are:
