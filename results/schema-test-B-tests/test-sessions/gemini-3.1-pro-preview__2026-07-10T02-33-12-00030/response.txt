Here is the evaluation of each proposed proof method based on your criteria:

**Method (A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z.**
1. **Does it succeed?** Yes. Under LPO, Rule 1 strictly decreases because `x` is a proper subterm of the LHS. Rule 2 (`F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`) strictly decreases because `F > G` and `F(x, y, S(n))` is strictly greater than both arguments of `G`: 
   - It is greater than `y` (by subterm property).
   - It is greater than `F(x, y, n)` because both have the same root `F`, they are identical up to the second argument, and the third argument `S(n) > n` (by subterm). Furthermore, the LHS `F(x, y, S(n))` strictly bounds all arguments `x`, `y`, and `n` of the RHS `F`.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** Yes. LPO is a purely syntactic methodology directly derivable from the rewrite rules alone, relying on no external axioms.

**Method (B) Polynomial interpretation over N**
1. **Does it succeed?** No. For a standard polynomial interpretation over natural numbers to successfully prove termination, every interpretation function must be strictly monotonic in all its arguments. The proposed interpretations `[F(x,y,n)] = x + n + 1` and `[G(a,b)] = b` ignore arguments `y` and `a` respectively. Therefore, rewriting an expression within those ignored arguments (e.g., `F(x, F(x, y, Z), n) -> F(x, x, n)`) results in no decrease in the polynomial's weight. 
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** While polynomial interpretations are a standard technique, this specific application fails the fundamental requirements of the technique (strict monotonicity).

**Method (C) Knuth-Bendix Order (KBO) with uniform symbol weights**
1. **Does it succeed?** No. In KBO, the weight of the left-hand side must be strictly greater than or equal to the weight of the right-hand side for all variable instances. In Rule 2, the variable `y` appears once on the LHS but is duplicated on the RHS (once inside `G`, once inside `F`). Since standard KBO requires all variables to have a uniform weight $w_0 > 0$, the RHS will end up strictly heavier than the LHS by at least $w_0$.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** It attempts to use standard derived syntax boundaries, but it fails mathematically.

**Method (D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F.**
1. **Does it succeed?** Yes. The system has only one defined symbol (`F`), so Rule 1 creates no dependency pairs (DPs) and Rule 2 generates exactly one DP: `F#(x, y, S(n)) -> F#(x, y, n)`. Applying the projection to the third argument yields `S(n)` for the LHS and `n` for the RHS. Because `n` is a strict subterm of `S(n)` (`S(n) |> n`), the subterm criterion successfully breaks the only dependency cycle, proving termination.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** Yes. The dependency pair framework and the subterm criterion are standard, purely structural mechanisms extracted exclusively from the rules.

**Method (E) Direct descent measure on the third argument**
1. **Does it succeed?** No. A valid global termination measure must strictly decrease for *every* rewrite step, regardless of the context it is applied in.
