Here’s the assessment of each method against the two questions.

### TRS
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(x,y,Z) &\to x \\
F(x,y,S(n)) &\to G(y, F(x,y,n))
\end{aligned}
\]

This is the usual primitive recursion shape on the 3rd argument, so termination should be provable.

---

## (A) LPO with precedence \(F > G > S > Z\)

1. **Succeeds?** **Yes.**  
   - Rule 1: \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\): in LPO, a term is greater than its variables, so this orients.
   - Rule 2: \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\): with \(F > G\), root precedence gives LHS \(>\) RHS provided LHS is also \(>\) RHS arguments; that holds since \(y\) is a variable and \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) (because \(S(n) > n\) by subterm property).
   So both rules orient decreasingly.

2. **Within benchmark boundary?** **Yes.**  
   Choosing an LPO precedence is a standard termination proof choice, not an illicit imported object-level axiom.

---

## (B) Polynomial interpretation
\[
[F(x,y,n)] = x+n+1,\quad [S(n)]=n+1,\quad [G(a,b)] = b,\quad [Z]=0
\]

1. **Succeeds?** **Yes.**  
   - Rule 1: LHS \(= x+0+1=x+1\), RHS \(=x\), strictly decreases.
   - Rule 2: LHS \(=x+(n+1)+1=x+n+2\), RHS \(=[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=[F(x,y,n)]=x+n+1\), strictly decreases.

2. **Within boundary?** **Yes.**  
   This is a standard interpretation method over \(\mathbb N\), fully acceptable.

---

## (C) KBO with uniform weights \(w(F)=w(G)=w(S)=w(Z)=1\)

1. **Succeeds?** **No** (as stated).  
   For rule 2:
   - \(|F(x,y,S(n))| = 1(F)+1(S)+(\text{vars }x,y,n)=5\)
   - \(|G(y,F(x,y,n))| = 1(G)+1(F)+(\text{vars }x,y,n \text{ with }y\text{ twice})=6\)
   LHS has **smaller** total weight, so it cannot be \(>\) RHS in KBO with these weights.

2. **Within boundary?** **Yes.**  
   It’s a legitimate in-boundary method attempt; it just fails.

---

## (D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion, projection to 3rd arg of \(F\)

1. **Succeeds?** **Yes.**  
   The only recursive call in rule 2 gives DP:
   \[
   F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n)
   \]
   Projecting to the 3rd argument yields \(S(n) \to n\), strict by subterm relation, so DP problem is finite/terminating.

2. **Within boundary?** **Yes.**  
   Standard DP framework and projection choice are metatheoretic proof machinery, not forbidden extra assumptions.

---

## (E) Direct measure on 3rd argument (as defined)

1. **Succeeds?** **No, not as stated (“each rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\)”).**  
   With
   \[
   \mu(F(x,y,n))=\mathrm{depth}_S(n)+1,\quad \mu(G(a,b))=\mu(b),\quad \mu(\text{var})=0
   \]
   check rule 2 at top:
   - \(\mu(F(x,y,S(n)))=\mathrm{depth}_S(n)+2\)
   - \(\mu(G(y,F(x,y,n)))=\mu(F(x,y,n))=\mathrm{depth}_S(n)+1\)
   good for top step.
   
   But rewriting can occur in non-top contexts. Example:
   \[
   G(y, F(x,y,Z)) \to G(y,x)
   \]
   \(\mu\) before \(=\mu(F(x,y,Z))=1\), after \(=\mu(x)=0\): decreases (fine).
   
   However, for a term like \(H(F(x,y,Z),F(x,y,S(Z)))\) (with an outer symbol from context in full term algebra), \(\mu\) is not defined unless extended; and even if extended ad hoc, the claim “each rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\)” is not established by the given definition alone. As stated, it is not a complete well-founded reduction ordering proof for all terms/contexts.

2. **Within boundary?** **Yes** in spirit (direct measure argument from given rules), but **incomplete as stated**.
