**Method (A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
1. **Does it succeed?** Yes. Under LPO, `F(x, y, Z) > x` holds trivially by the subterm property. For `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`, since `F > G` in the precedence, we verify that the LHS is strictly greater than all arguments of the RHS. `F(x, y, S(n)) > y` holds by the subterm property. `F(x, y, S(n)) > F(x, y, n)` holds because they share the root symbol `F`, their first two arguments are identical (`x=x`, `y=y`), and the third argument strictly decreases (`S(n) > n` by subterm property), alongside the LHS being strictly greater than all RHS subterms.
