### Analysis of each method

**(A)**  
1. Yes, succeeds.  
   LPO orients both rules under the given precedence: the first rule holds by the subterm property; the second rule holds because \(F>G\) and the left-hand side is strictly larger (in LPO) than both arguments of the right-hand side, which in turn reduces to the lexicographic comparison of arguments of \(F\) (using that \(S(n)>_{\mathrm{lpo}}n\)).  
2. Yes, stays inside the boundary.  
   LPO is a purely syntactic reduction order whose only data are the given signature and a precedence; no structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond the two rewrite rules are used.

**(B)**  
1. Yes, succeeds.  
   The interpretation maps every ground instance of a left-hand side to a strictly larger natural number than the corresponding right-hand side:
   - \([F(x,y,Z)]=x+1>x=[x]\),
   - \([F(x,y,S(n))]=x+n+2>x+n+1=[G(y,F(x,y,n))]\).  
   All polynomials are weakly monotonic, so the interpretation proves termination.  
2. Yes, stays inside the boundary.  
   Polynomial interpretations over \(\mathbb{N}\) are a standard metatheoretic construction that depends only on the two given rules; the carrier \(\mathbb{N}\) and the arithmetic operations are ordinary well-founded reasoning, not an imported object-level axiom.

**(C)**  
1. No, does not succeed.  
   With every symbol weight equal to 1 the weight of the left-hand side of the second rule is 5 while the weight of the right-hand side is 6. KBO therefore cannot orient that rule (the weight condition already fails).  
2. (Vacuous) The method as stated lies inside the boundary, but this is irrelevant because it fails to prove termination.

**(D)**  
1. Yes, succeeds.  
   The only dependency pair is \(F^\sharp(x,y,S(n))\to F^\sharp(x,y,n)\). Projecting onto the third argument of \(F^\sharp\) yields the proper-subterm relation \(S(n)\triangleright n\). The subterm criterion therefore shows that the dependency-pair problem is terminating, hence the original TRS is terminating.  
2. Yes, stays inside the boundary.  
   Dependency pairs and the subterm criterion are purely syntactic and extract their data exclusively from the two given rules; no external structural assumptions are required.

**(E)**  
1. No, does not succeed.  
   The function \(\mu\) is not strictly decreased by every rewrite step. Counter-example: the term \(F(F(Z,Z,Z),Z,Z)\) has \(\mu=1\); contracting the inner redex produces \(F(Z,Z,Z)\) whose measure is still 1. (The same phenomenon occurs for any redex that lies in the first or second argument of an \(F\).) Consequently the claimed descent argument is false.  
2. (Vacuous) Even if a repaired measure were substituted, the concrete argument given in the method relies on an informal “depth of \(S\)-chains” that is not fixed by the rewrite rules alone; the method as stated is therefore already outside the narrow boundary. This is again irrelevant because the method fails to prove termination.
