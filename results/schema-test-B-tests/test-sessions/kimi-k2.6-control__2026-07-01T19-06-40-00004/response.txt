**Method (A) – LPO with precedence F > G > S > Z**
1. **Yes.** Both rules are oriented: the first because a variable is smaller than any proper term containing it; the second because F > G and the recursive call F(x,y,n) is smaller in the lexicographic extension (since S(n) is greater than the variable n).
2. **No.** LPO requires imposing a total/partial precedence on function symbols. That precedence is an *arbitrary imported global structural choice*; nothing in the two rules determines that F > G > S > Z.

**Method (B) – Polynomial interpretation over ℕ**
1. **No.** Although each rule satisfies \([l] > [r]\), the interpretation is **not strictly monotone** in the first argument of G (and the second argument of F). Because \([G(a,b)] = b\), rewriting inside the first argument of G need not decrease the measure; e.g.  
   \(G(F(x,y,Z),z) \to G(x,z)\)  
   has value \([z]\) on both sides. Hence the ordering is not a reduction ordering and does not prove termination.
2. **No.** It imports the natural numbers with addition and a hand-crafted polynomial shape, i.e. an external semantic structure not derivable from the rewrite rules alone.

**Method (C) – KBO with uniform weights**
1. **No.** The second rule violates the variable condition of KBO: the variable \(y\) occurs once on the left-hand side but twice on the right-hand side (\(G(y,F(x,y,n))\)). Therefore KBO cannot orient \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\) for any precedence.
2. **No.** KBO depends on global choices of symbol weights and/or a precedence, neither of which is fixed by the given TRS.

**Method (D) – Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting F to its third argument**
1. **Yes.** The only dependency pair is  
   \(F^\sharp(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\sharp(x,y,n)\).  
   Projecting \(F^\sharp\) to its third argument maps the left side to \(S(n)\) and the right side to \(n\). Since \(n\) is a proper subterm of \(S(n)\), the subterm criterion shows that this DP cannot participate in an infinite chain; termination of the whole system follows.
2. **Yes.** The projection is dictated by the recursion pattern visible in the rules, and the subterm relation is inherent to the term algebra. The argument uses ordinary DP metatheory without appealing to an arbitrary external ordering or semantic domain.

**Method (E) – Direct descent measure on the third argument**
1. **No.** The measure is not strictly decreasing for *every* rewrite step. Because \(\mu(G(a,b)) = \mu(b)\), a redex occurring in the first argument of \(G\) is ignored; for example  
   \(G(F(x,y,Z),z) \to G(x,z)\)  
   leaves \(\mu\) unchanged (both sides map to \(\mu(z)=0\)). Thus \(\mu\) does not establish strong normalization as claimed.
2. **Yes.** The measure is extracted directly from the shape of the given rules (descent on the third argument). It does not rely on an arbitrary precedence, weight function, or externally imposed semantic ordering; it is a rule-derived structural argument.

### Methods satisfying both conditions
Only **(D)** succeeds in proving termination **and** stays within the benchmark boundary.
