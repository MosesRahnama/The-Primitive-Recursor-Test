| Method | Proves termination as stated? | Within the stated benchmark boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| (A) LPO, \(F>G>S>Z\) | Yes | Yes | Both rules orient strictly. Rule 1 follows by the subterm property. For rule 2, \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) lexicographically because \(S(n)>n\), and then \(F>G\) gives \(F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))\). LPO is a context-closed well-founded reduction order. |
| (B) Polynomial interpretation | No | Yes | The displayed interpretation strictly orients the rules only at their roots, but its induced strict order is not closed under contexts because \(G\) ignores its first argument and \(F\) ignores its second. For example, \(G(F(a,b,Z),c)\to G(a,c)\) has the same interpretation on both sides: both evaluate to \([c]\). Thus it is not a strict descent proof for arbitrary rewrite steps. |
| (C) KBO with all symbol weights \(1\) | No | Yes | Rule 2 violates KBO’s variable condition: \(y\) occurs once on the left but twice on the right, in \(G(y,F(x,y,n))\). Hence the left-hand side cannot be greater than the right-hand side in KBO, independently of the displayed uniform weights. |
| (D) Dependency pairs plus subterm criterion on \(F\)'s third argument | Yes | Yes | The recursive dependency pair is \(F^\#(x,y,S(n))\to F^\#(x,y,n)\), whose third argument strictly decreases by the subterm relation. The pair to \(G^\#\) cannot be part of an infinite dependency-pair chain, since there is no rule with root \(G\), hence no dependency pair starting at \(G^\#\). Thus the only cyclic component is eliminated by the projection. |
| (E) Direct \(\mu\)-descent as stated | No | Yes, if read as a syntactic proposed measure rather than as assuming all terms are numerals | The claimed fact that “each rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\)” is false. Since \(\mu(G(a,b))=\mu(b)\), a rewrite in the first argument of \(G\) is ignored: \(G(F(a,b,Z),c)\to G(a,c)\) leaves \(\mu\) unchanged. Also, as written, the measure is not fully specified on arbitrary constructor terms/substitutions, and treating every third argument as an \(S^k(Z)\) numeral would add an unjustified restriction. |

The explicit LPO precedence, polynomial interpretation, KBO weights, and dependency-pair projection are proof certificates chosen for this finite presented system; they are not additional object-level axioms about the rewrite relation. Thus they remain within the stated boundary. The failures of (B), (C), and (E) are proof-theoretic failures, not boundary violations.
