**Analysis of each method**

| Method | 1. Does it prove termination? | 2. Does it stay inside the boundary? |
|--------|------------------------------|--------------------------------------|
| **(A) LPO with precedence F > G > S > Z** | **Yes.** Both rules are oriented: <br>• `F(x,y,Z) > x` by the subterm property. <br>• `F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))` because `F > G` and `F(x,y,S(n))` is larger than each argument of `G`; in particular `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)` lexicographically since `S(n) > n`. | **Yes.** LPO is a purely syntactic simplification order. Choosing a precedence is a standard metatheoretic parameter, not an imported object‑level structural axiom. |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation over N** | **Yes.** The interpretation is weakly monotone and strictly decreasing on both rules: <br>• `[F(x,y,Z)] = x+0+1 = x+1 > x = [x]`. <br>• `[F(x,y,S(n))] = x+(n+1)+1 = x+n+2 > x+n+1 = [G(y,F(x,y,n))]`. | **Yes.** Polynomial interpretations are standard metatheoretic semantic reasoning; they do not import object‑level assumptions about the term structure. |
| **(C) KBO with uniform symbol weights 1** | **No.** Standard KBO requires the variable‑occurrence condition `|lhs|_v ≥ |rhs|_v` for every variable `v`. In the second rule `F(x,y,S(n)) → G(y,F(x,y,n))`, the variable `y` occurs once on the left but twice on the right, violating this condition. Hence KBO cannot orient the system, regardless of precedence or variable weight. | **Yes.** KBO is a standard syntactic order. Its failure here is due to an intrinsic limitation of the chosen weights, not because it imports external axioms. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion (project F to 3rd arg)** | **Yes.** The only dependency pair is `F#(x,y,S(n)) → F#(x,y,n)`. Projecting `F` to its third argument yields the pair `S(n) → n` on the arguments. Since `n` is a proper subterm of `S(n)`, the subterm criterion applies and deletes this DP, proving termination. | **Yes.** The DP framework with the subterm criterion operates entirely on the syntactic structure of the given rules; no extra structural axioms are imported. |
| **(E) Direct descent on the third argument (`depth_S`)** | **No.** The proposed measure does **not** strictly decrease under all rewrite steps. For example, reducing an inner `F` inside the third argument of an outer `F` can increase the number of `S` symbols in that position (e.g. when the second argument of the inner `F` contains `S`). Thus the argument that “each rewrite step strictly decreases `mu`” is unsound. | **No.** The method imports the assumption that the third argument is structured as an `S`‑chain (i.e. a natural number). This is an object‑level structural axiom not derivable from the rewrite rules themselves, which allow arbitrary terms in that position. |

**Methods that satisfy both conditions:** **(A), (B), and (D).**
