| Method | Proves termination as stated? | Stays within benchmark boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **(A) LPO with precedence `F > G > S > Z`** | **Yes** | **Yes** | Rule 1 is oriented by the subterm property: `F(x,y,Z) > x`. Rule 2 is oriented because `F > G`, and `F(x,y,S(n))` is greater than both arguments of `G`; in particular `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)` since `S(n) > n`. This is a standard syntactic termination proof using only the TRS. |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation** | **No, not as a standard standalone polynomial-interpretation proof** | **No as a claimed full-termination proof** | The rules decrease at the root: `F(x,y,Z)` maps to `x+1 > x`, and `F(x,y,S(n))` maps to `x+n+2 > x+n+1`. However the interpretation is not strictly monotone in all argument positions: `G(a,b)=b` ignores `a`, and `F(x,y,n)=x+n+1` ignores `y`. Thus a rewrite inside an ignored argument need not decrease the interpreted value, e.g. inside the first argument of `G` or the second argument of `F`. So this does not yield a reduction order for full rewriting. Treating ignored positions as irrelevant would amount to imposing an extra replacement-map/strategy restriction not present in the benchmark. |
| **(C) KBO with uniform weights** | **No** | **Yes** | The second rule cannot be oriented by KBO. Its RHS duplicates `y`: LHS has one occurrence of `y`, RHS has two. KBO’s variable condition requires every variable to occur at least as often on the LHS as on the RHS, so the rule fails regardless of precedence details. The method itself is syntactic and within the TRS boundary, but it does not prove termination. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projection to third argument of `F`** | **Yes** | **Yes** | The only dependency pair is `F#(x,y,S(n)) -> F#(x,y,n)`. Projecting to the third argument gives `S(n) ▹ n`, a strict subterm decrease. The subterm criterion eliminates the only recursive dependency pair, proving absence of infinite dependency chains. This uses only the given rewrite rules. |
| **(E) Direct descent measure on the third argument** | **No** | **No as a claimed full-termination proof** | At root steps the proposed measure decreases, but not for arbitrary rewrite steps under contexts. For example, a reduction inside the first argument of `G` is ignored by `mu(G(a,b)) = mu(b)`, and a reduction inside the second argument of `F` is ignored by `mu(F(x,y,n)) = depth_S(n)+1`. Therefore the claim that every rewrite step strictly decreases `mu` is false for full rewriting. Making it work would require an additional restriction on where rewriting may occur or a different global measure, neither of which is part of the stated method. |

Therefore, the methods that satisfy both conditions are:

**(A) LPO** and **(D) dependency pairs with the subterm criterion.**
