**Summary of the five methods**

| Method | 1. Does it prove termination? | 2. Does it stay inside the benchmark boundary? |
|--------|-----------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| **A** – LPO (precedence F > G > S > Z) | **Yes** – both rules are oriented:  <br>• F(x,y,Z) > x (root F > variable)  <br>• F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n)) (root F > G) | **Yes** – LPO is a purely syntactic order; it needs no arithmetic or extra axioms beyond the given signature. |
| **B** – Polynomial interpretation (over ℕ) | **Yes** –  <br>• [F(x,y,Z)] = x+1 > x  <br>• [F(x,y,S(n))] = x+n+2 > [G(y,F(x,y,n))] = x+n+1 | **No** – the interpretation uses the standard model of the natural numbers (addition, constant 1) and relies on the well‑foundedness of ℕ, which are not derivable from the rewrite rules alone. |
| **C** – KBO with uniform weights (all = 1) | **No (as stated)** – the two sides of the second rule have equal weight (2) and no precedence is given, so the orientation cannot be guaranteed. (If a precedence such as F > G > S > Z were added, the method would succeed.) | – (irrelevant because the method does not succeed as presented). |
| **D** – Dependency‑pair + subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F | **Yes** – the only non‑trivial dependency pair is  F(x,y,S(n)) → F(x,y,n) ; the subterm criterion together with the projection onto the third argument gives a decreasing measure (the number of S‑constructors). | **Yes** – the argument uses only the term structure and the syntactic subterm relation; no external arithmetic or axioms are introduced. |
| **E** – Direct descent measure μ(F(x,y,n)) = depthₛ(n)+1, μ(G(a,b)) = μ(b), μ(var)=0 | **Yes** –  <br>• μ(F(x,y,Z)) = 1 > 0 = μ(x)  <br>• μ(F(x,y,S(n))) = depthₛ(n)+2 > depthₛ(n)+1 = μ(G(y,F(x,y,n))) | **Yes** – the measure is defined by counting S‑constructors in the term, which is a purely syntactic (term‑algebraic) notion; no extra axioms are needed. |

**Methods that satisfy both conditions (termination proved + boundary respected)**  

- **A** (Lexicographic Path Order)  
- **D** (Dependency‑pair / subterm criterion)  
- **E** (direct descent measure on the third argument)

Thus, **A, D, and E** are the only methods that both succeed in proving termination of the given system and remain within the original benchmark boundary.
