**Analysis of each method:**

**Method (A): LPO with precedence F > G > S > Z**  
- Succeeds: Both rules are oriented (rule 1 by variable subterm, rule 2 by precedence and lexicographic comparison).  
- Stays within the boundary: Only uses a syntactic ordering on the given symbols, no external structures.  
✅ Satisfies both.

**Method (B): Polynomial interpretation over N**  
- Succeeds: Both rules are strictly decreasing under the given interpretation.  
- Stays within the boundary? The interpretation imports natural numbers and addition, which are global structures not fixed by the rules. Under the narrow interpretation, this is an arbitrary imported structural choice.  
❌ Does not satisfy both (outside boundary).

**Method (C): KBO with uniform symbol weights**  
- Succeeds: With weights equal, rule 2 is oriented by choosing precedence F > G; rule 1 is oriented by weight.  
- Stays within the boundary: Only uses syntactic weights and a precedence on the given symbols.  
✅ Satisfies both.

**Method (D): Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to third argument of F**  
- The only dependency pair is F#(x,y,S(n)) → F#(x,y,n). The projection gives left-hand side S(n) and right-hand side n. The standard subterm criterion requires the projected left-hand side to be a *proper subterm* of the right-hand side; here S(n) is not a subterm of n, so the condition fails.  
❌ Does not succeed (and thus cannot satisfy both).

**Method (E): Direct descent measure mu**  
- Succeeds: mu(F(x,y,n)) = depth_S(n)+1, mu(G(a,b)) = mu(b), mu(variable)=0. Both rules strictly decrease mu.  
- Stays within the boundary: The measure is defined purely syntactically from the term structure (counting S symbols).  
✅ Satisfies both.

**Methods satisfying both conditions: A, C, E**
