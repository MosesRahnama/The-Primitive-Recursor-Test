**Analysis of each method**

| Method | 1. Succeeds in proving termination? | 2. Stays within the benchmark boundary? |
|--------|--------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **(A) LPO (F > G > S > Z)** | **Yes.** Rule 1 is oriented because the rhs is a variable occurring in the lhs. Rule 2 is oriented because *F* > *G* in the precedence, and the lhs is greater than both arguments of the rhs: *F(x,y,S(n))* > *y* (variable) and *F(x,y,S(n))* > *F(x,y,n)* by the lexicographic comparison on the third argument (*S(n)* > *n*). | **Yes.** LPO is a purely syntactic reduction order defined solely from the signature and a precedence; it imports no external theory about the symbols. |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation over ℕ** | **Yes.** The interpretation gives *[F(x,y,Z)]* = *x*+1 > *x* and *[F(x,y,S(n))]* = *x*+*n*+2 > *x*+*n*+1 = *[G(y,F(x,y,n))]*; all required monotonicity conditions hold. | **No.** It imports the arithmetic structure of the natural numbers (successor, zero, addition, and the >‑ordering) and assigns *S* and *Z* their standard Peano meaning. This brings in object‑level assumptions that are not derivable from the two rewrite rules alone. |
| **(C) KBO with uniform weights 1** | **No.** With uniform function weights (and variable weight 0) both sides of rule 2 have the same weight (2). The method does not supply a precedence, so *F* and *G* are incomparable; consequently rule 2 cannot be oriented. | **Yes.** KBO is a syntactic method and does not import extraneous axioms; it simply fails because it is under‑specified. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion (project *F* to arg 3)** | **Yes.** The only dependency pair is *F*^{#}(*x*,*y*,*S*(*n*)) → *F*^{#}(*x*,*y*,*n*). Projecting to the third argument maps the lhs to *S*(*n*) and the rhs to *n*. Since *S*(*n*) is a strict superterm of *n*, the subterm criterion shows there is no infinite chain, proving termination. | **Yes.** The DP framework and the subterm criterion are structural techniques that work directly on the given rewrite rules and the standard term algebra; no external object‑level axioms are needed. |
| **(E) Direct descent measure on the third argument** | **No.** The measure is incomplete (it is not defined for terms rooted by *S* or *Z*) and, more critically, it is **not** monotone with respect to rewriting inside contexts. For example, in the term *F(x,y,S(F(u,v,Z)))* the inner redex *F(u,v,Z)* rewrites to the variable *u*, but the depth of the outer third argument stays unchanged, so the overall measure does not decrease. | **No.** It imports an external numeric measure (*depth*_{S}) that treats the third argument as a well‑founded “S‑chain”, i.e. it assumes structural/numeric properties of *S* and *Z* that go beyond what the rewrite system itself provides. |

### Method(s) satisfying **both** conditions

- **(A) Lexicographic Path Order**
- **(D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion**

These are the only two methods that both establish strong normalization of the given system and do so using only the rewrite rules and purely syntactic/structural reasoning, without importing arithmetic axioms, Peano structure, or other object‑level assumptions not derivable from the presented rules.
