**Analysis of the rewrite system**

The system consists of two rules for the defined symbol `F`:
1. `F(x, y, Z) → x`
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))`

The symbol `F` is ternary, `G` is binary, `S` is unary and `Z` is a constant.  
Termination is driven by the third argument of `F`: each recursive call peels off one `S` (or hits the base case `Z`).  Any correct termination proof must account for the fact that a redex can occur **inside** any argument of any symbol (e.g. inside the first argument of `G` or the second argument of `F`).  A measure that ignores a position in which a redex can appear will fail to decrease when a rewrite happens there.

---

### (A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence `F > G > S > Z`

1. **Does it succeed?**  
   *Rule 1:* `F(x,y,Z) > x` because the right‑hand side is a variable occurring in the left‑hand side.  
   *Rule 2:* `F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))` because `F > G` in the precedence and  
   `F(x,y,S(n)) > y` (variable) and `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)` (lexicographically `S(n) > n` since `S > Z` and `S(n)` contains `n`).  
   LPO is a reduction order (closed under contexts and substitutions), therefore it proves termination.

2. **Within benchmark boundary?**  
   LPO uses only the syntactic precedence and the subterm/lexicographic relations derivable from the signature.  It imports no external axioms about the data‑type.

**Verdict:** satisfies both conditions.

---

### (B) Polynomial interpretation over **N**  
`[F(x,y,n)] = x + n + 1`, `[S(n)] = n+1`, `[G(a,b)] = b`, `[Z] = 0`

1. **Does it succeed?**  
   The interpretation is **not strictly monotone** in the second argument of `F` (the variable `y` is ignored) and in the first argument of `G`.  Consequently, a rewrite step inside `y` or inside the first argument of `G` does **not** decrease the measure.  
   Example:  
   - Let `y` be `F(u,v,S(w))`.  The term `F(x, F(u,v,S(w)), S(n))` has value `x + (n+1) + 1`.  
   - After rewriting the inner `F`, we get `F(x, G(v,F(u,v,w)), S(n))` with the **same** value `x + (n+1) + 1`.  
   Since the measure need not decrease, the interpretation fails to establish termination.

2. **Within benchmark boundary?**  
   Polynomial interpretations are a standard meta‑level technique; they do not add object‑level axioms to the TRS.

**Verdict:** fails condition 1 (succeeds in condition 2).

### (C) Knuth–Bendix Order (KBO) with uniform weights  
`w(F)=w(G)=w(S)=w(Z)=1` (variables have weight 0)

1. **Does it succeed?**  
   With uniform weights the weight of the left‑hand side of rule 2 is `2` (one `F` + one `S`) and the weight of the right‑hand side is also `2` (one `G` + one `F`).  KBO resolves equal weights by a precedence on function symbols.  **No precedence is supplied**, so the method cannot orient the second rule.  Even if a precedence were allowed, the statement as given is incomplete and insufficient.

2. **Within benchmark boundary?**  
   KBO is a purely syntactic order; it stays within the boundary.

**Verdict:** fails condition 1 (succeeds in condition 2).

### (D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of `F`

1. **Does it succeed?**  
   The only dependency pair is  
   `F♯(x,y,S(n)) → F♯(x,y,n)`.  
   Projecting both sides to the third argument yields `S(n)` on the left and `n` on the right.  Since `n` is a **strict subterm** of `S(n)`, the subterm criterion applies and proves that the DP‑problem is finite.  Finite DP‑problems imply termination of the original TRS.

2. **Within benchmark boundary?**  
   Dependency pairs and the subterm criterion are derived solely from the rewrite rules and the term structure; no external data‑type axioms are imported.

**Verdict:** satisfies both conditions.

### (E) Direct descent measure on the third argument  
`μ(F(x,y,n)) = depth_S(n)+1`, `μ(G(a,b)) = μ(b)`, `μ(x)=0` for variables

1. **Does it succeed?**  
   The measure ignores the first two arguments of `F` and the first argument of `G`.  Therefore rewriting inside those positions does **not** change `μ`.  
   Example:  
   - `G(F(x,y,S(n)), a)` rewrites to `G(G(y,F(x,y,n)), a)`.  
   - Both terms have measure `μ(a)`, so the step does not decrease the measure.  
   Because the measure is not monotone in all positions where a redex can occur, it cannot prove termination.

2. **Within benchmark boundary?**  
   The idea of a weight function is internal to the system; it does not import external axioms.

**Verdict:** fails condition 1 (succeeds in condition 2).

### Methods satisfying **both** conditions

Only **(A)** and **(D)** prove termination while remaining entirely within the benchmark boundary (no imported structural axioms or object‑level assumptions).

**Answer:** **A** and **D**.
