| Method | Proves termination as stated? | Stays within the stated benchmark boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| (A) LPO | Yes | Yes | Both rules orient under \(F > G > S > Z\). For the recursive rule, \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) by lexicographic comparison of the third arguments, since \(S(n) > n\); and \(F>G\) gives orientation over the outer \(G\). |
| (B) Polynomial interpretation | No | Yes | The two rules are strictly decreasing at their roots under the displayed interpretation, but the interpretation is not strictly monotone in all arguments: e.g. \([G(a,b)]=b\) ignores \(a\), and \([F(x,y,n)]\) ignores \(y\). Thus a rewrite inside an ignored argument need not decrease the interpretation. For example, \(G(F(u,v,Z),b)\to G(u,b)\) has the same interpreted value on both sides. Hence this interpretation, as stated, is not a context-closed strict descent proof of termination. |
| (C) KBO | No | Yes | The second rule violates KBO’s variable-occurrence condition: \(y\) occurs once on the left but twice on the right: \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\). Therefore KBO cannot orient this rule, independently of the uniform weights. |
| (D) Dependency pairs plus subterm criterion | Yes | Yes | The relevant dependency pair is \(F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n)\). Projecting to the third argument gives \(S(n) \triangleright n\), a strict subterm decrease. The first rule generates no recursive dependency pair. This is derived entirely from the given TRS. |
| (E) Direct \(\mu\)-descent | No | Yes, if read merely as a syntactic proposed measure; but it is not a valid proof | The claimed strict decrease fails for arbitrary substitutions and contexts. For example, instantiate \(x\) in \(F(x,y,Z)\to x\) by \(F(u,v,S(Z))\). Then the left side has proposed measure \(1\), while the right side has measure \(2\). Also, rewriting inside an ignored argument can leave \(\mu\) unchanged, e.g. \(G(F(u,v,Z),b)\to G(u,b)\) has measure \(\mu(b)\) on both sides. Treating every substituted variable as measure \(0\), or assuming all terms are Peano numerals, would not be justified by the rules alone. |
