**System**
```
F(x, y, Z)     -> x
F(x, y, S(n))  -> G(y, F(x, y, n))
```
The only defined symbol is `F`; `G`, `S`, `Z` are constructors.  
Termination holds because every call to `F` peels off one `S` from its third argument; the recursive call is wrapped in `G`, but `G` has no rules, so the depth of the third argument strictly decreases in every `F`‑reduction.

---

### Analysis of the five methods

| Method | Does it prove termination? | Does it stay within the benchmark boundary?* |
|--------|----------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| **(A) LPO with precedence F > G > S > Z** | **Yes.** <br>• Rule 1: `F(x,y,Z) > x` because the left‑hand side contains the variable `x`. <br>• Rule 2: `F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))` because `F > G` and the left side is greater than each argument of the right side (`F(x,y,S(n)) > y` and `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)` by the lexicographic comparison of the argument triples `(x,y,S(n)) > (x,y,n)`). | **Yes.** LPO uses only a precedence on the function symbols that appear in the system; it imports no extra axioms. |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation `[F(x,y,n)] = x+n+1`, `[G(a,b)] = b`, `[S(n)] = n+1`, `[Z] = 0`** | **No.** <br>The interpretation is not strictly monotone in the second argument of `F` (the coefficient of `y` is 0). Consequently a rewrite inside the second argument does **not** decrease the measure: for example, <br>`F(Z, F(Z,Z,S(Z)), S(Z))` has measure `2`. After the inner redex `F(Z,Z,S(Z))` rewrites to `G(Z,F(Z,Z,Z))`, the term becomes `F(Z, G(Z,F(Z,Z,Z)), S(Z))`, whose measure is still `2`. Because the measure does not strictly drop on every step, the interpretation fails to establish termination. | **Yes.** Polynomial interpretations are a standard syntactic technique; they do not add structural axioms about the function symbols. |
| **(C) KBO with uniform weights `w(F)=w(G)=w(S)=w(Z)=1`** | **No.** <br>Knuth–Bendix Order requires that every variable occur at least as often on the left as on the right of each rule. In rule 2 the variable `y` occurs once on the left but twice on the right (once as the first argument of `G` and once inside the recursive `F`). This violation of the variable condition makes KBO impossible regardless of precedence. | **Yes.** KBO is a purely syntactic order defined by weights and precedence; it stays inside the boundary. |
| **(D) Dependency Pairs + subterm criterion (project to the 3rd arg. of `F`)** | **Yes.** <br>The only dependency pair is `F(x,y,S(n)) → F(x,y,n)`. Projecting to the third argument yields the comparison `S(n) ▷ n`, where `▷` is the proper subterm relation. Since `n` is a proper subterm of `S(n)`, the subterm criterion shows that no infinite DP‑chain exists; hence the system is terminating. | **Yes.** Dependency pairs and the subterm criterion are standard techniques that rely only on the given rewrite rules and the generic subterm ordering. |
| **(E) Direct descent `μ(F(x,y,n)) = depth_S(n)+1`, `μ(G(a,b)) = μ(b)`, `μ(var)=0`** | **No.** <br>The measure looks only at the third argument of an `F`‑redex. Rewriting a subterm that sits in the *second* argument of `F` (or the first argument of `G`) leaves `μ` unchanged. For instance, rewriting the inner redex in `F(Z, F(Z,Z,S(Z)), S(Z))` does not decrease the outer `μ` (both have `depth_S(S(Z))+1 = 2`). Thus `μ` does not strictly decrease on every rewrite step, so it cannot be a global termination measure. | **Yes.** The method uses only structural information present in the rules; it does not import external axioms. |

\*The benchmark boundary requires that the proof use **only** the presented rewrite system, without adding structural axioms (associativity, commutativity, etc.) or object‑level assumptions (e.g. that `S` and `Z` form a standard natural‑number model). All five methods are “internal” in this sense; they differ only in whether they actually succeed.

---

### Methods that satisfy **both** conditions
Only **(A)** and **(D)** prove termination of the given system while remaining entirely within the original benchmark boundary.
