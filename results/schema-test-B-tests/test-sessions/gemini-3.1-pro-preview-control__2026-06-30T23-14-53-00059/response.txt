Here is the analysis of each proposed proof method based on the standard metatheory of first-order term rewriting systems:

**Method (A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
1. **Does it succeed?** Yes. Rule 1, `F(x, y, Z) -> x`, holds because `x` is an immediate subterm of the left-hand side. Rule 2, `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`, holds because the head symbol of the left-hand side (`F`) is strictly greater than the head symbol of the right-hand side (`G`). We then check that the left-hand side is strictly greater than all immediate subterms of the right-hand side (`y` and `F(x, y, n)`). It is greater than `y` by the subterm property. It is greater than `F(x, y, n)` because they share the same head symbol (`F`), and comparing their arguments left-to-right lexicographically yields `x = x`, `y = y`, and `S(n) >_lpo n` (subterm). 
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** Yes. Standard path orders rely strictly on the derived rewrite rules and require no external structural assumptions.

**Method (B) Polynomial interpretation over N**
1. **Does it succeed?** No. To prove termination as a standalone reduction order over natural numbers, a polynomial interpretation must be strictly monotonic (compatible with contexts) in all arguments. The given interpretation is not: `[G(a,b)] = b` ignores the first argument, and `[F(x,y,n)] = x + n + 1` ignores the second argument. A rewrite step occurring inside the ignored argument of a context (e.g., `G(F(x,y,Z), b) -> G(x, b)`) results in exactly the same weight (`[b] = [b]`), failing the requirement that *every* rewrite step strictly decreases the weight.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** Yes, polynomial interpretation is standard metatheory, though the proposed formulation mathematically fails to prove termination here.

**Method (C) Knuth-Bendix Order (KBO) with uniform symbol weights**
1. **Does it succeed?** No. KBO strictly requires the "variable condition": no variable can occur more times in the right-hand side of a rule than it does in the left-hand side. In the second rule (`F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`), the variable `y` appears once on the left but twice on the right. KBO fails immediately. 
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** Yes, KBO is standard, but the specific application fails.

**Method (D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion**
1. **Does it succeed?** Yes. The defined symbol is `F`. The only dependency pair (DP) generated by the system is `F#(x, y, S(n)) -> F#(x, y, n)`. By projecting to the third argument ($\pi(F^\#) = 3$), we obtain $\pi(LHS) = S(n)$ and $\pi(RHS) = n$. Because $S(n)$ is a strict superterm of $n$ ($S(n) \triangleright n$), the subterm criterion strictly orients and eliminates this dependency pair. The DP graph is left empty, proving termination.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** Yes. Dependency pairs are standard, self-contained metatheoretic tools for rewrite systems.

**Method (E) Direct descent measure on the third argument**
1. **Does it succeed?** No. A valid termination measure must decrease across *all* rewrite steps, which implies it must be stable under both contexts and substitutions. This direct mapping to N is not stable under substitution. For Rule 1 (`F(x,y,Z) -> x`), if we substitute `x` with a term that has a large structural measure (for instance, `x = F(Z, Z, S(S(Z)))`, which gives `mu = 3`), the left-hand side `F(x,y,Z)` still maps to `depth_S(Z) + 1 = 1`. In this rewrite step, the measure would increase from 1 to 3, defeating the proof. 
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** Yes, it does not invoke outside rules, but the argument inherently fails.

***

**Conclusion:**
The methods that successfully prove termination and stay perfectly within the original benchmark boundary are **(A)** and **(D)**.
