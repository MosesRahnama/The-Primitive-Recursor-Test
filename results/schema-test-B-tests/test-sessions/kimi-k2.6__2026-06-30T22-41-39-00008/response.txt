**System:**  
\[
\begin{array}{l}
F(x, y, Z) \to x \\
F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n))
\end{array}
\]

---

### Method (A) – LPO with precedence \(F > G > S > Z\)

1. **Does it succeed?** **Yes.**  
   - Rule 1: \(F(x,y,Z) >_{\text{lpo}} x\) because \(x\) is a variable occurring in the left-hand side.  
   - Rule 2: Since \(F > G\), LPO requires \(F(x,y,S(n)) > y\) and \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\). The former holds by the variable rule. The latter holds because the heads are equal and the third arguments compare as \(S(n) >_{\text{lpo}} n\). Thus the rule is oriented.

2. **Within benchmark boundary?** **Yes.**  
   LPO is a purely syntactic simplification order; it uses only a precedence on the signature and imports no semantic axioms about the symbols.

---

### Method (B) – Polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\)

\([F(x,y,n)] = x+n+1,\; [S(n)] = n+1,\; [G(a,b)] = b,\; [Z] = 0\)

1. **Does it succeed?** **No.**  
   While each rule individually satisfies \([l] > [r]\), the interpretation is **not strictly monotone** in the second argument of \(F\) and the first argument of \(G\). Consequently it is not a reduction order. A counterexample to global decrease is rewriting inside an ignored position:  
   \[
   G(\,F(x,y,Z),\, b\,) \;\to\; G(x,b)
   \]
   Before and after, the measure equals \([b]\), so the measure does not strictly decrease.

2. **Within benchmark boundary?** **No.**  
   The assignment imports object-level semantic assumptions that are not derivable from the rules: \(S\) is interpreted as the successor, \(Z\) as zero, and \(G\) as second projection. These are structural assumptions about the intended meaning of the symbols, not consequences of the rewrite system alone.

---

### Method (C) – KBO with uniform weights \(w(F)=w(G)=w(S)=w(Z)=1\)

1. **Does it succeed?** **No.**  
   Knuth–Bendix Order requires that every variable occur at least as often on the left as on the right. Rule 2 violates this: the variable \(y\) occurs once on the left but **twice** on the right (once in the first argument of \(G\), once in the second argument of the recursive \(F\)). No choice of precedence or uniform weights can repair this.

2. **Within benchmark boundary?** **Yes.**  
   KBO is a purely syntactic path order; it does not rely on any external datatype axioms or object-level assumptions.

---

### Method (D) – Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of \(F\)

1. **Does it succeed?** **Yes.**  
   The only defined symbol with a recursive call is \(F\), yielding the single dependency pair  
   \[
   F^\sharp(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\sharp(x,y,n).
   \]
   Projecting to the third argument maps this pair to \(S(n) \to n\). Since \(n\) is a **proper subterm** of \(S(n)\), the strict subterm relation orients the DP. The subterm criterion therefore deletes the only dependency pair, proving termination.

2. **Within benchmark boundary?** **Yes.**  
   The DP framework with the subterm criterion uses only the syntactic structure of the given rules and the ordinary (syntactic) subterm relation; no external semantic axioms are imported.

---

### Method (E) – Direct descent measure on the third argument

\[
\mu(F(x,y,n)) = \text{depth}_S(n)+1,\quad \mu(G(a,b)) = \mu(b),\quad \mu(x)=0
\]

1. **Does it succeed?** **No.**  
   The measure ignores the first two arguments of \(F\) and the first argument of \(G\). Because redexes can appear in those positions, a rewrite step can leave \(\mu\) unchanged. For example:
   \[
   F(\,F(a,b,Z),\, y,\, S(n)\,) \;\to\; F(a, y, S(n))
   \]
   Both sides have measure \(\text{depth}_S(n)+2\). Similarly, rewriting inside the first argument of \(G\) leaves \(\mu\) unchanged. Hence \(\mu\) does not strictly decrease on every rewrite step.

2. **Within benchmark boundary?** **No.**  
   The method assumes that termination can be witnessed by tracking **only** the third argument via an \(S\)-depth, effectively treating \(S\) and \(Z\) as natural-number constructors and presupposing that the other arguments are irrelevant. These are structural/object-level assumptions not derivable from the rewrite rules alone.

---

## Methods satisfying **both** conditions

Only **(A)** and **(D)** succeed in proving termination **and** remain entirely within the benchmark boundary.
