**Termination (strong normalisation) of the given TRS**

The two rewrite rules are  

```
(1)  F(x, y, Z) → x
(2)  F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))
```

Only the third argument of `F` changes (from `Z` to a variable, or from `S(n)` to `n`).  Consequently any well‑founded measure that “counts” the number of leading `S`‑constructors in that argument proves termination.

Below we examine each proposed method.

| Method | (1) Does it prove termination? | (2) Does it stay inside the benchmark boundary? |
|--------|--------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------|
| **A** – LPO with precedence `F > G > S > Z` | Yes. Both rules are oriented:  `F(x,y,Z) > x` because `F` out‑ranks any variable, and `F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))` because the head symbol `F` out‑ranks `G`. | **No.** LPO requires a precedence order on the signature – an external global ordering that is not fixed by the rules themselves. This is an “imported structural choice.” |
| **B** – Polynomial interpretation over ℕ | Yes.  <br>· `[F(x,y,Z)] = x+1 > x`.  <br>· `[F(x,y,S(n))] = x+n+2 > x+n+1 = [G(y,F(x,y,n))]`. | **No.** The interpretation uses the natural numbers and their usual order – a concrete model that is not supplied by the rewrite system. |
| **C** – KBO with uniform weight 1 (no precedence given) | **No** (as stated).  The second rule gives equal weight on both sides, so the method cannot orient it without an additional precedence.  Even if a precedence were added, the method would still rely on an external ordering, placing it outside the boundary. | **No.** The missing precedence (or any needed precedence) is an imported global choice. |
| **D** – Dependency‑pair + subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of `F` | Yes.  The dependency pairs are <br>· `F(x,y,S(n)) → G(y,F(x,y,n))` <br>· `F(x,y,S(n)) → F(x,y,n)`.  The second pair has RHS (`F(x,y,n)`) as a proper subterm of the LHS; the subterm criterion therefore guarantees that no infinite chain exists. | **Yes.** The method uses only the term‑structure (subterm relation) and the definition of “defined symbols” – no external ordering, weights, or number domains. |
| **E** – Direct descent measure `μ(F(x,y,n)) = depth_S(n)+1`, `μ(G(a,b)) = μ(b)`, `μ(x)=0` | Yes.  <br>· `μ(F(x,y,Z)) = 1 > 0 = μ(x)`. <br>· `μ(F(x,y,S(n))) = depth_S(n)+2 > depth_S(n)+1 = μ(G(y,F(x,y,n)))`.  Hence every step strictly decreases μ. | **Yes.** The measure is defined in terms of the *depth of the constructor `S`* in the third argument – a property that follows directly from the given term syntax.  Counting these depths uses ordinary meta‑theoretic reasoning (induction on the natural numbers), which is allowed. |

### Methods that satisfy **both** conditions

- **D** – Dependency‑pair/subterm method.  
- **E** – Direct descent measure on the third argument.

Thus, the only methods that both succeed in proving termination **and** remain within the prescribed benchmark boundary are **D** and **E**.
