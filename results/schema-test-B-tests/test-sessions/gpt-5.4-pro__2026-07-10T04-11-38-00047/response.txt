Evaluating these as proofs of **full termination / strong normalization of the TRS under unrestricted rewriting**:

\[
\begin{aligned}
F(x,y,Z) &\to x\\
F(x,y,S(n)) &\to G(y,F(x,y,n))
\end{aligned}
\]

| Method | Succeeds? | Within benchmark boundary? | Why |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **(A) LPO with \(F > G > S > Z\)** | **Yes** | **Yes** | Rule 1: \(F(x,y,Z) > x\) by subterm/property of LPO. Rule 2: \(F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))\) since \(F>G\), and the LHS is \(>\) both arguments of the RHS; in particular \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) because same head \(F\) and \(S(n) > n\). Standard syntactic termination proof, no extra object assumptions. |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\)** | **No** | **Yes** | The rule inequalities hold at the root: \(x+1>x\) and \(x+n+2>x+n+1\). But as stated this does **not** give a reduction order for full rewriting, because the interpretation ignores rewrite-active positions: \(F\) ignores arg 2, \(G\) ignores arg 1. Example: \(G(F(a,b,Z),c) \to G(a,c)\), but both sides interpret to \([c]\). So it does not prove every rewrite step decreases. Still a standard meta-level method, so boundary is respected. |
| **(C) KBO with uniform symbol weights \(w(F)=w(G)=w(S)=w(Z)=1\)** | **No** | **Yes** | Rule 2 cannot be oriented by KBO: variable \(y\) occurs once on the LHS but twice on the RHS, violating KBO’s variable-occurrence condition. With uniform weights, the RHS is also not lighter anyway. This is still a standard termination method within the benchmark boundary. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion, projecting to arg 3 of \(F\)** | **Yes** | **Yes** | The only dependency pair is \(F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n)\). Projecting to the 3rd argument gives \(S(n) \triangleright n\), a strict subterm decrease, so the DP problem is closed by the subterm criterion. Purely syntactic, within boundary. |
| **(E) Direct measure on the 3rd argument, \(\mu(F(x,y,n))=\mathrm{depth}_S(n)+1,\ \mu(G(a,b))=\mu(b),\ \mu(x)=0\)** | **No** | **No** as stated | The claim “each rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\)” is false for unrestricted rewriting. Two failures: (i) **substitutions**: with \(x := F(u,v,S(Z))\), \(F(x,y,Z)\to x\) gives \(\mu=1 \to 2\), so it increases; (ii) **contexts**: \(G(F(a,b,Z),c)\to G(a,c)\) leaves \(\mu\) unchanged since \(\mu(G(a,b))=\mu(b)\). So the argument tacitly treats variables / ignored positions as inert, which is not licensed by the TRS alone. |

## Methods satisfying both conditions
**(A)** and **(D)** only.
