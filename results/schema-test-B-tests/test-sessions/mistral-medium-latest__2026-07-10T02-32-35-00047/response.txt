- **Termination**: LPO is a well-known termination method for TRS. For the given system:
  - The first rule `F(x, y, Z) -> x` is terminating because `F` is greater than `x` (a variable) in the precedence, and the arguments of `F` are compared lexicographically with the arguments of `x` (which has no arguments). This is valid in LPO.
  - The second rule `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))` requires comparing `F(x, y, S(n))` and `G(y, F(x, y, n))`. Since `F > G` in the precedence, the left-hand side is greater than the right-hand side, so the rule is terminating under LPO.
  - Thus, LPO succeeds in proving termination.
- **Boundary**: LPO is a purely syntactic method that only relies on the given rules and the precedence. It does not require any additional structural axioms or assumptions beyond the rules. Thus, it stays within the boundary.
- **Conclusion**: (A) satisfies both conditions.
