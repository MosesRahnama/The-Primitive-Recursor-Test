| Method | Proves termination? | Within benchmark boundary? |
|---|---:|---:|
| **(A) LPO with \(F>G>S>Z\)** | **Yes** | **No** |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation** | **No** | **No** |
| **(C) KBO with uniform weights** | **No** | **No** |
| **(D) Dependency pairs, third-argument projection** | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| **(E) Direct measure \(\mu\)** | **No** | **Yes, as stated** |

### (A) LPO

Both rules are oriented:

- \(F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} x\) by the subterm property.
- For the second rule, \(F>G\), and
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} F(x,y,n)
  \]
  because the first two arguments agree and \(S(n)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}n\).

Thus LPO proves termination.

Under the stated narrow boundary test, however, the supplied total precedence is an imported global ordering of the signature, including comparisons not fixed by the rules. Hence this method is outside the boundary. This is distinct from merely invoking the ordinary soundness theorem for LPO.

### (B) Polynomial interpretation

At the root, both rules decrease numerically:

\[
[F(x,y,Z)]=x+1>x,
\]
and
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=x+n+2>x+n+1
=[G(y,F(x,y,n))].
\]

Nevertheless, this is not a valid ordinary polynomial-interpretation termination proof because the interpretations are not strictly monotone in every argument:

- \(F\) ignores its second argument.
- \(G\) ignores its first argument.

Consequently, a rewrite inside an ignored argument need not decrease the interpretation of the surrounding term. The induced strict order is therefore not closed under all contexts. The method as stated does not prove strong normalization.

It also uses an imported global semantic interpretation over \(\mathbb N\), not structure fixed by the rules, so it is outside the stipulated boundary.

### (C) KBO

The second rule cannot be oriented by the stated KBO:

- It duplicates \(y\): the left side has one occurrence of \(y\), while the right side has two. This violates KBO’s variable-occurrence condition.
- With all symbol weights equal to \(1\), the right side also has greater total weight than the left side.

Thus this KBO does not prove termination. The externally selected global symbol weights also place it outside the boundary.

### (D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion

The only recursive dependency pair is

\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]

Projecting to the third argument gives

\[
S(n) \mathrel{\triangleright} n,
\]

a strict proper-subterm decrease. The subterm criterion therefore eliminates the only dependency-pair component and proves termination.

This remains within the boundary: the projection records the recursive descent explicitly present in the rule and does not require an imported global ordering or object-level assumption.

### (E) Direct measure

The claim that every rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\) is false for arbitrary first-order terms. For example,

\[
F(F(Z,Z,S(Z)),Z,Z)\to F(Z,Z,S(Z)).
\]

The proposed measure gives

\[
\mu(F(F(Z,Z,S(Z)),Z,Z))=1,
\qquad
\mu(F(Z,Z,S(Z)))=2,
\]

so the measure actually increases. Rewriting inside an argument ignored by the enclosing measure can likewise leave \(\mu\) unchanged.

As a syntactically defined attempted measure, this method does not itself import a global structural assumption, so it is within the boundary—but it fails. Restricting terms so that such counterexamples cannot occur would add an object-level assumption and would be outside the benchmark.

## Conclusion

The only listed method satisfying **both** conditions is:

\[
\boxed{\text{(D) Dependency pairs with the third-argument subterm criterion.}}
\]
