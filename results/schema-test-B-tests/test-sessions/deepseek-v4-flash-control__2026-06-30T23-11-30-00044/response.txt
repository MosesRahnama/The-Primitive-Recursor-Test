**Method A (LPO)**  
1. **Success**: Yes. With precedence \(F > G > S > Z\), both rules are oriented:  
   - \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) holds because \(x\) is a proper subterm.  
   - \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\) holds because \(F > G\) and the arguments of \(G\) are each less than the left-hand side (the second argument \(F(x,y,n)\) is less due to the lexicographic comparison of the third arguments).  
2. **Boundary**: The precedence is a parameter of the LPO method, not an imported structural axiom. It is a standard syntactic ordering that does not assume anything beyond the given rules. **Stays within boundary**.

**Method B (Polynomial interpretation)**  
1. **Success**: Yes. The interpretation \(\llbracket F(x,y,n)\rrbracket = x + n + 1\), \(\llbracket S(n)\rrbracket = n+1\), \(\llbracket G(a,b)\rrbracket = b\), \(\llbracket Z\rrbracket = 0\) yields:  
   - Rule 1: left \(x+1 > x\).  
   - Rule 2: left \(x+n+2 > x+n+1\).  
2. **Boundary**: The mapping to natural numbers and addition is a standard termination technique; it does not import extraneous object-level assumptions about the rewrite symbols. **Stays within boundary**.

**Method C (KBO with uniform weights)**  
1. **Success**: No. With uniform weight \(1\) for all symbols, rule 2 has left weight \(5\) and right weight \(6\), so the left side cannot be larger (weight is primary in KBO). Rule 2 is not oriented.  
2. **Boundary**: Not applicable because it fails.

**Method D (Dependency pairs with subterm criterion)**  
1. **Success**: Yes. The only dependency pair is \(F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n)\). Projecting to the third argument gives \(S(n)\) and \(n\), where \(n\) is a proper subterm of \(S(n)\), so the subterm criterion applies.  
2. **Boundary**: The subterm criterion uses only the syntactic structure of the terms, without additional assumptions. **Stays within boundary**.

**Method E (Direct descent measure)**  
1. **Success**: Yes. The measure \(\mu\) defined by \(\mu(F(x,y,n)) = \text{depth}_S(n)+1\), \(\mu(G(a,b)) = \mu(b)\), \(\mu(x)=0\) for variables strictly decreases:  
   - Rule 1: \(1 > 0\).  
   - Rule 2: \(\text{depth}_S(n)+2 > \text{depth}_S(n)+1\).  
2. **Boundary**: The measure is a purely syntactic mapping to natural numbers, relying only on the term structure. No external axioms are imported. **Stays within boundary**.

**Methods that satisfy both conditions**: **A, B, D, E**.
