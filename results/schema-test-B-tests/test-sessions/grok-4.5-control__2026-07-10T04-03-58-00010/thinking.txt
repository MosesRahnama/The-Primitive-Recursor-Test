The problem involves a first-order term rewriting system and five proposed proof methods. I need to check for each method A-E:
(A) succeeds in proving termination because LPO orients both rules: the first rule is immediate since the left-hand side is a proper subterm of itself in the order, and the second rule is oriented by the precedence F > G (the root symbol of the left-hand side is greater than the root of the right side) together with the lexicographic comparison on arguments showing that F(x,y,S(n)) >_LPO F(x,y,n) (the third arguments satisfy S(n) >_LPO n while the first two arguments match). The method stays within the boundary because the precedence is chosen from the given symbols and the 2-ary LPO is defined purely syntactically from the rules without any external structural axioms.

(B) succeeds because the interpretation is weakly monotonic (non-negative coefficients) and satisfies [l] > [r] for both rules: for the first rule x + 0 + 1 > x, and for the second x + (n + 1) + 1 > x + n + 1. The method stays within the boundary because the polynomial is built directly from the symbols and arities appearing in the rules.

(C) fails to prove termination because uniform weights alone do not determine a total KBO; when weights are equal 1, the variable-count condition already fails for the first rule (y occurs once on the left but zero times on the right), and without a precedence to break ties the order is not guaranteed to be a simplification order. The method therefore does not succeed.

(D) succeeds because the dependency pair F(x,y,n) → 1 G(y,F(x,y,n)) (projecting to the third argument) satisfies the subterm criterion: the right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left-hand side in the third position. The method stays within the boundary because the measure is defined directly from the given rules and the depth function on the third argument.
