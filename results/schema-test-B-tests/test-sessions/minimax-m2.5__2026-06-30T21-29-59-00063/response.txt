**Termination (strong‑normalisation) of**

```
F(x,y,Z) → x
F(x,y,S(n)) → G(y,F(x,y,n))
```

**Method‑by‑method check**

| Method | 1. Proves termination? | 2. Stays inside the benchmark boundary? |
|--------|------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| **A** – LPO with precedence F > G > S > Z | **Yes**.  The LPO induced by the precedence makes the left‑hand side of each rule larger than the right‑hand side (F > variable and F > G). | **Yes**.  LPO is a purely syntactic order; no extra axioms about the symbols are required. |
| **B** – Polynomial interpretation ( [F]=x+n+1, [S]=n+1, [G]=b, [Z]=0 ) | **Yes**.  Both rules satisfy [F(x,y,Z)]=x+1 > x and [F(x,y,S(n))]=x+n+2 > x+n+1=[G(y,F(x,y,n))]. | **No**.  The interpretation assigns the *successor* meaning to S and the zero meaning to Z and relies on the well‑foundedness of the natural numbers.  These are object‑level assumptions that are not derivable from the given rewrite rules. |
| **C** – KBO with uniform weights w(F)=w(G)=w(S)=w(Z)=1 | **Yes** (with any precedence that puts F > G, e.g. F > G > S > Z).  The first rule is weight‑decreasing; the second has equal weight, and the precedence F > G together with the lexicographic comparison on the third argument (S(n) > n) makes the left‑hand side larger. | **Yes**.  KBO is a syntactic ordering; the weight/​precedence choice does not introduce any external algebraic axioms. |
| **D** – Dependency‑pair + subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F | **Yes**.  The only dependency pair is (F(x,y,S(n)), G(y,F(x,y,n))).  Filtering to the third argument gives the pair S(n) → n, which is decreasing in the subterm order (S(n) ⊃ n).  Hence the subterm criterion is satisfied and termination follows. | **Yes**.  The dependency‑pair technique and the subterm order are purely term‑rewriting notions; no interpretation of the function symbols beyond the rules is needed. |
| **E** – Direct descent measure μ(F(x,y,n))=depthₛ(n)+1, μ(G(a,b))=μ(b), μ(x)=0 | **Yes**.  For the two rules we have μ(F(x,y,Z))=1 > 0=μ(x) and μ(F(x,y,S(n)))=2 > 1=μ(G(y,F(x,y,n))).  Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases μ. | **Yes**.  The measure is defined by counting the number of S‑constructors in the third argument; this is a syntactic rank function that requires no extra algebraic axioms. |

**Methods that satisfy both requirements**

- **A**, **C**, **D**, and **E** prove termination **and** stay within the original benchmark boundary.  

- **B** succeeds in proving termination but does **not** stay within the boundary because it imports the standard natural‑number semantics for S and Z.
