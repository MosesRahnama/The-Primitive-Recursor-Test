### Analysis of the TRS and Methods

The given TRS is
\[
\begin{align*}
F(x,y,Z)&\to x,\\
F(x,y,S(n))&\to G(y,F(x,y,n)).
\end{align*}
\]
It is a standard first-order term rewrite system (no equations, no additional sort constraints, free term algebra over the signature \(\{F,G,S,Z\}\)). Strong normalization (SN) means the absence of infinite rewrite sequences starting from *any* term. The five methods are assessed exactly as stated, with respect to (1) whether they correctly establish SN and (2) whether they remain inside the pure-TRS boundary (no added structural axioms, no object-level hypotheses that are not consequences of the two rules alone).

#### (A) LPO with precedence \(F>G>S>Z\)
- The first rule is oriented because every LPO is a simplification order, hence \(F(x,y,Z)\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}x\).
- For the second rule the root comparison \(F>G\) reduces the claim to the two inequalities \(F(x,y,S(n))\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}y\) (immediate) and \(F(x,y,S(n))\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}F(x,y,n)\). The latter holds by the lexicographic (or multiset) status of \(F\): the first two arguments are identical and the third arguments satisfy \(S(n)\succ_{\mathrm{lpo}}n\).
- Consequently both rules are strictly decreasing in a well-founded simplification order, which proves SN.
- The argument uses only the classical definition of LPO on the given signature; no external axioms are imported.
- **Both conditions hold.**

#### (B) Polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb{N}\)
\[
[F(x,y,n)]=x+n+1,\qquad
[S(n)]=n+1,\qquad
[G(a,b)]=b,\qquad
[Z]=0.
\]
- First rule: \([F(x,y,Z)]=x+1>x=[x]\).
- Second rule: \([F(x,y,S(n))]=x+n+2>[F(x,y,n)]=[G(y,F(x,y,n))]\).
- All interpretations are weakly monotonic, therefore every rewrite step (in any context, under any substitution) produces a strict decrease in \(\mathbb{N}\). Well-foundedness of \(>\) on \(\mathbb{N}\) yields SN.
- The interpretation is a pure semantic method applied to the two rules; it does not enlarge the TRS by structural axioms.
- **Both conditions hold.**

#### (C) KBO with uniform weights \(w(F)=w(G)=w(S)=w(Z)=1\)
- Variable weight is the standard value 1. The left-hand side of the second rule therefore receives weight 5 while the right-hand side receives weight 6.
- KBO demands non-increasing weight, which already fails. No choice of precedence can repair the failure.
- The method does **not** prove SN.
- (Boundary compliance is irrelevant once success has failed.)

#### (D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion (projection onto the third argument of \(F\))
- The only dependency pair is \(F^\sharp(x,y,S(n))\to F^\sharp(x,y,n)\).
- Projecting onto the third argument of \(F^\sharp\) yields the proper subterm relation \(S(n)\rhd n\). The subterm criterion therefore shows that every dependency-pair chain is finite.
- Hence the original TRS is SN.
- The argument is the ordinary syntactic dependency-pair framework; nothing beyond the two rules is assumed.
- **Both conditions hold.**

#### (E) Direct descent measure \(\mu\)
- The proposed function is not even defined on the whole term algebra (the clause “\(\mathrm{depth}_S(n)\)” is meaningless when the third argument is headed by \(F\) or \(G\), or is a variable).
- Even after a naïve totalisation (e.g., \(\mathrm{depth}_S(t)=0\) whenever \(t\) is not headed by \(S\)), \(\mu\) fails to decrease on every rewrite step. Counter-example:
  \[
  F\bigl(F(Z,Z,S(Z)),Z,Z\bigr)
  \;\to\;
  F\bigl(G(Z,F(Z,Z,Z)),Z,Z\bigr)
  \]
  both sides receive \(\mu\)-value 1. The claim “each rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\)” is therefore false, and the method does not establish SN.
- (Boundary compliance is irrelevant once success has failed.)

### Conclusion
Only methods (A), (B) and (D) both prove SN and stay inside the pure-TRS boundary.
