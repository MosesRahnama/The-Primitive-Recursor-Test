## Details

### (A) LPO with precedence `F > G > S > Z`
**Succeeds:** Yes.

- Rule 1: `F(x,y,Z) -> x`  
  LPO has the subterm/property for variables occurring in the term, so `F(x,y,Z) > x`.

- Rule 2: `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y, F(x,y,n))`  
  Since `F > G`, it is enough to show the left side is greater than both arguments of the right:
  - `F(x,y,S(n)) > y`
  - `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)` because same head `F`, first two args are equal, and `S(n) > n`.

So LPO proves termination.

**Boundary:** No.  
The proof depends on an externally chosen global precedence, which is exactly the kind of imported structural choice your boundary excludes.

---

### (B) Polynomial interpretation over `N`
\[
[F(x,y,n)] = x+n+1,\quad [S(n)] = n+1,\quad [G(a,b)] = b,\quad [Z]=0
\]

**Succeeds:** No, not as stated.

At the rule roots, it looks fine:

- Rule 1: `x+1 > x`
- Rule 2: `x+(n+1)+1 = x+n+2 > x+n+1 = [G(y,F(x,y,n))]`

But the interpretation is **not monotone in all argument positions**:

- `F` ignores its 2nd argument,
- `G` ignores its 1st argument.

So it does **not** yield a global reduction order for arbitrary contexts. For example:
- `G(F(u,v,Z), w) -> G(u, w)` is a rewrite step in the first argument of `G`,
- but both sides interpret to `w`.

So the measure need not strictly decrease on every rewrite step.

**Boundary:** No.  
This uses an externally chosen algebra/interpretation not fixed by the rules.

---

### (C) KBO with uniform weights `1`
**Succeeds:** No.

Rule 2 is the blocker:
\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y, F(x,y,n))
\]

Variable counts:
- on the left, `y` occurs once,
- on the right, `y` occurs twice.

KBO requires each variable to occur at least as often on the left as on the right. That fails here, so KBO cannot orient rule 2. With these weights, the right side is also heavier anyway.

**Boundary:** No.  
It depends on imported global weights (and in practice a precedence too).

---

### (D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, project to argument 3 of `F`
**Succeeds:** Yes.

The only recursive call is in rule 2, so the only dependency pair is:
\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n)
\]

Using the projection to the 3rd argument:
- left projects to `S(n)`
- right projects to `n`

and `S(n)` is a strict superterm of `n`. So the subterm criterion closes the DP problem immediately, hence termination follows.

**Boundary:** Yes, under your narrow reading.  
This uses standard metatheory, but the crucial projection is directly tied to the rule’s visible recursive descent on the 3rd argument, not an arbitrary imported global symbol order/weighting.

---

### (E) Direct descent measure on the 3rd argument
**Succeeds:** No.

The proposed claim “each rewrite step strictly decreases `μ`” is false.

Counterexample:
\[
F(F(u,v,S(Z)),\, y,\, Z) \to F(u,v,S(Z))
\]

Compute:
- \(\mu(F(F(u,v,S(Z)), y, Z)) = \text{depth}_S(Z)+1 = 1\)
- \(\mu(F(u,v,S(Z))) = \text{depth}_S(S(Z))+1 = 2\)

So `μ` **increases** on that step.  
The issue is the collapsing rule `F(x,y,Z) -> x`: instantiating `x` by a larger term can expose a term of bigger measure.

**Boundary:** Yes.  
This is a direct measure argument from the given rules, not an imported global precedence/weight system.
