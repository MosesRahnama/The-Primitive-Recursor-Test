The TRS has two rules, with \(F\) the only defined symbol. Termination (strong normalization) means that every rewrite sequence is finite. Analysis of each proposed method is as follows.

**(A)**  
The given LPO (with the stated precedence) orients both rules:  
- \(F(x,y,Z)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}x\) holds because the right-hand side is a proper subterm.  
- \(F(x,y,S(n))>_{\mathrm{LPO}}G(y,F(x,y,n))\) holds because \(F>G\), the left-hand side is larger than each argument of the right-hand side, and the third arguments compare by the subterm property of LPO.  
LPO is a reduction order, so both rules are decreasing and the TRS is terminating. The argument uses only the rewrite rules and the definition of LPO; no external axioms are required.  
Thus: succeeds, and stays inside the benchmark boundary.

**(B)**  
The given polynomial interpretation (into \(\mathbb{N}\)) makes every rule strictly decreasing:  
- \([F(x,y,Z)]=x+1>x=[x]\),  
- \([F(x,y,S(n))]=x+n+2>x+n+1=[G(y,F(x,y,n))]\).  
All interpretations are weakly monotone, therefore every rewrite step (in any context) strictly decreases the \(\mathbb{N}\)-value of a term. Well-foundedness of \(\mathbb{N}\) yields termination. The construction refers solely to the two rewrite rules.  
Thus: succeeds, and stays inside the benchmark boundary.

**(C)**  
In any KBO the variable condition \(|l|_x\ge|r|_x\) (for every variable \(x\)) is mandatory. The second rule duplicates \(y\), violating the condition. Consequently no choice of precedence can make the rule decreasing under the stated uniform weights, and KBO does not prove termination.  
Thus: does not succeed (boundary status is therefore irrelevant).

**(D)**  
The only dependency pair is \(F^\sharp(x,y,S(n))\to F^\sharp(x,y,n)\).  
The subterm-criterion processor that projects every \(F^\sharp\)-term onto its third argument turns this pair into the strict subterm decrease \(S(n)\rhd n\). The processor therefore deletes the only pair, proving that no infinite dependency chain exists. By the dependency-pair theorem the original TRS is terminating. The whole argument is obtained by purely syntactic inspection of the given rules.  
Thus: succeeds, and stays inside the benchmark boundary.

**(E)**  
The proposed function \(\mu\) is not a well-defined measure on all terms (it is silent on \(S\) and \(Z\)) and, under any total extension that respects the three displayed equations, fails to be strictly decreasing. A concrete counter-example is the root rewrite  
\[
F(G(a,F(b,c,S(Z))),y,Z)\;\to\;G(a,F(b,c,S(Z))),
\]  
for which every natural extension of \(\mu\) increases. The claim that “each rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\)” is therefore false, and the method does not establish termination. (In addition the appeal to the auxiliary function \(\mathrm{depth}_S\) imports a structural notion that is not derivable from the rewrite rules alone.)  
Thus: does not succeed, and does not stay inside the benchmark boundary.

**Methods that satisfy both requirements**  
Only (A), (B) and (D).
