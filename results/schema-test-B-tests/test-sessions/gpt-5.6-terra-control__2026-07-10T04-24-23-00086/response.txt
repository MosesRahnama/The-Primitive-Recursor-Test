| Method | Proves termination? | Within the stated benchmark boundary? |
|---|---:|---:|
| (A) LPO with \(F>G>S>Z\) | Yes | No |
| (B) Polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\) | Yes | No |
| (C) KBO with all symbol weights \(1\) | No | No |
| (D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion, third argument of \(F\) | Yes | Yes |
| (E) Proposed direct \(\mu\)-measure | No | Yes |

Therefore, **(D) is the only listed method satisfying both conditions.**

### (A) LPO

This does orient both rules.

* \(F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} x\) by the subterm property.
* For
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)),
  \]
  the root comparison uses \(F>G\). The left side is greater than \(y\) by subterm, and it is greater than \(F(x,y,n)\) because, at the third argument,
  \[
  S(n) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} n.
  \]

So LPO proves termination.

However, it does so by importing a chosen global precedence on symbols. That precedence is not fixed by the rewrite rules themselves. Under the stated boundary interpretation, this puts (A) outside the boundary.

### (B) Polynomial interpretation

The interpretation strictly decreases on both rules:

\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = x+0+1=x+1 > x=[x].
\]

For the second rule,

\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=x+(n+1)+1=x+n+2,
\]
whereas
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=[F(x,y,n)]=x+n+1.
\]

Thus the left-hand side is strictly larger by \(1\), for every valuation.

So this is a valid termination proof. But it depends on an externally selected global semantic interpretation into \(\mathbb N\), including the arbitrary assignments to \(F,G,S,Z\). Those choices are not determined by the rules. Hence it is outside the stated boundary.

### (C) KBO with uniform weights \(1\)

This does not orient the second rule.

Using the usual positive variable weight \(w_0\), the relevant weights are

\[
w(F(x,y,S(n))) = 2+3w_0
\]
and
\[
w(G(y,F(x,y,n))) = 2+4w_0.
\]

Thus the right-hand side is heavier than the left-hand side. In particular, with the usual \(w_0=1\),

\[
w(F(x,y,S(n)))=5,\qquad
w(G(y,F(x,y,n)))=6.
\]

Therefore KBO cannot orient that rule from left to right, regardless of precedence.

It also uses a chosen global weight scheme, so it is outside the boundary as well; but its primary defect is that it fails to prove termination.

### (D) Dependency pairs and subterm criterion

This succeeds.

The recursive dependency-pair content includes, in particular,

\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]

Projecting the third argument yields

\[
S(n) \triangleright n,
\]

a strict subterm decrease.

There is also a pair headed by \(G^\#\), arising from the outer \(G\) on the right-hand side, but \(G\) has no rewrite rules and therefore cannot participate in a recursive dependency-pair cycle. The only relevant cyclic component is the \(F^\#\)-to-\(F^\#\) pair, and that component is eliminated by the third-argument projection.

This projection is local to the recursive occurrence displayed by the rule itself: the rule changes the third argument from \(S(n)\) to \(n\). It does not require an imported global precedence, weight system, or semantic structure. Standard dependency-pair soundness is ordinary metatheory, not an additional object-level assumption. Thus (D) remains within the boundary.

### (E) Proposed direct \(\mu\)-measure

At a root redex, the proposed calculation looks promising:

\[
\mu(F(x,y,S(n)))=\operatorname{depth}_S(n)+2
\]
while
\[
\mu(G(y,F(x,y,n)))=\operatorname{depth}_S(n)+1.
\]

Likewise,

\[
\mu(F(x,y,Z))=1>\mu(x)=0.
\]

But this does **not** establish that every rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\), because the measure ignores the first two arguments of \(F\).

For example, consider a rewrite inside the first argument:

\[
F(F(x,y,Z),\,y,\,Z) \to F(x,\,y,\,Z).
\]

Both sides have third argument \(Z\), so both have measure

\[
\mu= \operatorname{depth}_S(Z)+1=1.
\]

Hence this rewrite step does not strictly decrease the proposed \(\mu\). The claim that every rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\) is false.

The measure is a syntactic one based on the displayed \(S\)-structure and does not itself import a prohibited global structural axiom, so it remains within the boundary; it simply fails as a termination proof.
