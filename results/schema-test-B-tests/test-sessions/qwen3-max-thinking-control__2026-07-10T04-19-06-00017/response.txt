### Analysis of Proof Methods for Termination of the Given TRS

The rewrite system is:
```
(VAR x y n)
(RULES

```
This system is terminating: recursion occurs strictly on the third argument of `F` (decreasing `S(n)` to `n`), with `G`, `S`, and `Z` acting as constructors (no rules rewrite them). Rule 1 reduces `F(x,y,Z)` to `x`, which may contain further reducible subterms, but the overall reduction depth is bounded by the syntactic size of the third argument. Below, each method is evaluated strictly as stated against the two criteria. The boundary condition is interpreted narrowly: a method is *outside* the boundary only if it depends on **object-level structural axioms or assumptions not derivable from the rewrite rules themselves** (e.g., Peano axioms like injectivity of `S`, equational theories like associativity). #### **Method (A): Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
- **Succeeds in proving termination? YES**  
  LPO orients both rules:  
  - Rule 1 (`F(x,y,Z) -> x`): `F(x,y,Z) >_lpo x` holds because `x` is a proper subterm of the left-hand side (LPO includes the subterm property; variables occurring as proper subterms satisfy `s >_lpo x`).  
  - Rule 2 (`F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y, F(x,y,n))`): Since `F > G` in precedence, and `F(x,y,S(n)) >_lpo y` (subterm) and `F(x,y,S(n)) >_lpo F(x,y,n)` (via lexicographic comparison of arguments: `(x,y,S(n)) >_lex (x,y,n)` because `S(n) >_lpo n` by subterm property), the rule is oriented.  
  LPO is a well-founded reduction order; orientation implies termination.
  
- **Within benchmark boundary? YES**  
  The precedence `F > G > S > Z` is **derivable from the rules**: `F` is the only symbol appearing on left-hand sides (defined symbol), while `G`, `S`, `Z` never appear on left-hand sides (constructors). Setting `F` greater than constructors is a standard, rule-derived choice (not arbitrary). The ordering among constructors (`G > S > Z`) is sufficient but not necessary (only `F > G` is required); however, this choice is a syntactic parameter of LPO, not an imported object-level axiom. LPO relies solely on term structure (subterm property) and precedence, both fixed by the signature (arity derivable from rules) and rule analysis. No external structural axioms (e.g., properties of `S` beyond its arity) are assumed.
