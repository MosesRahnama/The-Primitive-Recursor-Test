Here is a method-by-method check.

| Method | Proves termination? | Stays within benchmark boundary? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(A) LPO with precedence \(F > G > S > Z\)** | **Yes** | **Yes** | Rule 1: \(F(x,y,Z) > x\) by subterm/variable case. Rule 2: \(F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))\) because \(F > G\), and also \(F(x,y,S(n)) > y\) and \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) since \(S(n) > n\). Standard syntactic proof, no extra object-level assumptions. |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation** | **No** | **Yes** | At the root, both rules decrease: \(x+1 > x\) and \(x+n+2 > x+n+1\). But as stated this does **not** give a valid global termination proof, because the interpretation is not strictly monotone in all rewrite positions: \(F\) ignores its 2nd argument and \(G\) ignores its 1st. Example: \(F(a,\,F(u,v,Z),\,Z) \to F(a,u,Z)\), but both sides interpret to \(a+1\). So not every rewrite step decreases. Still, this is a standard meta-level method, so it stays within the boundary. |
| **(C) KBO with uniform weights \(w(F)=w(G)=w(S)=w(Z)=1\)** | **No** | **Yes** | Rule 2 cannot be oriented by KBO because of the **variable condition**: \(y\) occurs once on the left but twice on the right (\(G(y,F(x,y,n))\)). So KBO fails regardless of precedence. Still within benchmark boundary. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion, project 3rd arg of \(F\)** | **Yes** | **Yes** | The only dependency pair is \(F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n)\). With projection \(\pi(F^\#)=3\), this becomes \(S(n) \triangleright n\), a strict subterm decrease, so there is no infinite DP chain. Standard proof from the rules alone. |
| **(E) Direct measure on the 3rd argument** | **No** | **Yes** | The proposed \(\mu\) decreases for root contractions of \(F\), but **not for all rewrite steps in context** because it ignores many positions. Example: \(F(a,\,F(u,v,Z),\,Z) \to F(a,u,Z)\), yet both terms have \(\mu=1\). So the claim “each rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\)” is false as stated. The idea is still syntactic and within the benchmark boundary. |
