**Analysis of each method:**

**(A)**  
- Succeeds in proving termination? Yes. LPO is a strict simplification order. With the given precedence, both rules are strictly decreasing:  
  - `F(x, y, Z) > x` holds because the variable `x` occurs in the LHS.  
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) > G(y, F(x, y, n))` holds because `F > G` (so the LHS dominates every proper subterm of the RHS) and, for the embedded call, the heads are identical and the arguments are lexicographically decreasing (`S(n) > n` by the subterm property of LPO).  
  LPO is monotonic and stable under substitution, so every rewrite step (including those inside the first argument of `G`) strictly decreases. Hence there are no infinite reduction sequences.  
- Stays within boundary? Yes. The precedence is a choice over exactly the symbols appearing in the given rules; no external axioms, no object-level assumptions about inductive structure, and no global entities not fixed by the rules are imported. Ordinary soundness of LPO is metatheoretic reasoning only.

**(B)**  
- Succeeds in proving termination? No. Although the stated mapping makes the two rules strictly decreasing when viewed in isolation (`[F(x,y,Z)] = x+1 > x` and `x+n+2 > x+n+1`), the interpretation is not strictly monotonic in every argument: `[G(a,b)] = b` ignores its first argument and `[F(x,y,n)] = x+n+1` ignores its second. A rewrite inside the first argument of any `G` (or the second argument of any `F`) leaves the interpreted value unchanged. Because some rewrite steps do not strictly decrease the measure, absence of infinite chains cannot be concluded.  
- (Boundary irrelevant since the first condition already fails.)

**(C)**  
- Succeeds in proving termination? No. With every symbol weight 1 and variables of weight 1, the second rule increases weight:  
  `w(F(x,y,S(n))) = 4 + w(n)` while `w(G(y,F(x,y,n))) = 5 + w(n)`.  
  KBO therefore orients the rule in the wrong direction regardless of the tie-breaking precedence.  
- (Boundary irrelevant since the first condition already fails.)

**(D)**  
- Succeeds in proving termination? Yes. The only dependency pair is `F#(x,y,S(n)) → F#(x,y,n)`. Projecting both marked symbols onto their third argument turns the pair into `S(n) → n`. The subterm criterion recognises that `n` is a proper subterm of `S(n)`, so the pair is strictly decreasing under the embedding order. The resulting DP problem has no infinite chains; the original TRS is therefore terminating. The technique is monotonic by construction and accounts for reductions in all argument positions.  
- Stays within boundary? Yes. Every entity used (marked symbols, the projection, the subterm relation) is taken directly from the given rules and the standard DP theorem. No external structural axioms and no global choices beyond what the rules themselves supply are imported. Ordinary metatheoretic soundness of DP is not itself a violation.

**(E)**  
- Succeeds in proving termination? No. The claimed measure is not strictly decreasing on every rewrite step. Because `mu(G(a,b)) = mu(b)`, a contraction inside the first argument of `G` (e.g., reducing a redex `F(…)` supplied for `a`) leaves `mu` of the whole term unchanged. The stated argument (“each rewrite step strictly decreases mu”) is therefore false, so the method does not establish termination.  
- (Boundary irrelevant since the first condition already fails.)

**Methods satisfying both conditions:** A and D.
