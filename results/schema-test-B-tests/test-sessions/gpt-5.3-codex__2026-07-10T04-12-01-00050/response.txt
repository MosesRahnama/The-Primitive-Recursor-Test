Here’s the check for each method against the two questions.

---

### TRS
1. `F(x,y,Z) -> x`  
2. `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y, F(x,y,n))`

This is intuitively primitive recursion on the 3rd argument of `F`, so termination should be provable.

---

## (A) LPO with precedence `F > G > S > Z`

1. **Succeeds?** **Yes.**  
   - Rule 1: `F(x,y,Z) > x` holds in LPO (a term is greater than its variables/subterms contextually).
   - Rule 2: `F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))` can be oriented since top symbol `F` has higher precedence than `G`, and the RHS arguments are compatible (`y` is a subterm of LHS; `F(x,y,n)` is smaller than `F(x,y,S(n))` because `n < S(n)`).
2. **Within benchmark boundary?** **Yes.**  
   Pure reduction-order argument from the given rules only.

---

## (B) Polynomial interpretation over `N`
\[
[F(x,y,n)] = x+n+1,\quad [S(n)] = n+1,\quad [G(a,b)] = b,\quad [Z]=0
\]

1. **Succeeds?** **Yes.**
   - Rule 1: LHS = `x+0+1 = x+1` > RHS = `x`.
   - Rule 2: LHS = `x+(n+1)+1 = x+n+2`; RHS = `[G(y,F(...,n))]=[F(...,n)] = x+n+1`; strict decrease.
2. **Within benchmark boundary?** **Yes.**  
   Standard interpretation method, no extra object-level axioms.

---

## (C) KBO with uniform weights all 1

1. **Succeeds?** **No.**  
   For rule 2:
   - `w(F(x,y,S(n))) = 1 + x+y+(1+n) = x+y+n+2`
   - `w(G(y,F(x,y,n))) = 1 + y + (1+x+y+n) = x+2y+n+2`
   Requires LHS > RHS, but RHS has an extra `y` contribution, so not orientable in general.
2. **Within benchmark boundary?** **Yes** (it is a legitimate in-boundary method), but it **fails** to prove termination here.

---

## (D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion projecting 3rd arg of `F`

1. **Succeeds?** **Yes.**  
   Only recursive call comes from rule 2, giving DP essentially `F#(x,y,S(n)) -> F#(x,y,n)` (inside `G`, but DP captures the call).  
   Projecting to 3rd arg yields `S(n) -> n`, a strict subterm decrease, so no infinite DP chain.
2. **Within benchmark boundary?** **Yes.**  
   Standard DP framework using only the TRS structure.

---

## (E) Direct measure on 3rd argument (`depth_S`) with `mu(G(a,b))=mu(b)`

1. **Succeeds?** **As stated, effectively yes for this TRS.**  
   - Rule 1: `mu(F(...,Z)) = 1`, RHS variable gives `0`.
   - Rule 2: `mu(F(...,S(n))) = depth_S(n)+2`; RHS `mu(G(y,F(...,n)))=mu(F(...,n))=depth_S(n)+1`. Strictly decreases.
   The measure is tailored to the recursive argument and shows descent each rewrite.
2. **Within benchmark boundary?** **Yes.**  
   It is a direct well-founded measure derived from term structure/rules; no extra axioms needed.

---

## Methods satisfying both conditions

**A, B, D, and E** satisfy both:
- they do prove termination of the given TRS,
- and they stay within the stated benchmark boundary.

**C** is in-boundary but does **not** succeed.
