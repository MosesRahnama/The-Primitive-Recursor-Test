Here is a method-by-method check.

| Method | Proves termination as stated? | Stays within benchmark boundary? | Reason |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **(A) LPO with \(F > G > S > Z\)** | **Yes** | **Yes** | \(F(x,y,Z) > x\) by subterm property. For \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\): first \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) since the third argument decreases \(S(n) > n\); then because \(F > G\) and the LHS is also \(> y\), LPO orients the whole RHS. Standard in-bounds reduction-order proof. |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\)** | **No** | **Yes** | At the rule level it looks decreasing: \(x+1 > x\) and \(x+n+2 > x+n+1\). But as stated it does **not** give a context-closed reduction order, since \(G(a,b)=b\) ignores its first argument and \(F(x,y,n)=x+n+1\) ignores \(y\). Example: \(G(F(Z,Z,Z),Z) \to G(Z,Z)\) is a rewrite step, but both sides interpret to \(0\). So this interpretation does not prove termination of the full TRS. It is still an in-bounds meta-level method. |
| **(C) KBO with uniform weights \(w(F)=w(G)=w(S)=w(Z)=1\)** | **No** | **Yes** | The second rule fails immediately under KBO’s variable condition: \(y\) occurs once on the LHS but **twice** on the RHS (\(G(y,F(x,y,n))\)). So KBO cannot orient it, regardless of precedence details. In-bounds, but unsuccessful. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion, project 3rd arg of \(F\)** | **Yes** | **Yes** | The only defined symbol is \(F\), so the only DP is \(F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n)\). Projecting to the third argument gives \(S(n) \triangleright n\), a strict subterm decrease. That closes the only SCC, so the DP problem terminates. Standard in-bounds proof. |
| **(E) Direct measure \(\mu\) on the third argument** | **No** | **Yes** | As stated, \(\mu\) is not strictly decreasing for all rewrite steps. It ignores \(F\)’s first two arguments and \(G\)’s first argument, and it is not stable under substitutions. Example rule instance: \(F(F(Z,Z,Z),Z,Z) \to F(Z,Z,Z)\). Here \(\mu\) of both sides is \(1\). So the claimed “each rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\)” is false. The idea is still syntactic/in-bounds; it just does not work as stated. |
