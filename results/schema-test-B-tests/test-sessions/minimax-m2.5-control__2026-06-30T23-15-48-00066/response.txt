### Method A: Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z
1. **Succeeds in proving termination?**  
   Yes. Both rewrite rules are decreasing under LPO with the given precedence:  
   - Rule 1: \( F(x, y, Z) \to x \) has \( F(x, y, Z) > x \) because \( x \) occurs as a subterm.  
   - Rule 2: \( F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n)) \) has \( F(x, y, S(n)) > G(y, F(x, y, n)) \) because \( F > G \) in the precedence.

2. **Stays within the benchmark boundary?**  
   No. The method relies on an arbitrary precedence ordering on symbols that is not derived from the rewrite rules. This constitutes an imported global structural choice.

### Method B: Polynomial interpretation over ℕ
1. **Succeeds in proving termination?**  
   Yes. The interpretations satisfy \( [F(x, y, Z)] = x + 1 > x = [x] \) and \( [F(x, y, S(n))] = x + n + 2 > x + n + 1 = [G(y, F(x, y, n))] \). All functions are monotone.

2. **Stays within the benchmark boundary?**  
   No. The method interprets symbols in the natural numbers with the usual order, which is a structural model not derivable from the rules alone.

### Method C: Knuth-Bendix Order (KBO) with uniform symbol weights
1. **Succeeds in proving termination?**  
   Yes, with a precedence such as \( F > G \). Rule 1 has weight \( 2 > 0 \). Rule 2 has equal weight, and \( F > G \) ensures decrease.

2. **Stays within the benchmark boundary?**  
   No. The method requires choosing a precedence, which is an arbitrary global structural choice not fixed by the rules.

### Method D: Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F
1. **Succeeds in proving termination?**  
   Yes. The dependency pairs are \( F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n)) \) and \( F(x, y, S(n)) \to F(x, y, n) \). Projecting to the third argument yields \( S(n) \to n \), and the subterm criterion applies because \( n \) is a subterm of \( S(n) \). This proves termination.

2. **Stays within the benchmark boundary?**  
   Yes. The method uses only syntactic properties of the rewrite rules (dependency pairs and subterm criterion) without importing external structures or assumptions.

### Method E: Direct descent measure on the third argument
1. **Succeeds in proving termination?**  
   Yes. The measure \( \mu \) decreases for both rules:  
   - Rule 1: \( \mu(F(x, y, Z)) = 1 > 0 = \mu(x) \).  
   - Rule 2: \( \mu(F(x, y, S(n))) = \text{depth}_S(S(n)) + 1 = \text{depth}_S(n) + 2 > \text{depth}_S(n) + 1 = \mu(G(y, F(x, y, n))) \).

2. **Stays within the benchmark boundary?**  
   Yes. The measure is defined purely in terms of term structure (depth of \( S \)-symbols) and uses no external assumptions.

### Methods satisfying both conditions

- **E**: Direct descent measure on the third argument.
