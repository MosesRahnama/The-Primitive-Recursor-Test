**Analysis of each method**

| Method | 1. Does it prove termination? | 2. Does it stay within the benchmark boundary?* | Reasoning |
|--------|------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|-----------|
| **(A) LPO with precedence F > G > S > Z** | **Yes** | **Yes** | Both rules are oriented by the lexicographic path order.  Rule 1 is oriented because the right‑hand side variable *x* occurs in the left‑hand side.  Rule 2 is oriented because the root symbol *F* is larger than *G* in the precedence, and the recursive call *F(x,y,n)* is smaller than *F(x,y,S(n))* by the lexicographic comparison of arguments (the third argument *S(n)* is a proper super‑term of *n*).  LPO uses only the given precedence and the purely syntactic subterm/lexicographic relations; it imports no external semantics. |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation over ℕ** | **Yes** | **No** | The interpretation gives <br>• [*F(x,y,Z)*] = *x* + 1 > *x* = [*x*] <br>• [*F(x,y,S(n))*] = *x* + *n* + 2 > *x* + *n* + 1 = [*G(y,F(x,y,n))*].  Hence the order decreases.  However, the method **imports the natural numbers** as a semantic domain and interprets *S* as the successor and *Z* as zero.  This is an object‑level assumption about the meaning of the symbols that is not derivable from the rewrite rules alone, violating the benchmark requirement. |
| **(C) KBO with uniform weights w(F)=w(G)=w(S)=w(Z)=1** | **No** | (N/A – fails to prove) | With uniform weights both sides of rule 2 have weight 2 (|F(x,y,S(n))|=2, |G(y,F(x,y,n))|=2).  Knuth–Bendix order can then orient the rule only by a precedence between *F* and *G*.  Since the method **does not specify any precedence**, it cannot orient the second rule and therefore fails to establish termination. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion (project to 3rd arg. of F)** | **Yes** | **Yes** | The only dependency pair generated from the system is <br> *F(x,y,S(n)) → F(x,y,n)*.  Projecting defined symbol *F* to its third argument yields the comparison *S(n)* > *n* in the subterm ordering.  Because *n* is a proper subterm of *S(n)*, the subterm criterion applies and the DP‑problem is solved.  The argument uses only the intrinsic subterm relation of the term algebra; no external data‑type assumptions are needed. |
| **(E) Direct descent measure on the third argument** | **Yes** | **No** | The measure µ strictly decreases: <br>• µ(F(x,y,Z)) = 1 > 0 = µ(x) <br>• µ(F(x,y,S(n))) = depth_S(n)+2 > depth_S(n)+1 = µ(G(y,F(x,y,n))))).  However, defining µ requires the **“S‑depth”** function, i.e. counting the number of *S*‑constructors.  This imports a structural assumption that the third argument is built from *S* and *Z* (a “natural number” structure) that is not present in the bare rewrite system, violating the boundary condition. |

\*The benchmark boundary requires that strong normalization be established **from the presented rewrite system alone**, without importing structural axioms or object‑level assumptions (e.g. that *S* is a successor, that the third argument is a natural number, or the arithmetic of ℕ) beyond what is derivable from the given rules.

---

**Conclusion**

The only methods that **both** succeed in proving termination **and** respect the restriction of working purely from the rewrite system are:

* **(A) Lexicographic Path Order**  
* **(D) Dependency Pairs with the subterm criterion**  

Methods (B) and (E) succeed as termination proofs but import external semantic structure (ℕ or S‑depth). Method (C) is insufficient as stated and cannot orient the critical second rule.
