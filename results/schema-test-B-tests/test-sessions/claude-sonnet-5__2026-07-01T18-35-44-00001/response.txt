## Analysis of Each Method

**(A) LPO with precedence F > G > S > Z**

*Success:* Yes. Rule 1 is a subterm case (trivial). For rule 2, since F > G in the precedence, we need F(x,y,S(n)) to dominate both arguments of G(y,F(x,y,n)):
- F(x,y,S(n)) ≻ y (y is a proper subterm)
- F(x,y,S(n)) ≻ F(x,y,n): same head F, compare argument tuples lexicographically; (x,y,S(n)) vs (x,y,n) reduces to comparing S(n) vs n, and S(n) ≻ n by the subterm property.

Both conditions needed for LPO's recursive definition are met, so F(x,y,S(n)) ≻ G(y,F(x,y,n)) holds. LPO is a simplification order, so orienting both rules suffices for termination.

*Boundary:* Yes — purely a precedence/order defined over the given signature; no outside assumptions needed.

---

**(B) Polynomial interpretation**

*Success:* Yes.
- Rule 1: [F(x,y,Z)] = x+0+1 = x+1 > x = [x]. ✓
- Rule 2: [F(x,y,S(n))] = x+(n+1)+1 = x+n+2; [G(y,F(x,y,n))] = [F(x,y,n)] = x+n+1. Difference = 1 > 0. ✓

All interpretations are weakly monotonic in every argument (coefficients are 0 or 1, never negative), so this is a valid monotone algebra, and polynomial interpretations are a sound termination criterion.

*Boundary:* Yes — a standard algebraic interpretation built entirely from the symbols in the rules.

---

**(C) KBO with uniform weights = 1**

*Success:* No. Check rule 2: F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y,F(x,y,n)). The variable y occurs once on the left but **twice** on the right — this violates the basic variable-occurrence admissibility condition required for KBO to even be a well-defined reduction order. Moreover, computing raw weights: weight(LHS) = 1(F)+1(x)+1(y)+1(S)+1(n) = 5, weight(RHS) = 1(G)+1(y)+1(F)+1(x)+1(y)+1(n) = 6. Since weight(RHS) > weight(LHS), rule 2 cannot be oriented left-to-right under KBO regardless of precedence. The method fails outright.

*Boundary:* The attempt itself stays within the intended technique (weights/precedence over the given signature), but it simply does not succeed.

---

**(D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion**

*Success:* Yes. Since G is not a defined symbol, the only dependency pair is
F#(x,y,S(n)) -> F#(x,y,n).
Projecting π(F#(x,y,n)) = n gives π(l#) = S(n), π(r#) = n, and n is a proper subterm of S(n). This satisfies the subterm criterion for the (only) cycle in the dependency graph, so the DP problem is finite, proving termination.

*Boundary:* Yes — dependency pairs and the subterm criterion are derived mechanically from the rules themselves; no extra axioms are used.

---

**(E) Direct descent measure μ**

*Success:* No, as stated the claim "every rewrite step strictly decreases μ" is false. μ is defined ignoring the first two arguments of F and the first argument of G entirely:
μ(F(x,y,n)) does not depend on x or y, and μ(G(a,b)) does not depend on a.
Hence a rewrite occurring **inside** the x, y, or a positions (e.g. reducing an inner redex F(a,b,Z)->a sitting inside the first argument of an outer F, or inside G's first slot) leaves μ completely unchanged — it does not strictly decrease. Also, μ is only defined for terms headed by F, G, or variables, leaving no account of how μ behaves under S/Z-headed contexts, so closure under arbitrary contexts (needed for a rewrite relation on general terms, not just root redexes) is never established. The "argument" only checks the two root patterns, not general compatibility, so as a complete proof it does not succeed.

*Boundary:* Even setting success aside, the method implicitly needs an unproven monotonicity/context-compatibility assumption (that local decrease at a redex implies global decrease of μ for the whole term) that is not derived from the rules and is not verified — so even its logical status relative to the boundary is questionable. In any case, it fails condition 1.
