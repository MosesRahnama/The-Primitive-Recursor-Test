| Method | Proves termination as stated? | Stays within the stated benchmark boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **(A) LPO, \(F > G > S > Z\)** | **Yes** | **Yes** | Rule 1 is oriented by the subterm property: \(F(x,y,Z) > x\). For rule 2, \(F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))\) since \(F>G\), the left side is greater than \(y\), and it is greater than \(F(x,y,n)\) by lexicographic comparison of the third arguments, \(S(n)>n\). |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation given** | **No, not as a direct polynomial-interpretation termination proof** | **Yes** | The displayed interpretation strictly orients both rules at the root: rule 1 gives \(x+1>x\), and rule 2 gives \(x+n+2>x+n+1\). However, \(F\) ignores its second argument and \(G\) ignores its first argument. Thus the induced strict comparison is not strictly monotone under arbitrary contexts. A rewrite inside an ignored argument need not decrease the interpretation of the enclosing term. Therefore this interpretation does not by itself define the context-closed well-founded reduction order required for a direct TRS termination proof. |
| **(C) KBO with all symbol weights 1** | **No** | **Yes** | KBO requires the variable-occurrence condition: every variable must occur at least as often on the left as on the right. In rule 2, \(y\) occurs once on the left but twice on the right: \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\). Hence KBO cannot orient this rule, independently of the listed uniform weights. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs plus subterm criterion, third argument of \(F\)** | **Yes** | **Yes** | The relevant dependency pair is \(F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n)\). Projection to the third argument gives \(S(n) \triangleright n\), a strict proper-subterm decrease. Rule 1 creates no recursive \(F\)-call. Thus the dependency-pair cycle is eliminated by the subterm criterion. |
| **(E) Direct third-argument measure \(\mu\)** | **No** | **Yes, as a syntactic proposal, but it is unsound** | The claimed strict decrease fails for substitutions and for rewriting in arbitrary contexts. For example, an instance of rule 1 is \(F(s,t,Z)\to s\). The left side has proposed measure \(1\), while \(s\) can be an arbitrarily complex term with positive measure. Also, a rewrite inside the first or second argument of an enclosing \(F\), or the first argument of an enclosing \(G\), may leave the proposed measure unchanged because those arguments are ignored. Thus not every rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\). |
