**System**
\[
\begin{aligned}
&F(x, y, Z) \to x \\
&F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y,\;F(x, y, n))
\end{aligned}
\]

The system is terminating: each \(F\)‑step either deletes the \(F\) (rule 1) or peels one \(S\) off the third argument and buries the recursive \(F\) inside the *second* argument of a \(G\).  Because the third argument has only finitely many \(S\)’s, every derivation ends.  The question is whether each **listed** method actually proves this and whether it respects the benchmark boundary (“no imported structural axioms or object‑level assumptions”).

---

### (A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence \(F > G > S > Z\)

1. **Does it succeed?** **Yes.**  
   - Rule 1: \(F(x,y,Z) >_{\text{lpo}} x\) because \(x\) is a variable occurring in the left‑hand side.  
   - Rule 2: \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\text{lpo}} G(y,F(x,y,n))\) because \(F>G\).  The two arguments of \(G\) are checked:  
     * \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\text{lpo}} y\) (variable in the lhs).  
     * \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\text{lpo}} F(x,y,n)\) because the arguments compare lexicographically: \((x,y,S(n)) >_{\text{lpo}}^{\text{lex}} (x,y,n)\) since \(S(n)>_{\text{lpo}} n\), and the lhs is larger than every argument of the inner \(F\).  
   Since both rules satisfy the order, LPO establishes termination.

2. **Within boundary?** **Yes.**  
   LPO is a purely syntactic simplification order.  The precedence is a proof‑search parameter, not an object‑level assumption about the meaning of the symbols.

---

### (B) Polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\)

\[
[F(x,y,n)]=x+n+1,\quad [S(n)]=n+1,\quad [G(a,b)]=b,\quad [Z]=0
\]

1. **Does it succeed?** **No.**  
   Although \([F(x,y,S(n))] = x+n+2 > x+n+1 = [G(y,F(x,y,n))]\) and \([F(x,y,Z)]=x+1 > x\), the interpretation of \(G\) is a projection onto its second argument.  It is **not strictly monotone** in its first argument.  Consequently the induced ordering is **not closed under contexts**: a rewrite inside the first argument of \(G\) does not decrease the measure.  For example
   \[
   G(F(x,y,Z),\,u) \;\to\; G(x,\,u)
   \]
   leaves the interpretation unchanged (\([u]\) on both sides).  Hence this is **not** a valid reduction order and does not prove termination.

2. **Within boundary?** **No.**  
   The method imports the natural numbers, addition, and the specific semantic reading “\(S\) is successor, \(Z\) is zero”.  These are object‑level assumptions not derivable from the two rewrite rules alone.

---

### (C) Knuth–Bendix Order (KBO) with uniform weights \(w(F)=w(G)=w(S)=w(Z)=1\)

1. **Does it succeed?** **No.**  
   KBO requires \(\#_x(\text{lhs})\ge \#_x(\text{rhs})\) for every variable \(x\).  In rule 2 the variable \(y\) occurs **once** on the left and **twice** on the right.  No choice of weights or precedence can repair this: substituting a heavy term for \(y\) makes the right‑hand side strictly heavier than the left, violating stability under substitution.  Thus KBO cannot orient rule 2.

2. **Within boundary?** **Yes.**  
   KBO is a purely syntactic order and does not import external axioms.

---

### (D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of \(F\)

1. **Does it succeed?** **Yes.**  
   The only defined symbol is \(F\).  The only dependency pair is
   \[
   F^\sharp(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\sharp(x,y,n).
   \]
   Projecting the tuple symbol \(F^\sharp\) to its third argument yields the comparison \(S(n) \rhd n\) (proper subterm).  Because \(n\) is a proper subterm of \(S(n)\), the subterm criterion applies and shows this DP set cannot give rise to an infinite chain.  The remaining DP set is empty, so termination follows.

2. **Within boundary?** **Yes.**  
   The subterm criterion uses only the generic, syntactic well‑foundedness of the proper‑subterm relation on first‑order terms.  No imported axioms about \(S\) or \(Z\) are needed.

---

### (E) Direct descent measure on the third argument

\[
\mu(F(x,y,n)) = \text{depth}_S(n)+1,\quad \mu(G(a,b))=\mu(b),\quad \mu(\text{variable})=0
\]

1. **Does it succeed?** **No.**  
   Because \(\mu(G(a,b))=\mu(b)\), the first argument of \(G\) is ignored.  A redex may lie inside that first argument (e.g. when the variable \(y\) is instantiated to an \(F\)‑term), and rewriting it does **not** decrease \(\mu\):
   \[
   \mu\bigl(G(F(x,y,Z),\,u)\bigr)=\mu(u)=\mu\bigl(G(x,u)\bigr).
   \]
   Therefore \(\mu\) is **not** a global termination measure for this TRS.

2. **Within boundary?** **No.**  
   The measure \(\text{depth}_S\) treats \(S\) and \(Z\) as the constructors of an inductive data type.  This imports an object‑level structural assumption (that \(S\)‑depth is well‑founded) that is not derivable from the rewrite rules alone.

---

### Methods satisfying **both** conditions

Only **(A)** and **(D)** prove termination of the system as given and do so without importing external structural axioms or object‑level assumptions.

| Method | Succeeds? | Within boundary? |
|--------|-----------|------------------|
| (A) LPO | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| (B) Polynomial interpretation | No | No |
| (C) KBO | No | Yes |
| (D) DP + subterm criterion | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| (E) Direct descent measure | No | No |

**Result:** **(A)** and **(D)**.
