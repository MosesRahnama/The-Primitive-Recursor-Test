### Analysis of each method

The TRS has signature symbols \(\{F, G, S, Z\}\) and the two given rules only. It is terminating (the only recursive calls peel a leading \(S\) from the third argument of \(F\)). The boundary condition is interpreted narrowly as stated: a technique lies outside only when it depends on arbitrary imported global structural choices (or object-level axioms) that are not fixed by the two rules themselves. Ordinary soundness of a well-founded order, a polynomial interpretation, or a dependency-pair processor does not by itself violate the boundary.

**(A) LPO with precedence \(F > G > S > Z\)**  
1. Succeeds. Both rules are strictly decreasing:  
   - \(F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} x\) holds because LPO contains the subterm relation.  
   - \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} G(y,F(x,y,n))\) holds because \(F > G\) and the three arguments of the left-hand side are \(\ge_{\mathrm{LPO}}\) the corresponding arguments of the right-hand side (the decisive comparison \(S(n) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} n\) is again the subterm relation).  
   Hence the LPO is a reduction order compatible with the TRS, so the TRS is SN.  
2. Stays inside the boundary. The precedence is an ordinary parameter of LPO chosen from the finite signature of the given rules; no external structural axiom is required.

**(B) The stated polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb{N}\)**  
1. Succeeds. For every assignment of natural numbers to the variables one has  
   - \([F(x,y,Z)] = x+1 > x = [x]\),  
   - \([F(x,y,S(n))] = x+n+2 > x+n+1 = [G(y,F(x,y,n))]\).  
   All interpretations are weakly monotonic, therefore the interpretation is a reduction order and the TRS is SN.  
2. Stays inside the boundary. Polynomial interpretations are a standard, self-contained method whose only data are the two rules; the concrete polynomials are simply a witness discovered from those rules.

**(C) KBO with all symbol weights equal to 1**  
1. Does not succeed. Already the second rule fails the weight condition of KBO:  
   \[
   |F(x,y,S(n))| = 5 < 6 = |G(y,F(x,y,n))|
   \]
   (variables receive weight 1). When the weight of the left-hand side is strictly smaller, no precedence can make the rule KBO-decreasing.  
2. (Boundary status is irrelevant once the method fails.)

**(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion, projection on the third argument of \(F\)**  
1. Succeeds. The only defined symbol is \(F\), so the only dependency pair is  
   \[
   F^\sharp(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\sharp(x,y,n).
   \]
   Projecting every \(F^\sharp\)-term onto its third argument yields a strict subterm relation on that pair. The subterm criterion therefore proves that the DP problem has no infinite chains, hence the original TRS is SN.  
2. Stays inside the boundary. Both the dependency-pair construction and the subterm criterion are purely syntactic processors that operate on the given rules alone.

**(E) The stated direct measure \(\mu\)**  
1. Does not succeed. The claimed strict decrease fails for redexes that do not occur in a measured position. Concrete counter-example:  
   \[
   F(F(x,y,Z),y,Z) \to F(x,y,Z)
   \]
   has \(\mu=1\) on both sides. (In addition the definition of \(\mu\) is incomplete for terms whose third argument is not built solely from \(S\) and \(Z\).) An incorrect decrease argument does not establish SN.  
2. (Boundary status is irrelevant once the method fails.)

### Methods that satisfy both requirements
Only (A), (B) and (D) both prove termination and remain inside the stated benchmark boundary.
