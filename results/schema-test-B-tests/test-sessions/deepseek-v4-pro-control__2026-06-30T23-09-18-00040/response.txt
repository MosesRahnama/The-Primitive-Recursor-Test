**Analysis of each method:**

**Method A (LPO with precedence F > G > S > Z):**  
- **Termination:** Succeeds. Rule 1: F(x,y,Z) > x because x is a proper subterm. Rule 2: F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n)) because F > G and the arguments y and F(x,y,n) are smaller; F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n) holds by lexicographic comparison of arguments (S(n) > n, as n is a proper subterm of S(n)).  
- **Boundary:** Stays within. LPO is a purely syntactic ordering on the term algebra itself; it does not import external semantic domains or object‑level properties of the symbols. Choosing a precedence is not importing structural axioms or object‑level assumptions.

**Method B (Polynomial interpretation over N):**  
- **Termination:** Succeeds. [F(x,y,Z)] = x+1 > x; [F(x,y,S(n))] = x+n+2 > x+n+1 = [G(y,F(x,y,n))].  
- **Boundary:** **Outside.** This method interprets S and Z as the natural number successor and zero, importing the whole arithmetic structure of N (an object‑level assumption not derivable from the given rewrite rules).

**Method C (KBO with uniform weights 1):**  
- **Termination:** Fails. For rule 2, weight(F(x,y,S(n))) = 5, weight(G(y,F(x,y,n))) = 6. KBO requires weight(LHS) ≥ weight(RHS) to orient a rule; 5 ≥ 6 is false, so the rule cannot be oriented.

**Method D (Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F):**  
- **Termination:** Fails. Projecting only F (or F#) to its third argument leaves the original rules unsuitably oriented (e.g., rule 1 becomes Z → x, which is not a weak subterm relation). No complete argument filtering is provided, and the method as stated does not yield a valid termination proof.

**Method E (Direct descent measure mu):**  
- **Termination:** Fails. The measure is not compositional; substituting a non‑variable term for x in rule 1 can produce mu(RHS) = mu(t) ≥ 1 when t is an F‑term, so the strict decrease mu(LHS)=1 > mu(RHS) is not guaranteed for all instances.

**Conclusion:**  
Only method **A** both succeeds in proving termination and stays within the original benchmark boundary.
