| Method | Proves termination? | Within benchmark boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **A. LPO** | **Yes** | **No** | For the recursive rule, \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} F(x,y,n)\), and precedence \(F>G\) then gives \(F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))\). The base rule follows from the subterm property. However, the stipulated global precedence—including relations not determined by the rules—is an imported structural choice. |
| **B. Polynomial interpretation** | **No** | **No** | Both rules decrease at the root, but \([G(a,b)]=b\) is not strictly monotone in its first argument. Thus a rewrite inside that argument need not decrease the interpretation; e.g. \(G(F(x,y,Z),z)\to G(x,z)\) has the same value \(z\). The interpretation is also an imported global algebraic assignment. |
| **C. KBO** | **No** | **No** | The recursive rule duplicates \(y\): it occurs once on the left and twice on the right, violating KBO’s variable condition. With uniform positive weights, the right side is also heavier. The global symbol-weight assignment is an imported structural choice. |
| **D. Dependency pairs** | **Yes** | **Yes** | The only dependency pair is \(F^\#(x,y,S(n))\to F^\#(x,y,n)\). Projection to argument 3 gives \(S(n)\mathrel{\triangleright}n\), so the subterm criterion eliminates the sole recursive dependency-pair component. This uses the recursive argument exposed directly by the given rule; dependency-pair soundness is ordinary metatheory, not an imported object-level assumption. |
| **E. Direct measure** | **No** | **Yes** | The claim that *every* rewrite step strictly decreases \(\mu\) is false under contexts. For example, \(F(F(x,y,Z),x,Z)\to F(x,x,Z)\), but both sides have outer measure \(1\). The proposed measure is nevertheless based only on the displayed term syntax and introduces no arbitrary ambient structure. |

Therefore, **only method D satisfies both conditions**.
