### **(A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence $F > G > S > Z$**

1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.** 
   * **Rule 1 ($F(x, y, Z) \to x$):** Since $x$ is a strict subterm of $F(x, y, Z)$, we have $F(x, y, Z) \succ_{lpo} x$.
   * **Rule 2 ($F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n))$):** Since $F > G$ in precedence, we compare $F(x, y, S(n))$ against the arguments of $G$, namely $y$ and $F(x, y, n)$:
     * $F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{lpo} y$ holds because $y$ is a subterm of the LHS.
     * $F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{lpo} F(x, y, n)$ is evaluated by lexicographic comparison of their arguments because their root symbols are identical ($F = F$). Comparing arguments left-to-right: the first arguments are equal ($x = x$), the second arguments are equal ($y = y$), and the third arguments satisfy $S(n) \succ_{lpo} n$ (by the subterm property).
   * Both rules are strictly oriented, so LPO successfully proves termination.

2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes.** LPO is a purely syntactic reduction order that relies only on the structure of terms and a finite precedence, without importing external algebraic axioms.

### **(B) Polynomial interpretation over $\mathbb{N}$**

1. **Does the method succeed?** **No.**

2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **N/A** (since it does not succeed).

### **(C) Knuth-Bendix Order (KBO) with uniform symbol weights**

1. **Does the method succeed?** **No.**

### **(D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of $F$**

1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.**
   * The only defined symbol is $F$. The only dependency pair is:
     $$F^\sharp(x, y, S(n)) \to F^\sharp(x, y, n)$$
   * Applying the subterm criterion with the projection $\pi(F^\sharp) = 3$ yields:
     $$\pi(F^\sharp(x, y, S(n))) = S(n) \quad \text{and} \quad \pi(F^\sharp(x, y, n)) = n$$
   * Since $S(n) \rhd n$ (strict subterm relation), the dependency pair strictly decreases. 
   * This allows us to delete the dependency pair, leaving an empty DP set, which successfully proves termination.

2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes.** The dependency pair framework with the subterm criterion is a standard, purely syntactic metatheoretic technique that does not rely on any external axioms or semantic assumptions.
