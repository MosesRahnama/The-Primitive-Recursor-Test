### Analysis of Each Method

#### (A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence \( F > G > S > Z \)
- **Termination Proof**: Fails. The second rule \( F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n)) \) is not decreasing under LPO with the given precedence, as no argument of \( F \) is ≥ the rhs \( G(y, F(x, y, n)) \) in the LPO order.
- **Boundary**: Even if it succeeded, LPO is a syntactic method and would likely stay within the boundary, but it fails regardless.

#### (B) Polynomial Interpretation over \( \mathbb{N} \)
- **Termination Proof**: Succeeds. The interpretations satisfy \( [F(x,y,Z)] = x+1 > x \) and \( [F(x,y,S(n))] = x+n+2 > x+n+1 = [G(y, F(x,y,n))] \).
- **Boundary**: Outside the boundary. It imports the natural numbers and their well-ordering as a global structural choice not fixed by the rewrite rules.

#### (C) Knuth-Bendix Order (KBO) with uniform symbol weights
- **Termination Proof**: Fails. The weights of both sides of rule 2 are equal (2), and with uniform weights and no specified precedence, the rule is not decreasing.
- **Boundary**: Not applicable (fails to prove termination).

#### (D) Dependency Pairs with Subterm Criterion, Projecting to the Third Argument of \( F \)
- **Termination Proof**: Succeeds. The dependency pairs \( F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y, F(x,y,n)) \) and \( F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F(x,y,n) \), when projected to the third argument of \( F \), satisfy the subterm criterion (i.e., the projected rhs is a subterm of the projected lhs).
- **Boundary**: Within the boundary. The method uses syntactic techniques (dependency pairs and subterm criterion) without importing external structural assumptions.

#### (E) Direct Descent Measure on the Third Argument
- **Termination Proof**: Succeeds. The measure \( \mu(F(x,y,n)) = \text{depth}_S(n) + 1 \) strictly decreases for each rule:  
  - Rule 1: \( \mu(F(x,y,Z)) = 1 > 0 = \mu(x) \).  
  - Rule 2: \( \mu(F(x,y,S(n))) = \text{depth}_S(n)+2 > \text{depth}_S(n)+1 = \mu(G(y, F(x,y,n))) \).
- **Boundary**: Within the boundary. The measure is defined syntactically from the term structure and does not require external assumptions.

### Conclusion
Methods **D** and **E** satisfy both conditions: they succeed in proving termination and stay within the original benchmark boundary. Method **B** succeeds but is outside the boundary. Methods **A** and **C** fail to prove termination.
