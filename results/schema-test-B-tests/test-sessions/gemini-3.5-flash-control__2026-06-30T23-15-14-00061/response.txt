An analysis of each of the five proposed proof methods for the given first-order term rewriting system (TRS) is presented below:

---

### **Method (A): Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence $F > G > S > Z$.**

1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.** 
   * For the first rule, $F(x, y, Z) \rightarrow x$, we have $F(x, y, Z) >_{\text{lpo}} x$ because $x$ is a proper subterm of the left-hand side (LHS).
   * For the second rule, $F(x, y, S(n)) \rightarrow G(y, F(x, y, n))$, since $F > G$ in the precedence, we need to show:
     1. $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{\text{lpo}} y$ (holds because $y$ is a subterm of the LHS).
     2. $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{\text{lpo}} F(x, y, n)$. Under the standard left-to-right lexicographic status, we compare the arguments of $F$ from left to right: the first two arguments are identical ($x = x$ and $y = y$), and for the third argument, we have $S(n) >_{\text{lpo}} n$ by the subterm property. Thus, the inequality holds.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes.** LPO is a standard, purely syntactic termination order that operates directly on the rules without importing external structural axioms or domain assumptions.

---

### **Method (B): Polynomial interpretation over $\mathbb{N}$**

1. **Does the method succeed?** **No.** 
   * A valid polynomial interpretation must be strictly monotonic in all arguments of any function symbol to be compatible with rewriting. 
   * In this proposed interpretation, $[G(a,b)] = b$. This is not strictly monotonic in its first argument $a$ (the coefficient of $a$ is $0$). Consequently, a rewrite step occurring inside the first argument of $G$—such as $G(F(x,y,Z), b) \rightarrow G(x, b)$—would yield $[LHS] = b$ and $[RHS] = b$, failing to produce a strict decrease ($b \ngtr b$).
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes**, but the method fails to prove termination.

---

### **Method (C): Knuth-Bendix Order (KBO) with uniform symbol weights**

1. **Does the method succeed?** **No.** 
   * KBO requires that for every rewrite rule $l \rightarrow r$, the number of occurrences of any variable $x$ in $r$ must be less than or equal to its number of occurrences in $l$ (the variable condition).
   * For the rule $F(x, y, S(n)) \rightarrow G(y, F(x, y, n))$, the variable $y$ occurs once in the LHS but twice in the RHS. Because $|LHS|_y = 1 < 2 = |RHS|_y$, KBO cannot orient this rule.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes**, but the method fails to prove termination.

---

### **Method (D): Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of $F$.**

1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.** 
   * The only defined symbol (a symbol at the root of a LHS) is $F$. Thus, the only dependency pair generated from the rules is:
     $$F^\sharp(x, y, S(n)) \rightarrow F^\sharp(x, y, n)$$
   * Applying the subterm criterion by projecting $F^\sharp$ to its third argument ($\pi(F^\sharp) = 3$) yields the projection $S(n)$ for the LHS and $n$ for the RHS.
   * Since $S(n) \rhd n$ (the RHS projection is a proper subterm of the LHS projection), this dependency pair strictly decreases and can be removed, leaving an empty dependency graph. This successfully establishes termination.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes.** This is a syntactic metatheoretic analysis of the rewrite rules and does not import any external structural choices or domain axioms.

---

### **Method (E): Direct descent measure on the third argument**

1. **Does the method succeed?** **No.** 
   * For a termination proof via a reduction measure $\mu$ to succeed, every rewrite step $t \rightarrow t'$ must satisfy $\mu(t) > \mu(t')$.
   * Under the proposed measure, rewriting can occur within contexts that are ignored by the measure. For example, rewriting inside the first argument of $G$, such as $G(F(x, y, S(n)), b) \rightarrow G(G(y, F(x,y,n)), b)$, results in $\mu(LHS) = \mu(b)$ and $\mu(RHS) = \mu(b)$, failing to produce a strict decrease.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes**, but the method fails to prove termination.

---

### **Conclusion**

The methods that satisfy **both** conditions (successfully prove termination and stay within the benchmark boundary) are:

* **(A)** Lexicographic Path Order (LPO)
* **(D)** Dependency pairs with subterm criterion
