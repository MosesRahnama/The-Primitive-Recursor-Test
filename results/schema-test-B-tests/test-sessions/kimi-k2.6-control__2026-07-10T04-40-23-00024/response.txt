**Summary for each method**

| Method | 1. Succeeds as stated? | 2. Stays inside the benchmark boundary? |
|--------|------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| **(A) LPO** (precedence F > G > S > Z) | **Yes.** Rule 1 is oriented because a term is LPO‑greater than any variable it contains. Rule 2 is oriented because F > G and the left‑hand side is greater than each argument of the right‑hand side; in particular F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n) holds by the lexicographic comparison of the third arguments (S(n) > n). LPO is a simplification order, hence proving termination. | **Yes.** It uses only the syntactic precedence on the signature fixed by the rules. |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation** ([F]=x+n+1, [S]=n+1, [G]=b, [Z]=0) | **No.** The polynomial for F is **not strictly monotone** in its second argument (the coefficient of y is 0). Consequently the induced ordering is not closed under contexts. For example, the valid step **F(x, F(a,b,Z), Z) → F(x, a, Z)** leaves the interpretation unchanged (both sides interpret to x + 1). | **Yes.** Polynomial interpretations are standard metatheoretic machinery; they do not import external object‑level axioms about the symbols. |
| **(C) KBO** (uniform weight 1 for all symbols) | **No.** With uniform weights the two sides of rule 2 have equal weight (2 function symbols each). KBO also requires a precedence when weights are equal, and none is supplied. Moreover, rule 2 violates the KBO variable‑occurrence condition: y occurs once on the left but twice on the right. Hence KBO cannot orient the second rule. | **Yes.** KBO is a purely syntactic order. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion** (project to 3rd arg of F) | **Yes.** The only dependency pair arising from the system is **F♯(x,y,S(n)) → F♯(x,y,n)**. Projecting to the third argument gives left‑arg = S(n) and right‑arg = n. Since n is a proper subterm of S(n), the subterm criterion applies and the DP‑problem is finite. | **Yes.** Dependency pairs and the subterm criterion are standard, purely syntactic termination techniques. |
| **(E) Direct descent measure** (μ(F(x,y,n)) = depth_S(n)+1, μ(G(a,b)) = μ(b), μ(x) = 0) | **No.** The measure ignores the first two arguments of F and the first argument of G. Therefore it is **not monotone under contexts**. Valid counter‑steps include:<br>• **F(F(a,b,Z), c, Z) → F(a, c, Z)** (outer μ stays 1),<br>• **G(F(a,b,Z), c) → G(a, c)** (outer μ stays μ(c)).<br>Because μ does not decrease for these steps, it cannot establish termination. The definition is also incomplete for top‑most S‑ or Z‑contexts. | **Yes.** It attempts a purely syntactic count based on the given signature; it imports no external algebraic theory. |

**Methods that satisfy both conditions**

Only **(A)** and **(D)** succeed in proving termination while remaining within the original benchmark boundary.
