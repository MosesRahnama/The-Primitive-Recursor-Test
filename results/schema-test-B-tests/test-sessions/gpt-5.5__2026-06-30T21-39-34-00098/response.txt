| Method | Proves termination as stated? | Stays within benchmark boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **A. LPO, precedence `F > G > S > Z`** | **Yes** | **Yes** | Both rules are oriented by LPO. Rule 1 follows by subterm property: `F(x,y,Z) > x`. For rule 2, since `F > G`, it suffices that `F(x,y,S(n))` is greater than both arguments of `G`; it is greater than `y` by subterm property and greater than `F(x,y,n)` because the third argument decreases from `S(n)` to `n`. |
| **B. Polynomial interpretation** | **No** | **Yes** | At the root, the rules strictly decrease: `x+1 > x` and `x+n+2 > x+n+1`. But the interpretation is not strictly monotone/context-compatible: `G(a,b)=b` ignores its first argument, and `F` ignores `y`. A rewrite inside an ignored argument may leave the interpretation unchanged, e.g. `G(F(Z,Z,Z), Z) -> G(Z,Z)` has value `0 -> 0`. Thus this is not a valid standalone polynomial-interpretation termination proof for full rewriting. |
| **C. KBO with uniform weights** | **No** | **Yes** | The second rule cannot be oriented. The variable `y` occurs once on the left but twice on the right, violating the KBO variable condition. Also with uniform positive weights the right-hand side is heavier because of the duplicated `y`. |
| **D. Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projection to third argument of `F`** | **Yes** | **Yes** | The only relevant dependency pair is `F#(x,y,S(n)) -> F#(x,y,n)`. Projecting to the third argument gives `S(n) -> n`, a strict subterm decrease. The SCC is eliminated by the subterm criterion, proving termination. |
| **E. Direct descent measure on third argument** | **No** | **No, if used as a claimed proof of SN** | The measure is not stable under substitutions or contexts. For example, rule 1 can increase the measure under substitution: `F(F(Z,Z,S(Z)), Z, Z) -> F(Z,Z,S(Z))`, giving `mu` roughly `1 -> 2`. Also reductions inside ignored arguments may leave `mu` unchanged. To make the argument work one would have to assume, for example, root-only reduction or that variables/ignored arguments are inert, which is not derivable from the TRS. |

Therefore, the methods satisfying both conditions are:

**A and D.**
