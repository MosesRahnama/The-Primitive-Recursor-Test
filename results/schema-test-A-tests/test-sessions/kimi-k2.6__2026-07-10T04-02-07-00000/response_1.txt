**Assessment: Yes.** Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone; no imported induction schema or structural axioms about `S`/`Z` are needed.

**Reasoning:**  
The two rules are:

1. `F(x, y, Z) → x`  
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))`

Both are strictly oriented by a standard recursive path ordering (RPO) with precedence `F ≻ G ≻ S ≻ Z` (and lexicographic status for `F`):

- Rule 1: `F(x, y, Z)` is greater than its proper subterm `x`.  
- Rule 2: Because `F ≻ G`, the left-hand side is greater than the right-hand side provided it is greater than each argument of `G`. The LHS is greater than `y` (proper subterm), and greater than the recursive call `F(x, y, n)` because the heads are equal and the third arguments satisfy `S(n) ≻ n` by the subterm property.

Since RPO is a well-founded simplification ordering, it guarantees that *every* reduction sequence terminates. The duplication of `y` in rule 2 is harmless: `y` is copied into the first argument of `G` and the second argument of the recursive `F`, but it is **never** placed into the third, decreasing argument position. Consequently, the structural decrease on the third argument (stripping `S`) dominates any growth caused by copying.

Equivalently, the only dependency pair is `F(x, y, S(n)) → F(x, y, n)`, which is strictly decreasing under the polynomial interpretation  
`[F(x,y,n)] = [x] + ([y]+1)([n]+1)`, `[G(u,v)] = [u]+[v]+1`, `[S(n)] = [n]+1`, `[Z] = 0`, while both rules are weakly (in fact, for rule 1 strictly) oriented.  

Thus the system is strongly normalizable by pure syntactic means, without assuming that the third position ranges only over “natural numbers” or importing any object-level induction principle.
