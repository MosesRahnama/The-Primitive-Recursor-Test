Yes.

This TRS is strongly normalizing from the rules alone.

Why:
- The only defined symbols are `F` and `G`.
- The rewrite rules are:
  - `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`
- Rewriting can only happen at the root of a term headed by `F` whose third argument is either `Z` or `S(...)`.
- In the recursive rule, the recursive call is `F(x, y, n)`, where the third argument is a strict subterm of `S(n)`.

A simple well-founded measure proves termination:
- Measure an `F`-redex by the number of `S` constructors in its third argument.
- Rule 1 removes the redex completely.
- Rule 2 replaces `F(x,y,S(n))` by a term containing `F(x,y,n)`, so that measure strictly decreases by 1.
- `G` has no rewrite rules, so it cannot create new redexes except by containing the smaller recursive `F`.

Thus there cannot be an infinite rewrite sequence: every `F` computation descends on the third argument until it reaches `Z`, and then stops. So strong normalization is derivable directly from the presented system.
