Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

For example, use a recursive path ordering with:

- precedence \(F > G\) (with arbitrary precedence for \(S,Z\)),
- lexicographic argument status for \(F\).

Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) decreases by the subterm property.
- For \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\), the outer \(F\) is greater than \(G\), and the recursive call \(F(x,y,n)\) is smaller than \(F(x,y,S(n))\) because the third argument decreases from \(S(n)\) to \(n\).

Thus every rule strictly decreases a well-founded ordering, establishing termination—and hence strong normalization—for the presented first-order rewrite system, without importing additional object-level axioms.
