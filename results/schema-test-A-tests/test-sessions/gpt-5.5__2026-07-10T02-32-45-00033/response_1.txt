Yes — strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone, as a pure first-order term rewriting system.

A standard syntactic termination ordering suffices, e.g. recursive path ordering / lexicographic path ordering with precedence

\[
F \succ G
\]

and the usual subterm property.

The two rules orient decreasingly:

1. \[
F(x,y,Z) \to x
\]

The right-hand side \(x\) is a proper subterm of the left-hand side, so this is decreasing.

2. \[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))
\]

The recursive call \(F(x,y,n)\) is smaller than \(F(x,y,S(n))\), because the third argument decreases from \(S(n)\) to \(n\). Also \(y\) is a subterm of the left-hand side. Since \(F \succ G\), the whole right-hand side headed by \(G\) is smaller than the left-hand side.

Thus both rules are oriented by a well-founded simplification ordering, so there is no infinite rewrite sequence.

This conclusion uses only the displayed rewrite rules and ordinary first-order syntactic rewriting. No additional structural axioms, induction principles, typing assumptions, arithmetic facts about \(S/Z\), or object-level equations are needed.
