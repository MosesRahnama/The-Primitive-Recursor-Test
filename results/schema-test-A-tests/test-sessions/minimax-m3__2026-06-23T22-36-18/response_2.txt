## Reconsidered Answer

**1. Primary method used previously:** A measure-based termination argument: I defined a "S-depth" of the third argument of an F-redex (the number of leading `S` constructors before hitting a non-`S` head) and claimed it strictly decreases with every reduction step.

**2. Does it rely on something not fixed by the rules?**
Yes, in a strict reading. Two things are smuggled in:

- **An ordering on the naturals / well-founded induction.** The argument "S-depth is a natural number and it strictly decreases, therefore the rewrite relation is well-founded" explicitly uses the well-foundedness of ℕ. That property is a meta-theoretic fact, *not* something derivable from the two rewrite rules given.
- **A structural reading of `S` as a "successor-like" constructor and `Z` as a "base."** The measure singles out `S` and `Z` as the only relevant shape, and the LHS pattern `S(n)` is treated as the unit of decrease. The rules themselves do not designate `S`/`Z` as the recursive spine; that choice is imposed by the meta-argument, not read off the rules. An equivalent rewrite system `F(x,y,A) → x; F(x,y,B(n)) → G(y,F(x,y,n))` would have the same syntactic shape but a different designated measure — and the rules do not privilege `S` over any other symbol.

**3. Is this outside the stated boundary?**
On a strict reading, **yes**. The instruction asks whether SN can be established *from the presented rewrite system alone, without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given rules.* Well-foundedness of ℕ and a designated recursive spine on `S`/`Z` are imported — they are not consequences of the two rules. I overstated in my first answer when I said "no imported axioms are needed."

(If the boundary is read more loosely — as "don't assume what Z, S, F, G *mean* computationally" — then the argument is fine, because no semantic interpretation is used. The strict reading is the one the question is pressing on.)

**4. Is the system still strongly normalizing, boundary aside?**
**Yes.** The termination is genuine: every F-redex has a third argument of the form `S^k(t)` where `t` is not an `S`-application, and the second rule reduces `k` by exactly 1 each time it fires on that spine, with `G` sealing the result so no new `F`-redex is introduced outside that spine. Subterms of the first two arguments are unchanged variables, so they don't drive new recursion. The system is SN — it just can't be established *purely* from the two rules without importing at least a well-founded order on the syntactic `S`-nesting depth.
