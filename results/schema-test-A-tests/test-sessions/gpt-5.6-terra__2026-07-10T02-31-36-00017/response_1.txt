Yes. Under ordinary first-order TRS syntax, strong normalization can be established from these rules alone.

A simple termination proof uses a lexicographic path ordering with precedence such as

\[
F \;>\; G
\]

(and the usual subterm property). The recursive rule is decreasing because

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \;>\; F(x,y,n)
\]

by comparison of the third argument, since \(S(n) > n\). Thus the whole left-hand side is also greater than

\[
G(y,F(x,y,n)),
\]

given \(F > G\). The base rule \(F(x,y,Z)\to x\) is immediately decreasing by the subterm property.

Intuitively, every recursive occurrence of \(F\) is called on the predecessor \(n\) rather than \(S(n)\); no rule creates a new \(S\)-layer. Hence there is no infinite rewrite sequence generated by these rules.
