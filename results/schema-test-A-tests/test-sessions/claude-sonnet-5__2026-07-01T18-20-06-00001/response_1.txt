## Assessment: **Yes**, strong normalization can be established directly from the rules shown.

### Reasoning

The system consists of exactly two rules:

```
F(x, y, Z)    -> x
F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))
```

Key observations:

1. **`G` is uninterpreted.** There is no rule with `G` as the root of the left-hand side. Hence `G` behaves purely as a constructor: it can never trigger further rewriting on its own, and no rule can ever *reintroduce* an `F`-redex "underneath" a `G` that wasn't already present in a subterm being rewritten.

2. **The only recursive rule strictly decreases a subterm.** Rule 2 replaces `F(x, y, S(n))` by a term containing `F(x, y, n)` — note that `n` is a **proper subterm** of `S(n)`. No rule ever produces a term where the third argument of some `F`-instance grows (there is no rule creating `S(...)` from smaller data, nor any duplication of the successor argument that could be re-expanded).

3. **Termination follows by a simple structural/recursive argument:**
   - Base case: `F(x,y,Z) -> x` is already terminal (rewrites to a term with strictly fewer symbols and no `F` at that position).
   - Inductive step: assume all instances of `F(x,y,n)` are strongly normalizing (for the given `n`). Then `F(x,y,S(n))` reduces only to `G(y, F(x,y,n))`; further reduction can only occur inside `y` or inside the `F(x,y,n))` subterm, both of which are strongly normalizing by hypothesis (rewriting is a congruence — it only occurs at subterm positions here, since there is no other applicable rule at the root).
   - This is exactly a proof by induction on the structure of the third argument (equivalently, a multiset/subterm-based path ordering with precedence `F > G` and `S` treated monotonically), and it uses **nothing beyond the two rules given** — no extra axioms about `Z`, `S`, or `G` are needed, since none are used: `Z`, `S`, `G` are just free constructors here.

Thus the system is trivially and directly seen to be strongly normalizing using only:
- the shape of the two rules,
- the general (rule-independent, purely structural) fact that rewriting is compatible with subterm structure (congruence of the rewrite relation), which is part of the definition of first-order term rewriting itself, not an additional imported axiom.

No termination technique requiring external assumptions (e.g., semantic labeling with imported models, or extra ordering axioms not derivable from the signature) is needed. A basic recursive path order / structural induction argument, fully justified by the two rules and the definition of rewriting, suffices.

**Conclusion:** Strong normalization is established directly and elementarily from the presented rewrite system alone.
