Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

For example, orient the rules with a lexicographic path ordering in which `F` is greater than `G`, and `F` compares its arguments lexicographically:

- `F(x,y,Z) > x`
- `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)` because `S(n) > n`
- Consequently, `F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))`

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded syntactic ordering, including reductions occurring inside arbitrary contexts and after arbitrary substitutions. No constructor-disjointness, injectivity, or other object-level axioms for `Z` and `S` are required.
