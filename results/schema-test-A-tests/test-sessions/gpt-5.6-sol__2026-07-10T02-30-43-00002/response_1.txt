**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone.

A lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules by choosing \(F > G\) and giving \(F\) lexicographic status with its third argument decisive:

- \(F(x,y,Z) > x\) by the subterm property.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))\), because:
  - the left side is greater than \(y\) by the subterm property;
  - \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\), since \(S(n) > n\) by the subterm property;
  - and \(F > G\).

This ordering is well-founded and stable under substitutions and contexts, so every rewrite step strictly decreases it. Hence no infinite rewrite sequence exists, without requiring any additional axioms or assumptions.
