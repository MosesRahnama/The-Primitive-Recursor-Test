**Yes.** Strong normalization follows syntactically from the rewrite rules alone.

A recursive path ordering can orient both rules by choosing \(F \succ G\) and giving \(F\) lexicographic status:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\) by the subterm property.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n)\), since \(S(n) \succ n\) by the subterm property.
- Therefore \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ G(y,F(x,y,n))\), using \(F \succ G\) and the fact that the left side also dominates \(y\).

This ordering is well-founded and stable under substitutions and contexts, so every rewrite step strictly decreases it. No assumptions that terms are “natural numbers,” nor axioms about \(Z\) or \(S\), are needed; they are treated purely as syntactic function symbols.
