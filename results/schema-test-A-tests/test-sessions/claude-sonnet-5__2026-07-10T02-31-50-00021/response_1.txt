## Assessment: **Yes, strong normalization can be established directly from the given rules.**

### Reasoning

The system has only two rules:

```
F(x, y, Z)    -> x
F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))
```

**Key observations:**

1. **No rules for `G`.** The symbol `G` is a "pure constructor" from the point of view of this TRS — it never triggers a reduction at its own root. Any reduction happening inside a `G(t1, t2)` term must occur strictly within `t1` or `t2`.

2. **`F`'s reduction is structurally decreasing in its third argument.** The only two rules for `F` are keyed on the two constructors of the (implicit) natural number structure, `Z` and `S(n)`:
   - `F(x,y,Z)` fires the base case, producing `x` (no recursive call, no growth).
   - `F(x,y,S(n))` fires the step case, replacing the third argument `S(n)` by the strictly smaller subterm `n`. The number of `S`-constructors in the third argument strictly decreases with each `F`-rewrite step.

3. **No mechanism for duplication or growth of the "measure".** The first two arguments `x, y` are carried along unchanged (copied, not modified) and never interact with the third argument's constructor structure. There is no rule that could re-embed a larger term back into the third argument position — the third argument only ever shrinks by peeling one `S`.

4. **Termination argument is a simple structural induction.** Since:
   - every top-level `F`-redex strictly decreases the size (number of `S`s) of its third argument,
   - `G` has no rules and thus contributes no reduction steps at its own root,
   
   any reduction sequence on a term `t` can only consist of finitely many `F`-steps (bounded by the number of `S`'s in the relevant subterm at the start, since this number never increases) interleaved with reductions in subterms, which by structural induction on term size are themselves finite.

This is exactly the classical pattern of a **primitive-recursive definition scheme** (recursion on a unary/successor-style argument), whose termination is provable by induction on the term structure of the recursion argument, using nothing beyond what's stated in the rules — no additional axioms (like extensionality, external well-founded orders, or imported combinator theory) are needed.

### Conclusion
The termination proof is a direct, self-contained structural/size-decrease argument based solely on the two rewrite rules: the third argument to `F` strictly decreases (in constructor size) at every `F`-step, and `G` is inert with respect to rewriting. Hence strong normalization follows straightforwardly from the presented rules alone.
