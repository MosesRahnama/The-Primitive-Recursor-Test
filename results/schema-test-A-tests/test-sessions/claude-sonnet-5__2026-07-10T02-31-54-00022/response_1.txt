## Reasoning

The system consists of exactly two rules over the signature `{F/3, G/2, S/1, Z/0}`:

```
F(x, y, Z)    -> x
F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))
```

This is a textbook primitive-recursion scheme, and it falls into a class of systems provably terminating by simplification orderings — no semantic/model-theoretic input beyond the syntax of the rules is required.

**Rule 1** is trivially decreasing under any ordering satisfying the subterm property: the right-hand side `x` is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.

**Rule 2** can be oriented using the recursive path ordering (RPO) with the precedence `F > G`:

- Since `F ≻ G`, to show `F(x,y,S(n)) ≻ G(y, F(x,y,n))` it suffices to show the left side dominates *each* argument of the right side:
  - `F(x,y,S(n)) ≻ y`: holds by the subterm property (y is an immediate argument of the LHS).
  - `F(x,y,S(n)) ≻ F(x,y,n)`: same head symbol `F`, so compare argument tuples `(x,y,S(n))` vs `(x,y,n))`; the first two components are equal, and `S(n) ≻ n` by the subterm property. This gives the needed strict decrease under the standard (lexicographic or multiset) extension used in RPO.

Both conditions hold, so RPO with `F > G` orients rule 2 as well.

Since RPO is a well-founded order on **all** terms over the signature (not just those arising in some fixed derivation), and both rules are strictly decreasing under it, **every** rewrite step strictly decreases the RPO measure. This yields strong normalization for the whole system, for arbitrary terms (including terms where `x`, `y`, `n` are instantiated with arbitrary — even F/G-containing — subterms), because RPO membership is closed under substitution and monotonic under contexts.

No side conditions, no imported structural assumptions (e.g., no need for typing, no extra confluence assumptions, no external well-ordering on naturals) are needed beyond the syntactic shape of the two rules: the termination proof is fully internal to the given TRS.

**Conclusion:** SN follows directly and straightforwardly from the rules alone via a standard simplification ordering (RPO with `F ≻ G`); the system is a canonical example of a terminating primitive-recursive TRS.
