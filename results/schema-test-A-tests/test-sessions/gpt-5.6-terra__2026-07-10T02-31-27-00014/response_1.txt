Yes. Strong normalization can be established from these rewrite rules alone, viewed as an ordinary first-order TRS over finite terms.

A purely syntactic termination ordering suffices. For example, use a lexicographic recursive path ordering with \(F \succ G\). Then:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) decreases because \(x\) is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
- For
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)),
  \]
  the left-hand side is greater than \(F(x,y,n)\), since its third argument \(S(n)\) is greater than \(n\). It is also greater than \(y\). Since \(F \succ G\), it is therefore greater than \(G(y,F(x,y,n))\).

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded syntactic ordering, so there can be no infinite rewrite sequence.

No assumptions about naturals, constructor freeness, induction principles, or the intended meanings of `Z` and `S` are required. The result follows from the rule shapes themselves.
