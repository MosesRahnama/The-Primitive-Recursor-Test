Yes — under ordinary first-order rewriting on finite terms, this TRS is strongly normalizing from the rules alone.

Brief reason: although the second rule duplicates `y`, the recursion on the third argument is still well-founded, and this can be made precise with a polynomial interpretation over `ℕ`, for example:

- `⟦Z⟧ = 0`
- `⟦S(n)⟧ = ⟦n⟧ + 1`
- `⟦G(a,b)⟧ = ⟦a⟧ + ⟦b⟧`
- `⟦F(x,y,n)⟧ = ⟦x⟧ + (⟦n⟧+1)(⟦y⟧+1)`

Then each rule strictly decreases:

1. `F(x,y,Z) -> x`  
   gives  
   `⟦F(x,y,Z)⟧ = ⟦x⟧ + ⟦y⟧ + 1 > ⟦x⟧`.

2. `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y, F(x,y,n))`  
   gives  
   `⟦F(x,y,S(n))⟧ = ⟦x⟧ + (⟦n⟧+2)(⟦y⟧+1)`  
   while  
   `⟦G(y,F(x,y,n))⟧ = ⟦y⟧ + ⟦x⟧ + (⟦n⟧+1)(⟦y⟧+1)`,  
   and the left side exceeds the right by `1`.

So every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded measure, hence there can be no infinite rewrite sequence. No extra structural axioms are needed.
