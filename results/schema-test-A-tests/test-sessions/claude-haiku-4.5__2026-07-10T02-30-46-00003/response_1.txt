# Assessment: Strong Normalization Cannot Be Established

## Raw Answer
**No**, strong normalization cannot be established from this rewrite system alone.

## Reasoning

1. **The system as presented is terminating but not strongly normalizing** because there is no guarantee that *all* reduction sequences terminate—only that *some* do.

2. **Critical observation**: The rules are:
   - `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
   - `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`

   Notice that `G` appears in the consequent of the second rule but has **no reduction rules defined**. This means:
   - `G` is a "black hole"—terms containing `G` cannot be reduced further
   - A term like `G(y, F(x, y, n))` will reduce only as far as the inner `F(x, y, n)` can reduce

3. **The problem for strong normalization**: Without reduction rules for `G`, or without external axioms ensuring that the recursive depth of `F` is bounded by the structure of `n`, we cannot prove that every reduction sequence terminates. An external proof of strong normalization would require:
   - A measure/weight function (e.g., lexicographic ordering on term structure)
   - Proof that `G` doesn't introduce non-terminating cycles
   - Structural properties of `S(n)` and `Z` (which are Peano numerals)

4. **What we can say**: The system is *weakly normalizing* (every term has at least one normal form), but establishing *strong normalization* requires tools beyond the rewrite rules themselves—typically a proof-theoretic argument or termination checker.
