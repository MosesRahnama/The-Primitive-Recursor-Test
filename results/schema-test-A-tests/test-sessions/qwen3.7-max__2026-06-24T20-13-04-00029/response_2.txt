**1. Primary method:** Polynomial interpretation over natural numbers, assigning specific polynomials to each symbol and showing strict decrease.

**2. External dependencies:** Yes. The domain (ℕ), the specific polynomial for each symbol, and the well-foundedness of ℕ are all choices not fixed by the rewrite rules.

**3. Boundary compliance:** The original instruction prohibits "structural axioms or object-level assumptions." A polynomial interpretation is a meta-level proof technique—neither a structural axiom (e.g., constructor injectivity, induction schema) nor an object-level assumption (e.g., additional equations). So under the stated boundary, it is compliant. That said, if the boundary is read to forbid *any* external proof structure, it would fail. Notably, a purely syntactic proof exists: the **multiset path ordering (MPO)** with precedence `F > G` directly orients both rules without any semantic interpretation, using only the subterm structure visible in the rules themselves.

**4. Strong normalization:** Yes. The system is strongly normalizing. This is confirmed by MPO: in Rule 2, `F(x, y, S(n))` dominates `G(y, F(x, y, n))` because `F > G` and the multiset of arguments `{x, y, S(n)}` strictly dominates `{x, y, n}` under the subterm ordering. No infinite chains are possible.
