### Assessment
**Yes**, strong normalization (SN) of the given TRS can be established directly from the rewrite rules alone.

### Reasoning
The signature induced by the rules consists of the ternary symbol \(F\), the binary symbol \(G\), the unary symbol \(S\) and the constant \(Z\). There are no other rules, no equations, and no binding constructs. Consequently every redex is an instance of one of the two displayed rules, and every reduction step is an application of one of those rules (possibly in a context, and under arbitrary instantiation of the variables \(x,y,n\)).

SN follows by a simple interpretation into the well-founded set of natural numbers. Define a map \(|{\cdot}|\) on terms by
\[
\begin{align*}
|x| &= 0 &&\text{(for every variable \(x\))},\\
|Z| &= 0,\\
|S(t)| &= |t|+1,\\
|G(u,v)| &= |u|+|v|,\\
|F(a,b,c)| &= |a|+(1+|b|)\cdot|c|+1.
\end{align*}
\]
(The interpretation is strictly monotone in every argument.) Both rules are strictly decreasing under this map:
- \(|F(x,y,Z)|=|x|+1>|x|\),
- \(|F(x,y,S(n))|=|x|+(1+|y|)(|n|+1)+1 = |G(y,F(x,y,n))|+1\).

Hence every reduction step decreases the natural-number measure of the whole term. No infinite reduction sequence can exist.

The argument uses nothing beyond the two given rules and the finiteness of terms; it does not rely on any object-level induction principle, freeness axioms for \(S\) and \(Z\), or any other structural assumption that is not already implicit in the pure first-order TRS. (The same conclusion can be reached by a multiset path order after a suitable argument filtering, or by the dependency-pair method; all of these techniques again work from the rules alone.)

Thus SN is an intrinsic property of the presented rewrite system.
