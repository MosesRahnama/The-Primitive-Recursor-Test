Yes — strong normalization/termination can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.

Reasoning: the only rewrite rules are for terms headed by `F`, and each rule strictly reduces with respect to a standard well-founded term ordering such as lexicographic path ordering.

Take a precedence such as:

```text
F > G
```

with `F` compared lexicographically in its arguments.

Then:

1. Base rule:

```text
F(x, y, Z) -> x
```

The right-hand side `x` is a proper subterm of the left-hand side, so this is decreasing.

2. Recursive rule:

```text
F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))
```

The outer head `F` on the left dominates `G` on the right. It remains to check that the left-hand side is greater than the arguments of `G`:

- `F(x, y, S(n)) > y` by the subterm property.
- `F(x, y, S(n)) > F(x, y, n)` because the first two arguments are equal and the third argument decreases from `S(n)` to `n`.

Thus both rules orient strictly downward in a well-founded simplification order. The duplication of `y` in the second rule may increase term size, but it does not create a cycle or defeat termination under such an ordering.

So, for the first-order TRS exactly as given, with no additional equations or object-level axioms, strong normalization is provable.
