**Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.**

### Reasoning

**Structure of the system:**

The two rules are:
1. `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`

**Key observations:**

1. **The third argument strictly decreases.** In rule 2, `F(x, y, S(n))` rewrites with the recursive call `F(x, y, n)`, where `n` is a strict subterm of `S(n)`. The third argument goes from `S(n)` to `n`, which is structurally smaller under the standard subterm ordering on natural number terms.

2. **`G` is not defined by any rule.** There are no rules with `G` on the left-hand side, so `G(...)` is in normal form once its arguments are in normal form. It introduces no further reductions.

3. **A simple lexicographic path order (LPO) or recursive path order (RPO) works.** Orient the rules with precedence `F > G` (or simply track the decreasing third argument). The third argument of `F` decreases structurally at each recursive step, and the base case `Z` has no further `F`-reductions.

4. **No non-terminating cycles are possible.** `F` only calls itself with a smaller third argument, and `G` has no rewrite rules, so no cycle can form.

**Conclusion:** The system is **strongly normalizing** by a straightforward structural induction on the third argument of `F`, derivable purely from the given rules without any additional axioms.
