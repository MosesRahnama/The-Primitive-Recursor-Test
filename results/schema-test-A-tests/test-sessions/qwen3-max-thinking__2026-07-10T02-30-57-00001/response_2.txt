1. **Primary method**: Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO) with an explicitly *chosen* precedence (`F > G`, `F > S`, `F > Z`), supplemented by a structural descent argument on the third argument of `F`.

2. **Reliance on external assumptions?** **Yes.**  
   - LPO requires selecting a precedence *not fixed or implied* by the rules. The rules alone do not determine symbol order; this is a meta-level choice.  
   - Both LPO and the structural argument depend on the **well-foundedness of the subterm relation** (or natural-number measures derived from term structure). This is a structural axiom of term algebras, *not derivable* from the rewrite rules themselves. The rules specify reductions but do not encode or prove well-foundedness of term structure.

3. **Outside the stated boundary?** **Yes.**  
   The instruction explicitly forbids importing "structural axioms [...] beyond what is derivable from the given rules." Since:  
   (a) precedence choice is external and undetermined by rules,  
   (b) well-foundedness of subterm relation is a foundational axiom of term algebras (not derivable from rule patterns),  
   using either element violates the boundary constraint. Termination *cannot* be established *solely* from syntactic rule inspection without invoking these external metatheoretic pillars.

4. **Is the system still strongly normalizing?** **Yes.**  
   Under standard first-order TRS semantics (finite terms, subterm relation well-founded), termination is evident:  
   - Only `F` redexes exist; `G`, `Z`, `S`, variables are inert.  
   - Each Rule 2 application replaces `F(..., S(n))` with `F(..., n)` where `n` is a strict subterm; Rule 1 eliminates `F`.  
   - No duplication introduces new reducible `F` spines; all reduction paths on the third-argument spine are finite.  
   Strong normalization holds *in practice*, but its *proof* inherently requires structural axioms excluded by the strict boundary condition.
