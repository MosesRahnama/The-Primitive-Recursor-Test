Yes.

This TRS is strongly normalizing from the rules alone.

Reasoning, briefly:
- The only recursive call appears in  
  `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`
- In that step, the third argument of `F` strictly decreases from `S(n)` to `n`.
- There are no rewrite rules for `G`, `Z`, or `S`, and no other rule can increase that third argument or recreate a larger `F`.
- So every reduction of an `F` term can only peel off finitely many `S` constructors until it reaches:
  `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
- After that, no further rewrite is forced by these rules except possibly inside subterms already present in `x` or `y`; but those subterms are themselves finite terms, and the same decreasing argument applies to any `F` occurrences inside them.

A simple measure is: count, over all subterms `F(a,b,m)`, the number of `S` constructors in the third argument `m`. Each rewrite step decreases this measure by exactly 1, so infinite reductions are impossible.

So strong normalization is provable directly from the given rewrite system, with no extra axioms needed.
