Yes — **strong normalization is provable from these rules alone**.

Reason (briefly): define a simple well-founded measure on terms headed by `F` using the third argument:

- Let `|Z| = 0`, `|S(t)| = |t| + 1`.
- For a redex `F(x,y,n)`, take measure `|n|`.

Now check rules:

1. `F(x, y, Z) -> x`  
   This removes the `F` redex entirely (strict decrease).

2. `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`  
   The recursive `F` call now has argument `n` instead of `S(n)`, so its measure strictly decreases by 1.

No rule rewrites `G`, `S`, `Z`, variables, or arbitrary terms, so you cannot increase this descent again. Hence no infinite rewrite chain exists: every `F`-reduction bottoms out at `Z` and terminates.

So the TRS is strongly normalizing without extra axioms.
