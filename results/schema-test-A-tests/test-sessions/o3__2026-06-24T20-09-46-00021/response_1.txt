Assessment: Yes – the system is strongly normalizing, and this fact can be shown with nothing more than the two rules themselves.

Sketch of the argument  
1  No redex is created at the root of a term headed by G, S or Z, so every rewrite step must contract a redex whose outer-most symbol is F.

2  Associate with every term the lexicographic pair  

   (a)  Σ over all F(u,v,w) occurring in the term of the number of leading S-symbols in w  
   (b)  the total number of F-nodes in the term.

   Call this pair the size of the term.

3  For rule 1 F(x,y,Z) → x  
   – the redex contributes 0 to component (a) and 1 to component (b);  
   – after contraction the whole redex disappears, so component (b) strictly decreases while component (a) is unchanged.  
   Hence the size strictly decreases.

4  For rule 2 F(x,y,S(n)) → G(y, F(x,y,n))  
   – the redex contributes at least 1 to component (a) (one leading S);  
   – after contraction exactly one S is removed and no additional S is introduced;  
   – the number of F-nodes is the same before and after.  
   Hence component (a) strictly decreases, so the size strictly decreases.

5  Because the size is an ordered pair of natural numbers that strictly decreases at every rewrite step, no infinite reduction sequence exists.

Therefore every term has only finite rewrite sequences – i.e. the rewrite system is terminating (strongly normalizing) on its own, with no need for extra structural axioms or external assumptions.
