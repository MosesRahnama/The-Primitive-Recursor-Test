Yes, strong normalization of this system can be established from the presented rewrite rules alone, without importing additional axioms. A termination proof using a polynomial interpretation over the natural numbers works: assign

- \([Z] = 0\),
- \([S(n)] = n+1\),
- \([F(x,y,z)] = x + z + 1\),
- \([G(y,u)] = u\).

Then for any variable assignment with natural numbers:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\): left side interprets as \(x+1\), right as \(x\), so strictly decreases.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y, F(x,y,n))\): left interprets as \(x+(n+1)+1 = x+n+2\), right as \([F(x,y,n)] = x+n+1\), also strictly decreases.

Since the interpretation is monotone in all arguments (constant in unused arguments is fine) and maps to the well‑founded natural numbers, every rewrite step reduces the interpretation, guaranteeing termination. No object‑level or structural axioms beyond the rules themselves are needed.
