Yes. Under standard unrestricted first-order rewriting, strong normalization can be established from these rules alone.

For example, use a recursive path ordering with:

- precedence \(F > G\),
- lexicographic status for \(F\).

The first rule decreases because \(F(x,y,Z)\) is greater than its subterm \(x\). For the second rule,

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \;>\; G(y,F(x,y,n)),
\]

since \(F>G\), and the recursive \(F\)-term is smaller lexicographically: its first two arguments are unchanged, while \(S(n)>n\) by the subterm property.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded ordering. This argument is purely syntactic; it does not require treating \(Z\) and \(S\) as natural-number axioms or importing any additional object-level assumptions.
