Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

For example, orient the rules using a lexicographic path ordering with precedence \(F > G\) (the ordering of \(S\) and \(Z\) is otherwise immaterial):

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) decreases because \(x\) is a proper subterm.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\) decreases because \(F > G\), and the recursive \(F\)-argument has \(n\) in place of \(S(n)\); since \(S(n) > n\) by the subterm property, the recursive call is smaller.

The rules are also stable under rewriting in arbitrary contexts and substitutions. Hence the presented TRS is terminating, i.e. strongly normalizing, without requiring additional structural axioms or object-level assumptions.
