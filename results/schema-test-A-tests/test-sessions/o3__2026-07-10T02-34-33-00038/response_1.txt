Yes – the system is strongly normalising, and the fact can be proved from the two rules themselves without invoking any extra axioms.

Reasoning (sketch)

1.  The only redexes are headed by F.  
    •  Rule 1   F(x,y,Z)          → x  
    •  Rule 2   F(x,y,S(n))  → G(y, F(x,y,n))

2.  Put a simple measure on a ground term t:

       μ(t) = total number of S-symbols occurring in the third
               argument of every F that still appears in t.

    –  In rule 1 the unique F disappears, so μ strictly decreases.  
    –  In rule 2 one leading S in the third argument is stripped, so μ
       also strictly decreases (by 1).

    No rule ever introduces new S’s or new F’s; therefore μ is a
    natural-number measure that drops at every rewrite step.  Hence no
    infinite reduction sequence exists.

3.  The same argument can be formalised by a standard recursive-path or
    lexicographic path ordering with precedence F > G > S, so the
    rules can be oriented compatibly and termination (strong
    normalisation) follows purely syntactically from the TRS.

Consequently, strong normalisation is derivable from the rewrite system alone; no external structural principles or additional object-level axioms are needed.
