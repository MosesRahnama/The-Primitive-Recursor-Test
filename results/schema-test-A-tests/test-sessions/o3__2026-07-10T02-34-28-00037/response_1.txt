Assessment: Yes – strong normalization (termination of every ground term) can be proved using the two rules themselves, without importing any extra axioms or meta-theoretic assumptions.

Reasoning (sketch)

1. The only symbol that rewrites is F.  
   • Rule 1 removes the whole F-node when the third argument is Z.  
   • Rule 2 turns F(x,y,S(n)) into G(y, F(x,y,n)).  
     Here the third argument of the new F is the strict predecessor n of the old
     third argument S(n).

2. Define a well-founded measure on ground terms, e.g.  
      μ(t) = total number of S-symbols occurring as
             the third argument of an F in t.
   - In Rule 1 the redex F(x,y,Z) disappears, so μ strictly drops.  
   - In Rule 2 the redex F(x,y,S(n)) is replaced by F(x,y,n); one S is lost,
     so μ strictly drops as well.
   - G and S are constructors; they do not introduce new redexes, hence do not
     increase μ.

   Because μ is a natural number that decreases with every rewrite step, no
   infinite reduction sequence is possible.

3. Standard termination techniques (recursive path ordering, polynomial or
   natural–number interpretations, dependency pairs on the third argument) all
   succeed for the same reason: the third argument forms a structurally
   decreasing induction on the S/Z “natural numbers”.

4. Variables x, y, n pose no problem: after substitution they become ground
   subterms which themselves terminate by the same argument; putting a
   terminating term into a context cannot re-introduce a loop because no rule
   rewrites at G or S nodes.

Therefore the system is strongly normalizing, and this fact can be established
directly from the given rules alone.
