**Assessment: Yes.** Strong normalization can be established from the two rules alone using purely syntactic reasoning.

**Reasoning:**  
The rules describe iteration on the third argument of `F`:

- Rule 1 removes `F` entirely when its third argument is `Z`.
- Rule 2 consumes one `S` from the third argument, turning `F(x,y,S(n))` into an inner `F(x,y,n)` whose third argument is the proper subterm `n`.

No rule introduces the symbol `S` or creates an `F` with a third argument larger than the one just consumed. Consequently, if we take the **multiset of the third arguments of all `F`‑occurrences** in a term, every rewrite strictly decreases that multiset in the multiset extension of the well‑founded proper‑subterm order:

- **Rule 2** replaces one element `S(n)` by the strictly smaller element `n`.
- **Rule 1** deletes the element `Z` entirely.

Since the multiset extension of a well‑founded order is well‑founded, infinite reduction sequences are impossible. This argument uses only the shapes of the given rewrite rules and the structural fact that proper subterm descent is well‑founded on finite terms; it imports no object‑level axioms about arithmetic or induction.
