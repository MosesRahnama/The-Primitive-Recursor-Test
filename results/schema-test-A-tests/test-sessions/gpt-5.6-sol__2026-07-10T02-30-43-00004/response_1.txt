Yes. Strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone for finite first-order terms.

A lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules by choosing \(F \succ G\) and giving \(F\) lexicographic argument status:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\) by the subterm property.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ G(y,F(x,y,n))\), since the left side is above \(y\), and
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n)
  \]
  because \(S(n)\succ n\) by the subterm property.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded ordering stable under substitutions and contexts. The duplication of \(y\) in the second rule may increase term size but does not defeat this ordering. No additional structural or object-level assumptions are needed.
