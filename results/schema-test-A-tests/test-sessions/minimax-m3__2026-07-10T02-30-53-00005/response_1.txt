**Assessment: Yes, strong normalization can be established from the rules alone.**

**Reasoning.** Both rules strictly decrease the lexicographic measure `(|S|, |F|)`, where `|S|(t)` is the number of `S` symbols in term `t` and `|F|(t)` is the number of `F` symbols.

- **Rule 1:** `F(x, y, Z) → x`. Comparing the two sides:
  - `|S|` does not increase (the LHS has `S(x)+S(y)`, the RHS has `S(x)`; the difference is `S(y) ≥ 0`).
  - `|F|` strictly decreases (the LHS has `1 + f(x) + f(y)`, the RHS has `f(x)`; the difference is `1 + f(y) ≥ 1`).
  
  So lex `(|S|, |F|)` decreases.

- **Rule 2:** `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))`. 
  - `|S|` strictly decreases by exactly **1** (the leading `S` in `S(n)` is consumed; the inner `F(x, y, n)` has no top-level `S`).
  
  So lex `(|S|, |F|)` decreases regardless of what happens to `|F|`.

Since `(|S|, |F|)` is bounded below by `(0, 0)` and strictly decreases on every rewrite step, no infinite reduction sequence exists. This works without any imported axioms or structural assumptions — the only thing used is the syntactic symbol count, which is definable purely from the given rewrite rules.

**Conclusion:** The system is strongly normalizing, provable directly from the rules.
