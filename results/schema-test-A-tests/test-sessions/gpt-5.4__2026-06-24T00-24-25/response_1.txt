Yes — strong normalization can be established from the rewrite system alone.

Reasoning, briefly:

- The system has only one defined symbol being unfolded recursively: `F`.
- The recursion is on the third argument:
  - `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`
- In the second rule, the recursive call is `F(x, y, n)`, where the third argument is a proper subterm of `S(n)`.

A simple well-founded measure is: count the number of `S` constructors in the third argument of each reducible `F(...)` occurrence. Each rewrite of an `F` redex strictly decreases that measure for the rewritten occurrence, and no rule creates a larger third argument or reintroduces recursion on a non-smaller term.

Also, `G` has no rewrite rules, so it cannot generate further reductions by itself.

Thus there cannot be an infinite rewrite sequence: every chain of `F` reductions bottoms out at the `Z` case. So strong normalization follows directly from the given rules, with no extra axioms needed.
