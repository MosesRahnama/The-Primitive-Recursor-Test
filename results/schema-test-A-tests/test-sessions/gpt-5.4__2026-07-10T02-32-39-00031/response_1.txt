Yes.

This TRS is strongly normalizing from the rules alone.

Reasoning, briefly:
- The only recursive rule is
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`
- In that rule, the recursive call to `F` is on the strictly smaller third argument `n` instead of `S(n)`.
- `G` has no rewrite rules, so once created it cannot trigger further rewriting except inside its arguments.
- `Z` and `S` also have no rules; they just form the data being structurally recursed over.

So every rewrite sequence starting from any term can only reduce occurrences of `F` by decreasing the number of `S` constructors in the third argument until it reaches:
- `F(x, y, Z) -> x`

A simple measure is: for each redex `F(a,b,t)`, count the number of `S` constructors in `t` when `t` is a numeral-shaped term; more generally, use a recursive path/order or lexicographic measure where `F(..., S(n))` is bigger than `F(..., n)`. Each rewrite step strictly decreases that measure, and no rule increases the possibility of infinite rewriting elsewhere.

Hence there is no infinite rewrite sequence: the system is strongly normalizing.
