1. **Primary method:** Recursive path ordering (RPO/LPO) with a chosen precedence, and alternatively a polynomial interpretation.

2. **Does it rely on assumptions not fixed by the rewrite rules themselves?** Yes. The choice of precedence ordering (F > G > S > Z) and the specific polynomial interpretation are external proof constructions — they are tools brought in by the analyst, not derivable from the rules themselves. However, these are *standard termination proof techniques* applied to the rules, not *object-level axioms* or *structural axioms about the logic*. They are meta-level proof methods whose validity is checked against the given rules.

3. **Does that place the method outside the stated boundary?** This depends on how strictly one reads "established from the presented rewrite system alone." If the question asks whether the rules *by themselves* syntactically entail their own termination without any external reasoning apparatus, then trivially no rewrite system can prove its own termination (by analogy with Gödelian limitations). But if the question asks whether termination can be established using only information present in the rules — without importing additional rewrite rules, equational axioms, or assumptions about the objects — then **yes, it can**. The RPO and polynomial interpretation are proof *methods* applied to the given rules; they don't import external *axioms*. They are not "structural axioms or object-level assumptions" — they are verification techniques.

4. **Is the system strongly normalizing?** **Yes, absolutely.** The third argument of `F` is a Peano natural that strictly decreases on every recursive call (`S(n) → n`), `G` is a constructor with no rewrite rules, and the base case eliminates `F` entirely. Every reduction sequence must terminate. This is unambiguous and does not depend on any subtle assumptions.
