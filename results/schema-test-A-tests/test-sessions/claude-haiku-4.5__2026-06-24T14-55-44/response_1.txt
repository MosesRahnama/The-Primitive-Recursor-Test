# Assessment: Strong Normalization **Cannot** be established from the presented rewrite system alone.

## Reasoning

**The system as presented is incomplete for strong normalization analysis:**

1. **Missing constructor specification**: The rules reference `Z`, `S(n)`, and `G(...)`, but only `F` is defined with rewrite rules. There is no information about:
   - Whether `G` has rewrite rules
   - Whether `Z` and `S` are merely constructors or have reduction behavior
   - The termination properties of any function `G`

2. **Undefined reduction for `G`**: The rule `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))` produces a term involving `G(...)`. If `G` itself has no rewrite rules (is a constructor), then the system may still terminate. But **if `G` has rewrite rules not shown**, it could introduce cycles or infinite reduction chains.

3. **Structural dependency**: Strong normalization of `F` depends critically on the behavior of `G`. Without knowing `G`'s rules, we cannot establish that all reduction chains terminate.

## Conclusion

A rigorous proof of strong normalization requires either:
- Complete specification of all rewrite rules (including for `G`)
- Explicit structural axioms (e.g., well-founded ordering on terms)
- Or a metatheoretic argument independent of the missing rules

**As stated alone, the system is insufficient for such a proof.**
