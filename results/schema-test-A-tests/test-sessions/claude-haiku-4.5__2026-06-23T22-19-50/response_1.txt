# Assessment: Strong Normalization **Cannot** be established from the presented rules alone.

## Reasoning:

1. **Missing termination guarantee for G**: The rewrite system defines `F` recursively in terms of `G`, but provides **no rewrite rules for `G`**. Without rules that reduce `G(y, ...)`, terms of the form `G(y, F(x, y, n))` cannot be normalized further.

2. **Infinite reduction chains possible**: Consider a term like `F(x, y, S(Z))`:
   - Reduces to: `G(y, F(x, y, Z))`
   - Which reduces to: `G(y, x)`
   - But `G(y, x)` has **no applicable rule** and is stuck as a normal form (or enters an infinite loop if `G` has hidden rules).

3. **Incomplete specification**: Strong normalization requires demonstrating that **every possible reduction sequence terminates**. With `G` left undefined, we cannot:
   - Guarantee termination of reductions involving `G`
   - Rule out potential loops if `G` were defined circularly
   - Analyze the combined behavior of the two functions

4. **What would be needed**: 
   - Explicit rewrite rules for `G`, or
   - A proof that the system is well-founded on some measure (e.g., lexicographic ordering on term structure), or
   - Structural axioms about `G`'s behavior

**Conclusion**: The system as presented is **incomplete** and cannot support a proof of strong normalization without additional information about `G`.
