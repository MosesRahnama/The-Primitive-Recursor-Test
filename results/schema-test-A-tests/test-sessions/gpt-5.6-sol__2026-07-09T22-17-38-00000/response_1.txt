**Yes.** Under the standard interpretation of a first-order TRS over finite terms, strong normalization follows from the rules alone.

For example, a lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules by taking \(F \succ G\) and giving \(F\) lexicographic status:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\) by the subterm property.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ G(y,F(x,y,n))\), since the left side is above \(y\), and
  \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n)\) because \(S(n) \succ n\).

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded, context- and substitution-stable ordering. No structural or object-level assumptions about \(x,y,n\) are needed beyond ordinary finite first-order term syntax.
