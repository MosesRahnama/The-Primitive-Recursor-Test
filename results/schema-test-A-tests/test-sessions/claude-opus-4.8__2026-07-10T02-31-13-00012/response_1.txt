## Assessment

**Yes — strong normalization can be established from the presented system alone.**

## Reasoning

The system has two rules:

```
F(x, y, Z)    -> x
F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))
```

**Key structural observations (all derivable from the rules themselves):**

1. **The only defined symbol is `F`.** The symbols `Z`, `S`, `G` never appear as the head of a left-hand side, so they are constructors — they are inert and cannot trigger further reduction on their own.

2. **Every rewrite step reduces something measurable.** Look at the third argument of `F`:
   - Rule 1 (`F(x,y,Z) -> x`) removes an `F` entirely, replacing it with a subterm.
   - Rule 2 (`F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y, F(x,y,n))`) replaces `F(...,S(n))` with `F(...,n)` guarded inside a `G`. The third argument strictly shrinks from `S(n)` to `n`.

3. **No rule ever grows the third argument of any `F`, nor creates new `F`-redexes with larger third arguments.** The right-hand side of rule 2 contains exactly one recursive `F`, and its third argument is a strict subterm of the matched `S(n)`. Rule 1 creates no `F` at all.

**Termination measure.** Define a measure on terms by summing, over all `F`-subterm occurrences, the size (number of `S`/`Z` constructors) of their third argument — or more simply, use the multiset of the third arguments of all `F`-occurrences under the multiset path/subterm ordering. Each rewrite step strictly decreases this measure:
- Rule 1 deletes an `F`-occurrence (measure drops).
- Rule 2 replaces one `F(·,·,S(n))` occurrence by one `F(·,·,n)` occurrence (a strictly smaller third argument), and adds no new `F`.

Since the natural-number-style structure `S(...)/Z` on the third argument is well-founded and every step strictly decreases the measure, no infinite reduction sequence exists.

**Why no external axioms are needed.** The well-foundedness used here is the well-foundedness of the *subterm ordering on the third argument*, which is intrinsic to the term algebra generated by the given signature — it does not require importing arithmetic, an interpretation of `S`/`Z` as actual naturals, or any assumption about `G`. It follows purely from the shape of the rules (finite terms, strictly decreasing recursion argument, no term-growth of the relevant measure).

This is essentially provable by a lexicographic/recursive path ordering with `F` precedence above `G`, `S`, `Z`, which any standard termination checker (or a simple polynomial interpretation like `[F](x,y,z) = x + y + z + 1`, `[S](z) = z+1`, `[G](x,y)=x+y`, `[Z]=0`) confirms without external assumptions.

**Conclusion:** SN is establishable from the rewrite system alone.
