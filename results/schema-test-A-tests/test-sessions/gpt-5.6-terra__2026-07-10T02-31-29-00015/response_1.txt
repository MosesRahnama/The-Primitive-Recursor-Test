Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the two rewrite rules alone.

A standard well-founded term ordering, such as a lexicographic path ordering, suffices. Give `F` higher precedence than `G`, and compare `F` arguments lexicographically with the third argument relevant:

- `F(x,y,Z) -> x` decreases immediately because `x` is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
- `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y,F(x,y,n))` decreases because:
  - the recursive `F(x,y,n)` is smaller than `F(x,y,S(n))`, since `n` is a proper subterm of `S(n)`;
  - the outer symbol changes from higher-precedence `F` to lower-precedence `G`;
  - `y` is also a subterm of the left-hand side.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded ordering. The duplication of `y` in the second right-hand side does not prevent termination under this ordering. No additional structural axioms or object-level assumptions are needed.
