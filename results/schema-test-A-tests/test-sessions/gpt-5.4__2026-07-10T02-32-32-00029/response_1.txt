Yes.

This TRS is strongly normalizing from the rules alone.

Why:
- The only defined symbols on the left-hand side are `F(...)` terms.
- The recursive rule
  - `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`
  strictly decreases the third argument of `F` from `S(n)` to `n`.
- The base rule
  - `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
  ends the recursion.

So every rewrite sequence involving an `F` peels off one `S` from the third argument until it reaches `Z`, after which no further `F`-rewrite is possible at that occurrence.

A simple termination measure is: count, over all subterms of the form `F(a,b,t)`, the number of `S` constructors in `t` when `t` is a numeral built from `Z,S`. Each rewrite step decreases that measure by 1 for the redex being reduced and does not create any larger `F` call.

Also, `G` has no rewrite rules, so it cannot introduce new reductions.

Hence there is no infinite rewrite sequence: the system is strongly normalizing.
