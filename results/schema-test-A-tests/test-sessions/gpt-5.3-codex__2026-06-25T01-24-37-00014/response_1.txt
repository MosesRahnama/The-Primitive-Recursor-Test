Yes — **strong normalization can be established from these rules alone**.

Reason (brief): every rewrite step on a term of the form `F(x,y,S(n))` strictly decreases the third argument inside the recursive `F` call from `S(n)` to `n`. Although a `G(...)` is added around it, there is **no rule that rewrites `G`**, so that wrapping cannot create new redexes except the single recursive `F` subterm with smaller index. Eventually the third argument reaches `Z`, where `F(x,y,Z) -> x` ends the recursion.

So there is a clear well-founded descent (on the Peano numeral in the 3rd argument of `F`), and no rule can increase it or restart recursion. Hence no infinite rewrite sequence exists.
