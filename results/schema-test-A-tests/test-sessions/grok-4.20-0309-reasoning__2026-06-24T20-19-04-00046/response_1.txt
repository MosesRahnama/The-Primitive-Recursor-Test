The two rules exhaustively cover the possible forms of the third argument of `F` (either exactly `Z`, which is eliminated, or headed by `S`, which is peeled off with the recursive call placed in an argument to the inert symbol `G`). No rules exist for `G`, `S`, or `Z`, and no rule introduces fresh `S` constructors into third-argument position of any `F`. Consequently every redex occurrence is destroyed after a number of steps bounded by the finite depth of `S`-nesting already present in its third argument at the start of reduction. Duplication of the second argument `y` (and any redexes it contains) can increase term size and multiply redexes, yet the newly created copies remain independent sub-reductions whose own third-argument depths are strictly smaller than those already accounted for in the original term; the multiset of those depths, ordered lexicographically by depth and number of copies, is therefore well-founded and strictly decreases. A recursive path order (or polynomial interpretation) witnessing this decrease can be read off directly from the syntactic shape of the two rules, without any equations, typing, or inductive schema for the constructors beyond what the rules themselves license. Hence every reduction sequence is finite.
