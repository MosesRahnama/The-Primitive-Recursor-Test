Yes. Strong normalization can be established from these rules alone.

A simple termination argument uses a well-founded recursive/path ordering with precedence, for example:

\[
F \;>\; G
\]

(with ordinary subterm comparison).

- The first rule is decreasing because \(x\) is a proper subterm of \(F(x,y,Z)\):
  \[
  F(x,y,Z) \to x.
  \]

- For the second rule,
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)),
  \]
  the recursive \(F\)-call has a strictly smaller third argument: \(S(n) > n\). Thus
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n).
  \]
  Since \(F > G\), and the left-hand side is also greater than \(y\), it is greater than
  \[
  G(y,F(x,y,n)).
  \]

Hence every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded term ordering. The overlap between the two rules does not create a loop: either choice reduces according to that ordering. No additional object-level axioms or structural assumptions are needed.
