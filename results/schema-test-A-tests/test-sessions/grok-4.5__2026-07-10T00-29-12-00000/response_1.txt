### Assessment
**Yes**, strong normalization (SN) of the given TRS can be established from the rules alone.

### Reasoning
The signature consists of the constants/constructors \(Z\) (arity 0) and \(S\) (arity 1), together with the symbols \(F\) (arity 3) and \(G\) (arity 2). The only rewrite rules are the two shown; there are no rules for \(G\), \(S\) or \(Z\), and the right-hand sides introduce neither \(S\) nor \(Z\).

Consequently every rewrite step either
- removes one occurrence of \(Z\) (first rule) or
- removes one occurrence of \(S\) (second rule).

In particular the total number of \(S\)-symbols occurring in a term is a non-increasing natural-number measure that *strictly decreases* on every application of the second rule. Because that number is finite for every first-order term and cannot increase, only finitely many applications of the second rule are possible in any reduction sequence. Between those applications only the first rule can fire; each of its applications strictly decreases the number of \(F\)-symbols, which is likewise finite. Hence every reduction sequence is finite.

The argument uses nothing beyond the concrete shape of the two rules (how they affect symbol counts) and the fact that first-order terms are finite trees; no induction principle for an object-level sort of “natural numbers”, no freeness axioms for \(S/Z\), and no other structural assumptions are required.

(The same conclusion can be reached by a simple polynomial interpretation or by the recursive path order with precedence \(F>G\), both of which are likewise read off directly from the rules.)
