I need to assess if strong normalization can be proven just from these rules.

Strong normalization: every term reduces to normal form in finitely many steps, no infinite reduction sequences.

In this system, the rules are:

F(x, y, Z) → x

F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))

Assuming x,y are variables, Z constant, S function, G function.

No rules for G, 0 for Z,S.

In standard first-order term rewriting with finite terms, this is terminating because reductions recurse on smaller n (predecessor), and base case Z terminates.

To prove it formally, one uses induction on the structure of terms, specifically on the third argument being a numeral, and well-foundedness of naturals ( 0 < S(n) ).

But the rules don't include any axioms about the naturals: like that every nat is Z or S(something), S injective, no S(Z)=Z etc.

Those are structural axioms for the sort nat.

Without importing them, from the rewrite rules alone, you can't derive that there are no infinite descending chains in the third arg, because if there were a term m such that S(m) reduces to m or something, but there are no rules for S, so S doesn't reduce.

Since no rules involve S or Z reducing, the only way to reduce is via F rules, which require matching exactly Z or S(n) syntactically.

If a term's third arg is not Z or S(something), no reduction there.

If it is S(something), it can reduce the F, producing G with F on smaller.

But to know that repeated application eventually reaches Z without looping, you need that the nat structure is well-founded, i.e., no cycles like infinite S chain backward, but since terms finite, no cycles anyway.

In pure TRS, with no other rules, it is SN by construction, because term size decreases: each reduction either eliminates an F (when to x) or replaces F with G and F but with 1 less S in the arg.

The number of S symbols overall in the term decreases by 1 each time you apply the S-rule, and when Z-rule, decreases Z.

You can have multiple F's, but each application of S-rule removes one S from the third arg of one F.

If the third arg has S's, they get peeled one by one.

Since initial term finite, finite S's, can't have infinite sequence.

So, actually, yes, it terminates purely from the rules, no need for extra axioms, because the rewriting doesn't create new S or Z, and removes them when applied.

No loops possible because 1) no rule creates a cycle like A->B->A, 2) measure: total number of S+Z symbols in all third args of F's is a natural number that decreases.

You can define a measure on terms: the sum over all F's of the "nat-depth" of their third arg, 0 if not headed by S/Z, or depth +1 etc.

This measure decreases on each reduction step.

This can be derived from the rules: when you apply Z-rule, depth to 0; S-rule, depth decreases by 1, and G doesn't have F so no new depth.
