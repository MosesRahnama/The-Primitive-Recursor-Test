**Yes — strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.**

## Reasoning

The system has two rules:

```
F(x, y, Z)    -> x
F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))
```

**Key structural observations derivable directly from the rules:**

1. **`G` is not a defined symbol.** No rule has `G` at the head of its left-hand side. `G` only appears on a right-hand side and is therefore a pure constructor (it never triggers a rewrite). It contributes no recursion and creates no redexes.

2. **Both rules strictly decrease the third argument of `F`.** This is visible without any imported assumptions:
   - The first rule eliminates the `F`-redex entirely (replacing it by the variable `x`, a subterm of the redex with no `F` introduced unless already present in `x`).
   - The second rule rewrites `F(x, y, S(n))` to `G(y, F(x, y, n))`. The newly created `F`-call has third argument `n`, which is a strict subterm of `S(n)`.

3. **No rule increases the "S-height" of any third argument.** The variables `x`, `y`, `n` on the right are subterms of the matched left-hand side; nothing manufactures a larger `S(...)` structure in an `F` position.

**Termination argument (a simple measure suffices):**

Assign to each term a multiset of natural numbers, where each occurrence of a subterm `F(_, _, t)` contributes the number of `S`-symbols nested at the top of `t` (its "S-depth").

- Rule 1 removes one `F`-occurrence and introduces none new (the contractum is just `x`); the multiset strictly decreases.
- Rule 2 replaces an `F` with S-depth `k+1` by an `F` with S-depth `k`, while `G` adds no `F`-occurrence of its own. The multiset strictly decreases under the multiset ordering.

Both rules strictly decrease this measure under a well-founded (multiset) ordering, and rewriting is closed under contexts and substitutions because the measure is a sum over occurrences. This is a standard polynomial/multiset interpretation argument that uses **only** what the two rules expose.

## Conclusion

Strong normalization follows from the rules themselves: the recursion in the only recursive rule peels off exactly one `S`, `G` is an inert constructor, and no rule can regenerate or enlarge the `S`-structure of an `F`'s recursion argument. No external structural axioms or object-level assumptions are needed.
