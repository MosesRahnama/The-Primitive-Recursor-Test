Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

For example, orient the rules using a lexicographic recursive path ordering with precedence \(F > G\):

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) decreases because \(x\) is a proper subterm.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\) decreases because:
  - the left-hand side is greater than \(y\), and
  - \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\), since \(S(n) > n\) by the subterm property.

Thus every rewrite rule strictly decreases under a well-founded term ordering. Consequently, the system is terminating—and hence strongly normalizing—for all finite first-order terms, without requiring structural axioms or any additional object-level assumptions.
