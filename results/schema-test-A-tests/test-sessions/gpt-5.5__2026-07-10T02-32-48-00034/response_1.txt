Yes. Strong normalization/termination can be established from the presented TRS alone.

A direct termination proof is given by a polynomial interpretation over natural numbers:

- \(Z = 0\)
- \(S(n) = n+1\)
- \(G(a,b) = a+b\)
- \(F(a,b,c) = a + (c+1)(b+1)\)

This interpretation is strictly monotone in all arguments and uses the usual well-founded order \(>\) on \(\mathbb{N}\).

Check the rules:

1.  
\[
F(x,y,Z) = x + (0+1)(y+1) = x+y+1 > x
\]

2.  
\[
F(x,y,S(n)) = x + (n+2)(y+1)
\]
while
\[
G(y,F(x,y,n)) = y + x + (n+1)(y+1)
\]
and the left side is exactly one larger than the right side.

So every rewrite step strictly decreases this interpretation. Therefore there is no infinite rewrite sequence.

Intuitively, the second rule recurses on the third argument of \(F\), replacing \(S(n)\) by \(n\), and no rule can rebuild an \(S\)-successor around that argument or eliminate the surrounding \(G\) in a cyclic way. Thus, without adding any extra structural axioms or object-level equations, the system is strongly normalizing.
