Yes. Strong normalization can be established from these rules alone.

A standard recursive path ordering / lexicographic path ordering suffices. Give `F` higher precedence than `G`, and use lexicographic status for `F`.

- `F(x, y, Z) -> x` decreases immediately to a proper subterm.
- For  
  `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`:
  - the left side is greater than `y` because `y` is a subterm;
  - the left side is greater than `F(x, y, n)` because the first two arguments are unchanged and `S(n) > n`;
  - since `F > G`, the left side is therefore greater than `G(y, F(x, y, n))`.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases under a well-founded simplification ordering, so there can be no infinite rewrite sequence. No additional structural or object-level axioms are needed.
