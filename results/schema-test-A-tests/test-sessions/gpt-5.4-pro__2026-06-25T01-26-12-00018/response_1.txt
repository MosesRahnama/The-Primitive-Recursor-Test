Yes — on the ordinary reading of this as a first-order TRS over finite terms, strong normalization can be established from these rules alone.

Why:
- The only defined symbol is `F`.
- The only recursive rule is  
  `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`
  and its recursive call is on `n`, which is a strict subterm of `S(n)`.
- There are no rules for `G`, `S`, or `Z`, so no rewrite can rebuild a larger recursive argument for `F`.

A standard termination proof works directly, e.g.:
- **Dependency pairs:** the only DP is  
  `F#(x, y, S(n)) -> F#(x, y, n)`,  
  which decreases by the subterm criterion.
- Or **LPO/RPO** with precedence `F > G > S > Z`, which orients both rules.

So, without importing any extra structural/object-level axioms, the presented rewrite system is strongly normalizing. The duplication of `y` in the second rule may increase size, but it does not create a non-well-founded recursive loop.
