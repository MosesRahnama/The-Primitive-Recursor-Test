**Reasoning:** The TRS consists solely of the two unconditional rules for F (with G, S, Z as ordinary symbols). Both rules are compatible with a recursive path order (RPO) under a precedence such as F > G > S > Z (or any total extension making F strictly largest). For the first rule the root symbol F dominates any term to which it rewrites, and RPO's subterm property gives F(x, y, Z) > x. For the second rule the roots satisfy F > G, and the single recursive argument satisfies F(x, y, S(n)) > F(x, y, n) because S(n) > n by the subterm property; the extra argument y is a proper subterm of the left-hand side, so the RPO comparison holds. RPO is well-founded on finite terms, so every rewrite sequence is finite. The argument uses only the syntactic form of the given rules and the standard definition of RPO; no typing, no freeness of constructors, no induction schema, and no equations beyond the directed rules themselves are required. Because every redex is an instance of one of these two rules and each instance strictly decreases in the RPO, the whole system is strongly normalizing.
