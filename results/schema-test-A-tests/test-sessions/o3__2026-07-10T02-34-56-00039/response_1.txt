Raw assessment: Yes – strong normalization follows directly from the two rewrite rules; no extra axioms are needed.

Reasoning in brief
1.  Only the symbol F rewrites.  
2.  Rule 1, F(x,y,Z) → x, removes one occurrence of F completely.
3.  Rule 2, F(x,y,S(n)) → G(y, F(x,y,n)), “peels off’’ one outer S from the third argument and moves the remaining sub-term F one level deeper inside an inert constructor G.  
    •  Neither G nor S nor Z participates in any rule, so once created they never disappear.  
    •  Every application of Rule 2 strictly shortens the chain of leading S-constructors in the third argument of the unique remaining redex F.  

Hence a natural well-founded measure is

μ(F(a,b,c)) = (length of the S-prefix of c),
μ(any other term) = 0.

Each rewrite step decreases μ by exactly 1, so an infinite reduction sequence is impossible.  
Therefore all terms are strongly normalizing under the given rules alone.
