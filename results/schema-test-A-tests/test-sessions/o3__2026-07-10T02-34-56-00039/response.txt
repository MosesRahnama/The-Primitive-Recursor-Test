1. Primary method used  
   – A simple well-founded measure: μ(F(x,y,t)) = length of the S-prefix of the third argument.  Each rewrite step reduces this natural-number measure, yielding a termination proof.

2. Does that method rely on anything not fixed by the rules?  
   – It relies on choosing a particular interpretation/ordering (the natural-number ordering on the computed measure).

3. Does that put it outside the stated boundary?  
   – No.  The ordering is a meta-level tool; it adds no object-level axioms or extra rewrite rules, so it stays within the boundary.

4. Independent of boundary issues, do I still regard the system as strongly normalizing?  
   – Yes, the above measure (or any equivalent well-founded ordering) shows every reduction sequence is finite; hence the system is strongly normalizing.
