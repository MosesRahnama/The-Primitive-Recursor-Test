### Proof and Reasoning

We can prove strong normalization using a standard **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)**, which is a well-known simplification order. 

1. **Precedence**: We define the precedence of the function symbols as $F \succ G$.
2. **Status**: We compare arguments of $F$ using lexicographic comparison from left to right (or alternatively, just comparing the third argument).

Now we show that for both rewrite rules, the left-hand side (LHS) is strictly greater than the right-hand side (RHS) under this LPO ($LHS \succ_{lpo} RHS$):

* **Rule 1: $F(x, y, Z) \to x$**
  * Since $x$ is a proper subterm of the LHS, $F(x, y, Z) \succ_{lpo} x$ holds immediately by the subterm property of LPO.

* **Rule 2: $F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n))$**
  * Since the root symbol of the LHS is $F$ and the root symbol of the RHS is $G$, and we have the precedence $F \succ G$, we only need to show:
    1. $F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{lpo} y$ (which holds because $y$ is a proper subterm of the LHS).
    2. $F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{lpo} F(x, y, n)$.
  * To show $F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{lpo} F(x, y, n)$, we compare their arguments lexicographically:
    * The first arguments are identical ($x = x$).
    * The second arguments are identical ($y = y$).
    * The third argument of the LHS, $S(n)$, is strictly greater than the third argument of the RHS, $n$, because $n$ is a proper subterm of $S(n)$ ($S(n) \succ_{lpo} n$).
  * Therefore, Rule 2 is strictly decreasing.

Since both rules are strictly decreasing under a compatible LPO, the system is **strongly normalizing** (terminating) for all terms.
