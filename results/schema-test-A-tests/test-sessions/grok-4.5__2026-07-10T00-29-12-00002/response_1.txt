### Assessment
**Yes**, strong normalization (SN) of the given TRS can be established from the rules alone.

### Reasoning
The signature consists of the ternary symbol \(F\), the binary symbol \(G\), the unary symbol \(S\) and the constant \(Z\), with the two rules
\[
F(x,y,Z)\to x,
\quad
F(x,y,S(n))\to G(y,F(x,y,n)).
\]
(The remaining symbols that may appear in terms are treated as uninterpreted.)  Both rules are oriented by a variety of classical termination orders that depend only on the rewrite rules themselves and on the ordinary inductive definition of first-order terms:

- **Recursive path order.**  Take the precedence \(F>G>S>Z\) together with lexicographic (or multiset) status for \(F\).  The first rule is oriented because the right-hand side is a proper subterm.  The second rule is oriented because the root \(F\) is strictly larger than the root \(G\) and the recursive call \(F(x,y,n)\) is strictly smaller than the left-hand side in the chosen status (the third argument decreases from \(S(n)\) to \(n\)).  RPO is a simplification order, hence well-founded, and therefore proves SN.
- **Polynomial interpretation.**  Interpret into the well-founded domain \(\mathbb{N}\) by
  \[
  |Z|=0,\quad
  |S(t)|=|t|+1,\quad
  |G(u,v)|=|u|+|v|,\quad
  |F(x,y,n)|=|x|+(|y|+1)\cdot|n|+1
  \]
  (and \(|x|=x\) for variables).  Both rules receive a strictly larger value on the left-hand side, and every interpretation function is monotone.  Consequently the interpretation is a reduction order and again proves SN.

Neither argument imports equations, freeness axioms for \(S/Z\), an induction principle special to an object-level sort of “natural numbers”, or any other hypothesis that is not already present in the pure first-order rewrite relation generated by the two displayed rules.  (Of course the meta-theoretic well-foundedness of the chosen order is used, but that is part of the general apparatus of termination proofs and is not an object-level assumption about the TRS under consideration.)

The same conclusion can be reached by the dependency-pair framework or by a simple multiset measure on the “\(S\)-height” of every \(F\)-redex; all of these techniques stay inside the data given by the TRS.
