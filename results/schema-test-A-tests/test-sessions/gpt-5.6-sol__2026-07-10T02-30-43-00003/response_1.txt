**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone.

For example, a recursive path ordering can orient both rules by taking \(F > G\) and giving \(F\) lexicographic status with the third argument decisive:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\): the right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\): the recursive \(F\)-call strictly decreases from \(S(n)\) to its proper subterm \(n\), and the outer \(G\) is below \(F\) in the precedence.

Thus every rewrite step decreases a well-founded term ordering. Rewriting inside \(x\), \(y\), or \(n\) does not undermine this argument, and the rules neither duplicate terms nor introduce a reverse recursion. No structural or object-level axioms are required.
