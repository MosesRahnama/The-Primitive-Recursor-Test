**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone using a well-founded polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
[Z]&=0,\\
[S(n)]&=[n]+1,\\
[G(y,z)]&=[y]+[z],\\
[F(x,y,n)]&=[x]+([n]+1)([y]+1).
\end{aligned}
\]

Both rules strictly decrease this measure:

- \(F(x,y,Z)\to x\) decreases by \(y+1>0\).
- \(F(x,y,S(n))\to G(y,F(x,y,n))\) decreases by exactly \(1\).

These interpretations are monotone in every argument, so reductions inside arbitrary contexts also decrease the measure. Since \(>\) on \(\mathbb N\) is well-founded, no infinite rewrite sequence exists. No additional object-level or structural axioms are needed beyond the standard definition of finite first-order terms and rewriting.
