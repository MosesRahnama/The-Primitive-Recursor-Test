**Assessment:** Yes, strong normalization (termination) of this system can be rigorously established from the presented rewrite system alone, without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond the rules.

**Reasoning:**  
A well-founded lexicographic measure $(\#S(t),\ \#F(t))$—where $\#S(t)$ and $\#F(t)$ denote the *total syntactic occurrences* of the function symbols $S$ and $F$ in term $t$ (variables contribute 0)—strictly decreases with every rewrite step under the given rules:

- **Rule 1:** $F(x, y, Z) \to x$  
  - $\Delta\#S = -\#S(y) \leq 0$  
  - $\Delta\#F = -1 - \#F(y) \leq -1$  
  → If $\#S$ unchanged ($\#S(y)=0$), $\#F$ strictly decreases.

- **Rule 2:** $F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n))$  
  - $\Delta\#S = -1$ (the matched $S$ is eliminated; $G$ introduces no $S$)  
  - $\Delta\#F = +\#F(y)$ (may increase)  
  → $\#S$ strictly decreases, dominating any $\#F$ increase lexicographically.

This measure is:
- **Purely syntactic**: Counts depend only on explicit symbol occurrences in the term structure; variables are neutral (count 0), requiring no assumptions about their instantiation.
- **Context-stable**: Reduction occurs at a redex; the context remains unchanged, so the global measure change equals the redex-local change computed above.
- **Well-founded**: Lexicographic order on $\mathbb{N} \times \mathbb{N}$ is well-founded; every step reduces the pair.
- **Rule-derived**: The decrease properties follow *directly* from the left/right-hand side structures of the two rules—no external properties of $G$, $Z$, $S$, or arithmetic/datatype axioms are invoked. Matching is syntactic (standard TRS semantics), so no assumptions about $S$-injectivity or $Z \neq S(n)$ are needed.

No infinite reduction is possible: each step consumes at least one "resource" ($S$-symbol or $F$-symbol) in a well-ordered manner. Attempts to construct non-terminating sequences (e.g., nested $F$, variable arguments, $G$-introduction) fail because $\#S$ bounds Rule 2 applications, and residual $\#F$ bounds Rule 1 applications once $\#S=0$. The argument is self-contained within the rewrite rules and first-order term syntax.
