**Assessment:** Yes – the system is strongly normalising.

**Reasoning:**  
The only rewrite rules are  

1. \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\)  
2. \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\)

Both rules act on the third argument of \(F\).  The third argument is a natural number built from the constructors \(Z\) (zero) and \(S\) (successor).  Rule 2 strictly reduces the number of \(S\) symbols in that argument (it replaces \(S(n)\) by the smaller \(n\)), and rule 1 removes an \(F\) entirely.  No rule creates a new \(S\) or increases the size of the third argument, and there are no other rules that could generate fresh \(F\)s in a non‑decreasing way.

Define a measure \(\mu(t)\) = the total count of \(S\) symbols occurring in any subterm of the form \(F(\_, \_, s)\).  Each application of rule 2 decreases \(\mu\) by 1, while rule 1 removes an \(F\) (thus also reduces \(\mu\) because the inner \(F\) disappears).  Consequently every rewrite step makes \(\mu\) smaller, and since \(\mu\) is a natural number, any rewrite sequence must be finite.

Thus the rewrite system is terminating (strongly normalising) without needing any additional structural axioms or object‑level assumptions beyond what is already given in the rules.
