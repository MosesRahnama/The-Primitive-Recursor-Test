Yes, strong normalization can be established from the given rules alone.

Each reduction of `F` either removes the outermost `F` (when the third argument is `Z`) or replaces it with a new `F` whose third argument is a proper subterm of the original (when the third argument is `S(n)`). No other rules exist, so the only possible reductions involve `F` terms. The number of `S` symbols in the third argument of any `F` strictly decreases in every step, and there is no way to increase it. This syntactic measure (e.g., the multiset of depths of third arguments) is well-founded without needing any additional axioms or structural assumptions. The system is therefore terminating.
