## Reasoning

This is a straightforward termination argument that requires no external axioms or structural assumptions beyond what the rules provide.

**The system consists of two rules:**
1. `F(x, y, Z) → x` — base case
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))` — recursive case

**Termination argument using a simple measure:**

The third argument of `F` strictly decreases in the successor ordering:
- Rule 1: The term `F(x, y, Z)` reduces, eliminating `F` entirely
- Rule 2: `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))` — the third argument goes from `S(n)` to `n`, which is structurally smaller

**Key observations:**
- `G` is a constructor (no rules rewrite `G`-rooted terms)
- The only defined symbol is `F`
- Each application of a rule either:
  - Eliminates `F` (rule 1), or
  - Reduces the "size" of the third argument of `F` (rule 2)

This can be verified by a simple polynomial interpretation or a lexicographic path ordering (LPO) with precedence `F > G, S, Z`. The termination proof is entirely self-contained within the given rewrite rules—no additional axioms about the term structure, no imported lemmas, and no assumptions about the behavior of constructors are needed beyond their syntactic presence.
