Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone, under the ordinary first-order TRS reading of the displayed signature.

A standard lexicographic path ordering (or similar simplification ordering) orients both rules. For example, take \(F \succ G\), with lexicographic comparison on \(F\)'s arguments:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\) decreases by the subterm property.
- In
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)),
  \]
  the recursive \(F\)-occurrence has the same first two arguments and a strictly smaller third argument, since \(S(n) \succ n\). The outer symbol also decreases from \(F\) to \(G\).

Thus every rewrite step decreases a well-founded ordering. The fact that \(y\) is duplicated on the right can increase term size, but it does not defeat this ordering or permit an infinite rewrite sequence. No additional structural or object-level axioms are needed.
