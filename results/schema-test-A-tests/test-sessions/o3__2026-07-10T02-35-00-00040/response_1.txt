Assessment: YES – strong normalization can be proved from the rules shown, without importing any extra axioms or assumptions.

Reasoning (sketch)  
1.  Let μ(t) be a natural-number measure defined on every term t as  

    μ(t) = Σ over all occurrences of F(u,v,w) in t of
             (1 + heightS(w)),  

   where heightS(w) is the number of outermost S-symbols in w
   (heightS(Z)=0, heightS(S(k))=heightS(k)+1, heightS(any other term)=0).

2.  For every rewrite step the value of μ strictly decreases:  
    •  Rule 1  F(x,y,Z) → x removes one occurrence of F, so μ drops at
       least by 1.  
    •  Rule 2  F(x,y,S(n)) → G(y, F(x,y,n)) replaces one F whose
       height contribution was (1+heightS(S(n))) with another F whose
       contribution is (1+heightS(n)), i.e. μ decreases by exactly 1.

3.  Since μ is a natural number that decreases with every rewrite,
    no infinite reduction sequence is possible; hence every term is
    strongly normalizing.

All arguments rely solely on the given rewrite rules; no additional
structural principles or object-level axioms are required.
