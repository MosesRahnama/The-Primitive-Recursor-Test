## Assessment

**Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.**

### Reasoning

**The system has two rules:**
1. `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`

**Key observations:**

1. **The third argument strictly decreases.** In rule 2, `F(x, y, S(n))` rewrites by stripping one `S` constructor from the third argument, producing `F(x, y, n)`. Since `S(n)` is strictly larger than `n` under any standard term ordering (e.g., the subterm ordering or a simple size measure), each application of rule 2 reduces the size of the third argument by exactly 1.

2. **Rule 1 is a base case** that eliminates `F` entirely when the third argument is `Z`, producing `x` with no further `F` or `G` redexes introduced on the right-hand side (beyond whatever was already in `x`).

3. **`G` is never given rewrite rules**, so any `G`-headed term is already in normal form (with respect to `G` itself); the only rewriting that can continue is within its arguments.

4. **A simple polynomial/numeric interpretation works:** Assign `|F(x,y,n)| = |n| + 1` (where `|S(n)| = |n|+1`, `|Z|=0`). Rule 2 strictly decreases this measure. Rule 1 terminates trivially.

5. **No cycles or indirect recursion** exist that could circumvent termination — `G` has no rules, and `F` only recurses on a strictly smaller third argument.

**Conclusion:** The system is strongly normalizing by a straightforward structural/lexicographic argument on the third argument of `F`, derivable entirely from the given rules with no additional axioms needed.
