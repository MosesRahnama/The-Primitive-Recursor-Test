**Yes.** Strong normalization follows syntactically from the rewrite rules alone.

For example, orient both rules using a lexicographic recursive path ordering with \(F \succ G\):

- \(F(x,y,Z) \succ x\) by the subterm property.
- For the second rule,
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \succ F(x,y,n)
  \]
  because \(S(n) \succ n\), again by the subterm property. Also \(F(x,y,S(n)) \succ y\). Since \(F \succ G\), this gives
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) \succ G(y,F(x,y,n)).
  \]

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded, context- and substitution-stable ordering. Hence no infinite rewrite sequence exists, including on open terms. No structural axioms about \(Z\), \(S\), or the represented objects are needed.
