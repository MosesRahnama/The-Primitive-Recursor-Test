**Assessment:** Yes – the system is strongly normalising.

**Reasoning:** Define a measure μ(t) as the total number of S‑symbols occurring in the third argument of any sub‑term of the form F(·,·,·) (i.e. the sum of the “S‑depth’’ of all F‑subterms).  

* Rule 2 (`F(x,y,S(n)) → G(y,F(x,y,n))`) removes one leading S from the third argument of the outermost F, so μ decreases by 1.  
* Rule 1 (`F(x,y,Z) → x`) eliminates an F whose third argument already has S‑depth 0; it never increases μ (the outer F contributed 0, and any F‑subterms inside x keep their original depth). Moreover, each application of rule 1 strictly reduces the number of F‑symbols in the term.

Thus every rewrite step either reduces μ or, when μ stays the same, reduces the number of F‑symbols. Since μ is a natural number and cannot decrease infinitely, only finitely many rewrites can occur. No additional axioms or structural assumptions are required beyond the standard syntactic definition of rewriting and elementary induction on natural numbers. Consequently, strong normalisation follows from the given rules alone.
