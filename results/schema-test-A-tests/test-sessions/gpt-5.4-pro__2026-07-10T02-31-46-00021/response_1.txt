Yes — on ordinary finite first-order terms, strong normalization can be established from this TRS alone.

Brief reason: the system is directly orientable by a well-founded reduction ordering. For example, use the polynomial interpretation over `ℕ`:

- `⟦Z⟧ = 0`
- `⟦S(n)⟧ = ⟦n⟧ + 1`
- `⟦G(a,b)⟧ = a + b`
- `⟦F(x,y,n)⟧ = x + (n+1)(y+1)`

Then both rules strictly decrease:

1. `F(x,y,Z) -> x`
   - `⟦F(x,y,Z)⟧ = x + (0+1)(y+1) = x + y + 1 > x = ⟦x⟧`

2. `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y, F(x,y,n))`
   - `⟦F(x,y,S(n))⟧ = x + (n+2)(y+1)`
   - `⟦G(y,F(x,y,n))⟧ = y + x + (n+1)(y+1)`
   - the left is exactly `1` larger than the right.

Since this gives a strict decrease into a well-founded order, there cannot be any infinite rewrite sequence. So no extra structural or object-level assumptions are needed beyond the rewrite rules themselves and the standard meta-level well-foundedness argument.
