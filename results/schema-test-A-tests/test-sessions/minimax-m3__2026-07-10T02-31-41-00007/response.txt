## Reconsideration

**1. Method used previously:** Polynomial interpretation over ℕ, with a chosen monotone assignment of polynomials to each function symbol and verification that every rule strictly decreases the interpreted value.

**2. Reliance on non-rule-fixed assumptions:** The method does not import any object‑level structural axioms about `S`, `Z`, or `G` (e.g. that `S`/`Z` are free constructors, that `G` is associative, etc.). The only thing it leans on beyond the literal rules is the **well‑foundedness of the natural numbers** as the target ordering, plus the standard soundness theorem for polynomial interpretations.

**3. Is that within the stated boundary?** Yes, in my reading. Well‑foundedness of ℕ is a meta‑theoretic fact about the framework in which we certify termination, not a structural or object‑level axiom being grafted onto the signature. No symbol of the TRS is being endowed with extra algebraic properties; `G` is treated as an uninterpreted, rule‑free symbol (its only "property" is that it has no rules, which is exactly what the rule list says). So the proof stays inside the boundary.

That said, the honest acknowledgment is: *some* form of global well‑founded induction is unavoidable in any termination argument — the question is only whether you also smuggle in extra algebraic content about the symbols, which this proof does not.

**4. Is the system actually strongly normalizing (separate from boundary issues)?** Yes, clearly. An even simpler argument from the rules alone: every `F`‑redex either (a) is eliminated by rule 1, or (b) has its third argument's `S`‑depth reduced by 1 and is then placed under a `G`. Since `G` has no defining rules, once an `F`‑redex is buried under a `G` it can never re‑surface. So each `F`‑redex is reduced at most once, and the number of `F`‑redexes in any term bounds the reduction length. Strong normalization follows from the rule set as written, with no extra assumptions needed.

I stand by the assessment.
