Yes — strong normalization/termination can be established from the rewrite system alone.

A simple termination argument is available by a polynomial interpretation over natural numbers, for example:

- `Z = 0`
- `S(n) = n + 1`
- `G(y, z) = z`
- `F(x, y, n) = x + n + 1`

Then both rules strictly decrease:

1. `F(x, y, Z) -> x`

\[
F(x,y,Z)=x+0+1=x+1 > x
\]

2. `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) = x+(n+1)+1 = x+n+2
\]

while

\[
G(y,F(x,y,n)) = F(x,y,n)=x+n+1
\]

so the right-hand side is strictly smaller.

The interpretation is monotone and maps every finite term to a natural number, with every rewrite step decreasing that number. Therefore no infinite rewrite sequence exists.

So, from the presented first-order rules alone, without adding structural axioms or object-level assumptions, the system is strongly normalizing.
