The question is: Can strong normalization of this system be established from the presented rewrite system alone, without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what is derivabl...Yes, strong normalization can be established from the rules alone.

The key is that every reduction strictly decreases the “size” of the term in a way that is independent of any external axioms. Define the measure on terms as the number of occurrences of the symbol F (or equivalently, the depth of the F-nesting). The first rule replaces an F-term with its first argument, which contains no F at all.  The second rule replaces an F-term with a G-term whose immediate subterm is an F-term whose third argument is a proper subterm of the original third argument (because the rule is applied to S(n) and the 3rd argument of the new F is exactly n).  Since every reduction either removes an F entirely or replaces it with a strictly smaller F (in the subterm ordering), no infinite reduction sequence can exist; every chain of reductions must terminate after finitely many steps.  No structural properties of Z or S beyond the fact that they are distinct constants and that S is a unary constructor are required—the termination argument follows directly from the shape of the two rules.  Hence the system is strongly normalizing.  (G is a constructor with no rules, so it never participates in reductions.)  This is a classic example of a terminating TRS that can be proved SN by a simple recursive-path-order or by a straightforward size measure on F-occurrences.  No imported object-level assumptions are needed.  (The system is indeed SN.)  So the answer is yes.  (The question is whether SN can be established from the rules alone, and it can.) (Yes.)
