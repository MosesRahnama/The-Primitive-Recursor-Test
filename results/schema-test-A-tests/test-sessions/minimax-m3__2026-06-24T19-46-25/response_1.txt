## Reasoning

**Inventory of the signature**
- `F` is the *only* symbol with rewrite rules.
- `S`, `Z` are constructors (no rules apply to them, so `S(t)`, `Z` are always normal forms).
- `G` is a defined symbol with *no* rules, so `G(t₁, t₂)` is always a normal form.

**Shape of the reductions**
1. `F(x, y, Z) → x` — collapses in one step to a variable, which is irreducible.
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))` — the right-hand side has `G(y, ·)` (a normal form wrapper) around the recursive call `F(x, y, n)`.

**Well-founded measure**
The recursive call strictly decreases the third argument: `S(n)` is replaced by `n` (structurally smaller — one fewer `S` constructor). Use the well-founded order on the third argument of `F`:

- **Base** `t₃ = Z`: one-step reduction to `x` (a normal form). ✓
- **Step** `t₃ = S(t₃')`: reduces to `G(y, F(x, y, t₃'))`. The wrapper `G(y, ·)` is a normal form; the embedded `F(x, y, t₃')` terminates by the induction hypothesis on the strictly smaller third argument `t₃'`. ✓

**Conclusion**
Since the only ever-active redex is the head `F`, and each `F`-reduction peels off exactly one `S` from the third argument (or hits the `Z` base case), every reduction sequence has length bounded by the number of `S`s in the third argument of the initial `F` plus one. No other symbol can create new `F`-redexes (because `G` has no rules and `S`/`Z` are inert).

This is a textbook instance of *structural recursion* on a constructor (`S`/`Z`), and the termination proof is fully derivable from the rules as written — no imported axioms about arithmetic, induction schemes, or external well-orderings are required beyond the syntactic well-foundedness of constructor nesting, which is intrinsic to any first-order TRS.
