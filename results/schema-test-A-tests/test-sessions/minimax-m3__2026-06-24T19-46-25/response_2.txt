## Reassessment

**1. Method previously used:** A structural induction on the third argument of `F`, treating the "number of `S` constructors above `Z`" as a well-founded measure, with `Z` as the base case and `S(n)` reducing to a term containing `F(x, y, n)`.

**2. External dependencies of that method:** Yes. The argument assumes:
- A well-founded order on `S`-`Z`-terms (i.e., the natural-number ordering induced by the constructors).
- That `S` and `Z` behave as "constructors" — a designation inferred from the *absence* of rules, not from anything the rules assert.
- The well-foundedness of that order is a structural fact about the term algebra, not a consequence of the two rewrite rules as written.

**3. Boundary status:** Yes — this places the method outside the boundary stated in the original instruction. The rules `F(x, y, Z) → x` and `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))` do not, by themselves, license a well-founded measure on the third argument. That license comes from a separate assumption about the term algebra (well-foundedness of the constructor ordering), which is the kind of structural/object-level assumption the question explicitly excludes.

**4. Is the system still strongly normalizing?** As a matter of mathematical fact, yes — every reduction sequence from `F(x, y, S^k(Z))` has length at most `k+1`, and no other symbol can create new `F`-redexes (since `G` has no rules and `S`/`Z` are inert). But the *proof* of that fact requires importing a well-founded order (or an equivalent termination criterion such as the recursive path order with a precedence derived from the rule structure), and that is not derivable from the two rules alone. So: the system is SN, but SN is not established *from the rules as written without supplementation*.
