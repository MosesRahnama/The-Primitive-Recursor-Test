Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone, under the ordinary first-order syntactic reading of the symbols.

A recursive path ordering proves termination. Choose a precedence with:

\[
F \;>\; G.
\]

For the second rule,

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)),
\]

the left side is greater than `y` because `y` is a subterm, and it is greater than `F(x,y,n)` because the third argument strictly decreases from `S(n)` to `n`. Since \(F > G\), the whole left-hand side is greater than the `G`-term on the right.

The first rule,

\[
F(x,y,Z) \to x,
\]

also strictly decreases because `x` is a subterm of the left-hand side.

Thus every rewrite step decreases a well-founded syntactic ordering, so there can be no infinite rewrite sequence. No associativity, arithmetic interpretation of `S`/`Z`, induction principle, or other object-level assumption is needed.
