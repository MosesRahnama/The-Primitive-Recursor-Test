Assessment: Yes – the system is strongly normalising; the fact can be proved from the two rewrite rules alone without importing any extra axioms.

Reasoning (sketch).  
1. Give every ground term a multivariate measure  
   μ(t) = (s(t), f(t)) where  
 • s(t) = the total number of occurrences of the unary symbol S in t,  
 • f(t) = the total number of occurrences of the symbol F in t.  
   Compare such pairs lexicographically.

2. Behaviour of the rules.  
 • Rule 2 F(x,y,S(n)) → G(y,F(x,y,n))  
  removes exactly one outer S and does not introduce any new S; hence s decreases by 1 while f does not increase.  
 • Rule 1 F(x,y,Z) → x  
  leaves s unchanged but deletes one occurrence of F from the term, so f decreases by 1.

3. Because μ(t) is always a pair of non-negative integers and every rewriting step strictly reduces μ(t) (first component for rule 2, second component for rule 1) there cannot be an infinite sequence of rewrites. Hence all sequences terminate and the system is strongly normalising.

No other properties or external axioms are needed; the argument uses only the given rules.
