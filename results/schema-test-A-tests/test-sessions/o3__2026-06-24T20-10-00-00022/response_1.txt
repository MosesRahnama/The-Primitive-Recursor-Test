Yes – termination / strong‐normalisation can be proved from the two rules themselves, without importing any additional axioms.

Reasoning (sketch)

• The only symbol that can appear on the left–hand side of a rule is F.  
  Therefore the only redexes that can ever be created or contracted are headed by F.

• Associate with every term headed by F a natural‐number measure
        μ(F(t1 , t2 , t3)) := |t3|S
  where |t3|S is the number of occurrences of the constructor S along the outermost chain of the third argument (i.e. the “length” of the numeral: Z ↦ 0, S(Z) ↦ 1, …).  
  For all other symbols (G, S, Z, variables) put μ( … ) := 0.

• Rule 1    F(x , y , Z)  →  x  
  strictly decreases μ, because the right–hand side contains no F at all.

• Rule 2    F(x , y , S(n))  →  G(y , F(x , y , n))  
  replaces the redex by a term that contains one new subterm F(x , y , n) whose
  measure is μ(F(x , y , n)) = |n|S = |S(n)|S − 1, i.e. the measure drops by one.

• No rule rewrites G, S or Z, so the measure of every redex that can subsequently
  be reduced is strictly smaller than the measure of the redex that produced it.

• Hence μ is a strictly decreasing, non-negative, well-founded measure on every
  reduction sequence: an infinite descending chain is impossible, so every sequence of rewrites is finite.

This elementary measure argument (or, equivalently, an ordering such as the recursive path ordering with precedence F > G > S > Z) is derivable solely from the given rules; no structural induction principle or external axioms need to be assumed.

Therefore the presented system is strongly normalising on its own.
