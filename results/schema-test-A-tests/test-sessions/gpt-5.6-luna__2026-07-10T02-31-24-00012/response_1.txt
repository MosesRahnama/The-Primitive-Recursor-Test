Yes. Strong normalization can be established from the rewrite rules alone.

For example, use a recursive path ordering with precedence \(F > G\), taking \(F\)'s arguments lexicographically. Both rules strictly decrease:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\), since \(x\) is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\):
  - the left-hand side is greater than \(y\) by the subterm property;
  - it is greater than \(F(x,y,n)\) because the first two arguments are unchanged and \(S(n) > n\);
  - hence it is greater than the enclosing \(G\)-term under the precedence \(F > G\).

Thus every rewrite step decreases a well-founded ordering, so the system is strongly normalizing without requiring any additional structural axioms or object-level assumptions.
