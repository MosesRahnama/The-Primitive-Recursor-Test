## Reconsidered Assessment

**1. Primary method used:**
Structural descent on the third argument — informally, a well-founded measure based on the size/depth of the `S`/`Z` term in the third position.

**2. Does that method rely on assumptions not fixed by the rewrite rules?**
Yes. The argument requires:
- That `S` and `Z` are **constructors** forming a well-founded structure (i.e., no infinite chains `S(S(S(...)))`)
- A **well-founded ordering** (structural or numeric) over terms
- The **interpretation** that `n` is strictly smaller than `S(n)`

None of these are stated in or derivable from the rewrite rules themselves. The rules only say what `F` reduces to — they do not assert that `S`/`Z` are constructors, that terms are finite, or that any ordering is well-founded.

**3. Does this place the method outside the stated boundary?**
Yes. The termination argument imports structural assumptions about the signature that are not derivable from the given rules alone.

**4. Is the system still strongly normalizing (separately from boundary compliance)?**
Yes, almost certainly — under any standard interpretation of `S` and `Z` as Peano constructors, the system is straightforwardly strongly normalizing. But that judgment itself presupposes the same external interpretation.

**Summary:** The system is strongly normalizing under standard assumptions, but those assumptions cannot be derived from the rewrite rules alone, so strong normalization **cannot be established within the stated boundary**.
