Yes — **strong normalization can be established from these rules alone**.

Reason (briefly): every rewrite step strictly decreases the third argument of `F` from `S(n)` to `n` inside the recursive call:
- `F(x,y,Z) -> x` is a base case (no recursion),
- `F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y, F(x,y,n))` makes exactly one recursive `F` call on a structurally smaller numeral.

There are no rules for `G`, `S`, or `Z`, so once `F` reaches `Z`, rewriting stops. Thus no infinite rewrite sequence can occur. A simple well-founded measure is “the number of leading `S` constructors in the third argument of each reducible `F` occurrence,” which decreases on each `F`-reduction. Hence SN holds for this TRS as given.
