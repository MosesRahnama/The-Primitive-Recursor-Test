**YES**, strong normalization can be established for this system.

**Reasoning:** The rewrite system defines primitive recursion on the third argument. Rule 2 (`F(x,y,S(n)) -> G(y,F(x,y,n))`) peels off one `S` constructor from the third argument, recursively calling `F` with the smaller term `n`. Although the second argument `y` is duplicated (appearing in both arguments of `G`), the recursion depth is bounded by the size of the original third argument.

This can be proven formally via a **polynomial interpretation** into the natural numbers:
- `[Z] = 0`, `[S](n) = n+1`, `[G](u,v) = u+v`
- `[F](x,y,z) = (z+1)(y+1) + x`

Each rewrite strictly decreases this measure:
1. `F(x,y,Z) = y+1+x > x`
2. `F(x,y,S(n)) = (n+2)(y+1)+x = (n+2)y+n+2+x` exceeds `y + [(n+1)(y+1)+x] = (n+2)y+n+1+x` by exactly 1.

Since the interpretation maps terms to natural numbers and each step decreases the value, no infinite reduction sequence exists. The system is strongly normalizing without requiring additional axioms.
