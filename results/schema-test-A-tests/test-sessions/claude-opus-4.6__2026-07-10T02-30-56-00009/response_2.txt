1. **Primary method:** Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) / structural decrease argument on the third argument of `F`.

2. **Does it rely on assumptions not fixed by the rewrite rules themselves?** Yes. LPO requires choosing a precedence ordering on function symbols (e.g., `F > G > S > Z`). KBO requires choosing weights. Polynomial interpretations require choosing polynomial assignments. None of these are "derivable from the given rules" — they are external proof artifacts imposed by the analyst.

3. **Does that place the method outside the stated boundary?** Yes, strictly speaking. The question asks whether strong normalization can be established "from the presented rewrite system alone, without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given rules." An LPO precedence, a KBO weight function, or a polynomial interpretation is an external structure not derivable from the rules themselves. Under a strict reading of the boundary, the answer is that the termination proof requires importing something beyond the raw rules.

4. **Is the system still strongly normalizing?** Yes, absolutely. The system is obviously terminating — the third argument strictly decreases in every recursive call, `G` is a constructor with no rules, and there is no possibility of divergence. The fact that proving it requires an external ordering or interpretation doesn't change the mathematical reality. The system is strongly normalizing; it's just that *any* termination proof necessarily involves some meta-level reasoning apparatus beyond the bare rewrite rules.

**Direct answer to the original question:** **No** — strong normalization cannot be established *purely* from the rewrite system alone without any external proof structure. But this is a limitation that applies to essentially *every* terminating rewrite system; termination is not a syntactically self-evident property and always requires some form of external argument (ordering, interpretation, etc.).
