**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the rewrite rules alone.

A standard lexicographic path ordering can orient both rules by taking \(F \succ G\) and giving \(F\) lexicographic status:

- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\): the right-hand side is a proper subterm.
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\): the recursive \(F\)-call decreases in its third argument, since \(n\) is a proper subterm of \(S(n)\), and the outer \(G\) is below \(F\) in the precedence.

Although \(y\) is duplicated, this does not defeat the well-founded ordering. Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded term ordering, establishing strong normalization without assumptions that all terms have a particular constructor form or any other object-level axioms.
