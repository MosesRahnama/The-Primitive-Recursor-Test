Yes — under the standard reading of this as a first-order TRS on finite terms, strong normalization can be established from these rules alone.

Brief reason: there is a straightforward well-founded termination ordering for the rules. For example, use the polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb{N}\):

- \(\llbracket Z \rrbracket = 0\)
- \(\llbracket S(t) \rrbracket = \llbracket t \rrbracket + 1\)
- \(\llbracket G(a,b) \rrbracket = \llbracket a \rrbracket + \llbracket b \rrbracket + 1\)
- \(\llbracket F(a,b,c) \rrbracket = \llbracket a \rrbracket + \llbracket c \rrbracket(\llbracket b \rrbracket + 2) + 1\)

Then both rules strictly decrease:

1. \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\):
   \[
   \llbracket F(x,y,Z)\rrbracket = \llbracket x\rrbracket + 1 > \llbracket x\rrbracket
   \]

2. \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\):
   \[
   \llbracket F(x,y,S(n))\rrbracket
   = \llbracket x\rrbracket + (\llbracket n\rrbracket+1)(\llbracket y\rrbracket+2)+1
   \]
   while
   \[
   \llbracket G(y,F(x,y,n))\rrbracket
   = \llbracket y\rrbracket + \big(\llbracket x\rrbracket + \llbracket n\rrbracket(\llbracket y\rrbracket+2)+1\big)+1
   \]
   and the left side is exactly 1 larger.

So every rewrite step strictly decreases a natural-number measure, hence no infinite rewrite sequence exists. In short: **yes, SN is provable from the rewrite system itself; no extra object-level axioms are needed.**
