1. **Primary method used:** Polynomial interpretation and Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO) with a chosen precedence.

2. **Does that method rely on assumptions not fixed by the rewrite rules themselves?** Yes. The polynomial interpretation requires choosing specific polynomial assignments to function symbols. LPO requires choosing a precedence ordering on function symbols. Neither is "derivable from" the rewrite rules—they are external proof techniques with free parameters chosen by the analyst.

3. **Does that place the method outside the stated boundary?** Yes, strictly speaking. The question asked whether strong normalization can be established "from the presented rewrite system alone, without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given rules." Choosing an interpretation or precedence is an external methodological choice, not something derivable from the rules themselves.

4. **Is the system still strongly normalizing?** Yes. The system is genuinely strongly normalizing—every reduction sequence terminates. The third argument strictly decreases in the recursive rule (from `S(n)` to `n`), and `G` introduces no new `F`-redexes. This is a mathematical fact about the system, independent of whether the proof method falls inside or outside the stated boundary.
