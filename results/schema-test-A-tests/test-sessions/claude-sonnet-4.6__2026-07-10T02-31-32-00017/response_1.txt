**Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented rewrite system alone.**

### Reasoning

**Structure of the system:**

The two rules are:
1. `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`

**Key observations:**

1. **The third argument strictly decreases.** In rule 2, `F(x, y, S(n))` rewrites by peeling one `S` constructor off the third argument, producing a recursive call `F(x, y, n)`. The third argument goes from `S(n)` to `n` — a strict structural decrease under the standard subterm ordering on the `S/Z` natural number terms.

2. **No rules for `G`.** There are no rewrite rules with `G` on the left-hand side. `G` is essentially an uninterpreted/inert constructor in this system. Once `G(y, ...)` is produced, it does not trigger any further rewriting (beyond whatever is inside its arguments).

3. **Termination argument.** A simple **lexicographic path order (LPO)** or even a straightforward **structural induction** on the third argument of `F` suffices:
   - Every application of rule 2 strictly reduces the size of the third argument (from `S(n)` to `n`).
   - Rule 1 is a base case with no recursive calls.
   - Since `S/Z` terms are finitely deep, the recursion must terminate.

4. **No cycles or mutual recursion through `G`.** Since `G` has no rules, it cannot "feed back" into `F` or itself.

**Conclusion:** Strong normalization holds unconditionally for all ground terms, provable purely from the given rules via a simple subterm/size measure on the third argument of `F`. No additional structural axioms or object-level assumptions are needed.
