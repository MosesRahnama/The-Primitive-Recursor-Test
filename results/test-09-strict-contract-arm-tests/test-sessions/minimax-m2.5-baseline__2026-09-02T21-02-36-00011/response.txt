**Yes.**  

The rewrite rules are *simply terminating*: one can define a measure \(M : \mathbf{Trace} \to \mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\) (for example a pair \((\text{delta‑depth}(t),|t|)\) where  

* \(|t|\) is the total number of constructors in \(t\), and  
* \(\text{delta‑depth}(t)\) is the number of leading \(\delta\) constructors on the third argument of a \(\mathsf{rec\Delta}\) (or 0 otherwise),

and show that every rule **strictly decreases** this pair lexicographically:

* \(\mathsf{integrate}(\delta\,t)\to\mathbf{void}\) removes a \(\delta\) and several constructors, so \(M\) drops.  
* \(\mathsf{merge}\,(\mathsf{void},t)\to t\), \(\mathsf{merge}\,(t,\mathsf{void})\to t\) and \(\mathsf{merge}\,(t,t)\to t\) all replace a \(\mathsf{merge}\) node by one of its arguments, decreasing \(|t|\).  
* \(\mathsf{rec\Delta}\,b\,s\;\mathsf{void}\to b\) eliminates the \(\mathsf{rec\Delta}\) node, reducing \(|t|\).  
* \(\mathsf{rec\Delta}\,b\,s\;(\delta\,n)\to \mathsf{app}\,s\;(\mathsf{rec\Delta}\,b\,s\,n)\) removes **one** \(\delta\) from the third argument, i.e. decreases the first component of the measure; the total size may increase, but the lexicographic order gives priority to the delta‑depth, so the pair still gets smaller.  
* \(\mathsf{eqW}\,a\,a\to\mathbf{void}\) and \(\mathsf{eqW}\,a\,b\to\mathsf{integrate}(\mathsf{merge}\,a\,b)\) also reduce the measure (the former removes the whole term, the latter replaces an \(\mathsf{eqW}\) by a smaller \(\mathsf{integrate}\)‑term).

Since \(\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\) with the lexicographic order is well‑founded, there cannot be an infinite rewrite chain. The proof can be carried out inside Lean 4 by defining such a measure and proving its decrease for each constructor of `Step`, using the induction principle that comes from the definition of `Trace`. No extra rewrite rules, guards, side‑conditions, or hidden axioms are required. Hence strong normalization is provable from the calculus alone.
