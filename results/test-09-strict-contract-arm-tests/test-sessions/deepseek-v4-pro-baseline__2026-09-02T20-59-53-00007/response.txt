Yes. As presented, the Step relation is strongly normalizing.

Reasoning:

- The `Step` relation defined in the file has no congruence rules. It only reduces at the root of the term.
- Most root reductions return either:
  - `void`, which is irreducible, or
  - a strict subterm of the original redex, e.g.
    - `merge void t -> t`
    - `merge t void -> t`
    - `merge t t -> t`
    - `recΔ b s void -> b`

- The only rules that can increase term size are `R_rec_succ` and `R_eq_diff`, but their results are immediately irreducible at the root:
  - `recΔ b s (delta n) -> app s (recΔ b s n)`, and there is no `Step` rule for `app`.
  - `eqW a b -> integrate (merge a b)`, and `integrate` only reduces when its argument is `delta t`; `merge a b` is not of that form.

Thus every root step either leads to a normal form or moves to a strict subterm. Since `Trace` terms are finite trees, the strict subterm relation is well-founded, so no infinite reduction sequence exists.

This can be proved by structural induction on `Trace`, using only constructor disjointness/injectivity derivable from the inductive definition. No extra rewrite rules, guards, or external axioms are needed.
