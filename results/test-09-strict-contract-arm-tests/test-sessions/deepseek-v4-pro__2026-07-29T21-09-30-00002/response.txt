### 1. Additive failure of a naive size measure
Define `M(t)` as the number of constructors in the trace term `t`.  
For rule `R_rec_succ`:

- after: `app s (recΔ b s n)`

```
M(before) = 2 + M(b) + M(s) + M(n)
M(after)  = 2 + M(b) + 2·M(s) + M(n)
```
Hence `M(after) = M(before) + M(s) - 0` (in fact `M(after) > M(before)` whenever `M(s) ≥ 1`).  
There is **no strict drop**; a simple size function does not work.

### 2. Fix: recursive path ordering (RPO)
Choose the **precedence** on the constructors  
`recΔ > app > eqW > integrate > merge > delta > void`.

The key premise is that RPO with this precedence is a well‑founded strict ordering.  
We now verify explicitly that for every rule `l → r` we have `l ≻_RPO r` (using the standard definition where a term is greater than its subterms, and precedence decides between different root symbols).

- **R_int_delta**: `integrate(delta t) → void`  
  `integrate > void` and the constant is smaller. ✔
- **R_merge_void_left / _right / _cancel**: the left sides are `merge(...)` and the right side is a direct subterm.  
  `merge(void, t) ≻ t` by the subterm property. ✔ (same for the other two)
- **R_rec_zero**: `recΔ b s void → b` – subterm property. ✔
- **R_rec_succ**: `recΔ b s (delta n) → app(s, recΔ b s n)`  
  - `recΔ > app` in the precedence, and we also need the larger term to be greater than `s` and greater than `recΔ b s n`.
  - `recΔ b s (delta n) ≻ s` because `s` is a subterm.
  - `recΔ b s (delta n) ≻ recΔ b s n` because the two terms have the same root (`recΔ`), the first two arguments are identical, and the third argument `delta(n) ≻ n` (subterm property).
  Hence `recΔ b s (delta n) ≻ app(s, recΔ b s n)`. ✔
- **R_eq_refl**: `eqW a a → void` – `eqW > void`, constant smaller. ✔
- **R_eq_diff**: `eqW a b → integrate(merge a b)`

  `eqW > merge`, and `eqW a b ≻ a` (subterm), `eqW a b ≻ b` (subterm), so `eqW a b ≻ merge a b`. Thus the whole term dominates the `integrate` term. ✔

All rewrite rules strictly decrease under this RPO. Since RPO is a well‑founded ordering on finite first‑order terms, every reduction sequence must terminate. No extra structural axioms or side conditions were required.
