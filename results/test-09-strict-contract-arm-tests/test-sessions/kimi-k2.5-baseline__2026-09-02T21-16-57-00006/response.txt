**Assessment: Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone.**

**Reasoning:**

This is a first-order term rewriting system (TRS) with the following properties:

1.  **No Variable Binding**: The system contains no binders (like λ or ∀) and therefore no substitution operation that could lead to size explosion or capture issues. All rewrite rules are purely structural.

2.  **Elimination of Heavy Constructors**: The constructor `eqW` appears only on the left-hand sides of rules (R_eq_refl and R_eq_diff) and never on any right-hand side. Therefore, `eqW` is strictly eliminated and cannot be re-introduced. The rule `R_eq_diff` replaces `eqW a b` with `integrate (merge a b)`, which, while syntactically larger, eliminates the "heavy" `eqW` symbol in favor of "lighter" constructors (`integrate` and `merge`) that have strictly limited reduction behavior.

3.  **Well-Founded Decrease on Recursive Calls**: The `recΔ` operator implements primitive recursion. In `R_rec_succ`, the third argument `(delta n)` structurally decreases to `n`. Although the rule introduces an `app` constructor, standard termination measures (e.g., assigning ordinal weights where `recΔ b s n` has weight proportional to $2^{|n|}$ or using a lexicographic measure decreasing on the height of the third argument) show this is strictly decreasing. The `recΔ` operator is never applied to larger terms through reduction.

4.  **Termination of Auxiliary Rules**:
    *   `R_int_delta` removes a `delta` and `integrate`.
    *   The `merge` rules (R_merge_void_left, R_merge_void_right, R_merge_cancel) strictly decrease term size or map to one of their arguments.
    *   `R_eq_refl` maps to `void`.

5.  **No Cycles**: There is no rule that increases the "complexity" of a term in a way that could feed back into a previous form. The only locally expanding rule is `R_eq_diff`, but its result `integrate (merge a b)` immediately reduces via `merge` rules to a subterm of `a` or `b` (or a normal form), and `integrate` itself only reduces when its argument is `delta t`. This cannot create an infinite loop.

One can prove strong normalization formally by defining a well-founded measure $\mu : \text{Trace} \to \text{Ord}$ (ordinals) such as:
*   $\mu(\text{void}) = 0$
*   $\mu(\text{delta}\ t) = \mu(t) + 1$
*   $\mu(\text{integrate}\ t) = \mu(t)$ (or $\mu(t) + 1$ with $\omega$-weighting)
*   $\mu(\text{merge}\ a\ b) = \max(\mu(a), \mu(b)) + 1$
*   $\mu(\text{app}\ a\ b) = \mu(a) + \mu(b) + 1$
*   $\mu(\text{rec}_\Delta\ b\ s\ n) = \mu(b) + \mu(s) + \omega^{\mu(n)}$
*   $\mu(\text{eqW}\ a\ b) = \omega^{\mu(a) + \mu(b) + 1}$

Every rewrite rule strictly decreases this measure. Since the ordinals are well-founded, no infinite reduction sequence exists.
