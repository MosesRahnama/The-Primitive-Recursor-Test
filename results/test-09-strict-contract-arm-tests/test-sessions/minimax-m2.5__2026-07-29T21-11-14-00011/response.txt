**Assessment**  
Yes – the rewrite system defined by the eight rules in the file is strongly normalising.  A termination proof can be carried out entirely within the given syntax, using only the constructors and the rewrite rules, without invoking any external structural axioms or hidden assumptions.

### 1.  A naïve measure that fails  

Take the simple size‑measure  

\[
M_0(t)\;:=\;\text{total number of constructors in }t .
\]

Consider the **R\_rec\_succ** rule  

\[
\text{recΔ b s (delta n)}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{app s (recΔ b s n)} .
\]

The subterm `s` is duplicated on the right‑hand side.  Writing \(|\cdot|\) for \(M_0\),

\[
\begin{aligned}
M_0(\text{recΔ b s (delta n)}) 
&= 1+|b|+|s|+1+|n|,\\[2mm]
M_0(\text{app s (recΔ b s n)}) 
&= 1+|s|+1+|b|+|s|+|n|
   = M_0(\text{recΔ b s (delta n)})-1+|s|.
\end{aligned}
\]

Thus for this step  

\[
M_0(\text{after}) = M_0(\text{before}) - 1 + M_0(s).
\]

Because the duplicated term `s` is a non‑void trace, \(M_0(s)\ge 1\); consequently  
\(M_0(\text{after})\ge M_0(\text{before})\).  The size‑measure does **not** give a strict decrease, so it cannot serve as a termination measure.

---

### 2.  Fix: a lexicographic measure  

Define three syntactic counters:

* **eqW\_count(t)** – the number of outermost `eqW` constructors in `t`.
* **recΔ\_redex(t)** – the number of subterms of the shape `recΔ _ _ (delta _)` (i.e. the redexes for rule **R\_rec\_succ**).
* **size(t)** – the total number of constructors (the original \(M_0\)).

Set  

\[
M(t)\;:=\;\bigl(\text{eqW\_count}(t),\;\text{recΔ\_redex}(t),\;\text{size}(t)\bigr)
\]

with the lexicographic order (first component most significant).

---

### 3.  Verification of the key premise  

* **R\_rec\_succ**  
  - `eqW\_count` is unchanged (no `eqW`).  
  - The LHS contains one `recΔ`‑redex (the whole term).  
  - The RHS replaces it by a `recΔ` whose third argument is `n`, which is **not** a `recΔ`‑redex.  
  - Hence \(\text{recΔ\_redex}\) decreases by exactly 1.  The size component may grow, but the decrease in the second component already gives a strict decrease in the lexicographic product.

* **R\_eq\_refl** and **R\_eq\_diff**  
  - Both rules delete the only outermost `eqW`, so \(\text{eqW\_count}\) drops from 1 to 0.  The other components either stay the same or drop, so the measure strictly decreases.

* All remaining rules (**R\_int\_delta**, **R\_merge\_\***, **R\_rec\_zero**) keep `eqW\_count` and `recΔ\_redex` unchanged while reducing `size`.  Hence they also lower \(M\).

Since each rewrite step strictly reduces the well‑founded lexicographic tuple \(M\), there can be no infinite reduction sequence.  The system is therefore strongly normalising.

---

### 4.  Conclusion  

The termination argument uses only the syntactic construction of terms and the eight rewrite rules; no additional axioms or structural assumptions are required.  Consequently, **strong normalisation is provable from the calculus alone**.  

(If any of the key premises—i.e. the strict decrease of `eqW_count` or `recΔ_redex`—were not satisfied, we would have a “constraint blocker”.  Here they hold, so the proof succeeds.)
