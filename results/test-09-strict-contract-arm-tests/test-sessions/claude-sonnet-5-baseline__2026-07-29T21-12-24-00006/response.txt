## Assessment: Yes — strong normalization is provable directly from the given data, using an elementary size argument.

### Key structural observation

The inductive definition of `Step` contains **no congruence/context rules**. There is no clause of the form "if `Step a b` then `Step (delta a) (delta b)`" (or similarly for `merge`, `app`, `recΔ`, `eqW`, `integrate`). The eight constructors of `Step` are the *only* facts about the relation, and each one relates a term with a specific *root* shape to another term. Consequently, `Step` is not the compatible closure of a set of rules in the usual TRS sense — it only ever fires at the outermost constructor of a whole term. This fact, entirely visible from the file itself, is what makes a self-contained SN proof possible.

### A measure that works

Take `size : Trace → ℕ` the ordinary constructor-count of a `Trace` term. Inspect the eight rules:

- `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`, `R_eq_refl` all replace the whole term by one of its **proper subterms** (or drop wrapper constructors), hence strictly **decrease** `size`.

- `R_rec_succ` (`recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`) and `R_eq_diff` (`eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`) can **increase** `size`, but the resulting term is, unconditionally, a `Step`-normal form:
  - The result of `R_rec_succ` has head symbol `app`. No LHS pattern in `Step` has `app` as its head constructor, so no rule can ever match this term, regardless of what `s`, `b`, `n` are.
  - The result of `R_eq_diff` has head symbol `integrate`, and the only rule with head `integrate` is `R_int_delta`, which requires the argument to have head `delta`. Here the argument is `merge a b`, whose head is `merge`, a different constructor from `delta`, so no unification is possible for *any* `a`, `b`. Hence `integrate (merge a b)` is always irreducible.

So at most **one** application of `R_rec_succ` or `R_eq_diff` can ever occur in a reduction sequence, and only as its final step (since it produces an unconditional normal form). All other steps strictly decrease the well-founded measure `size : Trace → ℕ`.

### Conclusion of the argument

Any `Step`-sequence therefore looks like: a (possibly empty) strictly `size`-decreasing chain via rules {1,2,3,4,5,7}, optionally followed by a single terminal application of rule 6 or rule 8. Since `ℕ` under `<` is well-founded, the decreasing chain is finite (bounded by the initial `size`), and the possible extra terminal step adds at most one more. Hence every reduction sequence from any `Trace` term is finite.

This argument uses only:
- structural induction on `Trace` (built into the inductive type itself),
- case analysis on the eight `Step` constructors,
- well-foundedness of `<` on `ℕ` (a basic, non-object-level fact),

with no additional rewrite rules, side conditions, congruence closure, or external axioms introduced.

### Bottom line

Strong normalization **can** be established from the calculus exactly as presented — in fact it holds for an almost trivial reason: the absence of any congruence/contextual closure in `Step` means reduction is confined to root-level pattern matching, six of the eight rules are strictly size-decreasing, and the two size-increasing rules always land on an unconditional normal form. A short structural/measure induction, with no extra imported assumptions, suffices to prove it.
