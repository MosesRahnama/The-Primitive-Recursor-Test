Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone. A standard proof method using a well-founded measure function suffices. For instance, define a lexicographic measure \( M(t) = (e(t), d(t), s(t)) \), where:
- \( e(t) \) is the number of `eqW` constructors in \( t \),
- \( d(t) \) is the total delta-depth (the sum of the number of `delta` constructors in the third argument of all `recΔ` subterms),
- \( s(t) \) is the total number of constructors (weight).

Each rewrite rule strictly decreases this measure:
- `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_*`, `R_rec_zero`, `R_eq_refl` decrease \( s(t) \) or \( e(t) \).
- `R_rec_succ` decreases \( d(t) \) (the delta-depth) even if \( s(t) \) increases.
- `R_eq_diff` decreases \( e(t) \).

Since the measure is a well-founded order on natural numbers, every rewrite sequence is finite, establishing strong normalization without additional axioms.
