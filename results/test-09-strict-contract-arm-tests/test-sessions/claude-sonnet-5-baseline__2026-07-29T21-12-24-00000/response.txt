## Assessment: **Yes — strong normalization can be established from this calculus alone, and moreover it holds for a somewhat trivial/structural reason.**

### Key observation: `Step` is *not* closed under congruence

The crucial fact is that `Step` as inductively defined contains **only root-level rewrite instances** — there is no clause of the form "if `Step t t'` then `Step (app t s) (app t' s))`" or any other congruence/compatibility rule for any constructor. So `Step a b` holds only when `a` as a *whole term* matches one of the eight literal left-hand-side patterns. Reduction cannot occur inside a subterm unless the entire term happens to be one of those patterns.

This drastically limits what reduction sequences look like, and makes strong normalization provable by a direct, elementary argument (no logical relations, no reducibility candidates needed).

### The argument

Classify the eight rules by effect on a simple size measure (number of constructors):

- `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`, `R_eq_refl`: each strictly **decreases** size (a proper subterm or `void` replaces a larger term).
- `R_rec_succ` : `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`. This **increases** size, but its result is `app`-headed. Since **no** LHS pattern in `Step` has `app` as its outermost constructor, this result is unconditionally `Step`-irreducible (a normal form).
- `R_eq_diff` : `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`. This also **increases** size, but its result is `integrate (merge a b)`. The only pattern with `integrate` as head is `integrate (delta t)`, and `merge a b` can never syntactically be `delta _` (distinct constructors), so this result too is always `Step`-irreducible.

Thus every rewrite step either:
1. strictly decreases a well-founded measure (term size, valued in ℕ), or
2. lands immediately on a term that is provably a `Step`-normal form (by exhaustive pattern mismatch, decidable via the constructor structure of `Trace`).

Consequently no infinite `Step`-sequence can exist: an infinite sequence would need infinitely many size-decreasing steps (impossible, since size is a natural number bounded below) interleaved with size-increasing steps, but every size-increasing step is immediately terminal. This is exactly the shape of a well-founded induction / measure argument, provable purely by case analysis on the finitely many constructors of `Step`, using structural induction on `Trace` for the measure — nothing beyond what is already available in the file.

### Conclusion

Strong normalization is derivable **within the system as given**, via:
- a straightforward size/measure function on `Trace`,
- exhaustive case inspection of the eight `Step` rules,
- the observation (itself just unfolding the inductive definition) that `app`-headed and `integrate(merge _ _)`-headed terms never match any `Step` left-hand side.

No congruence closure, extra rewrite rules, side conditions, or external axioms need to be assumed — the absence of contextual/congruence rules in `Step` is precisely what makes the proof short and purely structural, rather than requiring a nontrivial reducibility-candidates or logical-relations construction (which would be needed if `Step` were the full compatible closure of these rules, including reduction under `app`, `merge`, `integrate`, etc.).
