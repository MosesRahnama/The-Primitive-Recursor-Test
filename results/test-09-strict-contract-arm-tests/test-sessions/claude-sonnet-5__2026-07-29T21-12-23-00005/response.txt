## Assessment: **Yes** — strong normalization (SN) is provable from the calculus exactly as given, but the proof hinges on a subtle structural fact, not on a naive size argument.

### Step 1: Exhibit the failure of the naive measure (as required)

Let `M(t)` = number of constructors in `t`. Check the rule `R_rec_succ`:

```
recΔ b s (delta n)  →  app s (recΔ b s n)
```

Here `s` is duplicated: it occurs once on the left, twice on the right (S = s).

- `M(before) = M(recΔ b s (delta n)) = 2 + M(b) + M(s) + M(n)`
- `M(after)  = M(app s (recΔ b s n)) = 2 + M(b) + 2·M(s) + M(n)`

So `M(after) = M(before) - 1 + M(S)` with `S = s`, exactly the additive pattern the contract flags. Since every `Trace` has `M(·) ≥ 1` (even `void` has size 1), `M(S) ≥ 1` always, so `M(after) ≥ M(before)`: **no strict drop**, and in fact strict *increase* whenever `M(s) ≥ 1`, i.e. always. A plain size measure cannot certify termination through this rule.

### Step 2: The actual fix, and its required premise

The fix is *not* to add a rule, guard, or axiom. It is to observe a fact already forced by the given `Step` definition: **`Step` has no congruence/compatibility closure**. The inductive `Step` contains exactly 7 constructors, all relating whole terms at the *root*; there is no clause of the form `Step a b → Step (C a) (C b)`. Hence a term can only be reduced when its *entire* shape matches one of the seven left-hand-side patterns:
`integrate(delta _)`, `merge void _`, `merge _ void`, `merge t t`, `recΔ _ _ void`, `recΔ _ _ (delta _)`, `eqW a a`, `eqW _ _`.

**Key premise needed for the fix:** the result of `R_rec_succ`, namely `app s (recΔ b s n)`, is *permanently `Step`-irreducible*, for arbitrary `s, n`.

**Verification:** all seven LHS patterns are headed by `integrate`, `merge`, `recΔ`, or `eqW`. Since `Trace`'s constructors are pairwise disjoint (a basic, free fact about inductive types — not an imported axiom), a term headed by `app` can never unify with any LHS pattern. So `Step (app s (recΔ b s n)) u` holds for no `u`. The premise discharges. (Symmetrically, `void`, `delta t` are always irreducible, and `integrate(merge a b)` from `R_eq_diff` is irreducible because `merge a b` can never equal `delta t`, again by constructor disjointness.)

### Step 3: Completed SN proof by strong induction on size

For every `t`, and every `u` with `Step t u`, one of two mutually exhaustive cases holds:

- **(i) Terminal case** (`int_delta`, `rec_succ`, `eq_refl`, `eq_diff`): `u ∈ {void, app(...), integrate(merge _ _)}`, which is *unconditionally* `Step`-irreducible (shown above) — hence trivially SN, independent of size.
- **(ii) Projection case** (`merge_void_left/right`, `merge_cancel`, `rec_zero`): `u` is a strict subterm of `t`, so `M(u) < M(t)` strictly.

By strong induction on `M(t) ∈ ℕ`: assume all terms of smaller size are SN. For any `u` with `Step t u`, either (i) `u` is trivially SN, or (ii) `M(u) < M(t)` so `u` is SN by IH. Hence all successors of `t` are SN, so `t` is SN. This closes the induction for all `t`.

### Conclusion

The duplication in `R_rec_succ` is real and defeats a plain size-decrease argument, exactly as the contract anticipates. But it is harmless here because the calculus, as literally specified, applies `Step` only at the root (no congruence closure), and the one rule that duplicates a subterm always produces an `app`-headed term that is provably a permanent normal form by constructor disjointness — a fact intrinsic to the given syntax, requiring no extra rules or side conditions. With that premise discharged, strong induction on term size gives strong normalization of the whole system, established purely from the presented syntax and rewrite rules.
