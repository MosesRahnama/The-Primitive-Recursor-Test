**Assessment: Strong normalization can be established.**

**Reasoning:**

The system is first-order and acyclic. No rewrite rule introduces a constructor that appears on the left-hand side of another rule in a way that creates infinite regeneration:

1.  **No duplication in single steps:** No rule duplicates a subterm $S$ in the sense that $M(\text{after}) = M(\text{before}) - 1 + M(S)$ with $M(S) \geq 1$. The only rule that increases the simple term size is `R_eq_diff` ($|eqW\ a\ b| \leadsto |integrate\ (merge\ a\ b)|$ increases by 1), but `eqW` never appears on the right-hand side of any rule, so this step cannot loop.

2.  **Recursion terminates:** The `recΔ` rules implement primitive recursion. In `R_rec_succ`, the third argument (the "counter" $n$) loses a `delta` constructor. Although the step embeds the result inside `app s`, the recursion variable strictly decreases. This can be captured by assigning an ordinal measure $\omega \cdot M(n) + M(b) + M(s)$ to `recΔ b s n`, where $M$ counts constructors. Since $\omega \cdot (k+1) > \omega \cdot k + c$ for any finite $c$, the step strictly decreases the ordinal.

3.  **Other rules decrease:** `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_*`, and `R_rec_zero` all strictly decrease the multiset of constructors or a simple weight. `R_eq_refl` also decreases.

Using a Recursive Path Ordering (RPO) with precedence $rec\Delta > app$ (and lexicographic status on the third argument of $rec\Delta$), $eqW > integrate > merge$, and $\delta > void$, all rules decrease. Since the ordering is well-founded, the system is strongly normalizable without additional axioms.
