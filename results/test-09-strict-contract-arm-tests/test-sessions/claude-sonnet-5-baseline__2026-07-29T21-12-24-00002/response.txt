## Assessment: Strong normalization holds, and can be proved from the rules alone.

**Method:** A recursive/lexicographic path ordering (RPO/LPO) with a suitable precedence on the seven constructors suffices to orient every rule strictly downward, which is enough to conclude SN (well‑foundedness of a syntactic path order that orients all rules is a standard, self-contained termination technique requiring no external assumptions).

**Precedence used:**
`eqW ≻ integrate ≻ merge`, and `recΔ ≻ app`, with all other relations filled in arbitrarily/transitively (no cycle is forced).

**Checking each rule:**

- `R_merge_void_left/right`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`: the RHS is a *proper subterm* of the LHS in each case, so they are oriented for free by the subterm property of any path order.

- `R_int_delta` (`integrate (delta t) → void`) and `R_eq_refl` (`eqW a a → void`): `void` is a nullary symbol, so the "different head symbol" clause of RPO applies vacuously once `integrate ≻ void` and `eqW ≻ void` (the latter following transitively from `eqW ≻ integrate ≻ void`).

- `R_eq_diff` (`eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`): using `eqW ≻ integrate ≻ merge` together with the fact that `a, b` are proper subterms of the LHS (`l ≻ a`, `l ≻ b`), RPO's recursive clause gives `l ≻ merge(a,b)` and then `l ≻ integrate(merge(a,b))`.

- `R_rec_succ` (`recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`), the only *duplicating* rule (the subterm `s` occurs twice on the right): this is exactly the classical primitive‑recursion pattern that LPO/RPO is designed to handle. Comparing `l = recΔ(b,s,delta n)` to `r = app(s, recΔ(b,s,n))`: `l ≻ s` by the subterm property, and `l ≻ recΔ(b,s,n)` because with `recΔ` given lexicographic status, the argument tuples `(b,s,delta n)` and `(b,s,n)` agree in the first two positions and `delta n ≻ n` (subterm) in the third. Since `recΔ ≻ app` in the precedence, this yields `l ≻ r`.

No cyclic precedence constraints arise (the required relations `eqW≻integrate≻merge`, `integrate≻void`, `recΔ≻app` are mutually consistent), so a genuine well-founded path order exists that strictly decreases across every rewrite step.

**On the rule overlap (`eqW a a` matching both `R_eq_refl` and `R_eq_diff`):** this affects confluence, not termination — both branches (`→ void` directly, or `→ integrate(merge a a) → integrate a`, possibly stuck thereafter) are themselves finite reduction paths, and the path-order argument above already accounts for both rules independently.

**Conclusion:** Since a single, uniform, well-founded RPO/LPO orients all eight rules strictly, every rewrite step strictly decreases in this order, so there is no infinite `Step`-sequence from any `Trace` term. Strong normalization is derivable directly from the given inductive syntax and the eight rules, with no additional axioms, side conditions, or external assumptions needed.
