**Reasoning**:  
The reduction relation `Step` applies only at the top level – there are no congruence rules that allow rewriting under constructors.  Every reduction sequence starting from any finite `Trace` term is therefore finite:

* **Size‑decreasing rules** (1–5, 7) strictly shrink a natural size measure (e.g., total number of constructors).  
* **Rule 6** (`recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`) increases size but produces an `app`‑headed term, and **no `Step` rule applies to `app`**. Thus this step always leads to an irreducible term.  
* **Rule 8** (`eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`) also increases size, but `integrate (merge …)` matches no left‑hand side of any rule (`integrate` only reduces when its argument is `delta …`). Hence it is also a terminal (normal) form.

Consequently, any reduction sequence consists of zero or more size‑decreasing steps, optionally followed by a single step via rule 6 or rule 8 that ends the sequence. Since the size is a non‑negative integer, the length of every sequence is bounded, proving strong normalization. The argument uses only the inductive definitions of `Trace` and `Step`, structural induction, and basic arithmetic – no external structural axioms or hidden side conditions are required.
