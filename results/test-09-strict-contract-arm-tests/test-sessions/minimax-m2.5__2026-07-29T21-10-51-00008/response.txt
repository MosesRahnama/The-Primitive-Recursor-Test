**Assessment:** Yes. The rewrite system is terminating, so strong normalisation can be proved from the calculus alone, using only the syntactic objects and the eight rewrite rules.

**Why it terminates (high‑level sketch).**  
Assign a well‑founded measure \(M(t)=\bigl(d(t),\,w(t)\bigr)\) where  

* \(d(t)\) = total number of \(\delta\)‑layers that appear in the third argument of any \(\mathsf{rec\Delta}\) subterm of \(t\) (i.e. the sum of the depths of \(\delta\) in those positions);  
* \(w(t)\) = a weighted sum of the constructors, with the following integer weights  

\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{constructor}&\text{weight}\\\hline
\mathsf{void}&0\\
\delta&1\\
\mathsf{integrate}&1\\
\mathsf{merge}&1\\
\mathsf{app}&0\\
\mathsf{rec\Delta}&1\\
\mathsf{eqW}&3
\end{array}
\]

The weight of a term is the sum of the weights of its outermost constructor plus the weights of its direct sub‑terms.  

Now we check each rewrite rule:

| Rule | \(M\)-comparison |
|------|-------------------|
| \(\mathsf{integrate}(\delta t)\to\mathsf{void}\) | \(d\) drops by 1 (the \(\delta\) disappears) and \(w\) drops by 2. |
| \(\mathsf{merge}\,\mathsf{void}\,t\to t\) | \(w\) drops by 1 (the \(\mathsf{merge}\) disappears). |
| \(\mathsf{merge}\,t\,\mathsf{void}\to t\) | same. |
| \(\mathsf{merge}\,t\,t\to t\) | \(w\) drops by \(1+ w(t)\). |
| \(\mathsf{rec\Delta}\,b\,s\,\mathsf{void}\to b\) | \(d\) drops (the argument is void) and \(w\) drops by \(1+w(s)\). |
| \(\mathsf{rec\Delta}\,b\,s\,(\delta n)\to\mathsf{app}\,s\,(\mathsf{rec\Delta}\,b\,s\,n)\) | the first component \(d\) decreases by exactly 1 (one \(\delta\) is removed); the second component may increase, but the lexicographic order prioritises the decrease in \(d\). |
| \(\mathsf{eqW}\,a\,a\to\mathsf{void}\) | \(w\) drops by 3. |
| \(\mathsf{eqW}\,a\,b\to\mathsf{integrate}(\mathsf{merge}\,a\,b)\) | \(w\) drops by 1 (3 → 2). |

All rules strictly decrease the lexicographic pair \((d,w)\); because \((ℕ,\le)\) is well‑founded, every reduction sequence is finite. Hence the calculus is strongly normalising.

---

### Dealing with the duplication step (contract)

1. **Duplicating subterm:** the rule  
   \(\mathsf{eqW}\,a\,b \to \mathsf{integrate}(\mathsf{merge}\,a\,b)\) copies both \(a\) and \(b\) on the right‑hand side.

2. **Additive failure with a naïve measure:**  
   Take the simple size measure \(|t|\) = total number of constructor symbols.  
   For the above rule,
   \[
   |\mathsf{integrate}(\mathsf{merge}\,a\,b)|
   = |a|+|b|+2
   = (|a|+|b|+1) - 1 + (|a|+|b|)
   = |\mathsf{eqW}\,a\,b| - 1 + |a| + |b|.
   \]
   Since \(|a|,|b|\ge 1\) for any non‑void term, the right‑hand side is **not** strictly smaller; the inequality \(|\text{after}| < |\text{before}|\) can fail.

3. **Fix:** replace the naïve size by the weighted sum \(w(t)\) above.  
   With \(w(\mathsf{eqW})=3\) and \(w(\mathsf{integrate})=w(\mathsf{merge})=1\) we obtain
   \[
   w(\mathsf{eqW}\,a\,b) = 3 + w(a) + w(b) 
   > 2 + w(a) + w(b) = w(\mathsf{integrate}(\mathsf{merge}\,a\,b)).
   \]
   Hence the duplication is compensated by the extra weight of the \(\mathsf{eqW}\) constructor.

4. **Key premise and verification:**  
   The premise is that there exists an integer weight assignment to the constructors such that for *every* rewrite rule the total weight of the left‑hand side is strictly larger than that of the right‑hand side.  
   The table above explicitly computes the weight differences for all eight rules; each difference is a positive integer (or, for the \(\mathsf{rec\Delta}\) rule, the first component \(d\) strictly decreases). Therefore the premise holds.

Because the premise is satisfied, the weighted measure (combined with the delta‑depth component) furnishes a decreasing measure for every rewrite step, proving strong normalisation without any extra axioms.
