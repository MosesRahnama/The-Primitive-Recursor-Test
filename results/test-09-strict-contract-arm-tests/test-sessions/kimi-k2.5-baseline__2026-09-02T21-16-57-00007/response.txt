**Assessment: Yes, strong normalization can be established.**

**Reasoning:**

The system is strongly normalizing because it admits a well-founded measure based on the interplay between the `delta` constructor and the recursion operator `recΔ`:

1.  **No creation of `delta`**: Critically, no rewrite rule introduces a `delta` constructor on the right-hand side. The `delta` count in any term is therefore non-increasing and strictly decreases when `R_int_delta` or `R_rec_succ` applies.

2.  **Bounded recursion**: The rule `R_rec_succ` (`recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`) performs structural recursion on the third argument. Each application consumes one `delta` wrapper from the third argument (reducing `delta n` to `n`). Since no rule can create new `delta`s, each instance of `recΔ` can fire at most *d*+1 times, where *d* is the initial number of `delta` constructors in its third argument. Although this rule duplicates the parameter `s`, the duplicated subterms are not in a position to regenerate the consumed `delta` in the third argument of the same `recΔ` instance.

3.  **Termination of other rules**: The remaining rules (`R_int_delta`, the `merge` rules, `R_rec_zero`, `R_eq_refl`, `R_eq_diff`) are all size-decreasing or eliminate constructors without creating new redexes that could lead to unbounded growth. The non-left-linear rules (`R_merge_cancel`, `R_eq_refl`) are collapsing rules that strictly decrease term size.

A suitable lexicographic measure combines (1) the multiset of `delta`-depths of all `recΔ` third arguments (which decreases with each `R_rec_succ` application regardless of duplication), and (2) the general term size. This establishes that every reduction sequence is finite.
