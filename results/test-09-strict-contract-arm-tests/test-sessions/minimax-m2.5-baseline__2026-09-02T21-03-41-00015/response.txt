**Answer:** Yes – the rewrite system is terminating.

**Why:** Each Step‑rule strictly decreases some syntactic measure that can be defined on terms. For example, count the number of outermost constructors of the following kinds (lexicographically ordered):

1. `eqW` – it disappears after at most two rewrites (`eqW a b → integrate (merge a b) →* void`).
2. The number of `delta` nesting in the third argument of a `recΔ` – the rule `R_rec_succ` replaces `recΔ … (delta n)` by `app … (recΔ … n)`, removing one `delta`.
3. The total size (number of constructor symbols) – every other rule (`R_int_delta`, `R_merge_*`, `R_rec_zero`, `R_eq_refl`) removes at least one constructor from the term and does not create new ones of a “higher” rank.

Thus every rewrite step makes this pair smaller in the lexicographic product, which is a well‑founded order. No rule can increase any component while decreasing a smaller one, and there is no cycle. Consequently all rewrite sequences are finite, i.e. the system is strongly normalising, and the proof can be carried out using ordinary structural induction on the term syntax, with no extra axioms beyond the given inductive definitions and rewrite rules.
