## Assessment

**Yes — strong normalization is derivable directly from this file, and in fact rather easily, for a structural reason that is worth making explicit.**

### Key observation: `Step` has no congruence/context closure

The inductive definition of `Step` contains *only* the eight listed root‑level rewrite axioms. There is **no** compatibility rule of the form

```
Step t t' → Step (delta t) (delta t')
Step t t' → Step (merge t s) (merge t' s)
...
```

etc. So `Step` is *not* the usual "one‑step reduction inside a context" relation of a term‑rewriting system — it only relates whole terms that match one of the eight fixed shapes at the root. A subterm buried inside `app`, `delta`, or as an argument of `integrate`/`merge`/`recΔ`/`eqW` that doesn't itself match a LHS pattern is simply inert; `Step` cannot reach into it.

### Consequence: every term admits only very short `Step`-chains

Going rule by rule:

- `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left/right`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`: each rewrites to a proper subterm of the LHS, strictly decreasing size.
- `R_eq_refl`, `R_eq_diff`: rewrite `eqW a b` to `void` or to `integrate (merge a b)`. The latter is headed by `integrate` applied to a `merge`-term (not a `delta`-term), so **no further rule matches it** — it is a `Step`-normal form regardless of what `a`, `b` are.
- `R_rec_succ`: this is the only size-increasing rule, `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`. But the result is headed by `app`, and **no rule has `app` as an outermost LHS pattern at all** — so this result is *always* a normal form, immediately, no matter how deep `n` or `s` are. The "recursive" structure `recΔ b s n` sitting inside is never unfolded further because there is no context rule to reach it.

Thus every possible one-step reduction either strictly shrinks a term-size measure, or lands in a term whose head symbol (`void`, `delta`, `app`, or a non-matching `integrate`/`merge`/`recΔ`/`eqW` combination) admits no further `Step` at all. There is no mechanism by which reduction can "return" to a larger redex or cycle, because nothing ever descends into subterms to re-expose them as new root redexes.

### Proof strategy available internally

This can be proved by ordinary well-founded induction on `Trace` (matching the derived structure/`sizeOf`), with a case split:
- for the six size-decreasing rules, immediate;
- for `R_eq_diff` and `R_rec_succ`, the successor term is shown directly (by a one-line case analysis on its head constructor and the absence of a matching LHS pattern) to be `Step`-irreducible.

From this one gets, without any extra axiom, that every `Step`-sequence has length at most 1 beyond an initial redex step (in the two exceptional cases) or is strictly decreasing (in the others) — hence no infinite `Step`-chain exists, i.e. the system is strongly normalizing.

### Caveat

This SN result is essentially a byproduct of the fact that the given `Step` relation is *not* closed under term contexts. If one were to (elsewhere) close `Step` under congruence to get a genuine compatible rewriting relation, the argument above would no longer apply, and termination would become a substantive question (plausibly still true here, given the shape of the rules, but not something forced by the present file). Restricted strictly to what is stated in this file, however, strong normalization is fully derivable from the syntax and the eight rules alone.
