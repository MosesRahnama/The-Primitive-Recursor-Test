**Assessment** – Yes.  Strong normalisation (SN) follows from the rewrite rules alone.  
No extra axioms or structural assumptions are required; a purely syntactic
measure that descends lexicographically on three simple counters suffices.

---

### 1.  Why a naïve “total‑size” measure fails  

Let  

\[
M_0(t)=\text{total number of constructor symbols in }t .
\]

* **R\_{\text{eq\_diff}}**  

\[
t=eqW\,a\,b\qquad
t' = \ integrate\,(merge\,a\,b)
\]

\[
M_0(t)=1+|a|+|b|,\qquad
M_0(t')=2+|a|+|b| = M_0(t)+1 .
\]

The step *increases* the measure; we obtain the additive failure  

\[
M_0(t')=M_0(t)-1+M_0(a)+M_0(b) \quad(\text{with }M_0(a)+M_0(b)\ge 1).
\]

* **R\_{\text{rec\_succ}}**  

\[
t = recΔ\,b\,s\,(δ\,n),\qquad
t' = app\,s\,(recΔ\,b\,s\,n)
\]

\[
M_0(t)=2+|b|+|s|+|n|,\qquad
M_0(t')=2+|b|+2|s|+|n| = M_0(t)+|s|.
\]

If \(s\) is non‑void then \(|s|\ge 1\) and the measure grows; again an additive
failure occurs because the right‑hand side *duplicates* the subterm \(s\).

Thus a single numeric measure cannot guarantee decrease for all rules.

---

### 2.  Fix – a lexicographic triple  

Define three counters on terms:

* **\(E(t)\)** – number of occurrences of the constructor `eqW`;
* **\(D(t)\)** – number of occurrences of the constructor `delta`;
* **\(S(t)\)** – total number of constructor symbols (the naïve size).

Set  

\[
M(t) \;:=\; \bigl(E(t),\,D(t),\,S(t)\bigr)
\]

and order triples lexicographically (first component most significant).  
All three components are natural numbers, so the lexicographic order is well‑founded.

---

### 3.  Verification of each rewrite rule  

We check that every rule makes \(M\) decrease in the lexicographic sense.

| Rule | Effect on \((E,D,S)\) |
|------|----------------------|
| **R\_{\text{int\_delta}}** `integrate (delta t) → void` | \(E\) unchanged (0), \(D\) decreases by 1 (the leading `delta` disappears), \(S\) also decreases. |
| **R\_{\text{merge\_void\_left}}** `merge void t → t` | \(E,D\) unchanged, \(S\) decreases by 1 (the leading `merge` is removed). |
| **R\_{\text{merge\_void\_right}}** `merge t void → t` | Same as above. |
| **R\_{\text{merge\_cancel}}** `merge t t → t` | \(E,D\) unchanged, \(S\) decreases (the left‑hand side has two copies of \(t\) plus a `merge`). |
| **R\_{\text{rec\_zero}}** `recΔ b s void → b` | \(E,D\) unchanged, \(S\) decreases (the `recΔ` and `void` disappear). |
| **R\_{\text{rec\_succ}}** `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)` | No `eqW` ⇒ \(E\) stays 0. The leading `delta` in the third argument is removed, so **\(D\) decreases by 1**; \(S\) may increase but is lower priority. |
| **R\_{\text{eq\_refl}}** `eqW a a → void` | **\(E\) decreases by 1** (the only `eqW` is eliminated); \(D\) and \(S\) also drop. |
| **R\_{\text{eq\_diff}}** `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` | **\(E\) decreases by 1** (the `eqW` disappears); \(D\) unchanged; \(S\) grows but is lexicographically behind \(E\). |

In every case at least one of the three components strictly decreases, and the
component that changes is **more significant** than the others.  Consequently
\(M(t') <_{\text{lex}} M(t)\) for each applicable rewrite step.

---

### 4.  Key premise  

*The premise* is that the three counters are defined purely from the syntax
(and are therefore intrinsic to every term) and that the lexicographic order
on \(\mathbb{N}^3\) is well‑founded.  Both are true in the given system:
\(E\) and \(D\) count occurrences of particular constructors; \(S\) is the sum
of all constructor symbols.  No extra rewrite rules, guards, or hidden
assumptions are needed.

Because each rewrite step reduces the triple, there cannot be an infinite
descending chain of terms; hence the rewrite system is **strongly normalising**.

---

**Conclusion** – Strong normalisation follows from the calculus alone.  The
lexicographic measure \((E,D,S)\) provides a terminating ranking for all
eight rewrite rules, and no external axioms are required.  No constraint
blocker arises.
