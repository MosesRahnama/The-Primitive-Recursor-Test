**Assessment:** Yes – the rewrite system is strongly normalising and this can be proved from the calculus alone.

**Why:**  
The eight rewrite rules form a first‑order term‑rewriting system in which every step removes a “redex” pattern and strictly decreases a certain well‑founded measure. A convenient measure is a lexicographic tuple  

\[
M(t)=\bigl(\#_{\text{eqW}}(t),\ \#_{\text{recΔ}}(t),\ \#_{\text{delta}}(t),\ |t|\bigr)
\]

where  

* \(\#_{\text{eqW}}(t)\) is the number of top‑level `eqW` constructors in `t`,  
* \(\#_{\text{recΔ}}(t)\) the number of top‑level `recΔ` constructors,  
* \(\#_{\text{delta}}(t)\) the total number of `delta` constructors occurring in the third argument of a `recΔ` (or any `delta`), and  
* \(|t|\) the ordinary size (total number of constructors).

One checks each rule:

* `R_int_delta` removes a `delta` inside `integrate`.  
* `R_merge_void_left/right` and `R_merge_cancel` delete a `merge` and a `void` (or a duplicate argument), decreasing \(|t|\).  
* `R_rec_zero` eliminates a `recΔ` (decreases \(\#_{\text{recΔ}}\)).  
* `R_rec_succ` removes the leading `delta` from the third argument of `recΔ`, decreasing \(\#_{\text{delta}}\).  
* `R_eq_refl` deletes an `eqW` (decreases \(\#_{\text{eqW}}\)).  
* `R_eq_diff` also removes the `eqW` (primary component \(\#_{\text{eqW}}\) goes down) while possibly increasing size; the primary component dominates the lexicographic order.

No rule ever increases the primary component of the measure, and every rule reduces either that primary component or, if it is unchanged, one of the later components. Consequently every rewrite step strictly decreases \(M(t)\) in a well‑founded lexicographic order, guaranteeing that no infinite rewrite sequence exists.

Thus, using a standard measure‑or‑recursive‑path‑ordering argument, strong normalisation follows directly from the given syntax and rewrite rules, with no additional axioms or side conditions.
