**Raw assessment:** Yes, strong normalization can be established from the presented calculus alone without importing any external axioms.

**Reasoning (following the required contract for the duplication step):**  
The only rule that duplicates a subterm is  

`Step (recΔ b s (delta n)) (app s (recΔ b s n))`  

which copies `s`. Take the natural size measure `M(t)` = number of constructors in `t`.  
- `M(before) = M(recΔ b s (delta n)) = M(b) + M(s) + M(n) + 2`  
- `M(after)  = M(app s (recΔ b s n)) = M(b) + 2·M(s) + M(n) + 2`  

Hence `M(after) = M(before) - 1 + M(s)`. When `M(s) ≥ 1` there is no strict drop – the size can increase.  

**Fix:** The system admits *only root reductions*. The inductive `Step` relation has no congruence rules; it never reduces inside `app`, `merge`, `integrate`, `delta`, `recΔ`, or `eqW`. After the duplication step the whole term becomes an `app`, and no `Step` rule matches an `app`-headed term. Therefore a term can take at most one `rec_succ` step (and then stops), regardless of how large `s` is. All other rules strictly decrease size. Every reduction sequence is finite.

**Key premise:** The reduction relation is exactly the top‑level closure given by the rules, with *no* context closure.  
**Verification:** Inspecting the inductive `Step` shows eight rules, each with a concrete root constructor (`integrate`, `merge`, `recΔ`, `eqW`). There is no rule of the form “if `Step t t'` then `Step (C[t]) (C[t'])`”. Thus only redexes at the root are ever contracted.

Since the duplication is confined to a single, dead‑end step and the rest of the system is obviously terminating, strong normalization holds trivially. No structural axioms or hidden assumptions are required.
