**Assessment: Yes, strong normalization holds.**

**Reasoning:**

The system admits a well-founded lexicographic measure that strictly decreases with every rewrite step. Define the complexity of a term as the tuple:

\[
\langle \#_{\text{eqW}},\; \#_{\delta},\; \#_{\text{integrate}},\; \#_{\text{merge}},\; \#_{\text{recΔ}},\; \text{size} \rangle
\]

where \(\#_c\) counts occurrences of constructor \(c\), ordered lexicographically.

1.  **`eqW` elimination (rules 7–8):**  
    Both `R_eq_refl` and `R_eq_diff` remove the outermost `eqW` constructor. No rule in the system introduces a new `eqW` (the conclusions contain only `void`, variables, `integrate`, `merge`, `app`, or `recΔ`). Hence the first component \(\#_{\text{eqW}}\) strictly decreases and never increases.

2.  **`delta` elimination (rules 1, 6):**  
    Once \(\#_{\text{eqW}} = 0\), we consider the second component. Rules `R_int_delta` and `R_rec_succ` each consume one `delta` constructor. No rule creates a `delta` (the right-hand sides of all rules are built from variables and non-`delta` constructors). Thus \(\#_{\delta}\) strictly decreases until it reaches 0.

3.  **Remaining constructors:**  
    With no `eqW` or `delta` remaining:
    *   `R_int_delta` is inapplicable (requires `delta`).
    *   `R_rec_succ` is inapplicable (requires `delta` in the third argument).
    *   `R_rec_zero` removes a `recΔ` (decreasing \(\#_{\text{recΔ}}\)).
    *   The `merge` rules (2–4) each remove one `merge` constructor (decreasing \(\#_{\text{merge}}\)).
    *   `integrate` on non-`delta` arguments is inert.

Since every possible rewrite step decreases this lexicographic measure and the measure is well-founded (a tuple of natural numbers), every reduction sequence must terminate. No hidden cycles or expansions exist because the only "expansive" rule (`R_eq_diff`) trades one `eqW` for `integrate` and `merge`, both of which are ranked lower in the measure than `eqW`, and neither `integrate` nor `merge` can reproduce `eqW` or `delta`.

Therefore, the calculus is strongly normalizing without requiring additional axioms or side conditions.
