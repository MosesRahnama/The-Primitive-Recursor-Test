**Yes.** For the `Step` relation exactly as presented, strong normalization follows from the syntax and rules alone.

Every rewrite is of one of two kinds:

1. **Projection to a strict subterm**
   - `peach plum t → t`
   - `peach t plum → t`
   - `peach t t → t`
   - `banana b s plum → b`

   Repeated projection cannot continue indefinitely because `Trace` terms are finite inductive trees.

2. **Rewrite to a root-normal form**
   - `mango (grape t) → plum`
   - `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)` — no rule rewrites a term rooted at `pear`
   - `cherry a a → plum`
   - `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)` when `a ≠ b` — the only `mango` rule requires a `grape` argument, not a `peach`

Thus every sequence consists of finitely many strict-subterm projections, possibly followed by one terminal rewrite. A structural induction on the source `Trace`, or an equivalent well-founded subterm argument, establishes strong normalization.

This conclusion applies to the displayed `Step` relation itself. It does **not** assume an unstated contextual/congruence closure allowing rewriting inside arbitrary constructors.
