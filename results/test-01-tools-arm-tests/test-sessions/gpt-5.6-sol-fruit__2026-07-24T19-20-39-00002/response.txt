**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the calculus exactly as presented.

The crucial point is that `Step` contains **no contextual/congruence rules**: rewriting occurs only at the root of a `Trace`.

Every permitted step is therefore either:

- a reduction to `plum`, which is irreducible;
- a reduction to a strict subterm (`peach …`, `banana … plum`), so finite term size/depth decreases; or
- one of the apparently expanding steps:
  - `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`, whose result has root `pear`;
  - `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)`, whose result has root `mango` but not of the reducible form `mango (grape t)`.

Both expanding targets are root-normal because no rule rewrites `pear`, and the only `mango` rule requires a `grape` argument. Thus an infinite `Step` sequence cannot arise. A straightforward well-founded measure or direct accessibility proof by structural induction establishes strong normalization without additional axioms, guards, or rewrite rules.

This assessment depends on the literal relation shown. A separately added closure under rewriting inside constructors would be a strengthened relation and is not part of this Lean file.
