**Assessment: Yes. The system is strongly normalizing, and this can be proved using only the syntax and rules shown.**

**Reasoning.**  
Treat the calculus as a standard first-order term-rewriting system (closed under contexts).  Define four structural measures on a `Trace` by plain recursion on the inductive type:

1. **G(t)** – total number of `grape` constructors in `t`.
2. **C(t)** – total number of `cherry` constructors in `t`.
3. **B(t)** – total number of `banana` constructors in `t`.
4. **S(t)** – total number of constructors (size) of `t`.

Now inspect each rule:

- **R_mango_grape** and **R_apple_orange** each consume one `grape`, so **G** strictly decreases.
- **R_cherry_refl** and **R_cherry_diff** each consume one `cherry`, so **C** strictly decreases.
- **R_banana_zero** consumes one `banana`, so **B** strictly decreases.
- The **peach** rules (2–4) leave **G**, **C**, and **B** unchanged but strictly decrease **S**.
- **R_cherry_diff** does increase **S**, but it decreases **C**, so it is already handled by the lexicographic ordering.

Crucially, *no right-hand side of any rule introduces a new `grape`, `cherry`, or `banana`*; those constructors are only copied from subterms of the left-hand side or removed entirely. Therefore none of **G**, **C**, or **B** can ever increase, and at least one of the four measures strictly decreases on *every* step.

Hence the map  
`t ↦ (G(t), C(t), B(t), S(t))`  
is a measure into `ℕ⁴` with the lexicographic order, which is well-founded.  Because every rewrite step makes this tuple smaller, there cannot be an infinite reduction sequence.

This argument uses only:
- structural recursion on `Trace` (available from the given inductive definition),
- basic arithmetic on natural numbers, and
- the standard well-foundedness of the lexicographic product of `ℕ`.

No additional rewrite rules, side conditions, guards, or object-level axioms about `Trace` are required.  Consequently, strong normalization is derivable from the presented calculus alone.
