### Why the system is well‑suited to a direct termination proof

**1. No binding, no substitution, no genuine "computation into" a duplicated subterm.**
The only rule that duplicates a subterm is
`R_rec_succ : recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`.
This looks like the System‑T recursor/iterator, whose strong normalization is classically proved via Tait's reducibility‑candidates method. But that heavy machinery is needed only because in a typed λ‑calculus the duplicated term `s` can later be *applied* (β‑reduced) and its behavior must be tracked through arbitrarily deep instantiation. Here `app` never appears on the left‑hand side of any rule — there is no rule of the form `Step (app x y) _`. So `app s t` is **inert**: nothing ever "runs" `s`; it is simply carried along as inactive syntax. This removes the higher‑order phenomenon that forces Tait's method, and reduces the problem to an ordinary first‑order term‑rewriting termination question.

**2. `delta` is never produced by any rule.**
Scanning all eight right‑hand sides (`void`, `t`, `app s (recΔ b s n)`, `integrate (merge a b)`, `b`, `void`), the constructor `delta` occurs on **no** right‑hand side. Hence the "unfolding budget" for `R_rec_succ`/`R_int_delta` (the delta‑nesting depth of the term being recursed on) can only shrink along any occurrence, never regrow. Duplicating `s` can raise the *total* symbol count, but it cannot manufacture new recursion depth anywhere — it only copies already‑fixed, finite structure.   I(recΔ b s t) = I(b) + I(t)·(I(s)+1),  I(app s t) = I(s) + I(t)
