**Assessment: yes.** Strong normalization can be established from the presented syntax and rules alone.

As Lean defines it, `Step` performs only **root-level** rewriting—there are no congruence rules allowing reduction inside constructors. The rules either:

- return a strict subterm (`merge` rules and `R_rec_zero`), or
- terminate immediately at `void`, or
- produce a term whose outer constructor has no applicable rule (`app …` or `integrate (merge … …)`).

Thus an infinite `Step` sequence is impossible.

Moreover, even if the intended relation were the usual contextual closure, the rules can be oriented by a standard well-founded recursive/lexicographic path ordering—for example with `recΔ ≻ app`, `eqW ≻ integrate ≻ merge`, and `integrate ≻ void`. The recursive call in `R_rec_succ` strictly decreases from `delta n` to `n`; the remaining rules either eliminate a defined symbol or select a subterm. The overlapping `eqW a a` rules introduce nondeterminism but no cycle.

So no additional structural axioms, guards, or object-level assumptions are needed for strong normalization.
