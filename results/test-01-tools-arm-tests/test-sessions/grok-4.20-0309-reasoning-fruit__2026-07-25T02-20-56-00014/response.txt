**No, strong normalization cannot be established from the presented calculus alone.**

The inductive definition of `Step` gives a purely syntactic, top-level one-step relation with exactly the eight listed rules (no congruence/context rules are present). Termination is intuitively plausible because:

- Most rules replace a term with a proper subterm (`peach` rules), a constant (`plum`), or an irreducible form whose head constructor (`pear`, or `mango` whose argument is not syntactically `grape _`) admits no further rule.
- The `banana` unfolding rule increases size but produces a `pear`-headed term that matches no LHS, so the chain stops.
- `cherry` either collapses to `plum` or produces `mango (peach a b)`, which is irreducible unless `peach a b` is *syntactically* of the form `grape _`.

However, rigorously proving there are no infinite chains requires facts that are *not* derivable from the `Step` constructors:

- **Disjointness / no-confusion**: e.g., no term is both a `peach _ _` and a `grape _`, so `mango (peach a b)` never matches `R_mango_grape`. Without this, one cannot rule out further reduction.
- **Injectivity** and the theory of equality needed to justify the side condition `{a b} (a ≠ b)` in `R_cherry_diff` and to perform exhaustive case analysis on the head constructor of an arbitrary `Trace`.
- Acyclicity of constructors (no infinite descending chains arising from identifications that the rewrite rules themselves do not prohibit).

These are the structural axioms automatically supplied by Lean’s `inductive` command. Any proof of well-foundedness (by induction on `Trace`, by a lexicographic measure on constructor depth + third-argument “numerals” for `banana`, or by case analysis on which rule can fire) must appeal to them. The rewrite rules alone supply no such distinctions, so SN is not provable within the strict boundary given. Adding explicit guards, extra rewrite rules, or congruence would violate the problem constraints.
