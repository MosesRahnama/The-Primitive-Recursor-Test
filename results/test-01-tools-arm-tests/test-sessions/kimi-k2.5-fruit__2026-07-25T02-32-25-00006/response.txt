**Assessment: Yes, strong normalization can be established.**

**Reasoning:** This first-order rewrite system terminates, as can be proven by the **recursive path ordering (RPO)** with the following precedence on constructors:
$$\text{cherry} \;>\; \text{banana} \;>\; \text{mango} \;>\; \text{pear} \;>\; \text{peach} \;>\; \text{grape} \;>\; \text{plum}$$

Using lexicographic status for all function symbols, every rewrite rule decreases the term in this ordering:

- **Rules 1, 7, 8:** The head symbol decreases (mango > plum, cherry > plum, cherry > mango).
- **Rules 2, 3, 4, 5:** The right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left-hand side.
- **Rule 6:** `banana(b, s, grape(n))` → `pear(s, banana(b, s, n))`. Since `banana > pear`, we need the LHS to be greater than both arguments of `pear`. The LHS is greater than `s` (proper subterm). For `banana(b, s, n)`, we compare lexicographically: the first two arguments are identical, and at the third position `grape(n) > n` (constructor over its subterm), so `banana(b, s, grape(n)) > banana(b, s, n)`. The duplication of `s` is handled automatically because RPO only requires the LHS to dominate each subterm of the RHS individually.

Since every reduction step decreases a well-founded measure (the RPO ordering), there are no infinite reduction sequences. The side condition `a ≠ b` in `R_cherry_diff` does not affect termination because the ordering holds for all ground instances where the rule applies.

*Alternative intuition:* One can also use a lexicographic measure `(c, g, s)` where `c` is the total number of `cherry` constructors (decreased by rules 7–8), `g` is the "grape-depth" of the third argument of outermost `banana` redexes (decreased by rule 6), and `s` is term size (decreased by rules 2–5). Since rule 6 strictly decreases the number of `grape` wrappers in the banana's third argument while cherries and grapes elsewhere cannot increase the top-level cherry count, the system is strongly normalizing.
