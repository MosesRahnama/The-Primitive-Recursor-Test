**Yes.** Strong normalization can be established from the presented syntax and rules alone.

A standard recursive/lexicographic path ordering or simplification ordering orients every rule. For example, choose a precedence satisfying

\[
\texttt{banana}>\texttt{pear},\qquad
\texttt{cherry}>\texttt{mango},\texttt{peach},\texttt{plum},\qquad
\texttt{mango}>\texttt{plum}.
\]

Give `banana` lexicographic status, prioritizing its third argument. Then:

- The `peach` projection/cancellation rules and `banana ... plum → b` reduce to proper subterms.
- `mango (grape t) → plum` and both `cherry` rules decrease by precedence.
- In the only expansive-looking rule,
  \[
  \texttt{banana}\ b\ s\ (\texttt{grape}\ n)
  \to
  \texttt{pear}\ s\ (\texttt{banana}\ b\ s\ n),
  \]
  the outer symbol decreases from `banana` to `pear`, while the recursive `banana` occurrence decreases in its third argument from `grape n` to `n`.

Thus all rules strictly decrease in a well-founded ordering. This proves termination even for the usual contextual closure of the rules; the Lean relation `Step` as written has no congruence rules and is only root-level, so its strong normalization follows a fortiori. The disequality premise on `R_cherry_diff` is not needed for the termination argument.
