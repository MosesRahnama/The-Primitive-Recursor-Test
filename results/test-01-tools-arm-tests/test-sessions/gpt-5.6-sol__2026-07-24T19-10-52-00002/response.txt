**Yes.** Strong normalization can be established from the given syntax and rewrite rules alone.

A standard recursive/lexicographic path ordering can orient every rule strictly downward. Choose, for example, a precedence satisfying

- `recΔ ≻ app`,
- `eqW ≻ integrate ≻ merge`,
- `integrate ≻ void` and `eqW ≻ void`,

with lexicographic status for `recΔ`. Then:

- The merge and zero rules return proper subterms.
- `integrate (delta t) → void` and the equality rules decrease by precedence.
- In the recursive rule,
  `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`,
  the residual recursive call decreases because `delta n` is strictly greater than its proper subterm `n`; the outer `app` is below `recΔ`.
- The overlap at `eqW a a` is harmless: both possible reducts are smaller in the same ordering.

Thus a well-founded simplification ordering proves termination, even for the usual contextual closure of these rules. For the literal Lean relation shown—which has no congruence/context constructors—the result is easier still, since `Step` only rewrites at the root. No additional rewrite rules, guards, structural axioms, or object-level assumptions are needed.
