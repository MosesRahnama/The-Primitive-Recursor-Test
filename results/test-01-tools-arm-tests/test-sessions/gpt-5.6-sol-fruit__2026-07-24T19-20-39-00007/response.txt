**Yes.** Strong normalization can be established from the presented syntax and rewrite rules alone.

For the `Step` relation exactly as defined in Lean, the argument is especially direct:

- The projection rules
  - `peach plum t → t`
  - `peach t plum → t`
  - `peach t t → t`
  - `banana b s plum → b`
  
  return a strict subterm, so they strictly decrease term size.

- Every other rule produces a term that cannot take another root-level `Step`:
  - `mango (grape t) → plum`
  - `banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)` — no rule has `pear` at its root.
  - `cherry a a → plum`
  - `cherry a b → mango (peach a b)` — the only `mango` rule requires a `grape` argument, not a `peach`.

Thus any non-size-decreasing step is immediately terminal, while any continuing step strictly descends to a proper subterm. An infinite `Step` chain is therefore impossible.

Moreover, even if one considered the usual contextual closure of these rules, termination can be oriented by a standard recursive path ordering—for example, with suitable precedence such as `banana > pear` and `cherry > mango, peach`, while using the strict decrease from `grape n` to `n`. The duplication of `s` in the banana rule does not defeat such an ordering.

So no additional axioms, guards, rewrite rules, or object-level assumptions are needed.
