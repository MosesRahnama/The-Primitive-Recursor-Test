**Reasoning**

The presented calculus is a finite first‑order term‑rewriting system on the inductive type `Trace`.  No extra rewrite rules, guards, or hidden side conditions are needed to prove that every reduction sequence terminates.

The only rules that do not obviously shrink syntactic size are `R_rec_succ` and `R_eq_diff`:

* `R_eq_diff` removes one `eqW` and introduces one `integrate` and one `merge`.  Because **no rule creates an `eqW`**, the total number of `eqW` constructors in a term is a non‑negative integer that strictly decreases every time an `eqW`‑rule fires.  Hence only finitely many `eqW` steps are possible.
* `R_rec_succ` is exactly the primitive‑recursor unfolding for a unary natural number (`void` = zero, `delta` = successor).  It strips one `delta` from the *third* argument of `recΔ`.  Although it duplicates the step argument `s`, the duplicated subterm is a **proper subterm** of the redex, and the new recursive call `recΔ b s n` is strictly smaller than the original `recΔ b s (delta n)` in the subterm order on the third argument.

Consequently the whole system can be oriented by a single well‑founded reduction ordering, for instance the **lexicographic path ordering (LPO)** with precedence

```
recΔ > app > eqW > integrate > merge > delta > void
```

and `recΔ` given lexicographic status.  Under this precedence:

* `recΔ b s (delta n) > app s (recΔ b s n)` because `recΔ > app`, `s` is a proper subterm of the left‑hand side, and `recΔ b s n < recΔ b s (delta n)` by the subterm property on the third argument.
* `eqW a b > integrate (merge a b)` because `eqW > integrate` and `merge a b < eqW a b` (since `merge < eqW` and both arguments of `merge` are proper subterms of `eqW a b`).
* Every other rule decreases either by the subterm property or because the left‑hand side’s top symbol is greater than the right‑hand side constant.

LPO is a standard, well‑founded reduction ordering; orienting all rules under it yields strong normalization.  The argument uses only the inductive structure of `Trace` and the rules as given—no imported object‑level axioms or strengthening of the system is required.
