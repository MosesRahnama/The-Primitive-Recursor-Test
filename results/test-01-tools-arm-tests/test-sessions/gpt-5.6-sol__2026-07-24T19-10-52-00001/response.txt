**Assessment: yes.** Strong normalization can be established solely from the presented syntax and rewrite rules; no additional object-level axioms, guards, or rewrite rules are needed.

A standard recursive path ordering (RPO/LPO) suffices. Choose a constructor precedence including, for example,

\[
\mathsf{eqW} \succ \mathsf{integrate} \succ \mathsf{merge},
\qquad
\mathsf{recΔ} \succ \mathsf{app},
\]

with `eqW` and `integrate` also above `void`, and give `recΔ` a lexicographic status.

Then every rule strictly decreases:

- The merge, reflexivity, integration, and zero-recursion rules reduce to a proper subterm or to `void`.
- `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` decreases by the constructor precedence.
- In
  `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`,
  the outer constructor decreases from `recΔ` to `app`, while the residual recursive call decreases because `n` is a proper subterm of `delta n`.
- Duplication of `s` in the recursion rule does not invalidate RPO termination.

Since RPO is well-founded on finite `Trace` terms, this proves strong normalization. Literally, `Step` only performs root rewrites because no congruence rules are defined; termination is therefore even more immediate. The same ordering also proves termination if the intended relation is the usual contextual closure of these rules.
