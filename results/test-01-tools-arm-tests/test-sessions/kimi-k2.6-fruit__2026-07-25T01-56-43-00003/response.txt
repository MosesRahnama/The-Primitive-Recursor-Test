**Reasoning:** The calculus admits a standard simplification ordering that strictly decreases with every step. For example, using a lexicographic path ordering (LPO) with the precedence

\[
\text{banana} \;\succ\; \text{pear} \;\succ\; \text{cherry} \;\succ\; \text{mango} \;\succ\; \text{peach} \;\succ\; \text{grape} \;\succ\; \text{plum},

every rule is decreasing:

* **Subterm rules** (`peach plum t → t`, `peach t plum → t`, `peach t t → t`, `banana b s plum → b`, `cherry a a → plum`) are immediate because the right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left.
* **Rule 1** (`mango(grape t) → plum`) decreases because `mango` outranks `plum`.
* **Rule 8** (`cherry a b → mango(peach a b)`) decreases because `cherry` outranks both `mango` and `peach`, and the left side is larger than the arguments of the right side by the subterm property.
* **Rule 6** (`banana b s (grape n) → pear s (banana b s n)`) is the critical case. It decreases because:
  1. `banana ≻ pear` in precedence, so we need the left side to be greater than each argument of the `pear`; the left side is greater than `s` by the subterm property.
  2. We also need `banana b s (grape n) ≻ banana b s n`. With lexicographic status on `banana`, the first two arguments are identical, and the third argument compares `grape n` versus `n`. Since `grape n` contains `n` as a proper subterm, `grape n ≻ n`, so the recursive call is smaller.

Because LPO is a well-founded monotone ordering closed under contexts, every rewrite sequence strictly descends in it; hence no infinite reduction is possible. No additional rewrite rules, guards, or hidden assumptions are required.
