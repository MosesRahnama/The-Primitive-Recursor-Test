**Assessment: yes.** Strong normalization can be established from the presented syntax and rewrite rules alone.

A standard **lexicographic path ordering (LPO)** suffices. For example, choose a precedence extending constraints such as

\[
\texttt{cherry} \succ \texttt{banana} \succ \texttt{mango}
\succ \texttt{peach} \succ \texttt{pear} \succ \texttt{grape}
\succ \texttt{plum}.
\]

Then:

- The peach cancellation rules and `R_banana_zero` reduce to proper subterms.
- `mango (grape t) → plum` and the cherry rules decrease by root-symbol precedence.
- For the only potentially problematic rule,
  \[
  \texttt{banana}\ b\ s\ (\texttt{grape}\ n)
  \to
  \texttt{pear}\ s\ (\texttt{banana}\ b\ s\ n),
  \]
  the outer root decreases from `banana` to `pear`, while the recursive `banana` occurrence decreases lexicographically because its third argument changes from `grape n` to the proper subterm `n`.
- Although `R_cherry_diff` can increase ordinary term size, it decreases in LPO because `cherry` is above both `mango` and `peach`. The inequality premise is not needed for the termination argument.

LPO is well-founded on finite `Trace` terms and is stable under contexts and substitutions. Thus even the usual contextual closure of these rules is strongly normalizing. The literal Lean `Step` relation shown is weaker—it only rewrites at the root—so it is strongly normalizing as well. No extra rewrite rules, guards, or object-level assumptions are required.
