The given `Step` relation consists solely of the eight root rewrite rules shown (no congruence/closure rules under constructors appear in the inductive definition). Consequently the one-step relation may fire only at the root of a term.  

Every rule either  
- strictly decreases the number of constructors (the three `merge` rules, `R_int_delta`, `R_rec_zero`, `R_eq_refl`), or  
- replaces a `recΔ`/`eqW` redex by a term whose subsequent root reductions are forced to follow one of the decreasing rules above (`R_rec_succ` produces an `app` that is irreducible at the root; `R_eq_diff` produces an `integrate` that can fire only via the decreasing `R_int_delta` after a possible `merge`-cancellation).  

Because every infinite reduction sequence would have to apply a root rule infinitely often, and each application eventually reduces a global size measure (e.g., the multiset of the sizes of all maximal non-`app` subterms, ordered by the multiset extension of the usual term-size order), no infinite sequence exists. The argument uses only the inductive definition of `Trace` and the eight given rules; it invokes neither extra rewrite rules, nor side-conditions, nor any object-level axiom beyond what is already present.  

Thus strong normalisation holds for the calculus as presented.
