**Raw assessment: yes — strong normalization appears establishable from the presented calculus alone.**

Interpreting `Step` literally as the one-step rewrite relation generated by the listed constructors, there is no apparent source of infinite reduction. Most rules strictly erase structure or return a subterm. The only size-increasing rules are:

```lean
eqW a b        ↦ integrate (merge a b)
recΔ b s (delta n) ↦ app s (recΔ b s n)
```

but neither introduces a cycle:

- `eqW` is eliminated and no rule recreates `eqW` except from already-existing subterms.
- `recΔ b s (delta n)` recurses only on the syntactically smaller third argument `n`; it does not create a larger third argument.
- `app` has no rewrite rule at the root.
- `delta` is never produced by a rewrite rule around an arbitrary term.
- The unguarded overlap between

```lean
eqW a a ↦ void
eqW a b ↦ integrate (merge a b)
```

does create nondeterminism, but not nontermination.

A standard proof route would be to orient the rules by a well-founded simplification ordering such as lexicographic path ordering / recursive path ordering with a precedence along the lines of:

```text
recΔ  > app
eqW   > integrate > merge
```

with constructors such as `void`, `delta`, etc. ordered suitably below the symbols that eliminate them. The critical rule is:

```lean
recΔ b s (delta n) ↦ app s (recΔ b s n)
```

This can be oriented because the recursive call has the same head symbol `recΔ` but a strictly smaller third argument, since `n` is a subterm of `delta n`. The extra `app s` wrapper and the duplication of `s` do not by themselves create a cycle under such an ordering.

So, within the boundary you specified — no added guards, no side conditions such as `a ≠ b`, no imported object-level equations, no structural axioms beyond ordinary first-order syntactic rewriting — the presented rules are compatible with a direct strong-normalization argument.

What the calculus does **not** obviously give is confluence or uniqueness of normal forms, because `eqW a a` has two distinct one-step reducts:

```lean
void
integrate (merge a a)
```

But that affects determinism/confluence, not strong normalization.
