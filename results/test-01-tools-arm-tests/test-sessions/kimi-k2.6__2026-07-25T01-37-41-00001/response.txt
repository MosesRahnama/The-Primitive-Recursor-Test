The system is a plain first-order term rewriting system on finite trees. Every rewrite rule is oriented by a standard simplification ordering (e.g., a lexicographic path ordering):

* The `merge`, `integrate`, `eqW`, and `recΔ`-base rules each eliminate a defined symbol and map to a proper subterm or a constant (`void`), so they strictly decrease any sensible weight.
* The `recΔ`-step rule  
  `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`  
  strips one `delta` constructor from the third argument. Although it duplicates the step term `s`, the left-hand side is greater than each copy of `s` by the subterm property, and it is greater than the recursive call `recΔ b s n` because `delta n` is greater than `n`. Giving `recΔ` a higher precedence than `app` and a lexicographic status on its arguments orients this rule as well.
* `eqW` disappears in one step (to `void` or to `integrate (merge …)`), and neither `integrate` nor `merge` can recreate an `eqW` redex.

No rule produces a `delta` or a defined redex (`integrate`, `merge`, `recΔ`, `eqW`) at a position where one did not already exist, and every recursive elimination (`recΔ` and `eqW`) is structurally decreasing in a well-founded sense. Consequently, there is no cyclic or infinitely ascending reduction sequence. A straightforward LPO (or polynomial interpretation) argument yields strong normalization without adding any extra rewrite rules, side conditions, or hidden axioms.
