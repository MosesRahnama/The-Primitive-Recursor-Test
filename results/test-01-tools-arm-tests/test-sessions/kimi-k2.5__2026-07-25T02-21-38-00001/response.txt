**Assessment: Yes – the system is strongly normalizing.**

**Reasoning**

The calculus contains only finitary, first‑order rewrite rules.  Strong normalization can be proved by exhibiting a well‑founded measure that is strictly decreased by every step.  A convenient measure is the lexicographic triple  

\[
\mu(t) \;=\; \bigl(\;M(t),\;E(t),\;|t|\;\bigr)
\]

where  

* \(M(t)\) is the multiset of *depths* of all **`recΔ`** sub‑terms of \(t\).  
  The depth of a sub‑term `recΔ b s n` is the total number of **`delta`** constructors occurring in \(n\) (i.e. the “natural number” that \(n\) denotes).  Multisets of natural numbers are well‑founded under the multiset ordering.

* \(E(t)\) is the number of **`eqW`** constructors in \(t\).

* \(|t|\) is the total size (number of constructors) of \(t\).

One checks that each rule strictly lowers \(\mu\):

| Rule | Effect on \(M\) | Effect on \(E\) | Effect on \(|t|\) |
|------|-----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| `integrate (delta t) → void` | Removes any depths inside \(t\) | unchanged | decreases |
| `merge void t → t` | loses depths in the discarded `void` | unchanged | decreases |
| `merge t void → t` | loses depths in the discarded `void` | unchanged | decreases |
| `merge t t → t` | loses depths in the discarded copy of \(t\) | unchanged | decreases |
| `recΔ b s void → b` | removes the depth‑0 element and all depths inside the discarded \(s\) | unchanged | decreases |
| `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)` | replaces a depth \(d+1\) by a depth \(d\) (multiset decreases) | unchanged | increases, but \(M\) already decreased |
| `eqW a a → void` | loses depths inside the two copies of \(a\) | decreases by 1 | decreases |
| `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` | unchanged (no `recΔ` created) | decreases by 1 | increases, but \(E\) already decreased |

Because the ordering on multisets of naturals is well‑founded, and the lexicographic product of well‑founded orders is well‑founded, \(\mu\) is well‑founded.  Every reduction step makes \(\mu\) smaller, so no infinite reduction sequence exists.

Consequently, strong normalization follows solely from the syntax and rewrite rules given; no additional object‑level axioms or side‑conditions are required.
