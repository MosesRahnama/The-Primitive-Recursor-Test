**Reasoning.**  
The rewrite system is first-order and finite. It can be oriented with a Recursive Path Ordering (RPO) using the precedence

\[
\texttt{recΔ} \;>\; \texttt{eqW} \;>\; \texttt{integrate} \;>\; \texttt{merge} \;>\; \texttt{delta} \;>\; \texttt{app} \;>\; \texttt{void}.
\]

Checking the rules:

* **Integrate/merge rules.** These are subterm-decreasing or replace a symbol by a proper subterm (`merge t\,t \to t`, `integrate (delta t) \to void`, etc.), so they decrease in any simplification ordering.
* **Recursor.** `recΔ b s (delta n) \to app s (recΔ b s n)` strips a `delta` wrapper from the third argument. In RPO this is a decrease because `recΔ > app` and the multiset comparison on the arguments gives `{b, s, delta n} \gg \{b, s, n\}` (since `delta n` is greater than its proper subterm `n`). The base case `recΔ b s void \to b` is a proper subterm decrease.
* **Equality witness.** `eqW a a \to void` clearly decreases. The “difference” rule `eqW a b \to integrate (merge a b)` replaces the high-precedence symbol `eqW` with lower-precedence ones; RPO validates this because `eqW > integrate`, `eqW > merge`, and `eqW a b` is greater than each of its proper subterms `a` and `b`.

Crucially, no rule introduces an `eqW` or `delta` occurrence that was not already present. Because every rule is oriented by a well-founded reduction ordering (RPO), *every* reduction sequence terminates. No additional rewrite rules, side-conditions, or imported axioms are required.
