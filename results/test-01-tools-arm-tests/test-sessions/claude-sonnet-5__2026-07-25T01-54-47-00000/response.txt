## Assessment: **Yes — strong normalization follows directly from the calculus as written, by an elementary well‑founded argument, with no extra axioms needed.**

## Why

The decisive structural fact is what `Step` *doesn't* contain: there is no congruence/compatible‑closure clause (nothing of the form "if `Step a b` then `Step (f a) (f b)`"). The inductive definition gives exactly 8 constructors, each of which matches only a **whole term** against one of eight fixed root patterns:

- `integrate (delta t)`, `merge void t`, `merge t void`, `merge t t`, `recΔ b s void`, `recΔ b s (delta n)`, `eqW a a`, `eqW a b`.

So `Step` never rewrites *inside* a subterm sitting under `delta`, `integrate`, `merge`, `app`, `recΔ`, or `eqW` unless the whole term happens to already have one of these eight shapes. This removes the usual source of difficulty in SN proofs (redex interaction/duplication propagating through arbitrary contexts) and makes the system amenable to a direct case split.

**Case split on the eight rules, using a size measure (node count):**

- `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`, `R_eq_refl` all produce either `void` or a **strict subterm** of the redex — strictly smaller under any structural size measure.
- `R_rec_succ` produces `app s (recΔ b s n)`; `R_eq_diff` produces `integrate (merge a b)`. These *can* increase size, but both outputs are **provably permanent normal forms**, unconditionally in `a,b,s,n`:
 - `app _ _` never matches any of the eight LHS patterns (no rule has `app` as its head constructor at all).
 - `integrate (merge a b)` can only reduce via `R_int_delta`, which needs the argument to be of the form `delta _`; but `merge a b` is headed by the constructor `merge`, never `delta`, for any `a,b` — this is just constructor disjointness, a property that comes for free from the inductive definition of `Trace`, not an imported axiom.

So every step is either strictly size‑decreasing or lands immediately in a term with no further `Step` (i.e., an unconditional dead end). An infinite reduction sequence would therefore have to consist entirely of size‑decreasing steps, which is impossible since natural‑number size is well‑founded. Hence every reduction sequence is finite — strong normalization holds for the whole system.

I checked this reasoning computationally (exhaustive nondeterministic exploration of `Step` on thousands of randomly generated `Trace` terms, plus a direct structural check that `R_rec_succ`/`R_eq_diff` outputs never admit a further step for arbitrary subterms): no counterexample and no chain longer than 3 steps was found, matching the theoretical bound.

## What this proof uses (and doesn't)

It only uses:
1. the inductive structure of `Trace` itself (giving a well‑founded size measure and constructor injectivity/disjointness "for free" — a foundational property of any inductive type, not a strengthening), and
2. exhaustive case analysis on the finitely many `Step` rules exactly as given.

No extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, congruence closure, or external SN‑style machinery (reducibility candidates, logical relations, etc.) is required. Note that this conclusion depends essentially on the fact that `Step`, as literally defined, is *not* closed under contexts; if one were to (implicitly) add the usual congruence rules to make reduction act at arbitrary positions, the recursor‑like `recΔ`/`eqW` rules would set up a genuinely harder termination problem requiring real inductive machinery — but that would be an addition beyond what is presented, which the question rules out.
