**Assessment: Yes — the system is strongly normalizing, and this can be established from the calculus as presented.**

**Reasoning**

The `Step` relation is a set of *head* rewrite rules: it closes reduction under no contexts or congruences. A term can reduce only when its outermost constructor matches one of the left-hand sides.

Consider the right-hand side of each rule:

| Rule | RHS | Status |
|------|-----|--------|
| `R_int_delta` | `void` | Normal form (no rule for `void`). |
| `R_merge_void_left` | `t` | **Proper subterm** of `merge void t`. |
| `R_merge_void_right` | `t` | **Proper subterm** of `merge t void`. |
| `R_merge_cancel` | `t` | **Proper subterm** of `merge t t`. |
| `R_rec_zero` | `b` | **Proper subterm** of `recΔ b s void`. |
| `R_rec_succ` | `app s (recΔ b s n)` | Normal form: there is no rule for `app`, so the term is stuck regardless of its subterms. |
| `R_eq_refl` | `void` | Normal form. |
| `R_eq_diff` | `integrate (merge a b)` | Normal form: the only rule for `integrate` requires its argument to be `delta t`; `merge a b` is syntactically a `merge`, and the constructors `merge` and `delta` are disjoint, so no rule applies. |

Thus every rewrite either:
1. moves to a **proper subterm** (`merge` and `rec` zero-rules), or
2. reaches a **normal form in one step** (all other rules).

Because `Trace` is an inductively defined type of finite trees, the proper-subterm relation is well founded. A reduction sequence can therefore contain only finitely many “proper-subterm” steps, and at most one “terminal” step (after which no further root reduction is possible). No rule can re-create a redex at the root from a term that has just reduced, so infinite reduction is impossible.

Overlaps such as `eqW a a` (which can reduce by `R_eq_refl` or `R_eq_diff`) merely create local non-determinism; every choice leads to a normal form.

Hence strong normalization follows directly by structural induction on traces — equivalently, by proving `Acc Step t` for every `t : Trace` — using only the syntax and the rewrite rules given. No additional axioms, side-conditions, or imported object-level assumptions are required.
