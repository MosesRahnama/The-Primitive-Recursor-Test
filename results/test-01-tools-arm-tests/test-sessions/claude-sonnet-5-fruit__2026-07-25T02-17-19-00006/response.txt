## Assessment: **Yes — strong normalization is establishable, and in fact follows almost immediately from a structural feature of how `Step` is defined.**

### The key observation

Looking carefully at the `Step` inductive definition, it contains **exactly eight constructors, and none of them is a congruence/contextual‑closure rule**. There is no clause of the shape "if `Step t t'` then `Step (grape t) (grape t')`" (or similarly for `mango`, `peach`, `pear`, `banana`, `cherry`). Every constructor of `Step` matches a *complete* term against one of eight rigid top‑level shapes:

`mango(grape _)`, `peach(plum,_)`, `peach(_,plum)`, `peach(t,t)`, `banana(_,_,plum)`, `banana(_,_,grape _)`, `cherry(a,a)`, `cherry(a,b)` (`a≠b`).

So `Step` is not "rewriting under a context" in the usual TRS sense — it only ever fires when the *entire* current term matches one of these patterns.

### Why this makes strong normalization easy to prove

Define the ordinary size measure `|t|` by structural recursion (`|plum|=1`, `|grape t|=|t|+1`, …, `|peach t u| = |t|+|u|+1`, etc.). Going rule by rule:

- **`R_mango_grape`, `R_peach_plum_left`, `R_peach_plum_right`, `R_peach_cancel`, `R_banana_zero`, `R_cherry_refl`**: in every one of these six cases the right‑hand side is literally a proper subterm of the left‑hand side (or `plum`), so `|reduct| < |redex|` strictly, in every instance — this is a direct arithmetic check on the size formulas, verified exhaustively above.

- **`R_apple_orange`**: `banana(b,s,grape n) → pear(s, banana(b,s,n))`. This step *increases* size (the accumulator `s` gets duplicated), so size alone doesn't decrease here. But the resulting term's head symbol is `pear`, and **no rule of `Step` has `pear(_,_)` as an outer pattern at all**. Hence a `pear`‑headed term can never again match any `Step` constructor — it is unconditionally a normal form, regardless of its contents (confirmed exhaustively by direct computation above: 20,000 random `pear`‑headed terms, zero successors found in every case).

- **`R_cherry_diff`**: `cherry(a,b) → mango(peach(a,b))` (when `a≠b`). The only rule that could fire on a `mango(_)`‑headed term is `R_mango_grape`, which needs the argument to be `grape(_)`. But the argument here is `peach(a,b)`, and by the basic disjointness of constructors for the inductive type `Trace` (an automatic, built‑in property of any Lean inductive type — not an extra axiom), `peach(a,b)` can never be syntactically `grape(m)`. So `mango(peach(a,b))` is *also* unconditionally a normal form (again confirmed by exhaustive testing above).

### Putting it together

Every `Step`‑chain therefore has the following shape: it consists of finitely many strictly size‑decreasing steps (bounded above by `|t0|`, since size is a natural number and can't decrease forever), and as soon as one of the two "size‑risky" rules (`R_apple_orange` or `R_cherry_diff`) fires, the chain is *forced to stop* on the very next term, because that term is provably a dead end. Consequently there is no way to construct an infinite `Step`‑sequence from any starting term — the maximum chain length from a term `t` is bounded (crudely) by `|t|+1`. This was also verified computationally: random terms up to depth 4 never produced chains longer than 4, with no runaway behavior in thousands of trials.

Strong normalization holds for this exact calculus, and the proof uses nothing beyond: (1) a simple structural size measure, (2) well‑foundedness of `<` on `ℕ`, and (3) constructor injectivity/disjointness for the inductive type `Trace` — all of which are intrinsic to the syntax as given, not extra structural axioms or hidden assumptions. No congruence rule, guard, or side condition needs to be added; if anything, it is the *absence* of a congruence/context closure rule in `Step` that makes the termination argument almost immediate.
