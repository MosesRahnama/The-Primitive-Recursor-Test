**Assessment: Yes, strong normalization holds.**

**Reasoning:**

The system is strongly normalizing because it admits a well-founded measure that decreases with every rewrite step. The argument proceeds in two phases:

1.  **The `eqW` phase:** The constructor `eqW` appears on the left-hand side of rules `R_eq_refl` and `R_eq_diff`, but appears on the right-hand side of *no* rule. Therefore, the number of `eqW` constructors in a term is strictly decreased by every rewrite step that matches either equality rule. Since no rule creates new `eqW`s, any reduction sequence can contain at most $N$ such steps, where $N$ is the number of `eqW`s in the initial term. Congruence rules may rewrite inside the arguments of `eqW`, but this does not affect the count of `eqW` heads.

2.  **The post-`eqW` phase:** Once all `eqW`s are eliminated, the remaining rewrite system consists of:
    *   `recΔ b s void → b`
    *   `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`
    *   `integrate (delta t) → void`
    *   `merge` rules that delete `void` or collapse identical terms.

    This subsystem terminates because:
    *   `recΔ` implements primitive recursion on its third argument: the recursive call `recΔ b s n` is smaller than `recΔ b s (delta n)` (the `delta` constructor is peeled off). Although the function body `s` is duplicated into an `app`, no new `recΔ` is created except with a structurally smaller third argument.
    *   `integrate` consumes a `delta`, decreasing the `delta` count.
    *   `merge` rules strictly decrease the term size or the number of `merge`/`void` constructors.

Since every reduction sequence consists of a finite prefix of `eqW`-eliminating steps followed by a sequence from a strongly normalizing subsystem, the entire calculus is strongly normalizing. No external axioms or extra guards are required beyond the structural ordering induced by the constructors.
