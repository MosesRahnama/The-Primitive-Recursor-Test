**Reasoning:**

The system corresponds to a fragment of Gödel's System T (primitive recursive arithmetic extended with higher-type constructors). Strong normalization holds because:

1.  **Primitive Recursion is Well-Founded:** The `recΔ` rules implement standard primitive recursion over natural numbers encoded as `void` (zero) and `delta` (successor). The rule `R_rec_succ` strictly decreases the number of nested `delta` constructors in the third argument. Although the rule syntactically increases term size by wrapping the recursive call in `app s [...]`, the number of possible unfoldings is bounded by the initial depth of the inductive argument. This is the standard structural termination argument for recursors.

2.  **No Generation of Critical Redexes:** The `eqW` rules eliminate `eqW` constructs and never reintroduce them. The right-hand sides (`void` and `integrate (merge a b)`) contain only constructors (`integrate`, `merge`) whose reduction rules (`R_int_delta`, `R_merge_*`) are strictly decreasing (removing constructors) or lead immediately to normal forms. There is no rule that creates a new `recΔ` or `eqW` redex from the reduction of another.

3.  **Absence of Fixed-Point Constructions:** The `app` constructor is inert (no rewrite rules apply to it). Consequently, the calculus lacks self-application or fixed-point operators that could support general recursion. `recΔ` is the only source of "looping" behavior, and it is strictly controlled by the well-founded ordering on its numeric argument.

4.  **Local Termination of Auxiliary Rules:** The `merge` and `integrate` rules are strongly normalizing (they strictly decrease the number of constructors), and `eqW` terminates in at most two steps (either to `void` or to `integrate` of a normal form).

Because every reduction path either strictly decreases the measure "number of `delta` constructors under `recΔ`" or eliminates an `eqW`/`merge`/`integrate` redex without introducing new ones of greater complexity, there are no infinite reduction sequences. The system is strongly normalizing without requiring additional structural axioms or object-level assumptions.
