**Assessment: Yes. Strong normalization (SN) holds and can be established from the calculus alone.**

**Reasoning.**

1. **Root-only reduction.** The inductive definition of `Step` provides only head rewrite rules. It contains *no congruence constructors* (nothing like “if `Step t t'` then `Step (C t) (C t')`”). Consequently `Step` applies exclusively at the root of a term; subterms are never rewritten independently.

2. **Every rule either decreases size or yields an immediate normal form.** Count term constructors:
   - `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`, and `R_eq_refl` all replace the left-hand side with a subterm (or `void`) that is strictly smaller.
   - `R_eq_diff` produces `integrate (merge a b)`. The only rule for `integrate` requires its argument to have the form `delta t`. Because `merge` and `delta` are disjoint constructors of the inductive type `Trace`, `merge a b` can never be equal to `delta t`. Hence the result is irreducible.
   - `R_rec_succ` produces `app s (recΔ b s n)`. The constructor `app` never appears in any left-hand side, so the result is also irreducible.

3. **No cycles are possible.** The two “size-increasing” rules (`R_eq_diff` and `R_rec_succ`) output terms that are root-stuck, so no rewrite can follow them. Every other rewrite strictly decreases the number of constructors. Because `Trace` is an inductive type, all terms are finite; therefore only finitely many size-decreasing steps can occur.

4. **Bound on reduction length.** Any reduction sequence consists of at most `|t|` size-decreasing steps (where `|t|` is the number of constructors in the initial term), optionally followed by a single `R_eq_diff` or `R_rec_succ` step. Thus every sequence terminates, and the system is strongly normalizing without any imported axioms or extra side conditions.
