### Reasoning

#### Interpretation A: Root-Level Reduction (Strictly as written in Lean 4)
In the provided Lean 4 file, `Step` is defined as a inductive relation with exactly 8 rules. There are **no congruence rules** (compatibility rules) allowing reductions to happen inside subterms (e.g., no rule of the form `Step t t' → Step (delta t) (delta t')`). Thus, all reductions must occur at the root (top level). 

Under this root-only reduction relation, strong normalization is trivial to show because every valid reduction either immediately terminates or strictly reduces the structural size/complexity of the term:
1. **Irreducible Roots:** The constructors `void`, `delta t`, and `app s t` cannot be reduced at the root because no rule pattern matches them.
2. **`integrate t`:** This can only step at the root if $t = \text{delta } t'$ (via `R_int_delta`), reducing to `void`, which is immediately irreducible.
3. **`eqW a b`:** This can step via:
   - `R_eq_refl`: `eqW a a` $\to$ `void` (immediately irreducible).
   - `R_eq_diff`: `eqW a b` $\to$ `integrate (merge a b)`. Since `merge a b` starts with the `merge` constructor (and is not of the form `delta t`), `integrate (merge a b)` is root-irreducible.
4. **`merge t1 t2`:** This can only step to `t1` or `t2` (via `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, or `R_merge_cancel`), both of which are strictly smaller subterms.
5. **`recΔ b s n`:** This can step via:
   - `R_rec_zero`: `recΔ b s void` $\to$ `b` (strictly smaller subterm).

Because every root-step either strictly reduces the size of the term or yields a root-irreducible term, there can be no infinite reduction sequence.
