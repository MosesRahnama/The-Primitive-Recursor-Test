**Assessment: yes.** Strong normalization follows from the presented finite syntax and rewrite rules alone.

As Lean defines it, `Step` performs only root rewrites; there is no contextual-closure rule. The only size-increasing rules,

- `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`
- `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`,

produce terms whose outer constructors (`app` and, in this case, `integrate` over a `merge`) cannot themselves trigger another root rewrite. All other rules eliminate an outer constructor or expose a proper subterm. Thus no infinite `Step` chain is possible.

Even if one takes the stronger, customary compatible closure under all term contexts, termination can still be established using a standard recursive path ordering. For example, choose a precedence containing

```text
recΔ > app
eqW > integrate > merge
integrate > void
```

and give `recΔ` lexicographic status with its recursive argument compared appropriately. Then:

- the recursive argument decreases from `delta n` to `n`;
- the `eqW` rule is oriented by constructor precedence;
- all merge, zero, reflexivity, and cancellation rules reduce to proper subterms or smaller-root terms.

The overlap between `R_eq_refl` and the unconditional `R_eq_diff` does not create a cycle. Therefore no additional structural axioms, guards, rewrite rules, or object-level assumptions are needed.
