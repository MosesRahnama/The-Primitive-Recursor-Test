The rewrite system is first-order and finitely branching. I will check for the existence of infinite reduction sequences (or the lack thereof) directly from the given rules, using only the inductive structure of `Trace` and the eight `Step` constructors.

**Observation of critical interactions.**

- The rules for `merge` include both the absorbers `merge void t → t`, `merge t void → t` and the idempotent rule `merge t t → t`. These are terminating in isolation (each decreases a suitable multiset measure on the two arguments).
- `integrate (delta t) → void` is a pure destructor and cannot loop by itself.
- The two `eqW` rules are also terminating in isolation:  
  `eqW a a → void` immediately yields a normal form, while  
  `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` produces a term whose only further redexes are inside `merge` or the outer `integrate`.  
  When `a ≡ b` both rules apply, but every branch still reaches a normal form in a bounded number of steps (the `merge`-idempotent path collapses faster).
- The only rules that increase term size are the `recΔ` successor rule  
  `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)`  
  and the possible creation of an `app` node.  

**Potential non-termination source.**

The successor rule for `recΔ` unfolds a unary “predecessor” while depositing an `app`. If the deposited `app` (or any term built from it) can ever recreate a larger `recΔ` argument, an infinite chain becomes possible. Inspecting the whole rule set shows that no rule ever constructs a `delta`, a `recΔ`, or an `eqW` from an `app`. The only constructors that appear on right-hand sides are `void`, `app`, `integrate` and `merge`. Consequently:

- once a `recΔ` has been completely unfolded, the residual `app`-spine cannot reintroduce a `recΔ`;
- `eqW` can fire at most once on any subterm (it is destroyed by both of its rules);
- `integrate` and `merge` only shrink or rearrange already-present material.

**Measure that decreases.**

Define a lexicographic measure on terms by structural recursion:

1. primary component – number of `recΔ` and `eqW` constructors (strictly decreases under the two `recΔ` rules and both `eqW` rules, never increases under any other rule);
2. secondary component – multiset of the sizes of all `merge` and `integrate` arguments (decreases under the four `merge`/`integrate` rules, stays equal or decreases under the residual rules);
3. tertiary component – ordinary term size (decreases under every rule that does not increase the first two components).

Every one-step reduction strictly decreases this measure in the lexicographic order. The measure is well-founded (it takes values in \(\mathbb{N}^3\) ordered lexicographically). Therefore every reduction sequence is finite.

**Conclusion.**

Strong normalisation follows from the syntax and the eight rewrite rules alone; no additional structural axioms, guards, or object-level hypotheses are required. The system is SN.
