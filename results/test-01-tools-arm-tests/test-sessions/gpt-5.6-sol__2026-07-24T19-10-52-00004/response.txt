**Assessment: yes.** Strong normalization follows from the presented syntax and rules alone; no additional rewrite rules, guards, or object-level axioms are needed.

A strictly monotone interpretation into `Nat` can orient every rule. For example, define:

\[
\begin{aligned}
|void| &= 0\\
|\delta t| &= |t|+1\\
|integrate\ t| &= |t|+1\\
|merge\ a\ b| &= |a|+|b|+1\\
|app\ a\ b| &= |a|+|b|\\
|recΔ\ b\ s\ n| &= |b|+(|n|+1)(|s|+1)\\
|eqW\ a\ b| &= |a|+|b|+3.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every rule strictly decreases this measure. The only less immediate cases are:

\[
\begin{aligned}
|recΔ\ b\ s\ (\delta n)|
 &= |b|+(|n|+2)(|s|+1),\\
|app\ s\ (recΔ\ b\ s\ n)|
 &= |b|+(|n|+1)(|s|+1)+|s|,
\end{aligned}
\]

so the decrease is exactly \(1\); and

\[
|eqW\ a\ b|=|a|+|b|+3
>
|integrate(merge\ a\ b)|=|a|+|b|+2.
\]

All constructor interpretations are strictly monotone in their arguments. Consequently, this proves termination not only for the literal root-level `Step` relation shown, but also for its usual closure under arbitrary term contexts. The unguarded overlap between `R_eq_refl` and `R_eq_diff` when `a = b` creates nondeterminism, but not nontermination.
