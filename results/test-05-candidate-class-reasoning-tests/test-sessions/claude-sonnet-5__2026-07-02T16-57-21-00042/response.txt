## Setup

To show `Step` is terminating via a measure `μ : Trace → Nat`, one needs

  `Step t t' → μ t > μ t'`

for **every** rule. I checked this for `mu1`, `mu2`, `mu3` against all seven rule schemas.

## Rules that all three measures handle correctly

For `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left/right`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`, `R_eq_refl`, and `R_eq_diff`, all three candidates give a strict decrease, essentially because each of these rules either deletes a subterm outright or replaces the whole term by a strictly smaller structural piece, and the constants chosen in all three definitions are large enough to cover the “+1/+2/+3” bookkeeping. (E.g. for `R_eq_diff`, all three give `μ(lhs) - μ(rhs) = 1 > 0`.)

## The critical failure: `R_rec_succ`

The rule
```
recDelta b s (delta n)  ⟶  app s (recDelta b s n)
```
duplicates the subterm `s`: it occurs once on the left (inside `recDelta`) but **twice** on the right (once as the head of `app`, once still inside the nested `recDelta`). Every one of the three measures is a purely additive/compositional size function in which `mu(s)` contributes with total coefficient 1 on the left-hand side. Computing symbolically with `B = μ(b), S = μ(s), N = μ(n)`:

- **mu1**: `μ(lhs) = 2+B+S+N`, `μ(rhs) = 2+B+2S+N` ⇒ `μ(lhs) - μ(rhs) = -S`.
- **mu2**: `μ(lhs) = 6+B+S+N`, `μ(rhs) = 6+B+2S+N` ⇒ `μ(lhs) - μ(rhs) = -S`.
- **mu3**: `μ(lhs) = 2+B+S+N`, `μ(rhs) = 3+B+2S+N` ⇒ `μ(lhs) - μ(rhs) = -1-S`.

So in **all three cases** `μ(lhs) ≤ μ(rhs)`, i.e. the measure is non-increasing at best and typically *increases* along this step — the exact opposite of what's needed.

**Concrete instances confirming this:**

- With `b = s = n = void`: `mu1` gives `2 → 2` (tie), `mu2` gives `6 → 6` (tie), `mu3` gives `2 → 3` (strict increase).
- With `s = delta void` (so `μ(s)=1`), `b = void`, `n = delta void`: `mu1` gives `3 → 4` (strict increase), matching the predicted `-S = -1`.

Thus `mu1` and `mu2` at best stagnate (fail strictness) and actively increase once `s` is non‑trivial; `mu3` fails unconditionally, even in the minimal all‑`void` case.

## Diagnosis

This is not a matter of retuning constants: as long as `mu(recDelta b s n)` counts `μ(s)` with coefficient 1 and `mu(app a b)` counts `μ(a)` with coefficient ≥1 (both true for all three definitions), the right-hand side of `R_rec_succ` will contain an extra, unrestricted copy of `μ(s)` that the left-hand side does not have to dominate — since `μ(s)` is unbounded, no fixed additive constants can compensate. A valid termination argument for this calculus needs a measure that does **not** linearly re-charge `s`'s size on both sides — e.g., a lexicographic measure whose primary component is `μ(n)` alone (ignoring `b, s` at top priority), or a multiset/RPO-style order — rather than any of these three simple weighted structural sums.

## Conclusion

- **mu1**: invalid — fails to strictly decrease on `R_rec_succ` (ties when `μ(s)=0`, strictly increases otherwise).
- **mu2**: invalid — same failure pattern as `mu1` on `R_rec_succ`.
- **mu3**: invalid — fails `R_rec_succ` unconditionally (strictly increases for every `b, s, n`, including the all-`void` case).

None of the three candidates is a valid termination measure for the full `Step` relation as given; all three are falsified by the same rule, `R_rec_succ`, due to the duplication of the step-function argument `s`.
