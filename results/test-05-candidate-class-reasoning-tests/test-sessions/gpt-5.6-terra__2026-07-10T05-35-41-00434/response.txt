All three fail as strict `Nat`-valued termination measures for `Step`. The obstruction is `R_rec_succ`.

Let \(B = \mu(b)\), \(S = \mu(s)\), and \(N = \mu(n)\).

| Candidate | LHS of `R_rec_succ` | RHS of `R_rec_succ` | LHS − RHS |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `mu1` | \(2 + B + S + N\) | \(2 + B + 2S + N\) | \(-S\) |
| `mu2` | \(6 + B + S + N\) | \(6 + B + 2S + N\) | \(-S\) |
| `mu3` | \(2 + B + S + N\) | \(3 + B + 2S + N\) | \(-1-S\) |

Thus:

- **`mu1`**: not decreasing on `R_rec_succ`.
  - It is equal when `s = void`.
  - It increases whenever `mu1 s > 0`.

- **`mu2`**: likewise not decreasing on `R_rec_succ`.
  - Equal for `s = void`.
  - Increases whenever `mu2 s > 0`.

- **`mu3`**: strictly increases on every `R_rec_succ` step, by at least one.

For example, instantiate `R_rec_succ` with `b = void`, `s = void`, and `n = void`:

```lean
recDelta void void (delta void)
  -->
app void (recDelta void void void)
```

The measure values are:

| Candidate | Before | After |
|---|---:|---:|
| `mu1` | 2 | 2 |
| `mu2` | 6 | 6 |
| `mu3` | 2 | 3 |

So this one instance already disproves strict descent for all three.

All the other rewrite rules do strictly decrease each candidate:

- `R_int_delta`, both `R_merge_void_*` rules, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`, and `R_eq_refl` decrease by a positive amount.
- `R_eq_diff` decreases by exactly `1` for each of `mu1`, `mu2`, and `mu3`.

The reason the recursive-success rule defeats these measures is that the source contains one occurrence of the step argument `s`, while the target contains it twice: once as the argument of `app`, and once inside the recursive `recDelta b s n`. A simple additive structural measure of these forms therefore charges `s` twice after the rewrite.
