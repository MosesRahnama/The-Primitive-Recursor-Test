Here’s the quick audit of each candidate as a **strictly decreasing one-step termination measure** (`Step l r -> μ l > μ r` for all rules).

## Rule-by-rule constraints (derived)

Most rules are easy and always decreasing for any size-like measure, except two critical ones:

1. **`R_rec_succ`**  
   `recDelta b s (delta n) -> app s (recDelta b s n)`  
   This imposes:
   \[
   w_{rec} + w_\delta > w_{app} + w_{rec}
   \Rightarrow w_\delta > w_{app}.
   \]
   So delta must weigh more than app.

2. **`R_eq_diff`**  
   `eqW a b -> integrate (merge a b)`  
   This imposes:
   \[
   w_{eq} > w_{int} + w_{merge}.
   \]

Everything else is automatically decreasing given positive constructor costs.

---

## Candidate `mu1`

Weights (constant parts):  
- `delta=1, integrate=1, merge=1, app=1, rec=1, eqW=3`.

Check critical rules:
- `R_rec_succ`: needs `delta > app`, but `1 > 1` is false (actually equal).
- `R_eq_diff`: needs `eqW > integrate+merge`, i.e. `3 > 1+1=2` true.

So `mu1` **fails** because `R_rec_succ` is not strictly decreasing (it is equal).

---

## Candidate `mu2`

Weights:  
- `delta=1, integrate=2, merge=2, app=1, rec=5, eqW=5`.

Critical checks:
- `R_rec_succ`: needs `1 > 1` (delta > app), false.
- `R_eq_diff`: needs `5 > 2+2=4`, true.

So `mu2` **fails** for the same reason (`R_rec_succ` non-strict).

---

## Candidate `mu3`

Weights:  
- `delta=1, integrate=2, merge=1, app=2, rec=1, eqW=4`.

Critical checks:
- `R_rec_succ`: needs `1 > 2`, false (actually increases).
- `R_eq_diff`: needs `4 > 2+1=3`, true.

So `mu3` **fails**, and more strongly: `R_rec_succ` increases by 1.

---

## Bottom line

None of `mu1`, `mu2`, or `mu3` is a valid strict global descent measure for `Step`.

- `mu1`: invalid  
- `mu2`: invalid  
- `mu3`: invalid  

All are blocked by `R_rec_succ` (delta not heavier than app).
