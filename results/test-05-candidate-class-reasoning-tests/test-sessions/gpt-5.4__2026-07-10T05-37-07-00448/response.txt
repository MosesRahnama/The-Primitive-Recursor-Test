## Overall conclusion

All three candidates fail as strict one-step descent measures.

### Why they all fail
The core obstruction is the rule

- `recDelta b s (delta n)  ->  app s (recDelta b s n)`

On the right-hand side, the term `s` is duplicated in the measure accounting:

- once as the left child of `app`
- once inside `recDelta b s n`

But on the left-hand side, `s` appears only once inside `recDelta`.

Any additive size measure of the form used in `mu1`, `mu2`, `mu3` will tend to increase here unless the `recDelta` node carries enough extra weight to pay for the extra copy of `s`, and in fact enough in a way that depends on `mu s`. A fixed constant bonus on `recDelta` cannot compensate for an arbitrarily large `s`.

So:

- `mu1`: invalid
- `mu2`: invalid
- `mu3`: invalid
