**Verdict: all three candidates fail, all on the same rule (R6, `recDelta b s (delta n) → app s (recDelta b s n)`).**

The root cause is structural: R6 **duplicates** the subterm `s` on the RHS (once inside `app`, once inside the recursive `recDelta`). Any measure of the given linear/additive form assigns cost `mu(s)` on the LHS but `2·mu(s)` on the RHS. Since the LHS only strips one `delta` (worth at most a bounded constant) while the RHS reconstructs `recDelta` plus an `app` wrapper, the extra `mu(s)` term is unbounded and cannot be compensated by any fixed constant.

Concretely, for each measure the deficit on R6 is:
- mu1: `mu1 s` (fails, non-strict even when s=void)
- mu2: `mu2 s` (fails, non-strict when s=void)
- mu3: `1 + mu3 s` (fails strictly always)

None of the three is a valid termination measure. To handle R6, a measure cannot be linear in `mu(s)` inside `recDelta`; it would need something like a multiplicative/higher-degree dependence on the recursion argument `n` that dominates the duplication of `s` (e.g., a term proportional to `mu(n)·mu(s)` or making the recDelta weight grow with `n`).
