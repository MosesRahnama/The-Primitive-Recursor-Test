All three candidate measures (mu1, mu2, mu3) fail to be valid termination measures for the given rewrite system because they do not strictly decrease on the rule `R_rec_succ` (the sixth rule). For that rule, the left‑hand side `recDelta b s (delta n)` reduces to `app s (recDelta b s n)`. In each measure the right‑hand side duplicates the subterm `s` (appearing once inside the `app` and once inside the nested `recDelta`), while the left‑hand side contains `s` only once. Because the measures are additive linear functions, the duplication of `s` makes the right‑hand side at least as large as the left‑hand side, and often larger, violating the required strict decrease.

Concretely:
- **mu1**: `mu1(recDelta b s (delta n)) = 2 + mu1(b) + mu1(s) + mu1(n)` and `mu1(app s (recDelta b s n)) = 2 + mu1(b) + 2·mu1(s) + mu1(n)`. When `mu1(s) = 0` the two sides are equal; otherwise the right‑hand side is larger.
- **mu2**: `mu2(recDelta b s (delta n)) = 6 + mu2(b) + mu2(s) + mu2(n)` and `mu2(app s (recDelta b s n)) = 6 + mu2(b) + 2·mu2(s) + mu2(n)`. The same situation holds.
- **mu3**: `mu3(recDelta b s (delta n)) = 2 + mu3(b) + mu3(s) + mu3(n)` and `mu3(app s (recDelta b s n)) = 3 + mu3(b) + 2·mu3(s) + mu3(n)`. Here the right‑hand side is always strictly larger, so the measure even increases.

All other rewrite rules do satisfy the strict decrease condition, but the failure on `R_rec_succ` alone disqualifies each candidate. Hence none of mu1, mu2, or mu3 is a valid termination measure for this calculus.
