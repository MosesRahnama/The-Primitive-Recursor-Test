To assess the three proposed termination measures (`mu1`, `mu2`, and `mu3`), we must verify if they satisfy the strictly decreasing property for all rewrite rules defined in the `Step` relation. For a measure `mu` to prove termination, the inequality **`mu (LHS) > mu (RHS)`** must hold for every rule.

All three candidates are strictly linear, additive measures (each node adds a constant weight to the sum of the measures of its subterms). 

### 1. Analysis of the Successful Rules
All three candidates successfully prove that the measure strictly decreases for Rules 1 through 5, as well as Rules 7 and 8. 

To highlight how this works, let's look at **Rule 8 (`R_eq_diff`)**: `eqW a b -> integrate (merge a b)`
* **`mu1`**: `LHS = 3 + mu a + mu b`. `RHS = 1 + (1 + mu a + mu b) = 2 + mu a + mu b`. (3 > 2, Valid)
* **`mu2`**: `LHS = 5 + mu a + mu b`. `RHS = 2 + (2 + mu a + mu b) = 4 + mu a + mu b`. (5 > 4, Valid)
* **`mu3`**: `LHS = 4 + mu a + mu b`. `RHS = 2 + (1 + mu a + mu b) = 3 + mu a + mu b`. (4 > 3, Valid)

Similarly, variables that are dropped (e.g., in `eqW a a -> void`) or conserved/reduced without duplication easily satisfy the strictly decreasing requirement for all three candidates.

### 2. The Failure of Rule 6 (`R_rec_succ`)
None of the three candidates are mathematically capable of proving termination for **Rule 6**. 

Rule 6 is defined as:
`recDelta b s (delta n) -> app s (recDelta b s n)`

Notice that the subterm `s` appears **once** on the LHS but is duplicated to appear **twice** on the RHS. Because all three candidates are purely additive, this duplicate `s` adds a full, unbounded `mu s` term to the RHS. Let's look at the algebraic breakdown for all three measures:

* **For `mu1`**:
  * **LHS**: `1 + mu1 b + mu1 s + mu1 (delta n) = 1 + mu b + mu s + (1 + mu n) = 2 + mu b + mu s + mu n`
  * **RHS**: `1 + mu1 s + mu1 (recDelta b s n) = 1 + mu s + (1 + mu b + mu s + mu n) = 2 + mu b + 2*(mu s) + mu n`
  * **Inequality**: LHS > RHS simplifies to `2 > 2 + mu1 s`, which means `0 > mu1 s`. Because `mu1 s` is a natural number (always `≥ 0`), this is strictly impossible.

* **For `mu2`**:
  * **LHS**: `5 + mu2 b + mu2 s + (1 + mu2 n) = 6 + mu b + mu s + mu n`
  * **RHS**: `1 + mu2 s + (5 + mu b + mu s + mu n) = 6 + mu b + 2*(mu s) + mu n`
  * **Inequality**: LHS > RHS simplifies to `6 > 6 + mu2 s`, meaning `0 > mu2 s`. This is impossible.

* **For `mu3`**:
  * **LHS**: `1 + mu3 b + mu3 s + (1 + mu3 n) = 2 + mu b + mu s + mu n`
  * **RHS**: `2 + mu3 s + (1 + mu b + mu s + mu n) = 3 + mu b + 2*(mu s) + mu n`
  * **Inequality**: LHS > RHS simplifies to `2 > 3 + mu3 s`, meaning `-1 > mu3 s`. This is impossible.

### Conclusion
**All three candidates fail.** 
They effectively cover 7 of the 8 rules. However, because `s` can be an arbitrarily large term and these measures are purely linear/additive, no assignment of constant constructor weights can counterbalance the duplication of `s` in Rule 6 (`R_rec_succ`). To prove termination for this calculus, you would need a non-linear termination measure (like a polynomial interpretation involving multiplication) or a dependency pair framework, rather than simple additive weights.
