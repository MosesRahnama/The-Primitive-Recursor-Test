A termination measure must strictly decrease for every constructor of `Step`. All three candidates fail on `R_rec_succ`.

Let
\[
B=\mu_i(b),\quad S=\mu_i(s),\quad N=\mu_i(n).
\]

For `R_rec_succ`, the source is `recDelta b s (delta n)` and the target is `app s (recDelta b s n)`.

### `mu1`

\[
\mu_1(\text{source})=2+B+S+N
\]
while
\[
\mu_1(\text{target})=2+B+2S+N.
\]

Thus the target is larger by \(S\). In particular, when `s = void`, the two measures are equal, so strict decrease fails.

### `mu2`

\[
\mu_2(\text{source})=6+B+S+N
\]
and
\[
\mu_2(\text{target})=6+B+2S+N.
\]

Again, the target is larger by \(S\), and equality occurs for `s = void`. Hence `mu2` is not a termination measure.

### `mu3`

\[
\mu_3(\text{source})=2+B+S+N
\]
whereas
\[
\mu_3(\text{target})=3+B+2S+N.
\]

The target is always larger by \(1+S\). Thus `mu3` fails even when `s = void`.

All three candidates do strictly decrease on the other rule families (`R_int_delta`, the merge rules, `R_rec_zero`, and the equality rules), but failure on `R_rec_succ` is enough to reject them. Therefore, **none of `mu1`, `mu2`, or `mu3` is a valid termination measure for the given rewrite calculus**.
