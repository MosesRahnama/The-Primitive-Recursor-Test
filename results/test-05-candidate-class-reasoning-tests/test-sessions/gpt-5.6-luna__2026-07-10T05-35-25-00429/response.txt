All three candidates fail as strict termination measures because of the `R_rec_succ` rule:

```lean
Step (recDelta b s (delta n)) (app s (recDelta b s n))
```

The right-hand side duplicates the contribution of `s`: it appears once as the outer `app` argument and again inside the recursive term.

### `mu1`

For `R_rec_succ`:

```text
mu1 (recDelta b s (delta n))
  = 2 + mu1 b + mu1 s + mu1 n

mu1 (app s (recDelta b s n))
  = 2 + mu1 b + 2 * mu1 s + mu1 n
```

Thus the right-hand side is larger by `mu1 s`. It is not strictly decreasing whenever `s` is non-`void`.

The other rules do decrease under `mu1`, including `R_eq_diff`, where the measure decreases by `1`.

### `mu2`

For `R_rec_succ`:

```text
mu2 (recDelta b s (delta n))
  = 6 + mu2 b + mu2 s + mu2 n

mu2 (app s (recDelta b s n))
  = 6 + mu2 b + 2 * mu2 s + mu2 n
```

Again, the right-hand side is larger by `mu2 s`. Hence `mu2` also fails.

The other rules decrease under `mu2`.

### `mu3`

For `R_rec_succ`:

```text
mu3 (recDelta b s (delta n))
  = 2 + mu3 b + mu3 s + mu3 n

mu3 (app s (recDelta b s n))
  = 3 + mu3 b + 2 * mu3 s + mu3 n
```

The right-hand side is larger by `1 + mu3 s`, so this candidate fails even when `s = void`.

The other rules decrease under `mu3`.

### Conclusion

None of `mu1`, `mu2`, or `mu3` is a valid strict measure for all constructors of `Step`. The common obstruction is `R_rec_succ`; the additive measures do not account for the duplication of `s` on the right-hand side.
