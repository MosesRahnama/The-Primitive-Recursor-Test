### Why this method

I chose **primitive/structural recursion on the third argument** (equivalently, ordinary induction on that ℕ) for three tightly linked reasons that stay inside the boundary you set:

1. **The equations already exhibit exactly that recursion scheme and nothing else.**  
   The two defining equations are the classic base/step pair for recursion on a Peano natural number: the call on `Suc i` mentions only the call on the immediate predecessor `i`, and the call on `0` is non-recursive. No other recursive occurrences, no recursion on `w` or `G`, and no auxiliary recursive functions appear. Consequently the termination measure is literally the constructor structure already written in the syntax; one does not have to invent a measure, a well-founded relation, or an extra equation.

2. **It requires zero strengthening.**  
   Many other standard techniques (lexicographic measures, size change, dependency pairs, semantic interpretations, \ldots) would force one either to add guards, to assume extra properties of `best_eclass_term`/`!`/`@`, or to re-phrase the definition. Structural recursion needs none of those: totality of the helper is given, the list operations are the ordinary total constructors of the ambient syntax, and every reduction step decreases the third argument under the predecessor relation generated by the very constructors `0`/`Suc` that occur in the equations. That is all.

3. **It is complete for the given equations.**  
   Because every possible reduction is forced to follow one of the two equations, an inductive argument whose base case is literally the first equation and whose inductive step is literally the second equation covers the entire function. There are no residual cases, no overlapping patterns, and no hidden calls that would require a different method.

In short, the method is the most economical one that is already spelled out by the syntax you supplied; any other approach would have been either weaker or would have stepped outside the “equations alone, no extra assumptions” constraint.
