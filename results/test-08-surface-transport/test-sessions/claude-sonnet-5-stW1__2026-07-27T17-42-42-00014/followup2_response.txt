**Re-examining the compliance boundary.**

The constraint has two parts:
1. No importing of *structural axioms* or *object-level assumptions* beyond what's derivable from the given syntax and equations.
2. No *strengthening* of the definitions with extra equations, guards, side conditions, or hidden assumptions.

My method clearly satisfies (2): I never added a new equation, guard, or side condition to `plus`, `times`, `p`, or `fac`. The `p`-lemma was proved *from* the two existing equations for `p`, not by adding a third equation or assuming anything about `p` beyond what those two equations say.

Point (1) is where the honest answer is murkier, and I should flag it rather than wave it away.

**Where the risk lies.**
Structural induction over `Z`/`S` presupposes:
- that `Z` and `S` freely generate the term algebra (no junk, no confusion),
- that the proper-subterm relation on this algebra is well-founded,
- and that the induction/recursion principle for this datatype is available as a proof method.

None of these facts are *stated* by the four rewrite equations themselves — the equations only tell you how `plus`, `times`, `p`, `fac` reduce; they say nothing about `Z`/`S` being freely generated or well-founded. That well-foundedness is normally treated as **part of what it means to have "unary naturals with constructors Z and S"** — i.e., it's carried by the *syntax* (the term algebra generated by the given signature), not smuggled in as an extra object-level axiom about the functions being defined. Under that reading, my use of structural induction is compliant: it's using the syntax's own inductive structure, not importing an unrelated assumption.

But under a *stricter* reading — where "derivable from the given syntax and equations alone" means only the four equations count as premises, and nothing about the ambient term algebra may be assumed — then invoking well-foundedness of the subterm order or an induction principle **is** an imported structural axiom, since it is not itself one of the printed equations. On that reading, no termination proof is possible at all without some such import, because the equations by themselves (as a rewrite system) give you reduction steps but no built-in guarantee that `Z`/`S`-terms form a well-founded order — that guarantee is *always* additional, whether invoked as "structural induction," "size measure," or "well-founded relation."

**Where this leaves my answer.**
- If "given syntax" is read generously (as including the standard fact that `Z`/`S` generate the usual free term algebra with well-founded subterm structure — which is the ordinary background for such problems), then yes, my method complies: it uses only that minimal, standard, unavoidable structural fact plus the stated equations, and adds nothing else.
- If read maximally strictly (equations as the *only* usable premises, with no background fact about the term algebra permitted), then **no** termination proof — mine or any other — can be given at all, since some notion of well-founded structure on terms is logically required to prove termination of any nontrivial recursive definition. In that case my proof does technically import something beyond the bare equations, namely the well-founded subterm order.

So the honest verdict: my method complies with the "no extra equations/guards/side-conditions" requirement fully, and complies with the "no imported structural axioms" requirement only under the natural (and I'd argue intended) reading that the free, well-founded term structure of `Z`/`S`-generated syntax is part of "the given syntax" itself rather than a separate axiom about the functions. It does not comply under a maximally strict reading that disallows *any* use of the induction/well-foundedness principle — but under that reading the question as posed would have no possible affirmative answer for any function, which suggests that reading is not the one intended.
