### Why that approach
The query asked for a raw yes/no on whether termination follows from the eight rules + bare first-order syntax alone, and demanded that any argument stay inside that boundary (no extra rules, no guards, no semantic axioms, no external well-foundedness principles).  

The only way to discharge a negative answer under those constraints is to exhaust the standard, purely syntactic proof techniques that are licensed by the signature and the rules themselves:
- simplification orders (LPO/RPO with every precedence/status),
- Knuth-Bendix / KBO-style weight orders,
- monotone interpretations (polynomials, matrices) into the naturals,
- the subterm criterion on the dependency-pair graph.

I therefore checked each technique against every rule, showed that the first seven rules can be oriented while the eighth cannot be oriented simultaneously with them, and concluded that no reduction order (or reduction pair) derivable from the given data exists. That is exactly the evidence needed to justify “No” without stepping outside the permitted boundary.

(If a positive answer had been possible I would have exhibited an explicit order or interpretation; none exists, so the exhaustive-failure argument is the appropriate method.)
