### Why this method

I chose a direct multiset measure on the second arguments of `exec_walk_weight` for three practical reasons that fit the constraints of the problem:

1. **The rules themselves dictate the measure**  
   Inspection of the three rules shows that the only recursive call (rule 3) replaces the list argument  
   `cons(x,cons(y,xs))`  
   by the strictly shorter list  
   `cons(y,xs)`.  
   Rules 1 and 2 simply eliminate the call.  
   No other symbol carries a recursive call.  
   Consequently the size of that list is a natural, self-contained progress measure; packaging those sizes into a multiset makes the argument work for terms that may contain several independent calls.

2. **It stays strictly inside the given syntax**  
   The measure is defined solely from the free term algebra generated by the symbols that appear in the rules.  
   It needs no extra rewrite rules, no guards, no type system, no well-founded order imported from outside, and no semantic interpretation of `add` or `app`.  
   That matches the requirement “from the presented rules alone.”

3. **It is elementary and easy to check exhaustively**  
   Once the measure is written down, verifying the three rules takes a few lines and covers every redex.  
   More sophisticated tools (RPO, polynomial interpretations, dependency pairs, \ldots) also work, but they are heavier than necessary for a system whose recursion is this simple; the multiset argument already gives a complete, self-contained proof.

In short, the shape of the recursion immediately suggests “the list gets shorter,” the multiset construction turns that observation into a rigorous well-founded order, and the whole argument uses nothing beyond the three rules.
