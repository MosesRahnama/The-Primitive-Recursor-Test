I chose a **syntactic, modular descent argument** because it matches both the equations and your restriction against importing object-level axioms.

In particular:

1. **The equations naturally form rewrite rules.**  
   Reading them left-to-right lets us ask the minimal termination question: can there be an infinite sequence of equation applications?

2. **Each recursive dependency has an evident constructor decrease.**
   - `plus` decreases its second argument.
   - `times` decreases its first argument and invokes the already-terminating `plus`.
   - `p` removes one `S` from its recursive argument.
   - `fac` is less immediately structural, because its recursive argument is `p (S x)`, not literally `x`. But on a unary numeral, the given `p` equations themselves prove
     \[
     p(S^{n+1}Z)\to^*S^nZ,
     \]
     so the recursive `fac` call is eventually on a strictly smaller numeral.

3. **It requires no semantic assumptions about naturals.**  
   The measure is simply the number of `S` constructors in a finite input term. Induction here is meta-level induction over the finite syntax presented to the rewrite system—not an imported object-level induction axiom, nor an assumption that every abstract “natural” is either `Z` or `S x`.

4. **A modular proof addresses all introduced work.**  
   Showing only that `fac` descends would be insufficient, because it creates `p` and `times` calls, and `times` creates `plus` calls. Proving termination in dependency order
   \[
   \texttt{plus},\quad \texttt{times},\quad \texttt{p},\quad \texttt{fac}
   \]
   makes explicit that every generated computation terminates.

5. **It exposes the key distinction caused by the missing equation.**  
   Because there is no rule for `fac Z`, recursion eventually reaches an irreducible term rather than a unary numeral. A rewrite-termination proof correctly reports that computation cannot continue forever, while not pretending that the equations define a total numeral-valued factorial.

I avoided proving “factorial is total” semantically because that would require at least a missing base equation for `fac Z`. I also avoided relying solely on a generic term ordering: the `fac` decrease is mediated by evaluating `p`, so the direct syntactic argument is clearer and makes no hidden strengthening of the rules.
