**Verdict:** Yes, if “termination” means termination of the equations oriented left-to-right as a rewrite system: there is no infinite reduction from any unary-constructor input. This follows from the finite syntax of terms alone.

However, the equations do **not** define total unary-natural-valued functions on all inputs. In particular, `p Z` and `fac Z` are stuck, and even `fac (S Z)` does not reduce to a numeral. Thus, if “termination” means “successfully returns a unary natural on every input,” the verdict is **no**.

Let \(\bar n = S^n Z\).

### Termination of the rewrite system

- `plus x Z = x` is a base rule.
- `plus x (S y) = S (plus x y)` decreases the number of `S` constructors in the second argument. Hence `plus a \bar n` has at most \(n\) recursive `plus` steps.

- `times Z y = Z` and `times x Z = Z` are base rules. Their overlap at `times Z Z` is harmless because both produce `Z`.
- `times (S x) y = plus (times x y) y` decreases the first argument of the recursive `times` call by one constructor. The newly introduced `plus` call terminates by the preceding argument. Hence `times \bar m \bar n` terminates by induction on \(m\).

- `p (S Z) = Z` is a base rule.
- `p (S (S x)) = S (p (S x))` removes one leading `S` from the argument of the recursive `p` call. Consequently, purely by induction on finite constructor syntax,
  \[
  p(\bar{n+1}) \;\to^*\; \bar n.
  \]
  No equation for `p Z` is needed for rewrite termination: `p Z` is simply irreducible.

- For
  ```text
  fac (S x) = times (fac (p (S x))) (S x)
  ```
  take a ground input `fac \bar{n+1}`. The `p` call reduces finitely:
  \[
  p(\bar{n+1})\to^*\bar n.
  \]
  Thus the only recursive `fac` call eventually has argument \(\bar n\), one constructor smaller. Induction on \(n\), together with termination of `p`, `times`, and `plus`, excludes an infinite reduction. At \(n=0\), the descent reaches `fac Z`, for which there is no rewrite rule, so reduction stops.

This is a syntactic well-foundedness argument on finite constructor terms; it does not assume an object-level induction axiom, injectivity of `S`, or any additional equation.

### Important limitation

The stopping point is not generally a numeral. For example,
```text
fac (S Z)
→ times (fac (p (S Z))) (S Z)
→ times (fac Z) (S Z)
```
and the final term is irreducible: `fac Z` has no rule, and the surrounding `times` call cannot match any of its left-hand sides.

Therefore:

- **Strong normalization / absence of infinite computation:** established.
- **Total computation to a unary natural on every unary-natural input:** not established and in fact false for the presented rules.
- **A total factorial definition:** not obtained without adding a missing base equation such as one for `fac Z`, which the stated boundary forbids.
