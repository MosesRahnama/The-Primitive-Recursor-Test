Yes, termination of all these functions on all inputs can be established from the given equations alone, without importing extra axioms or strengthening the definitions. Here is the termination argument.

**Verdict:** Terminating.

**Argument:**
We use the standard method of well-founded induction on the *size* of terms, where size is the number of `S` constructors: `|Z| = 0`, `|S t| = 1 + |t|`. All functions are shown to decrease this measure in every recursive call, either directly or via a simple lemma derivable from the equations.

1. **`plus`**  
   Equations: `plus x Z = x`, `plus x (S y) = S (plus x y)`.  
   Recursive call: `plus x y`. The second argument decreases from `S y` (size ≥1) to `y` (size one less). First argument unchanged. Termination immediate.

2. **`times`**  
   Equations: `times Z y = Z`, `times x Z = Z`, `times (S x) y = plus (times x y) y`.  
   Recursive call: `times x y`. The first argument decreases from `S x` to `x` (size one less). The outer call to `plus` is terminating by (1). Termination follows.

3. **`p`**  
   Equations: `p (S (S x)) = S (p (S x))`, `p (S Z) = Z`.  
   Recursive call: `p (S x)`. The argument decreases from `S (S x)` to `S x` (size one less). Termination immediate.

4. **`fac`**  
   Equation: `fac (S x) = times (fac (p (S x))) (S x)`.  
   Recursive call: `fac (p (S x))`. We need to know that `p (S x)` is “smaller” than `S x`. From the defining equations of `p` we can prove by structural induction that `p (S x) = x` holds for all `x`:  
   - If `x = Z`: `p (S Z) = Z`.  
   - If `x = S x'`: `p (S (S x')) = S (p (S x')) = S x'` by induction hypothesis.  
   Hence the normal form of `p (S x)` is `x`, and `|p (S x)| = |x|`. The original argument to `fac` has size `|S x| = 1 + |x|`. Therefore the recursive call is on a term of strictly smaller size. The functions `times` and `p` inside the right-hand side are already known to terminate.

Since every recursive call reduces the size of at least one argument while no equations increase sizes unboundedly, there are no infinite reduction sequences. Inputs that do not match any equation (e.g. `fac Z`) are already in normal form and thus also trivially terminate.
