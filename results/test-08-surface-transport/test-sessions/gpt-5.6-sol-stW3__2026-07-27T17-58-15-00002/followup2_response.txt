The arithmetic assignments are not object-level assumptions such as “\(f2\) really is multiplication” or “\(f4\) satisfies factorial axioms.” They are a **meta-level interpretation** used to construct a well-founded reduction pair. We do not add equations or rewrite rules to the TRS, and the interpreted identities are verified directly for every displayed rule and every valuation of the variables.

Likewise, the dependency-pair theorem is a standard termination proof method, explicitly permitted by the prompt. It uses only:

- their syntactic recursive calls,
- a well-founded order on \(\mathbb N\).

No guards, side conditions, constructor injectivity, induction axioms about the object terms, or assumptions about which terms may occur are introduced.

There is one technical clarification I should have made explicit: the marked-symbol projections do **not** orient every cross-component dependency pair with one global reduction pair. For example,

\[
F2(f1(x),y)\to F0(f2(x,y),y)
\]

is not always weakly decreasing under the stated projections. That is not required after dependency-graph decomposition. The proof must proceed component by component:

- the \(F0\) SCC uses \(F0(x,y)\mapsto y\);
- the \(F2\) SCC uses \(F2(x,y)\mapsto x\);
- the \(F3\) SCC uses \(F3(x)\mapsto x\);
- the \(F4\) SCC uses \(F4(x)\mapsto x\).

The cross-symbol pairs only lead downward in the acyclic component graph:

\[
F4\longrightarrow F2,\ F3,\qquad F2\longrightarrow F0,
\]

with no paths back. Therefore they cannot occur infinitely often in a dependency-pair chain and need not be oriented by the SCC-local processors. All original rewrite rules remain weakly oriented by the unmarked interpretation.

So the method itself complies, and the termination conclusion remains valid. My original presentation was slightly too compressed because it should have explicitly said that the strict projections are applied **separately after SCC decomposition**, rather than suggesting that one global processor handles all dependency pairs at once.
