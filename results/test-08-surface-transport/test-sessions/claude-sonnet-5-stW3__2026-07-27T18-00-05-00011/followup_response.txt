I picked the dependency-pair (DP) framework with usable rules specifically because a **single global argument — one simplification order, one polynomial/RPO interpretation applied uniformly to all rules — provably cannot work here**, and I wanted a method that both respects that obstruction and is still fully derivable from the given rules alone (no extra axioms).

Here's the reasoning that led me there:

**1. I first checked whether a naive monotone algebra (direct rule-by-rule interpretation) could close the whole system.**

Rule 6, `f3(f1(f1(v0))) -> f1(f3(f1(v0)))`, forces any monotone interpretation `[f3]`, `[f1]` validating it as a *strict* decrease to satisfy something like `C(x+2) > C(x+1)+1` — i.e., `f3` needs to look "expansive" or at least non-shrinking under naive orientation, because the right side has an *extra* `f1` wrapped around the recursive call.

But rule 8, `f4(f1(v0)) -> f2(f4(f3(f1(v0))),f1(v0))`, only terminates if `f3(f1(v0))` is *smaller* than `f1(v0)` in whatever measure drives `f4`'s recursion — i.e. `f3` needs to behave like a **strict predecessor/size-reducer** feeding into `f4`.

So `f3` is being asked to play two incompatible roles simultaneously under one global order: expansive-enough-to-orient-rule-6-strictly, and reductive-enough-to-shrink-the-argument-in-rule-8. That's the classic signature situation where **plain simplification orders (LPO, KBO, single polynomial interpretation for all rules at once) fail**, even though the system is actually terminating.

**2. This is exactly the situation DP + usable rules is designed for.**

The DP method doesn't require every rule to strictly decrease under one order — it only requires:
- the *dependency pairs* within each cycle (SCC) of the dependency graph to weakly-or-strictly decrease, with at least one strict decrease per cycle, and
- the *usable rules* (the ordinary rewrite rules that can fire underneath the recursive positions) to only weakly decrease (`≥`), not strictly.

This asymmetry — strict for cycles, weak for usable rules — is precisely the loophole needed: it lets rule 6 and rule 7 (the defining rules for `f3`) be satisfied with **equality** (`≥`) using a predecessor-style interpretation, while the `F4` dependency pair still gets a **strict** decrease from that same interpretation. A single global rule-based order has no such asymmetry to exploit; DP is the standard method that introduces it.

**3. Decomposing into SCCs was necessary, not optional.**

Once I saw the four defined symbols `f0,f2,f3,f4` don't call each other cyclically (only in acyclic chains, `f4→f2→f0` and `f4→f3`), splitting into independent SCCs let me pick a *different* small, tailored interpretation for each recursion (`F0`, `F2`, `F3`, `F4` separately) rather than needing one interpretation to simultaneously satisfy all recursions' demands. This modularity is a built-in feature of the DP framework (SCC decomposition of the dependency graph) and is what made the truncated-predecessor trick for `f3` local to the `{F3}` and `{F4}` components, without having to worry about interference from `f0`/`f2`'s rules.

**4. It stays within the stated boundary.**

The whole argument uses only: the dependency pairs mechanically extracted from the given rules, the dependency graph mechanically computed from the given rules, and elementary well-founded interpretations (ℕ with successor/truncated-predecessor) — no new rewrite rules, no side conditions, no imported termination axioms. That satisfies the constraint of the problem while actually succeeding, unlike a naive single-order attempt.

So: DP + usable rules was chosen because it's the minimal standard escalation from "plain simplification order" that is *known to handle exactly this expansive-rule/reductive-rule tension* (rule 6 vs. rule 8), and it let me verify termination compositionally, one SCC at a time, using only elementary arithmetic interpretations.
