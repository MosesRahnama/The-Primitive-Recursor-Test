The polynomial interpretation I used directly reflects the natural decreasing measure in the system: the length of the list argument in `exec_walk_weight`.

**Why it was chosen:**

- The rewrite rules operate on a simple inductive datatype (`nil` / `cons` lists), and the recursion in the third rule reduces the list from `cons(x, cons(y, xs))` to `cons(y, xs)` — one element shorter.
- This suggests that the number of `cons` constructors in the second argument strictly decreases. The interpretation `⟦cons(x, l)⟧ = ⟦l⟧ + 1` (with `⟦nil⟧ = 0`) makes that length explicit, and `⟦exec_walk_weight(w, l)⟧ = ⟦l⟧ + 1` then produces a strict decrease for every recursive call.
- Polynomial interpretations over the naturals are a standard, self-contained termination proof method that requires no external structural axioms (like induction schemas) or extra reasoning about term sizes. They work by assigning monotone functions to symbols and checking that each rewrite rule reduces the interpretation.
- The method easily handles the other symbols (`add`, `app`, `zero`) by giving them neutral or zero interpretations, ensuring they don't interfere with the decrease.

In short, the polynomial interpretation was a direct, clean fit for the explicit structural recursion present in the system.
