## Verdict: **Termination can be established.**

## Argument

**Setup.** The signature relevant to these rules consists of the defined symbol `exec_walk_weight/2`, together with the constructors `nil`, `cons/2`, and the symbols `zero`, `add/2`, `app/2` which appear only as *output* symbols (they never occur as the outermost symbol of a redex on the left-hand side of any rule, since no rule has `zero`, `add(...)`, or `app(...)` as its head). Hence the rewrite system only ever fires on terms whose head is `exec_walk_weight`, and rewriting a term `exec_walk_weight(w, l)` replaces it by a term built from `zero`, `add`, `app`, `w`, and a *strict subterm* of `l` (specifically the tail of the outer `cons`-`cons` structure) plugged into further `exec_walk_weight` calls, or by `zero` directly.

**Measure.** Define a measure on terms
```
μ(exec_walk_weight(w, l)) = size(l)
```
where `size` counts constructor symbols in the second argument along its `cons`-spine (e.g. `size(nil) = 0`, `size(cons(x,xs)) = 1 + size(xs)`, extended arbitrarily to non-list terms since no rule applies there anyway). This is a well-founded measure into ℕ (with the usual order on naturals), since `size` returns a natural number for every finite term.

**Case-by-case decrease.**

1. **Rule `exec_walk_weight(w,nil) -> zero`.**
   The right-hand side contains no occurrence of `exec_walk_weight` at all, so there is nothing further to compare: this rule cannot create an infinite rewrite sequence on its own, and it does not introduce any new redex of the defined symbol. Trivially terminating (base case, μ irrelevant since no recursive call is spawned).

2. **Rule `exec_walk_weight(w,cons(x,nil)) -> zero`.**
   Same situation: the right-hand side is the constant `zero`, containing no `exec_walk_weight` subterm. No recursive call is generated, so this rule contributes only finitely to any derivation (one step, then the term `zero` is a normal form since no rule rewrites `zero`).

3. **Rule `exec_walk_weight(w,cons(x,cons(y,xs))) -> add(app(app(w,x),y), exec_walk_weight(w,cons(y,xs)))`.**
   The only occurrence of `exec_walk_weight` on the right-hand side is the subterm `exec_walk_weight(w,cons(y,xs))`. Its measure is
   ```
   μ(exec_walk_weight(w,cons(y,xs))) = size(cons(y,xs)) = size(xs)+1,
   ```
   while the measure of the left-hand side is
   ```
   μ(exec_walk_weight(w,cons(x,cons(y,xs)))) = size(cons(x,cons(y,xs))) = size(cons(y,xs)) + 1 = size(xs)+2.
   ```
   Hence the new `exec_walk_weight`-subterm has strictly smaller measure ( `size(xs)+1 < size(xs)+2` ). The other new subterm produced, `app(app(w,x),y)`, contains no occurrence of `exec_walk_weight` (since `w`, `x`, `y` are variables/subterms of the original term, not headed by `exec_walk_weight` by assumption on well-formed instantiation — and even if `w` itself happened to contain occurrences of `exec_walk_weight`, no rule rewrites inside `app`, `add`, or `zero` positions, since those are not defined symbols here), so it generates no further rewriting under this TRS.

**Conclusion.** Every application of Rule 3 strictly decreases the measure `μ` of the (unique, single) `exec_walk_weight`-headed subterm that can still be rewritten, and Rules 1–2 terminate the recursion outright by replacing the term with `zero` (a term with no further applicable rule, since no rule has left-hand side headed by `zero`, `add`, or `app`). Since `μ` maps into the well-founded set (ℕ, <), there is no infinite decreasing sequence, and therefore no infinite rewrite sequence starting from any term can exist: every derivation must terminate after finitely many steps determined by the finite descent of `size` along the `cons`-spine of the second argument.

This argument uses only the given three rules, the definition of `size` on the term's own subterm structure (no external axioms), and well-foundedness of `<` on ℕ — a standard, admissible termination technique (measure/embedding into a well-founded order, a simplification-order-style argument) requiring no additional rules, guards, or side conditions beyond what's given.

**Hence the system is terminating.**
