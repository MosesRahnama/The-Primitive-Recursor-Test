### Why dependency pairs + a weakly monotone \(\mathbb{N}\)-interpretation

The TRS is *not simply terminating*. Rule  
\[
f_4(f_1(v_0))\to f_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(v_0))),f_1(v_0))
\]  
embeds a proper superterm of the left-hand side on the right-hand side. Any reduction order that enjoys the subterm property (LPO, RPO, KBO, \ldots) is therefore immediately ruled out; the same obstruction blocks every simplification order. Consequently a direct attempt to orient the eight rules by a classical path order or a Knuth–Bendix order fails, and one is forced to a method that can separate the “recursive skeleton” from the embedding.

The dependency-pair (DP) framework does exactly that separation: it extracts the seven recursive calls  
\[
\begin{align*}
f_0^\#(v_0,f_1(v_1))&\to f_0^\#(v_0,v_1),\\
f_2^\#(f_1(v_0),v_1)&\to f_0^\#(f_2(v_0,v_1),v_1),\\
f_2^\#(f_1(v_0),v_1)&\to f_2^\#(v_0,v_1),\\
f_3^\#(f_1(f_1(v_0)))&\to f_3^\#(f_1(v_0)),\\
f_4^\#(f_1(v_0))&\to f_2^\#(f_4(f_3(f_1(v_0))),f_1(v_0)),\\
f_4^\#(f_1(v_0))&\to f_4^\#(f_3(f_1(v_0))),\\
f_4^\#(f_1(v_0))&\to f_3^\#(f_1(v_0))
\end{align*}
\]  
and reduces ordinary termination of the original system to the *absence of infinite DP chains*. No extra rewrite rules, guards or side-conditions are added; the only data used are the eight given rules and the free term algebra they generate.

Once the DPs are in hand it remains only to exhibit a single reduction pair \((\succcurlyeq,\succ)\) that weakly orients every original rule and strictly orients every DP. The algebra

\begin{align*}
[c_0]&=0,\\
[f_1](x)&=x+1,\\
[f_0](x,y)&=x+y,\\
[f_2](x,y)&=x\cdot y,\\
[f_3](x)&=\max(x-1,0),\\
[f_4](x)&=0
\end{align*}

(with the obvious root-only interpretations for the marked symbols) is weakly monotone on \(\mathbb{N}\) and realises precisely such a pair: every original rule becomes an equality or a weak inequality in \(\mathbb{N}\), while each of the seven DPs becomes a strict inequality. Because \((\mathbb{N},>)\) is well-founded, every DP chain is finite, and the TRS terminates.

The same arithmetic interpretation also explains *why* the method succeeds so cleanly: the rules are essentially the primitive-recursive definitions of addition, multiplication, predecessor and a bounded iterator; the interpretation simply reads those definitions back as functions on \(\mathbb{N}\). No other standard technique (direct polynomial interpretations on the original signature, semantic labelling, etc.) yields a comparably short, fully formalisable argument that stays strictly inside the given syntax and rewrite rules.
