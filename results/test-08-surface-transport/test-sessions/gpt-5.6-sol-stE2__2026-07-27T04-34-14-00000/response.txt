Let \(E=\texttt{extract\_prefix}\) and \(K=\texttt{extract\_prefix\_k}\).

### Rule 1

\[
E(w,graph,\texttt{zero}) \to \texttt{nil}.
\]

This decreases because \(E\succ\texttt{nil}\).

### Rule 2

\[
E(w,graph,\texttt{succ}(i))
\to
K(w,graph,i,E(w,graph,i)).
\]

First,

\[
E(w,graph,\texttt{succ}(i))
>
E(w,graph,i),
\]

because the roots are equal and, lexicographically, the third argument decreases from `succ(i)` to its proper subterm \(i\).

The left side also exceeds \(w\), `graph`, and \(i\) by the subterm property. Since \(E\succ K\) and the left side exceeds every argument of the right-hand `K`, LPO gives the required strict decrease.

### Rule 3

\[
K(w,graph,i,memo)
\to
\texttt{append}\bigl(memo,
 \texttt{cons}(
   \texttt{best\_eclass\_term}(w,memo,\texttt{nth}(graph,i)),
   \texttt{nil})\bigr).
\]

The left side exceeds each variable argument \(w,graph,i,memo\) by the subterm property. It also exceeds:

- `nth(graph,i)`, since \(K\succ\texttt{nth}\) and it exceeds `graph` and \(i\);
- `best_eclass_term(...)`, since \(K\succ\texttt{best_eclass_term}\) and it exceeds all its arguments;
- the enclosing `cons`, since \(K\succ\texttt{cons}\);
- `nil`;
- and finally the enclosing `append`, since \(K\succ\texttt{append}\).

Thus this rule also strictly decreases. The duplication of `memo` does not invalidate the proof: LPO is stable under substitutions and can orient duplicating rules.

Therefore every rewrite step strictly decreases a well-founded, context- and substitution-closed ordering. Hence no infinite rewrite sequence exists, using only the given syntax and rules.
