The method I used is a direct, standard termination analysis rooted in **syntactic structural recursion**. The reasoning goes like this:

1. **Check each recursive call for immediate constructor-guarded descent.** For `plus`, `times`, and `p`, every recursive call passes an argument that is a **proper syntactic subterm** of the pattern the function is matching on (e.g., `plus x (S y)` recurses on `y`, which is a subterm of `S y`). This guarantees termination purely from the **well-foundedness of the term tree**—no induction, no external assumptions, just the fact that terms are finitely generated from `Z` and `S`. That’s the gold standard for “no imported axioms” because it rests only on the grammar.

2. **Identify calls that are not syntactically structural.** `fac (S x)` recurses on `p (S x)`. The argument `p (S x)` is a function call, not a constructor pattern’s subterm. To prove that this call eventually terminates, one would need to argue that `p (S x)` is “smaller” than `S x` under some well-founded measure. The only natural measure is the number of `S` constructors, and that requires knowing that `p` reduces its argument (specifically, that `p (S x)` equals `x`). Proving that property demands **structural induction on `x`**, because the equations for `p` only describe its behavior stepwise:  
   - `p (S (S x)) = S (p (S x))` reduces the argument but produces a term that still has an `S` on the outside; we need to know that eventually the `S Z` base case is reached and the whole thing collapses to `x`. That is exactly what induction proves.  
   Without induction, you cannot derive `p (S x) = x` as an equational consequence from the given equations alone (equational logic without induction is too weak). So termination cannot be justified within the allowed boundaries.

3. **Why this method and not something else?** Any termination argument must in the end exhibit a decreasing measure that respects the recursive calls. If a call is not constructor-guarded, the measure typically depends on a *semantic property* of another function (here, that `p` strictly reduces the size). The only way to establish that semantic property from the equations is to perform induction over the datatype. Since the problem explicitly forbids importing structural axioms (i.e., assuming an induction principle for the naturals) and restricts us to what is derivable from the syntax and equations, the methodical step of separating syntactic structural recursion from non‑structural recursion immediately shows that `fac` is beyond the pale. Hence the verdict.
