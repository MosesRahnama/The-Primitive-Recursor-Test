### Why this method

I chose a direct algebraic interpretation (into \(\mathbb{N}\) with a mix of linear and exponential polynomials) for three tightly related reasons that stay inside the problem constraints:

1. **The shape of the rules forces a measure that decreases on a nested constructor.**  
   Rules 1–2 are base cases that erase an \(f_3\)-redex completely. Rule 3 is a single recursive step whose right-hand side rebuilds an \(f_1/f_2\)-context around a strictly smaller recursive call \(f_3(v_0,f_0(v_2,v_3))\).  The second argument of \(f_3\) is therefore the only plausible induction parameter, and it is built from the binary symbol \(f_0\).  A simple additive polynomial cannot bound the growth of the surrounding \(f_1/f_2\) context while still guaranteeing a strict drop on that recursive call; an exponential in the second argument absorbs the context easily and still yields a natural-number decrease.  The concrete functions I gave are the smallest ones that make the three inequalities hold simultaneously for every ground instantiation.

2. **It is a complete, self-contained reduction order derived only from the given signature and rules.**  
   Once the interpretation is fixed, verification is pure arithmetic on the three left- and right-hand sides—no extra rewrite rules, no guards, no side conditions, no appeal to any object-level axiom beyond the inductive definition of the term algebra.  Monotonicity of each interpreting function automatically lifts the ground inequalities to a rewrite order, and well-foundedness of \(>\) on \(\mathbb{N}\) finishes the proof.  That matches exactly the boundary you set.

3. **It is among the lightest standard techniques that succeed here.**  
   Syntactic methods (RPO/LPO/KBO with any precedence/status) fail on rule 3 because the recursive call sits under an extra \(f_1/f_2\) layer whose symbols would have to be both larger and smaller than \(f_3\).  Dependency-pair or matrix interpretations also work but require more machinery (dependency graphs, usable rules, etc.) that is unnecessary for a three-rule system.  The exponential interpretation is therefore the most economical argument that still inspects every rule directly and stays strictly inside the given syntax.

In short, the method was selected because it is forced by the recursive structure, needs nothing beyond the presented rules, and yields a fully explicit, checkable termination proof.
