### Verdict
**Yes**, termination can be established from the given rules alone (via a standard arithmetic interpretation into the well-founded naturals, inducing a monotone reduction order). No extra rules, guards, side-conditions, or imported object-level axioms are required.

### Termination argument
Interpret every ground term in the carrier \(\mathbb{N} = \{0,1,2,\dots\}\) by the following strictly monotone functions (the interpretation of a constant symbol is a fixed element of \(\mathbb{N}\); the interpretation of an \(n\)-ary function symbol is a function \(\mathbb{N}^n\to\mathbb{N}\)):

- \([[c_0]] = 0\)
- \([[c_1]] = 0\)
- \([[f_0]](x,y) = x + y + 1\)
- \([[f_1]](x,y) = x + y\)
- \([[f_2]](x,y) = x + y\)
- \([[f_3]](x,y) = (x + 1) \cdot 2^y\)

(The unique homomorphic extension to all ground terms is well-defined by structural recursion. Each function is strictly increasing in every argument.)

It is enough to verify \([[\ell\sigma]] > [[r\sigma]]\) in \(\mathbb{N}\) for every rule \(\ell\to r\) and every ground substitution \(\sigma\). (Monotonicity of the interpretation then guarantees that the strict order on ground terms is closed under contexts, hence a reduction order; well-foundedness of \(>\) on \(\mathbb{N}\) immediately yields termination of the TRS.)

- **Rule 1.**  
  \([[f_3(v_0,c_0)]] = (v_0 + 1) \cdot 2^0 = v_0 + 1 \ge 1 > 0 = [[c_1]]\).

- **Rule 2.**  
  \([[f_0(v_1,c_0)]] = v_1 + 1\), therefore  
  \([[f_3(v_0,f_0(v_1,c_0))]] = (v_0 + 1) \cdot 2^{v_1 + 1} \ge 2 > 0 = [[c_1]]\).

- **Rule 3.**  
  Write \(A = [[v_0]]\), \(B = [[v_1]]\), \(C = [[v_2]]\), \(D = [[v_3]]\).  
  The left-hand side evaluates to  
  \([[f_3(v_0,f_0(v_1,f_0(v_2,v_3)))]] = (A + 1) \cdot 2^{B + C + D + 2}\).  
  The right-hand side evaluates to  
  \(A + B + C + (A + 1) \cdot 2^{C + D + 1}\).  
  The required inequality therefore simplifies to  
  \((A + 1) \cdot 2^{B + C + D + 2} > A + B + C + (A + 1) \cdot 2^{C + D + 1}\).  
  Letting \(M = (A + 1) \cdot 2^{C + D + 1}\) (so \(M \ge 1\)) this is exactly  
  \(M \cdot 2^{B + 1} > A + B + C + M\),  
  i.e.  
  \(M \cdot (2^{B + 1} - 1) > A + B + C\).  
  The left-hand side is at least  
  \((A + 1) \cdot 2^{C + 1} \cdot (2^{B + 1} - 1)\).  
  The elementary inequalities \(2^{k} \ge k + 1\) (any \(k \in \mathbb{N}\)) and non-negativity of all variables make the claim immediate by case analysis on \(B\):
  - if \(B = 0\) then the factor \((2^{B + 1} - 1) = 1\) and one obtains \((A + 1) \cdot 2^{C + 1} \ge (A + 1)(C + 1) > A + C\);
  - if \(B \ge 1\) then the factor \((2^{B + 1} - 1) \ge 3\) and the exponential \(2^{C + 1}\) already dominates the linear term \(A + B + C\).

  In all cases the inequality is strict.

Thus every rewrite step strictly decreases the interpretation of a ground term. Consequently no infinite rewrite sequence exists.

(The same interpretation also shows that the three rules are non-overlapping on ground terms and that every reduction sequence is in fact bounded by the value of the initial term, but those observations are superfluous for termination.)
