The method I used—a polynomial interpretation into the natural numbers—complies with the stated constraints.

The prompt requires that the termination argument:

1. **Does not import structural axioms** (like associativity, commutativity, etc.) that are not present in the rewrite system.
2. **Does not import object-level assumptions** beyond what is derivable from the syntax and rules.
3. **Does not strengthen the system** with extra rewrite rules, guards, or side conditions.

Polynomial interpretations are a standard, accepted technique in term rewriting for proving termination. They do **not** alter the rewrite system in any way; they define a measure function `[·]` from terms to ℕ, show that each rewrite strictly decreases that measure, and conclude termination by the well-foundedness of the natural numbers. The arithmetic used (addition, multiplication, exponentiation, etc.) is a **meta‑level** tool, not an object‑level axiom about the terms. No new equality or rewrite rule is added, and no property of the function symbols (beyond their arity and the given rules) is assumed.

Therefore, the proof stays within the boundary: it relies solely on the given syntax and rewrite rules to define the interpretation, and the well‑foundedness of ℕ is a foundational assumption of mathematics, not an imported structural axiom about the term algebra.
