I chose a **polynomial/interpretation method** over a purely syntactic ordering (like recursive path ordering) because several rules in this system are not structurally decreasing.

Specifically:

- **Rule 5**: `f2(f1(v0),v1) → f0(f2(v0,v1),v1)` — the left side embeds `f2(v0,v1)` inside `f0`, but the top symbol changes from `f2` to `f0`. Moreover, the argument to `f2` on the left is `f1(v0)` while on the right it’s just `v0`, so a direct syntactic subterm embedding fails because `f1(v0)` is not a subterm of `f0(f2(v0,v1),v1)`.  
- **Rule 8**: `f4(f1(v0)) → f2(f4(f3(f1(v0))), f1(v0))` — the right side contains `f4` applied to a structurally larger argument than `f1(v0)`, i.e. `f3(f1(v0))` is not obviously smaller, and nested recursion prevents simple structural decrement.

A purely syntactic ordering (like RPO) would need carefully chosen status and precedence, and it’s not obvious it can handle Rules 5 and 8 simultaneously without extra semantic content.

The semantic interpretation approach gives a **uniform**, **monotone** mapping of every term to a natural number, showing that each rewrite step strictly decreases the weight. It’s a standard, complete-in-practice method for first‑order systems where structural comparisons fail, and it requires no hidden axioms or side conditions — just arithmetic.
