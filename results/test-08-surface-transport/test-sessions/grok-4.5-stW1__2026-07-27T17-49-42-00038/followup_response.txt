### Why dependency pairs + algebraic interpretation

The given equations form a pure first-order TRS (no conditions, no extra symbols, no imported lemmas).  The recursive structure is not purely structural:

- `plus` and `times` recurse on a constructor argument,
- `p` recurses on a non-constructor context (`S(S x)`),
- `fac` makes a recursive call on the *result* of another defined function (`p(S x)`), which is not a literal subterm.

A direct “structural induction on the last argument” therefore does not go through for every equation without additional justifications that would effectively add hidden axioms or guards.  Likewise, a plain recursive-path order needs a precedence/status that is not uniquely determined by the equations themselves, and a pure equational noetherian-induction argument simply reinvent a termination order under another name.

The dependency-pair (DP) method is the lightest standard technique that

1. extracts *exactly* the recursive calls that appear in the given right-hand sides (the six DPs listed in the previous answer),
2. asks only for a reduction pair \((\succsim,>)\) that makes every original rule weakly decreasing and every DP strictly decreasing,
3. can be discharged by a simple monotone algebra whose carrier and operations are defined from the same constructors that appear in the equations (here \(\mathbb{N}\) with addition, multiplication, monus and factorial).

All of the required inequalities are literal arithmetic consequences of substituting the eight defining equations into the interpretation; nothing beyond those equations is used.  The method therefore stays inside the boundary stated in the query, scales to the mild non-structural recursion present in `p`/`fac`, and yields a fully formal, machine-checkable termination certificate.  Other modern techniques (matrix interpretations, semantic labelling, \ldots) would also work but are heavier than needed for this system.
