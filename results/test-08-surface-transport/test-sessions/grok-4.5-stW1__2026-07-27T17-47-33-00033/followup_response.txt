### Why this method
I chose ordinary structural induction on the inductive syntax of the unary naturals (freely generated by the constructors \(Z\) and \(S\)) for three tightly linked reasons that stay inside the problem boundary you set:

1. The syntax itself already supplies the induction principle and the subterm (structural decrease) relation. No well-foundedness axiom, no external order, no measure function, and no object-level assumption beyond “terms are built from \(Z\) and \(S\)” is required. That matches the demand to use only what is “derivable from the given syntax and equations.”

2. Every equation can be examined verbatim as a rewrite rule. For the first three functions the recursive calls are literally on immediate subterms, so the inductive step is immediate. For \(\mathit{fac}\) the same syntax lets us derive (again by a trivial induction that uses only the two equations of \(p\)) the ground identity \(p\,(S\,x)\to^*x\). Once that identity is in hand the recursive call of \(\mathit{fac}\) is also on a subterm, and we are done. Nothing is added to any definition—no extra equation, guard, or side-condition.

3. Alternative techniques (lexicographic path orders, dependency pairs, size-change graphs, interpretation methods, \ldots) either presuppose a well-founded relation that must be imported from outside or require one to strengthen the system with auxiliary functions or constraints. Those moves would violate the “equations alone” restriction. Structural induction needs none of them.

Hence the method is the most economical one that still discharges every equation while remaining strictly inside the stated limits.
