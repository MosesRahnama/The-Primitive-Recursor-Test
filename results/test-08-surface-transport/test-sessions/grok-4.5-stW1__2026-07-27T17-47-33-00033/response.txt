### Verdict
**Yes**, termination (i.e., every reduction sequence starting from a ground application of these functions to unary numerals is finite) can be established from the given syntax of unary naturals and the presented equations alone.

### Termination Argument
The syntax supplies the free inductive generation of the domain by the constructors \(Z\) and \(S\). This justifies ordinary structural induction (and the subterm relation) on ground terms; no external structural axiom, well-foundedness postulate, guard, side-condition or extra equation is added to any definition. The given equations are used solely as rewrite rules. We discharge the claim for each function in turn; every equation is examined explicitly.

**plus.**  
- Equation \(\mathit{plus}\,x\,Z=x\): irreducible after one step.  
- Equation \(\mathit{plus}\,x\,(S\,y)=S\,(\mathit{plus}\,x\,y)\): the sole recursive invocation is on the immediate subterm \(y\).  
Structural induction on the second argument therefore yields termination of every ground instance.

**times.**  
- Equation \(\mathit{times}\,Z\,y=Z\): irreducible after one step.  
- Equation \(\mathit{times}\,x\,Z=Z\): irreducible after one step.  
- Equation \(\mathit{times}\,(S\,x)\,y=\mathit{plus}\,(\mathit{times}\,x\,y)\,y\): the recursive invocation is on the immediate subterm \(x\); the surrounding \(\mathit{plus}\) terminates by the preceding case.  
(The two base-case equations may overlap on \(\mathit{times}\,Z\,Z\), yet both sides reduce to the same normal form \(Z\) in finitely many steps.) Structural induction on the first argument therefore yields termination of every ground instance.

**p.**  
- Equation \(p\,(S\,Z)=Z\): irreducible after one step.  
- Equation \(p\,(S\,(S\,x))=S\,(p\,(S\,x))\): the sole recursive invocation is on the immediate subterm \(S\,x\).  
(The term \(p\,Z\) is already a normal form.) Structural induction on the argument therefore yields termination of every ground instance.

**Derived property needed only for the next case (still obtained solely from the equations + syntax).**  
By structural induction on \(x\) the two equations for \(p\) imply the ground reduction
\[
p\,(S\,x)\;\to^*\;x.
\]
(Base: \(x=Z\) uses the second \(p\)-equation. Step: \(x=S\,y\) uses the first \(p\)-equation together with the induction hypothesis.)

**fac.**  
- Equation \(\mathit{fac}\,(S\,x)=\mathit{times}\,(\mathit{fac}\,(p\,(S\,x)))\,(S\,x)\).  
  First apply the already-established termination of \(p\). The derived property above rewrites the recursive argument exactly to the immediate subterm \(x\). The surrounding \(\mathit{times}\) terminates by the earlier case.  
(The term \(\mathit{fac}\,Z\) is already a normal form.) Structural induction on the argument therefore yields termination of every ground instance.

All four functions are thereby terminating on all ground inputs. No equation has been omitted, strengthened or guarded, and the only inductive reasoning employed is that supplied directly by the inductive syntax of the unary naturals.
