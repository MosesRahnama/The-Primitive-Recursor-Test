## Verdict: **Terminating** — provable by the Recursive Path Order (RPO)

The system

1. `f5(v0,v1,c0) -> c1`
2. `f5(v0,v1,f0(v2)) -> f6(v0,v1,v2,f5(v0,v1,v2))`
3. `f6(v0,v1,v2,v3) -> f2(v3,f1(f4(v0,v3,f3(v1,v2)),c1))`

can be shown terminating using a **Recursive Path Order** with a suitable precedence and lexicographic status, without adding any extra rules, side conditions, or semantic assumptions. (A plain polynomial/numeric interpretation over ℕ actually *fails* here, because rule 3 relates the free variables `v2,v3` with no numeric constraint linking them; RPO succeeds because it compares term *structure*, and both `v2` and `v3` are genuine subterms of the left‑hand side `f6(v0,v1,v2,v3)`.)

### Precedence and status

Take the strict precedence
```
f6 >_prec f5 >_prec f2 >_prec f1 >_prec f4 >_prec f3 >_prec f0 >_prec c1 >_prec c0
```
with lexicographic (left‑to‑right) status for `f5` (needed for rule 2); all other symbols only need the basic RPO clauses.

### Rule 1: `f5(v0,v1,c0) -> c1`

Root of LHS is `f5`, root of RHS is the constant `c1`. Since `f5 >_prec c1`, and `c1` has no arguments to dominate, the RPO clause “`f >_prec g` and `s ≥ t_j` for all args `t_j` of `t`” holds vacuously. Hence `f5(v0,v1,c0) >_rpo c1`.

### Rule 2: `f5(v0,v1,f0(v2)) -> f6(v0,v1,v2,f5(v0,v1,v2))`

Here `f5 >_prec f6`. We must show the LHS dominates every argument of the RHS:

- `v0`, `v1` are direct subterms of the LHS ⇒ dominated (subterm property).
- `v2` is a subterm of `f0(v2)`, which is a subterm of the LHS ⇒ dominated.
- `f5(v0,v1,v2)`: same head symbol `f5` as the LHS, so compare argument tuples lexicographically:
  `(v0,v1,f0(v2))` vs `(v0,v1,v2)`. First two components equal; third component compares `f0(v2)` vs `v2`, and `f0(v2) >_rpo v2` by the subterm property. Hence the LHS lexicographically dominates, giving
  `f5(v0,v1,f0(v2)) >_rpo f5(v0,v1,v2)`.

All four RHS-arguments are dominated by the LHS, and `f5 >_prec f6`, so the whole rule is oriented.

### Rule 3: `f6(v0,v1,v2,v3) -> f2(v3,f1(f4(v0,v3,f3(v1,v2)),c1))`

Here `f6` has the top precedence among all symbols occurring on the RHS (`f2,f1,f4,f3,c1`). We must show `s = f6(v0,v1,v2,v3)` dominates every subterm of the RHS built from these lower symbols:

- `v3` is a direct subterm of `s` ⇒ dominated.
- `c1`: `f6 >_prec c1`, no arguments to check ⇒ dominated.
- `f3(v1,v2)`: `f6 >_prec f3`; its arguments `v1,v2` are direct subterms of `s` ⇒ dominated ⇒ `s >_rpo f3(v1,v2)`.
- `f4(v0,v3,f3(v1,v2))`: `f6 >_prec f4`; its arguments `v0`, `v3` are subterms of `s`, and `f3(v1,v2)` is dominated as just shown ⇒ `s >_rpo f4(...)`.
- `f1(f4(...),c1)`: `f6 >_prec f1`; both its arguments are dominated (previous two bullets) ⇒ `s >_rpo f1(...)`.
- Finally, `f2(v3, f1(...))`: `f6 >_prec f2`; both arguments (`v3`, `f1(...)`) are dominated ⇒ `s >_rpo f2(v3,f1(...))`.

Thus the RHS as a whole is dominated by the LHS, so rule 3 is oriented — including correctly handling the fact that `v3` is *duplicated* in the RHS: RPO’s subterm property guarantees each copy is already ≤ the original term, so duplication does not break the order.

### Conclusion

RPO is a monotonic, stable, well-founded order on terms (a simplification order), and all three rules are oriented left‑to‑right by it under the precedence given above. Since every rewrite step (at any position, under any substitution) strictly decreases the RPO order, no infinite rewrite sequence exists.

**Hence the system is terminating**, and this is established purely from the rewrite rules themselves via a standard, admissible termination technique (RPO), with no additional axioms, guards, or side conditions introduced.
