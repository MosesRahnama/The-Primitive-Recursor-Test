I chose **RPO (Recursive Path Order)** rather than any other termination technique for several concrete reasons tied to the shape of this particular system:

**1. The system is a pure term-algebra recursion, with no arithmetic content.**
All function symbols (`f0,...,f6,c0,c1`) are uninterpreted constructors/defined symbols with no numeric meaning attached anywhere in the rules. A polynomial/interpretation-based proof would require *inventing* weights or algebra over ℕ (e.g., assigning `f5 ↦ 2x+1`, etc.) — that is extra semantic content not present in, or derivable from, the syntax itself. RPO, by contrast, is defined *purely* from the term structure and a precedence on symbols, so it stays strictly within what the rules themselves supply: no external numeric universe, no chosen coefficients, nothing beyond the syntax.

**2. The recursion structure is exactly the kind RPO is built to capture.**
Rule 2 recurses by replacing `f0(v2)` with `v2` — a literal subterm relation. Rule 3 uses arguments (`v2`, `v3`) that are *already* subterms of the left-hand side, just repackaged under new symbols. This is precisely the pattern RPO is designed to detect: "the head symbol decreases in precedence, and everything on the right is either a proper subterm of the left or built recursively from things that are." No global measure or potential function needs to be guessed — the order falls directly out of the term embedding relation plus a precedence.

**3. Variable duplication makes naive polynomial interpretations awkward.**
In rule 3, `v3` occurs twice on the right (once directly, once nested inside `f4`). A polynomial interpretation needs strict monotonicity/multiplicativity assumptions to handle this safely, which effectively injects extra structural assumptions about how symbols "add up" — assumptions not forced by the rules. RPO handles duplication for free via its subterm property (a term is always ≥ each of its own subterms, however many times they're reused), so no additional argument or side-assumption is needed.

**4. It's the minimal, most direct method that stays inside the stated boundary.**
Techniques like dependency pairs with argument filtering, or semantic labeling, or user-chosen interpretations, all require adding auxiliary structure (extra pairs, filterings, interpretation domains) on top of the given rules. Since the task explicitly disallows importing extra axioms/guards/assumptions, I wanted a method whose only "extra" input is a precedence — a strict total order on the finitely many symbols already appearing in the system, which is not an axiom about the semantics of those symbols but just a comparison choice used internally by the order. RPO with a suitable precedence and lexicographic status was sufficient to orient all three rules, so there was no need to reach for a heavier framework.

In short: the rules describe a straightforward structural descent (peeling `f0`, reusing existing subterms), the term algebra has no arithmetic semantics to interpret, and there's variable duplication that disfavors naive polynomial methods — all of which point directly at RPO as the simplest and most syntactically faithful termination certificate.
