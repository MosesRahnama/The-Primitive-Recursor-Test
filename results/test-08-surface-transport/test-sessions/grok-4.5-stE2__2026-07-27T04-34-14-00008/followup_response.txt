The method was chosen because the TRS is a plain first-order system whose recursive structure is exactly what lexicographic path orders are designed to capture.

- The only recursion is the structural descent on the third argument of `extract_prefix` (the Peano numeral).  
  Rule 2 replaces `succ(i)` by `i` while keeping the two other arguments identical; LPO’s left-to-right status therefore makes the recursive call strictly smaller by a single lexicographic comparison.  
- The remaining two rules simply replace a defined symbol by a term headed by a smaller symbol (`extract_prefix_k`, `append`, \ldots). A precedence that ranks the defined symbols in the order they appear in the call graph orients both rules at once.  
- No associative-commutative symbols, no conditional rules, and no need for semantic interpretations appear, so a pure syntactic simplification order suffices. LPO is the most direct such order: once the precedence and status are fixed, every rule is oriented by a routine check that never leaves the given syntax.

Other standard techniques (polynomial interpretations, dependency pairs, Knuth-Bendix order, \ldots) also succeed, but each requires either more arithmetic bookkeeping or an auxiliary analysis that is unnecessary once a suitable LPO is exhibited. Hence LPO gives the shortest, fully self-contained termination argument that stays strictly inside the presented signature and rules.
