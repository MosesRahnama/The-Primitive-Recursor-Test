I chose that method because the rules are not obviously decreasing under ordinary syntactic measures such as term size, depth, or the subterm relation.

In particular:

- The \(f4\)-rule substantially increases term size:
  \[
  f4(f1(x))\to f2(f4(f3(f1(x))),f1(x)).
  \]
  Its recursive argument \(f3(f1(x))\) is not a proper subterm of \(f1(x)\).

- The \(f2\)-rule duplicates its second argument:
  \[
  f2(f1(x),y)\to f0(f2(x,y),y).
  \]
  This makes simple size and occurrence-count arguments unsuitable.

- The \(f3\)-rule moves an \(f1\) from inside \(f3\) to outside:
  \[
  f3(f1(f1(x)))\to f1(f3(f1(x))),
  \]
  so the relevant decrease is obscured by a simultaneous increase elsewhere.

What all recursive rules do have in common is that their continued applicability depends on an argument exposing an \(f1\) at its root. That suggested measuring not total size, but the maximum number of leading \(f1\)'s that a term can expose by rewriting. The quantity \(P(t)\) was chosen precisely to capture this operational resource.

It handles the difficult \(f4\)-cycle particularly cleanly:

\[
f4(f1(x))
\rightsquigarrow f4(f3(f1(x))).
\]

For the recursive \(f4\) to fire again, \(f3(f1(x))\) must reduce to some \(f1(y)\). The \(f3\)-rules can produce an outer \(f1\) only by consuming one that was available inside \(x\). Therefore

\[
P(f1(y))<P(f1(x)).
\]

Thus every reactivation of \(f4\) consumes a natural-number resource, even though neither term size nor direct subterm ordering decreases.

Conceptually, this is a specialized dependency-chain/minimal-counterexample proof:

1. Choose a minimal hypothetical nonterminating term, ensuring its proper subterms terminate.
2. Show \(f0,f2,f3\) preserve termination on terminating arguments.
3. Conclude that any infinite behavior must pass through infinitely many recursive \(f4\)-activations.
4. Rank those activations by \(P\), which strictly decreases.

I picked it because it follows the exact syntactic control flow of the rules and requires no interpretation of \(c0\) and \(f1\) as actual zero and successor, no constructor-disjointness assumptions, and no added rules or guards. Other standard techniques might also work, but this measure directly exposes the otherwise hidden descent in the \(f4\)-\(f3\) interaction.
