One qualification is important: \(P(t)\) was defined only for already-terminating terms. Therefore it cannot simply be treated as a global reduction ordering on all terms. The argument must be organized as a minimal-counterexample/strong-normalization proof:

1. Proper subterms of a minimal nonterminating term are terminating.
2. Hence \(P\) is defined on the relevant arguments.
3. \(f0,f2,f3\) preserve termination on terminating arguments.
4. Any remaining infinite behavior must involve repeated \(f4\)-activations.
5. Those activations strictly decrease \(P\).

That organization avoids circularity. My initial answer indicated this structure, but some closure arguments were compressed into “can be formalized by induction.” So my assessment is:
