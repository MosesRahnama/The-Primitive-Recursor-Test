I chose the **Dependency Pair (DP) framework with Polynomial Interpretations** because it was uniquely suited to satisfy the strict negative constraints in your prompt while successfully proving termination. 

Here is the exact breakdown of why this specific method was necessary and optimal:

### 1. The Constraint: No Structural Axioms or Object-Level Assumptions
In standard type theories (like Coq, Agda, or Lean), termination is usually verified via **syntactic structural recursion**. A function is accepted if recursive calls are made on strict syntactic sub-terms of the input.

In your definition of `fac`, the recursive call is `fac (p (S x))`. Syntactically, `p (S x)` is *larger* than `S x` (it contains `S`, `x`, and `p`). A standard termination checker will reject this. To fix it in those systems, you have to prove an object-level lemma: `p (S x) = x`. But proving that requires **mathematical induction**, which is a structural axiom. 

Because your prompt explicitly forbade importing structural axioms or proving object-level assumptions, all standard type-theoretic approaches were dead on arrival.

### 2. The Solution: Term Rewriting Systems (TRS)
By shifting the perspective from Type Theory to **Term Rewriting Systems**, termination becomes a meta-level property called *Strong Normalization*. In TRS, we don't need to prove that `p (S x)` equals `x` inside an object logic. We only need to prove that applying the rewrite rules strictly decreases some well-founded measure. 

### 3. Why the Dependency Pair (DP) Framework?
Even within TRS, proving termination without DPs is difficult. If you try to find a single measure where *every* rule strictly decreases (e.g., $L > R$), you run into trouble with the `fac` rule:
`fac (S x) -> times (fac (p (S x))) (S x)`

To make the left side strictly greater than the right side, the measure of `fac (S x)` must be larger than the measure of the multiplication *and* the recursive call combined. This is incredibly difficult to map mathematically without causing contradictions in the other rules.

The **Dependency Pair Framework** solves this elegantly. It separates the problem into two distinct tasks:
1. **The original rules** only need to be *weakly decreasing* ($L \ge R$). This allows us to essentially "ignore" the growth caused by helper functions like `times`.
2. **The recursive calls (DPs)** are isolated and must be *strictly decreasing* ($L > R$). 

### 4. Why Polynomial Interpretations over $\mathbb{N}$?
Once the DP framework isolates the recursive calls, we need a way to mathematically guarantee they decrease. Standard syntactic orderings (like Lexicographic or Recursive Path Orders) fail here for the same reason syntactic structural checkers fail: `p (S x)` is not syntactically smaller than `S x`.

**Polynomial Interpretations** allow us to map the abstract syntax to concrete algebra. 
* By mapping `S(x)` to $x + 1$ and `p(x)` to $x \dot{-} 1$ (monus), the math does the heavy lifting for us. 
* The meta-level evaluation simplifies $(x + 1) \dot{-} 1$ to $x$. 
* The strict descent $x + 1 > x$ is trivially true in the natural numbers.

### Summary
I picked this method because standard syntactic methods fail on `p (S x)`, and proving it via structural induction was forbidden by your prompt. DPs + Polynomials bypasses the syntax entirely, using algebraic semantics to prove strong normalization purely at the meta-level, perfectly satisfying your constraints.
