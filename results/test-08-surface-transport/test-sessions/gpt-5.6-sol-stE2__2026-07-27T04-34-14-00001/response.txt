**Verdict: Yes.** Termination follows syntactically from the three rules alone. No interpretation of `zero`/`succ` as natural numbers, and no axioms for `append`, `nth`, graphs, or memoization, are needed.

Use the **lexicographic path ordering (LPO)** with lexicographic status and a precedence satisfying, for example,

\[
\texttt{extract\_prefix}
\succ
\texttt{extract\_prefix\_k}
\succ
\texttt{append},\texttt{cons},\texttt{best\_eclass\_term},
\texttt{nth},\texttt{nil}.
\]

The remaining symbols, including `succ` and `zero`, may be placed arbitrarily in a total extension of this precedence.

Let \(>\) denote this LPO.

### Rule 1

\[
\texttt{extract\_prefix}(w,graph,\texttt{zero}) \to \texttt{nil}.
\]

This decreases because

\[
\texttt{extract\_prefix} \succ \texttt{nil}.
\]

### Rule 2

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\texttt{extract\_prefix}(w,graph,\texttt{succ}(i))\\
&\quad\to
\texttt{extract\_prefix\_k}
 (w,graph,i,\texttt{extract\_prefix}(w,graph,i)).
\end{aligned}
\]

First,

\[
\texttt{succ}(i)>i
\]

by the strict subterm property. Therefore, using lexicographic comparison of the arguments,

\[
\texttt{extract\_prefix}(w,graph,\texttt{succ}(i))
>
\texttt{extract\_prefix}(w,graph,i).
\]

The left-hand side is also greater than \(w\), `graph`, and \(i\) by the subterm property. Since

\[
\texttt{extract\_prefix}\succ\texttt{extract\_prefix\_k},
\]

it is consequently greater than the entire right-hand side.

This uses only the syntactic fact that \(i\) is a proper subterm of `succ(i)`; it does not assume that `succ` denotes numerical succession.

### Rule 3

\[
\begin{aligned}
&\texttt{extract\_prefix\_k}(w,graph,i,memo)\\
&\quad\to
\texttt{append}
 \bigl(memo,
   \texttt{cons}(
      \texttt{best\_eclass\_term}
       (w,memo,\texttt{nth}(graph,i)),
      \texttt{nil})
 \bigr).
\end{aligned}
\]

The left-hand side is greater than each of its arguments by the subterm property. Moreover, its root symbol precedes every root symbol introduced on the right:

- it dominates `nth(graph,i)` because it dominates `graph` and \(i\);
- it then dominates `best_eclass_term(...)`;
- it dominates `nil` and hence `cons(...)`;
- finally, it dominates the complete `append(...)` term.

Although `memo` occurs twice on the right, LPO permits this orientation: each occurrence is still strictly below the whole left-hand side. Thus duplication does not invalidate the proof.

Since LPO is well-founded on finite first-order terms and is closed under substitutions and contexts, every rewrite step strictly decreases the ordering. Therefore, **the presented rewrite system is terminating on all finite first-order terms**.
