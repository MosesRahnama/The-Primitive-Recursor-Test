I chose **lexicographic path ordering (LPO)** because the rules have exactly the syntactic shape it handles well:

1. **There is an obvious call hierarchy.**  
   `extract_prefix` calls `extract_prefix_k`, while `extract_prefix_k` produces only helper symbols such as `append`, `cons`, `best_eclass_term`, and `nth`. A precedence
   \[
   \texttt{extract\_prefix} \succ \texttt{extract\_prefix\_k} \succ \text{helpers}
   \]
   captures that hierarchy directly.

2. **The only recursive call is visibly smaller by the subterm relation.**  
   Rule 2 changes
   \[
   \texttt{extract\_prefix}(\ldots,\texttt{succ}(i))
   \quad\text{to}\quad
   \texttt{extract\_prefix}(\ldots,i).
   \]
   LPO uses the purely syntactic fact that \(i\) is a proper subterm of `succ(i)`. Thus it requires no assumption that `succ` represents arithmetic succession.

3. **LPO handles nested right-hand sides.**  
   In Rule 2, the smaller recursive call is nested inside `extract_prefix_k`. In Rule 3, several helper applications are nested inside one another. LPO can show that the left-hand side dominates each nested component and therefore the complete right-hand side.

4. **It tolerates variable duplication.**  
   Rule 3 duplicates `memo`. Consequently, a naïve term-size argument would fail: the right-hand side can be much larger than the left. LPO does not require literal size to decrease; each occurrence of `memo` is still a proper subterm below the whole left-hand side.

5. **It automatically gives the necessary closure properties.**  
   A termination ordering must remain decreasing under arbitrary substitutions and when rewriting inside contexts. LPO is a standard well-founded reduction ordering with those properties.

Other methods could potentially work—for example, dependency pairs combined with a suitable reduction ordering—but they would be more machinery without removing the need to orient these terms. A direct numeric size measure would be awkward because of duplication and nesting. LPO was therefore the most direct method that relied only on the displayed syntax and rules.
