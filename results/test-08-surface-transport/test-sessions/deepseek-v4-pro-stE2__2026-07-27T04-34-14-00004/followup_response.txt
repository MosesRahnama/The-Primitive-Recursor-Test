I chose a **syntactic termination method** (a simplification ordering, specifically a recursive path ordering) because the problem’s constraints demand a proof that relies **only on the structure of the given rewrite rules**, without any external axioms, semantic interpretations of the function symbols, or imported object-level knowledge. Here’s why that fit perfectly:

1. **No external semantics required**  
   The symbols `zero`, `succ`, `nil`, `cons`, `append`, `best_eclass_term`, `nth` are completely uninterpreted. Any termination argument that appeals to their mathematical meaning (e.g., “`nth` returns a shorter graph” or “`append` preserves length”) would be an imported structural axiom. A syntactic ordering like RPO works **purely on the term structure**—it compares function symbols and subterm relations without needing to know what those symbols “do.” That keeps the proof entirely inside the system.

2. **The recursion pattern is straightforward**  
   The only recursive rule is  
   `extract_prefix(w, graph, succ(i)) → extract_prefix_k(…, extract_prefix(w, graph, i))`.  
   This exhibits a classic **decrease-by-one** pattern on the third argument: from `succ(i)` to `i`. A simplification ordering can easily capture this by comparing arguments under the subterm property. No lexicographic combinations, status functions, or polynomial weights are needed—so the simplest applicable syntactic method suffices.

3. **No problematic features**  
   The system is non‑duplicating, non‑conditional, and has no mutual recursion that could reintroduce larger terms. There are no unordered pairs that might require a more refined termination method. An RPO with a suitable precedence handles all three rules uniformly and immediately.

4. **Minimal assumptions, maximum transparency**  
   RPO orders terms by a fixed precedence of function symbols and the subterm relation. Both are **derivable solely from the rules** shown. No hidden guards, no side conditions, no strengthening. This directly satisfies the prompt’s requirement to “stay within that boundary.”

In short, the method was chosen because it is sound, complete for this kind of system, and demands nothing from the user beyond the syntax of the rules—exactly matching the “no imported object-level axioms” constraint.
