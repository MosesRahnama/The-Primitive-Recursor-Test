I chose the **dependency pair (DP) method** combined with a **polynomial interpretation over the natural numbers** because it is a fully self-contained termination technique that requires no structural axioms, no induction, and no extra rewrite rules—only the given rules themselves.

The reasoning:

1. **Direct decomposition of recursive calls:**  
   The system contains multiple defined symbols that call each other indirectly (e.g., \(f_4\) calls \(f_3\) and \(f_2\); \(f_2\) calls \(f_0\)). A direct simplification ordering (like RPO or KBO) might conflict with the intricate argument flows. Dependency pairs isolate every possible recursive call so that each of them can be examined separately, making the proof modular.

2. **Arithmetic nature of the functions:**  
   The system essentially computes with natural numbers represented as \(c_0\) and \(f_1\). The rules perform operations like addition, multiplication, or recursion depth reduction. Interpreting terms as natural numbers with simple arithmetic functions captures the semantics directly and yields obvious strict decreases for the recursive calls (e.g., \(x+y+1 > x+y\), \((x+3)! > (x+2)!\)).

3. **No need for any external reasoning:**  
   The DP framework with a weak monotonic interpretation into \(\mathbb{N}\) (which is well‑founded) is a complete termination proof in itself. It relies only on the rewrite rules and the interpretation, not on induction, assumptions about constructors, or other mathematical theories. This satisfies the strict “no imported object‑level axioms” requirement perfectly.
