### Why this method?

I chose the **dependency-pair (DP) framework + a single monotone algebra interpretation as reduction pair** for three tightly related reasons that are forced by the shape of the given rules themselves.

1. A direct (strict) simplification order or a single polynomial/matrix/algebra interpretation on the original signature is impossible.  
   The two rules for \(f_3\)
   \[
   f_3(f_1(f_1(x)))\to f_1(f_3(f_1(x))),\qquad
   f_3(f_1(c_0))\to c_0
   \]
   force any monotone interpretation to satisfy
   \[
   [f_3](n+2)\;\ge\;[f_3](n+1)+1
   \]
   for infinitely many \(n\), i.e. \([f_3]\) must be unbounded and grow at least linearly.  
   Feeding that lower bound into the rule for \(f_4\)
   \[
   f_4(f_1(x))\to f_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(x))),f_1(x))
   \]
   immediately produces a contradiction on \(\mathbb{N}\) (or on any well-founded domain that admits a strictly monotone embedding of the constructors).  
   Hence every method that tries to orient *all* original rules with a *strict* order fails; one is forced to move to a weak order on the rules and a strict order only on the recursive calls—the classic DP setting.

2. The recursive structure is simple enough that the DP problem collapses to a handful of inequalities that a very small algebra can solve.  
   After the usual DP transformation one obtains only seven pairs, three of which are “unmarked” recursive calls that decrease an obvious measure (the \(f_1\)-height). The remaining four pairs are oriented by a single interpretation that uses nothing more than addition, multiplication and factorial—operations whose monotonicity and well-foundedness are elementary and do not require any extra axioms beyond the rewrite rules. No usable rules, argument filtering, or modular decomposition is needed; a single reduction pair already orients everything.

3. The method stays strictly inside the “rules alone” boundary demanded by the query.  
   - No new rewrite rules are added.  
   - No side conditions, guards or sort constraints appear.  
   - The algebra is defined on the free term algebra generated by the given signature; its monotonicity follows from the syntactic shape of the interpretations, not from any semantic property of an intended model.  
   - The only well-founded order used is the standard order on \(\mathbb{N}\), which is justified solely by the fact that every decreasing sequence of natural numbers is finite—an observation that does not rely on any object-level axiom of the TRS.

Taken together, these three observations make DP+algebra the lightest method that both succeeds and respects the “nothing beyond the given rules” constraint. More heavyweight techniques (RPO with status, semantic labelling, arctic interpretations, etc.) also work, but they are unnecessary once the seven DPs have been oriented by the four-line interpretation shown earlier.
