### Verdict
Yes, termination of the given TRS can be established from the rules alone (via the dependency-pair method paired with an explicit monotone algebra interpretation over \(\mathbb{N}\) that serves as a reduction pair). No additional rewrite rules, guards, side-conditions, structural axioms, or object-level assumptions are required.

### Termination argument
The defined symbols (those appearing at the root of a left-hand side) are \(f_0,f_2,f_3,f_4\); the constructors are \(c_0,f_1\). The dependency pairs (DPs) of the system are therefore exactly the following seven pairs (variables renamed for readability):

- (DP1) \(f_0^\sharp(x,f_1(y))\to f_0^\sharp(x,y)\)
- (DP2) \(f_2^\sharp(f_1(x),y)\to f_0^\sharp(f_2(x,y),y)\)
- (DP3) \(f_2^\sharp(f_1(x),y)\to f_2^\sharp(x,y)\)
- (DP4) \(f_3^\sharp(f_1(f_1(x)))\to f_3^\sharp(f_1(x))\)
- (DP5) \(f_4^\sharp(f_1(x))\to f_2^\sharp(f_4(f_3(f_1(x))),f_1(x))\)
- (DP6) \(f_4^\sharp(f_1(x))\to f_4^\sharp(f_3(f_1(x)))\)
- (DP7) \(f_4^\sharp(f_1(x))\to f_3^\sharp(f_1(x))\)

(The unmarked rules themselves generate no further DPs.) By the standard dependency-pair theorem it is sufficient to exhibit a reduction pair \((\succcurlyeq,>)\) such that every original rule satisfies \(\ell\succcurlyeq r\) and every DP satisfies \(s>t\). Such a pair is obtained from the following interpretation into the well-founded domain \(\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}\) (all functions are weakly monotonic, therefore the induced relations are stable under contexts and substitutions):

- \([c_0]=0\)
- \([f_1](x)=x+1\)
- \([f_0](x,y)=x+y\)
- \([f_2](x,y)=x\cdot(y+1)\)
- \([f_3](x)=\max(x-1,0)\)
- \([f_4](x)=(x+1)!\)
- \([f_0^\sharp](x,y)=y\)
- \([f_2^\sharp](u,v)=u+v\)
- \([f_3^\sharp](z)=z\)
- \([f_4^\sharp](n)=n!+n+1\)

(The factorials are the ordinary ones with the convention \(0!=1\).)

#### Orientation of the original rules (\(\ell\succcurlyeq r\))
- \(f_0(x,c_0)\to x\): \(x+0=x\).
- \(f_0(x,f_1(y))\to f_1(f_0(x,y))\): \(x+(y+1)=1+(x+y)\).
- \(f_2(c_0,x)\to c_0\): \(0\cdot(x+1)=0\).
- \(f_2(x,c_0)\to c_0\): \(x\cdot 1=x\ge 0\).
- \(f_2(f_1(x),y)\to f_0(f_2(x,y),y)\): \((x+1)(y+1)=x(y+1)+(y+1)\ge x(y+1)+y\).
- \(f_3(f_1(f_1(x)))\to f_1(f_3(f_1(x)))\): \(\max(x+1,0)=x+1=1+\max(x,0)\).
- \(f_3(f_1(c_0))\to c_0\): \(\max(0,0)=0\).
- \(f_4(f_1(x))\to f_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(x))),f_1(x))\): \((x+2)!=(x+1)!\cdot(x+2)\).

All inequalities are non-strict, as required.

#### Orientation of the dependency pairs (\(s>t\))
- (DP1): \(y+1>y\).
- (DP2): \((x+1)+y>y\).
- (DP3): \((x+1)+y>x+y\).
- (DP4): \(x+2>x+1\).
- (DP5): \((x+1)!+(x+1)+1>(x+1)!+(x+1)\) (i.e., the concrete instance of \([f_2^\sharp]([f_4]([f_3](x+1)),x+1)\)).
- (DP6): \((x+1)!+(x+1)+1>x!+x+1\) (holds for \(x=0\) by direct evaluation \(3>2\), and for \(x\ge 1\) because \((x+1)\cdot x!+x+2>x!+x+1\)).
- (DP7): \((x+1)!+(x+1)+1>x+1\).

All inequalities are strict, as required.

Consequently every DP chain is finite, and the original TRS is terminating.

(Note that a single monotonic algebra making every original rule *strictly* decreasing cannot exist: the rules for \(f_3\) force \([f_3]\) to be unbounded and at least linearly increasing, which together with the lower bounds implied by the rules for \(f_0\) and \(f_2\) makes the inequality demanded by the rule for \(f_4\) contradictory on \(\mathbb{N}\). The dependency-pair refinement is therefore essential, but it stays entirely within the given signature and rules.)
