I chose a **well-founded measure on finite constructor syntax**, combined with a dependency analysis, because it fits the exact constraint of the question.

In particular:

1. **The equations are naturally rewrite rules.**  
   Reading them left-to-right lets termination mean “there is no infinite sequence of applications of these equations.”

2. **Most recursive calls decrease an obvious syntactic argument.**
   - `plus` decreases its second argument.
   - `times` decreases its first argument.
   - `p` decreases the unary constructor depth of its argument.

   These decreases can be checked directly from the displayed equations without assuming external facts about natural numbers.

3. **`fac` is not plainly structurally recursive.**  
   Its recursive argument is `p (S x)`, not syntactically just `x`. Therefore, simply declaring that `fac` is structurally recursive would hide the key obligation: proving that `p (S x)` is smaller than `S x`.

   The equations for `p` establish exactly that:
   \[
   p(Sx)\to^*x
   \]
   for constructor numerals \(x\). Equivalently, a syntactic size interpretation assigns `p (S x)` one less rank than `S x`. Thus the `fac` recursion is well-founded without adding the unstated equation `p (S x) = x`.

4. **The method handles the overlapping `times` equations.**  
   `times (S x) Z` matches both
   `times x Z = Z` and the recursive `times` rule. A termination argument must cover either rewrite choice. The measure does: one choice stops immediately, and the other strictly decreases the first argument.

5. **It separates termination from totality.**  
   A stuck term is terminating even if it is not a constructor numeral. Since no equation defines `fac Z`, evaluation can stop at a term containing `fac Z`. The measure proves absence of infinite computation; it does not silently assume that every call returns a numeral.

I therefore used this method because it is direct, checks every rule—including the non-obvious `fac`/`p` interaction and the `times` overlap—and relies only on finiteness and shape of the given syntax, rather than imported induction principles or extra defining equations.
