**Verdict:** Yes—interpreting the displayed equations in the usual way as left-to-right rewrite rules, every computation on finite unary-natural inputs terminates. This follows syntactically; no additional equations, guards, or object-level induction axioms are needed.

However, termination here does **not** imply totality: `p Z` and `fac Z` are irreducible but are not constructor values. Consequently, `fac` computations ultimately become stuck around `fac Z` rather than producing a unary numeral.

Let \(\bar n=S^n Z\). The argument uses only the finite constructor depth \(n\), a metatheoretic property of the given syntax.

### `plus`

For `plus x \bar n`:

- If \(n=0\),  
  `plus x Z → x`.
- If \(n>0\),  
  `plus x (S y) → S (plus x y)`, reducing the second argument’s constructor depth by one.

Thus there are exactly \(n+1\) `plus` rewrites, at most.

### `times`

For `times \bar m \bar n`:

- `times Z y → Z` terminates immediately.
- `times x Z → Z` terminates immediately.
- `times (S x) y → plus (times x y) y` reduces the first argument’s constructor depth from \(m+1\) to \(m\). The resulting `plus` call terminates by the preceding argument because its second argument is the fixed numeral \(\bar n\).

When the input is `times (S x) Z`, both the second and third rules apply. Either choice terminates: the second rule returns `Z` immediately, while the third decreases the first argument and creates only terminating `plus ... Z` work. At `times Z Z`, the first two rules both yield `Z`. Hence the overlaps introduce no infinite reduction.

### `p`

For unary numerals:

- `p Z` has no applicable equation and is therefore already a normal form.
- `p (S Z) → Z`.
- `p (S (S x)) → S (p (S x))` reduces the argument of the recursive `p` call by one `S`.

Consequently, for \(n\geq1\),

\[
p(S^n Z)\;\longrightarrow^*\;S^{n-1}Z,
\]

in finitely many steps.

### `fac`

- `fac Z` has no applicable equation and is therefore a normal form.
- For \(n\geq0\),

\[
fac(S\bar n)
\;\to\;
times(fac(p(S\bar n)),S\bar n).
\]

By the `p` equations alone,

\[
p(S\bar n)\longrightarrow^*\bar n.
\]

Therefore the only new recursive `fac` call becomes `fac \bar n`, whose numeral argument has one fewer `S`. Repeating this process eventually reaches `fac Z`.

No infinite rewriting can be hidden in the surrounding `times` terms. Their second arguments are nonzero numerals, while their first arguments ultimately contain the irreducible term `fac Z`; hence no `times` left-hand side matches there. In any event, all independently applicable `plus`, `times`, and `p` reductions have already been shown terminating.

Thus every left-to-right reduction starting from these functions on unary-natural inputs is finite.

The qualification is important:

- **Rewrite termination:** yes.
- **Successful evaluation to a unary-natural constructor value:** no, not for `p Z` or `fac`; the missing `fac Z` equation means positive factorial calls ultimately get stuck with `fac Z`.

If the displayed equations were instead allowed to rewrite in both directions as unrestricted equations, termination would not hold as a meaningful property—for example, `x ↔ plus x Z` immediately permits looping. The verdict above uses the standard orientation of function-defining equations from left to right.
