I chose a **direct well-founded descent argument on constructor depth**, organized according to the call dependencies, because it matches the equations most closely:

- `plus` decreases the number of `S` constructors in its second argument.
- `times` decreases its first argument, delegating finite work to `plus`.
- `p` removes one unary successor overall.
- `fac` delegates to `p`, so its recursive call has a predecessor argument.

This method is useful here because it relies only on the finite syntax of unary numerals. It does not require importing induction principles or arithmetic facts as object-level axioms. Any induction involved is ordinary metatheoretic reasoning about finite terms.

I did not use raw term size because several rules increase it:

```text
times (S x) y → plus (times x y) y
fac (S x) → times (fac (p (S x))) (S x)
```

Nor would a simplistic “recursive call is a syntactic subterm” check work for `fac`, since `p (S x)` is not literally a subterm of `S x`.

There is one subtlety in my earlier presentation: saying that `p (S n)` first normalizes to `n` implicitly describes an evaluation order. For a fully order-independent rewrite-termination argument, the measure should instead assign values directly to `S`/`p` terms, for example

\[
|Z|=0,\qquad |S(t)|=|t|+1,\qquad |p(t)|=\max(|t|-1,0).
\]

Both `p` equations preserve this value:

\[
|p(S(Sx))|=|x|+1=|S(p(Sx))|,
\]

and

\[
|p(SZ)|=0=|Z|.
\]

But every `fac` unfolding strictly lowers the value of the argument of the unique recursive `fac` call:

\[
|p(Sx)|=|x|<|Sx|.
\]

Thus even if rewriting occurs inside `p` before or after another applicable step, it cannot create an infinite chain of `fac` calls. The subsidiary `p`, `times`, and `plus` recursion is then controlled by their respective decreasing constructor arguments.

So I picked the direct descent method because it exposes the actual reason for termination while staying within the requested boundary. A dependency-pair or reduction-order proof could formalize the same reasoning, but would add machinery without changing the essential argument.
