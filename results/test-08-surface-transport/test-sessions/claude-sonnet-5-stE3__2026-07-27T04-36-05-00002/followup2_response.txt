## Requirement 2: "Without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given syntax and rewrite rules"

**Satisfied, with an important distinction to make explicit.**

The precedence `f5 > f6 > f2 > f1 > f4 > f3 > c1 > c0 > f0` and the lexicographic status are **not object-level axioms about the system's semantics** — they are *parameters of the proof method itself*. This is the standard and load-bearing distinction:

- An **object-level axiom** would be something like "assume `f3(x,y) = f3(y,x)`" or "assume `v2 < f0(v2)` numerically as an external fact about the domain." I introduced nothing like this.
- A **proof-method parameter** (precedence, status) is external to the rewrite system but internal to the *technique of RPO*, which is a general-purpose, syntax-only well-founded ordering machine. Choosing a precedence doesn't presuppose anything about what the function symbols "mean" — it only orders symbols for the purpose of comparing terms structurally.

Crucially, **every strict decrease I established reduces to two purely syntactic facts**, both directly verifiable from the rules as written:

1. **Subterm containment** — e.g., `v2` occurring inside `f0(v2)` on the LHS, or `v0,v1,v2,v3` occurring inside `f6(v0,v1,v2,v3)` on the LHS. This is pure syntax, nothing imported.

I should make explicit that I implicitly relied on this (it's a **necessary condition** for RPO to apply at all, and it holds for all three rules by direct inspection — no variable appears on a RHS that wasn't already on the corresponding LHS). This is not an assumption *imported* from outside; it is a fact read directly off the given rules.

So the entire termination argument bottoms out in: (a) the subterm relation, which is intrinsic to term syntax, and (b) an ordering choice on the finite symbol set `{f5,f6,f2,f1,f4,f3,c1,c0,f0}`, which is a free parameter of the proof method, not a hidden assumption about the rewrite system's behavior.
