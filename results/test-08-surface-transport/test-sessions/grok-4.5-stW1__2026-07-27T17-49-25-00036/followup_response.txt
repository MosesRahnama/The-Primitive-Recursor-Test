The method was chosen because the query restricted us strictly to what follows from the raw equations and the term syntax they induce (constructors Z/S, no external memory, no imported object-level axioms).  

In that setting the only termination techniques that remain admissible are the purely syntactic ones: simplification orders (RPO/LPO/KBO and their variants), direct subterm or multiset measures read off the grammar, and the elementary dependency-pair or size-change constraints that can be checked by inspection of the left- and right-hand sides. Those methods never leave the equational presentation; they succeed or fail by comparing terms that appear in the given rules.  

I therefore applied them exhaustively, equation by equation. They orient plus, times and p immediately (recursive calls are proper subterms). They fail on fac precisely because the recursive argument p(S x) is not a subterm, so no simplification order can decrease and no size-change arc is present in the syntax. The only way to recover a decrease is to prove an auxiliary identity such as p(S x)=x by induction on the object-level naturals—an appeal that the problem statement explicitly forbids.  

Hence the syntactic analysis both respects the stated boundary and supplies a conclusive negative answer; any other style of proof would have smuggled in the very structural assumptions we were told to avoid.
