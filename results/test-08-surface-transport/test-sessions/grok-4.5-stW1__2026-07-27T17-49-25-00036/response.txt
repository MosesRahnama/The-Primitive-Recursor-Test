### Verdict
**No**, termination of these functions on all inputs cannot be established from the presented equations alone.

### Termination argument
The equations define a first-order term rewriting system over the constructors \(\mathrm{Z}\) and \(\mathrm{S}\). Termination (strong normalization) on all inputs would require showing that every reduction sequence is finite. Standard syntactic methods that stay strictly within the given syntax and equations (e.g., simplification orders such as LPO/RPO/KBO, or direct subterm measures derived from the grammar) succeed on some of the equations but fail on the system as a whole; any complete argument for the remaining cases necessarily imports a structural/well-foundedness principle on the object-level naturals (or an equivalent inductive assumption), which lies outside what the equations and syntax alone make available. The equations are examined one-by-one below.

- **plus**.  
  \[
  \mathrm{plus}\,x\,\mathrm{Z}\to x,\qquad
  \mathrm{plus}\,x\,(\mathrm{S}\,y)\to\mathrm{S}\,(\mathrm{plus}\,x\,y).
  \]
  The sole recursive call is on the proper subterm \(y\) of the second argument. Any simplification order with precedence \(\mathrm{plus}>\mathrm{S}\) (lexicographic or multiset status) orients both rules. The decrease is therefore purely syntactic (subterm order on the term algebra generated by the given constructors) and requires no object-level assumption.

- **times**.  
  \[
  \mathrm{times}\,\mathrm{Z}\,y\to\mathrm{Z},\qquad
  \mathrm{times}\,x\,\mathrm{Z}\to\mathrm{Z},\qquad
  \mathrm{times}\,(\mathrm{S}\,x)\,y\to\mathrm{plus}\,(\mathrm{times}\,x\,y)\,y.
  \]
  The recursive call is on the proper subterm \(x\) of the first argument; the two base cases are non-recursive. The same style of simplification order (now with \(\mathrm{times}>\mathrm{plus}\)) orients every rule. Again the justification is internal to the syntax of the equations.

- **p**.  
  \[
  \mathrm{p}\,(\mathrm{S}\,(\mathrm{S}\,x))\to\mathrm{S}\,(\mathrm{p}\,(\mathrm{S}\,x)),\qquad
  \mathrm{p}\,(\mathrm{S}\,\mathrm{Z})\to\mathrm{Z}.
  \]
  The recursive call is on the proper subterm \(\mathrm{S}\,x\) of the argument. A simplification order with \(\mathrm{p}>\mathrm{S}\) orients both rules by a direct subterm decrease (the missing clause for \(\mathrm{p}\,\mathrm{Z}\) merely makes the symbol partial; it does not create infinite reductions). The argument remains syntactic.

- **fac**.  
  \[
  \mathrm{fac}\,(\mathrm{S}\,x)\to\mathrm{times}\,(\mathrm{fac}\,(\mathrm{p}\,(\mathrm{S}\,x)))\,(\mathrm{S}\,x).
  \]
  There is no base-case equation. The only recursive call is on the term \(\mathrm{p}\,(\mathrm{S}\,x)\), which is **not** a subterm of the argument \(\mathrm{S}\,x\). Consequently every simplification order fails: the subterm property forces \(\mathrm{p}\,(\mathrm{S}\,x)>\mathrm{S}\,x\), monotonicity then forces \(\mathrm{fac}\,(\mathrm{p}\,(\mathrm{S}\,x))>\mathrm{fac}\,(\mathrm{S}\,x)\), and the right-hand side is therefore strictly larger than the left-hand side, a contradiction.  

  No other measure derived solely from the grammar of the equations (symbol counts, height, etc.) decreases on this rule either, because the call is mediated by an auxiliary function symbol. Establishing that the call is nevertheless decreasing requires an auxiliary object-level fact such as
  \[
  \forall x.\;\mathrm{p}\,(\mathrm{S}\,x)=x
  \]
  (so that the argument of the recursive \(\mathrm{fac}\) is strictly smaller in the natural-number order). The displayed identity is not an equational consequence of the given equations for \(\mathrm{p}\); it is proved by induction on \(x\). That induction step is precisely a structural axiom on the object-level naturals and is therefore forbidden. Without it one cannot rule out infinite unfolding for arbitrary inputs, nor close a well-founded induction on those inputs that would show every ground reduction sequence is finite.

Because the system contains the \(\mathrm{fac}\) equation, the whole collection of functions falls outside the fragment whose termination can be read off from the syntax and equations alone. (The same obstruction appears in any attempt to apply size-change analysis or dependency-pair constraints directly to the equations: the necessary size-change arc from \(\mathrm{S}\,x\) to \(\mathrm{p}\,(\mathrm{S}\,x)\) is not present in the syntax and must again be recovered by an inductive argument about \(\mathrm{p}\).)

Thus termination on all inputs cannot be established within the stated boundary.
