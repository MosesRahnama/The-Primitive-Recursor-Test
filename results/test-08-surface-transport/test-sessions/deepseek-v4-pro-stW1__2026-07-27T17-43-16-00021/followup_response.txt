I chose **structural induction on the unary natural numbers** (the form of terms built from `Z` and `S`) for two reasons:

1. **Minimality – no imported axioms.**  
   The prompt explicitly forbids importing object‑level axioms beyond the given equations. The syntax of unary naturals itself already defines an inductive data type: every closed term is either `Z` or of the form `S t`, and this well‑founded structure is inherent in the term algebra. Structural induction follows directly from the constructor formation rules – it doesn’t require an external principle like “Peano induction” as a separate logical axiom. I used only:
   - the syntactic guarantee that each `S`‑nesting has a unique depth,
   - rewriting with the given equations,
   - and the fact that the recursive calls reduce that depth.

   No separate induction schema was imported; the reasoning stays completely within what is derivable from the syntax and the equations.

Structural induction on that measure (or directly on the term structure) is the most straightforward way to turn that observation into a rigorous termination proof, and it doesn’t need any strengthening of the definitions or hidden assumptions.
