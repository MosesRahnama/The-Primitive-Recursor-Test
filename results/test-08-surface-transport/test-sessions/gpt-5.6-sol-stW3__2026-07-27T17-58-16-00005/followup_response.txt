I chose a **dependency-pair proof combined with a semantic interpretation** because the system has two distinct features that are awkward for a single direct reduction order:

1. **The rules encode arithmetic recurrences.**  
   Their shapes strongly suggest:
   - \(f1\): successor,
   - \(f0\): addition,
   - \(f2\): multiplication,
   - \(f3\): predecessor,
   - \(f4\): factorial.

   With those interpretations, every rewrite rule becomes an exact numerical equality. This gives a natural weak orientation of all rules and ensures that rewriting between recursive calls cannot increase the chosen measure.

2. **The recursive decrease is clearer at the call level than at the whole-term level.**  
   For example,
   \[
   f4(f1(x))\to f2(f4(f3(f1(x))),f1(x))
   \]
   makes the right-hand side syntactically larger and embeds the recursive \(f4\)-call under \(f2\). A direct “term size decreases” argument therefore fails. But dependency pairs extract the relevant recursive call:
   \[
   F4(f1(x))\to F4(f3(f1(x))),
   \]
   where the argument decreases from \(x+1\) to \(x\) under the interpretation.

The same pattern occurs for the other recursive components:

- \(F0(x,f1(y))\to F0(x,y)\): decrease in the second argument.
- \(F2(f1(x),y)\to F2(x,y)\): decrease in the first argument.
- \(F3(f1(f1(x)))\to F3(f1(x))\): decrease in its argument.
- \(F4(f1(x))\to F4(f3(f1(x)))\): decrease in its argument.

Projection interpretations for the marked symbols expose exactly these decreases. Meanwhile, the other dependency pairs only move between different defined symbols and do not belong to cycles, so they do not need to decrease strictly.

Thus the method was chosen because it cleanly separates:

- **global compatibility with every rewrite rule**, supplied by the arithmetic interpretation, from
- **strict descent of recursive calls**, supplied by dependency pairs and projections.

A direct polynomial or size-based proof would be substantially less natural—especially because \(f4\) has factorial growth and its rule expands the term—whereas the dependency-pair method matches the recursive structure of the presented rules without adding anything to the object-level system.
