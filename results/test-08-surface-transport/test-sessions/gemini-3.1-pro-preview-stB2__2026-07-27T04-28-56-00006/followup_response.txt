I chose the **Lexicographic Path Order (LPO)** because it is the simplest, most direct method for proving termination of this specific type of purely structural, first-order rewrite system. 

Here is a breakdown of exactly why LPO was the ideal fit for your system and constraints:

**1. It is purely syntactic (No hidden assumptions)**
Your prompt explicitly forbade external memory, object-level axioms, or structural assumptions. LPO operates strictly on the literal shape of the terms. It does not require mapping symbols to mathematical domains (like integers), meaning there is zero risk of accidentally importing external semantics like integer arithmetic. 

**2. It natively captures "Structural Decrease"**
The core reason your system terminates is that the recursive call processes a strictly smaller piece of the input list: it goes from `cons(x, cons(y, xs))` down to `cons(y, xs)`. Because `cons(y, xs)` is a literal subterm of the original argument, LPO recognizes it as "smaller" automatically via its built-in **subterm property**. I didn't need to define a custom size function or measure to prove the list was getting shorter.

**3. It easily handles the introduction of new symbols**
In the recursive rule, the right-hand side introduces the symbols `add` and `app`, which were not present on the left-hand side. Some methods struggle with this because the overall "size" of the term might seem to grow. LPO handles this elegantly through **symbol precedence**. By simply declaring `exec_walk_weight > add` and `exec_walk_weight > app`, LPO guarantees that any combination of `add` and `app` applied to existing variables is strictly "smaller" than the original `exec_walk_weight` term. 

**Why not other methods?**
* **Polynomial Interpretations / Knuth-Bendix Order (KBO):** These require assigning numerical weights or mathematical functions to every symbol (e.g., defining `cons(x,y) = x + y + 1`). This adds unnecessary arithmetic complexity and edges closer to importing external mathematical assumptions, which you asked to avoid.
* **Dependency Pairs Framework:** While extremely powerful, it is usually overkill for a system with just one recursive function. It would add unnecessary boilerplate (calculating dependency graphs) only to eventually rely on something like LPO anyway to finish the proof. 

In short, LPO was chosen because it cleanly proves termination by doing exactly what you asked: looking at nothing but the rules, the variables, and the structural nesting of the syntax.
