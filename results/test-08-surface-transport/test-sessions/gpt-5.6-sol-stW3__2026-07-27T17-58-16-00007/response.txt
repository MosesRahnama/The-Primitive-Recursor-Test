**Verdict: yes.** Termination follows from the presented rewrite rules alone. A dependency-pair argument establishes it without adding rewrite rules, guards, or object-level assumptions.

Let \(R\) denote the given TRS. Its defined symbols are \(f_0,f_2,f_3,f_4\); \(f_1\) and \(c_0\) are constructors.

### Dependency pairs

Using marked defined symbols \(F_i\), the dependency pairs are:

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_0(x,f_1(y))&\to F_0(x,y),\\
F_2(f_1(x),y)&\to F_0(f_2(x,y),y),\\
F_2(f_1(x),y)&\to F_2(x,y),\\
F_3(f_1(f_1(x)))&\to F_3(f_1(x)),\\
F_4(f_1(x))&\to F_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(x))),f_1(x)),\\
F_4(f_1(x))&\to F_4(f_3(f_1(x))),\\
F_4(f_1(x))&\to F_3(f_1(x)).
\end{aligned}
\]

The possible cyclic components are therefore only the self-components headed by \(F_0,F_2,F_3,F_4\). The other pairs go one-way between different marked roots and cannot participate in a cycle.

### A measure for minimal dependency chains

By the standard dependency-pair theorem, if \(R\) were nonterminating, there would be an infinite minimal dependency chain. All proper instantiated subterms in such a chain are terminating.

For a terminating term \(t\), define

\[
\mu(t)=\max\{\,\text{number of leading }f_1\text{ symbols in }u
             \mid t\to_R^*u,\ u\text{ is an }R\text{-normal form}\,\}.
\]

This maximum exists because \(R\) is finitely branching and \(t\) is terminating. We have:

1. If \(t\to_R^*s\), then \(\mu(s)\leq\mu(t)\).
2. \(\mu(f_1(t))=1+\mu(t)\).
3. \(\mu(f_3(f_1(t)))=\mu(t)\).

For the third identity, normalize \(t\). If its normal form begins with \(f_1\), rule

\[
f_3(f_1(f_1(x)))\to f_1(f_3(f_1(x)))
\]

moves exactly one such \(f_1\) outside \(f_3\), preserving the total leading \(f_1\)-depth. If the normal form is \(c_0\), rule

\[
f_3(f_1(c_0))\to c_0
\]

gives depth zero. If it begins with neither \(f_1\) nor \(c_0\), the \(f_3\)-term is stuck at its root and also has depth zero.

Now consider each cyclic component.

#### \(F_0\)

Successive instances would require

\[
y_i\to_R^* f_1(y_{i+1}).
\]

Hence

\[
\mu(y_i)\geq \mu(f_1(y_{i+1}))
=1+\mu(y_{i+1}),
\]

so \(\mu(y_i)\) decreases strictly. No infinite \(F_0\)-chain exists.

#### \(F_2\)

The self-pair likewise requires

\[
x_i\to_R^* f_1(x_{i+1}),
\]

and therefore \(\mu(x_i)>\mu(x_{i+1})\). No infinite \(F_2\)-chain exists.

#### \(F_3\)

Connecting

\[
F_3(f_1(x_i))
\]

to the next left-hand side \(F_3(f_1(f_1(x_{i+1})))\) requires

\[
x_i\to_R^* f_1(x_{i+1}).
\]

Again \(\mu(x_i)>\mu(x_{i+1})\), excluding an infinite \(F_3\)-chain.

#### \(F_4\)

Successive instances of the \(F_4\) self-pair require

\[
f_3(f_1(x_i))\to_R^* f_1(x_{i+1}).
\]

Consequently,

\[
1+\mu(x_{i+1})
=\mu(f_1(x_{i+1}))
\leq \mu(f_3(f_1(x_i)))
=\mu(x_i).
\]

Thus \(\mu(x_i)>\mu(x_{i+1})\), so no infinite \(F_4\)-chain exists.

### Remaining rules

The rules

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_0(x,c_0)&\to x,\\
f_2(c_0,x)&\to c_0,\\
f_2(x,c_0)&\to c_0,\\
f_3(f_1(c_0))&\to c_0
\end{aligned}
\]

produce no dependency pairs because their right-hand sides contain no defined-root calls. They are nevertheless fully covered by the dependency-pair theorem as ordinary rewrite steps between dependency-pair links.

Since every possible cyclic dependency component is terminating, there is no infinite minimal dependency chain. Therefore the original rewrite system is terminating.

No structural axioms, additional rules, side conditions, or assumptions about the represented objects are needed.
