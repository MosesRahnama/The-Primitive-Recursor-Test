**Verdict:** Yes—if “termination” means that evaluation/reduction admits no infinite sequence on ground unary-natural inputs. This follows from the syntax and equations alone. However, `fac` is **not total as a unary-natural-valued function**: because there is no equation for `fac Z`, computations eventually become stuck rather than return a numeral.

Let \(\bar n=S^n Z\). Use meta-level induction on the finite constructor structure of such terms; this is not an added object-level axiom.

- **`plus`**
  - `plus x Z = x` terminates immediately.
  - `plus x (S y) = S (plus x y)` strictly removes one `S` from the second argument of the recursive call.
  
  Thus `plus` terminates on all numeral inputs.

- **`times`**
  - `times Z y = Z` terminates immediately.
  - `times x Z = Z` terminates immediately. At `times Z Z`, both equations yield `Z`, so the overlap causes no problem.
  - `times (S x) y = plus (times x y) y` makes the only recursive `times` call with the first argument shortened from `S x` to `x`. After that call, the already-terminating `plus` is used.
  
  Hence `times` terminates on numeral inputs by induction on its first argument.

- **`p`**
  - `p (S Z) = Z` is the base case.
  - `p (S (S x)) = S (p (S x))` removes one `S` from the argument of the recursive `p` call.
  
  Consequently, directly from these equations,
  \[
  p(S\bar n)\to^* \bar n.
  \]
  There is no equation for `p Z`, so `p Z` is stuck, but it is not divergent.

- **`fac`**
  - There is no equation for `fac Z`; therefore `fac Z` is already a normal form.
  - For a positive numeral:
    \[
    fac(S\bar n)
      \to times(fac(p(S\bar n)),S\bar n),
    \]
    and the `p` equations reduce \(p(S\bar n)\) to \(\bar n\). Thus the sole recursive `fac` occurrence descends from \(S\bar n\) to \(\bar n\). Repeating this descent must reach `fac Z`.

No equation can then reduce `fac Z`. In particular:
\[
fac(SZ)\to times(fac(p(SZ)),SZ)
       \to times(fac Z,SZ),
\]
which is stuck because neither `times Z y`, `times x Z`, nor `times (Sx)y` matches its arguments. Larger factorial inputs similarly descend finitely to a term containing this blockage.

Therefore the equations suffice to prove **termination/no infinite descent on unary-natural inputs**, but they do **not** suffice to prove that `fac` computes a unary natural on every input. Adding `fac Z = S Z`, for example, would repair totality, but that equation is not present and must not be assumed.
