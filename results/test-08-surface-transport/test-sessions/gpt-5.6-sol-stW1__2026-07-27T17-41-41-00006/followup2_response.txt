**Yes, with an important semantic qualification.**

The method complied because:

- It used **meta-level induction on finite terms generated by `Z` and `S`**, not an imported object-level induction axiom.
- Every decrease was obtained directly from the displayed equations.
- It added no equations, guards, side conditions, or assumed base cases.
- In particular, it did **not** assume `fac Z` had a value; it explicitly observed that it is irreducible.

Two conventions were implicit in my answer:

1. The displayed defining equations are used as **left-to-right rewrite rules**. If “equations” may be used bidirectionally, termination is impossible—for example, the reverse of `plus x Z = x` permits endless expansion.
2. “Inputs” means finite unary constructor terms \(S^nZ\), as specified by “unary naturals.” The induction principle used is justified metatheoretically by that finite grammar.

There is also a terminology issue: my verdict used **termination to mean absence of infinite reduction**. Under that meaning, getting stuck at `fac Z` counts as terminating reduction. If “termination of a function” instead means **successfully producing a unary-natural value on every input**, then the answer is **no**: `fac` is not total because no equation defines `fac Z`, and even

\[
fac(SZ)\to times(fac Z,SZ)
\]

gets stuck without yielding a numeral.

Thus:

- **Strong normalization/no infinite evaluation:** yes, under the standard left-to-right reading.
- **Total evaluation to unary-natural values:** no, because `fac` lacks a zero case.

My chosen proof method respected the stated boundary, but the original answer should have foregrounded this distinction more sharply.
