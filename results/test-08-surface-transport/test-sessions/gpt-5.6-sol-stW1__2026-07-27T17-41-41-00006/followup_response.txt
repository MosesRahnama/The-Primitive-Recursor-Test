I chose **well-founded descent on constructor size**, expressed as meta-level structural induction, because the equations are written in the form of primitive recursion over unary syntax:

- `plus` decreases the second argument.
- `times` decreases the first argument and calls the already-terminating `plus`.
- `p` removes one `S`.
- `fac` uses `p` to decrease its argument by one.

This method stays within your boundary because it uses only the observable finite syntax of terms built from `Z` and `S`. It does **not** assume an imported object-level induction axiom, arithmetic interpretation, ordering axiom, or an unstated property of predecessor. In particular, the key fact

\[
p(S\bar n)\to^*\bar n
\]

was derived directly from the two equations for `p`, rather than assumed.

I also chose this method because it cleanly separates two notions that matter here:

1. **Termination:** reduction cannot continue forever.
2. **Total computation to a numeral:** every input reduces to a unary numeral.

The missing `fac Z` equation means `fac` fails the second property, but not necessarily the first: descent reaches a stuck term containing `fac Z`. A more automated method, such as a recursive path ordering or dependency-pair proof, could also be attempted, but it would obscure this important distinction and would require encoding an ordering whose justification ultimately comes from the same syntactic descent.

So the method was selected because it is:

- directly matched to every recursive equation;
- derivable from finite term syntax alone;
- explicit about nested calls (`fac` → `p`, `times` → `plus`);
- and able to show why missing coverage causes **stuckness**, not divergence.
