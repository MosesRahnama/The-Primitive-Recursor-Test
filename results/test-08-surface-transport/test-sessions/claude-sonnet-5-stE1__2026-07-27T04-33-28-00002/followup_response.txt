## Why I picked well-founded/structural recursion on the third argument

**1. It's the minimal-commitment method available.**
The task explicitly forbids importing extra axioms, side conditions, or strengthening the definition. Well-founded recursion on `ℕ` under `<` isn't an "extra" assumption I'm importing from outside — it's *definitionally bound up* with what the type `ℕ` (via `0`/`Suc`) already is. Any method I use has to bottom out somewhere in "what natural numbers are," and the well-foundedness of `<` on `ℕ` is about the cheapest, most intrinsic fact available — cheaper than, say, invoking a general termination-checker heuristic or assuming something about `best_eclass_term`'s behavior beyond totality.

**2. The recursion pattern is already syntactically the standard shape.**
Look at the two equations again:
- `extract_prefix w G 0 = []` — base case, matches `0`.
- `extract_prefix w G (Suc i) = ... extract_prefix w G i ...` — recursive call, matches `Suc i`, and the recursive call's third argument is literally the predecessor `i`.

This is *exactly* the shape of primitive recursion on `ℕ`, so I didn't need to invent a clever measure or a lexicographic order — the equations themselves hand me the measure (the third argument) and the decrease (`Suc i → i`). Choosing any other measure would be over-engineering; choosing a weaker method (e.g., trying to argue termination structurally on `w` or `G`) wouldn't work, since neither `w` nor `G` changes or shrinks across the call.

**3. I had to isolate what `best_eclass_term` could and couldn't contribute.**
Since it's asserted only to be *total*, with no equations of its own given, I cannot use its internal structure for anything — I can only use the fact that whenever it's *called*, it returns a value with no further obligations. So it plays no role in decreasing any measure and isn't itself a recursive call to `extract_prefix`. That means the *entire* termination burden rests on the second equation's one recursive occurrence of `extract_prefix`, and nowhere else. This is why the argument had to explicitly separate "totality of `best_eclass_term`" (assumed, license to treat it as a black box) from "termination of `extract_prefix`" (to be proved) — conflating them would smuggle in more than what's given.

**4. Well-founded recursion is the standard, checkable proof method for exactly this pattern.**
Rather than reaching for a more powerful or exotic technique (size-change principle, lexicographic combinations, sized types, etc.), I used the simplest sufficient tool: a single natural-number measure with the standard order. Since primitive recursion over `ℕ` is a *strictly simpler special case* of well-founded recursion, invoking the general well-founded framework was safe, standard, and already tight enough to close the proof — using something more powerful would violate the spirit of "no more than derivable from the given syntax and equations," and using something weaker (e.g. plain structural recursion without articulating the order) would have left the well-foundedness step implicit rather than justified.

**In short:** the equations *themselves* already exhibit a decreasing recursion on `Suc i → i`; the only question was whether I was entitled to conclude termination from that pattern without extra assumptions, and the answer is yes, because well-foundedness of `<` on `ℕ` is intrinsic to the type being recursed over, not an imported axiom — so I used exactly that, and nothing more, addressing each equation as required.
