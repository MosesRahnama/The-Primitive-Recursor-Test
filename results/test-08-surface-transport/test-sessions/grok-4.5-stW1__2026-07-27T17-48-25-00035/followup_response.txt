### Why this method

The query imposed a hard boundary: decide termination **solely** from the equations as written, using only what the syntax of the patterns and the licensed rewrite steps immediately give you. No imported structural induction principles, no well-foundedness axioms for \(\mathbb{N}\), no constructor injectivity/distinctness lemmas, no extra guards, no semantic model of “predecessor,” and no strengthening of any equation.

The most direct method that stays inside that boundary is ordinary term-rewriting analysis:

- examine the left-hand sides for constructor coverage (or lack of it),
- observe whether every recursive call is on a syntactic immediate subterm,
- check whether every closed reduction sequence ends in a pure constructor term.

That is exactly what the preceding answer does, equation by equation. Any other standard technique (e.g., a formal inductive proof in a type theory, a size-change argument that appeals to an external well-founded order, a domain-theoretic interpretation, \ldots) would have required precisely the extra object-level assumptions the query forbade. Hence the purely syntactic rewrite examination was the only method that answered the question that was actually asked.
