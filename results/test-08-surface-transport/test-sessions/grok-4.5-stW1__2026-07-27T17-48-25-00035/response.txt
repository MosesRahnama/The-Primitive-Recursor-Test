### Verdict
**No.**

Termination (in the sense of reaching a constructor-only normal form on every closed input built from the constructors \(Z\) and \(S\)) cannot be established from the given equations alone.

### Argument
The rewrite relation induced by the equations is considered. An input is a closed term over \(\{Z,S\}\). A function terminates on that input when every rewrite sequence reaches a normal form consisting solely of constructors (a numeral). No additional equations, guards, or side-conditions are added, and the only properties used are those immediately visible from the syntax of the patterns and the rewrite steps licensed by the equations themselves. Structural induction/well-foundedness principles, injectivity/distinctness axioms for \(S\) and \(Z\), or any semantic interpretation of “predecessor” are not imported.

- **plus**  
  The two equations exhaust the possible forms of the second argument.  
  - \(plus\,x\,Z\to x\) (base).  
  - \(plus\,x\,(S\,y)\to S\,(plus\,x\,y)\) (recursive call on the immediate subterm \(y\)).  
  Every closed second argument is therefore peeled in finitely many steps; the first argument is never inspected. Termination on all inputs follows by a trivial descent on the number of \(S\) constructors appearing in the second argument (a quantity read off directly from the syntax).

- **times**  
  The three equations together cover every combination of constructors in the two arguments:  
  - \(times\,Z\,y\to Z\),  
  - \(times\,x\,Z\to Z\),  
  - \(times\,(S\,x)\,y\to plus\,(times\,x\,y)\,y\).  
  The only recursive call is on the immediate subterm \(x\) of a first argument headed by \(S\). The auxiliary call to \(plus\) has already been shown to terminate. Hence every closed pair of arguments yields a finite reduction to a numeral, again by descent on the number of \(S\) constructors in the first argument.

- **p**  
  The patterns that appear are \(S\,(S\,x)\) and \(S\,Z\).  
  - There is no equation for the input \(Z\). The term \(p\,Z\) is already a normal form that is not a numeral.  
  - Consequently \(p\) fails to produce a constructor result on every input, merely by inspection of the left-hand sides.  
  (The recursive equation that does exist decreases the constructor count, but this is irrelevant once a single uncovered input has been exhibited.)

- **fac**  
  Only one equation is present:  
  \(fac\,(S\,x)\to times\,(fac\,(p\,(S\,x)))\,(S\,x)\).  
  There is no equation for \(fac\,Z\).  
  Moreover, the equations of \(p\) (the only ones that can rewrite an argument of \(fac\)) yield, for every concrete numeral \(S^n Z\) with \(n\ge 1\),
  \[
  \begin{align*}
  p\,(S\,Z)&\to Z,\\
  p\,(S\,(S\,y))&\to S\,(p\,(S\,y)).
  \end{align*}
  \]
  Hence a finite number of \(p\)-steps (exactly the steps licensed by the two displayed equations) transform the recursive call into a call \(fac\,Z\). The latter term matches no left-hand side, so the whole reduction ends in a normal form still containing the symbol \(fac\). That normal form is not a numeral. The same stuck term appears for every closed input; therefore \(fac\) never terminates with a constructor result on any input.

Because already two of the four functions fail to terminate on all inputs, termination of the whole set cannot be established from the given equations.
