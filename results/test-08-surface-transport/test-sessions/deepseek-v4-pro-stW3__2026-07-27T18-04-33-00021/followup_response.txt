I chose **matrix interpretations** because the system includes a factorial function (`f4`), which is not simply terminating—its value grows faster than any fixed polynomial. Consequently, simpler methods like linear polynomial interpretations over \(\mathbb{N}\) (or the usual lexicographic path order LPO) cannot directly orient the recursive rule  
`f4(f1(v0)) → f2(f4(f3(f1(v0))), f1(v0))`  
without making the interpretation non‑monotonic or violating polynomial bounds.

Matrix interpretations replace plain numbers with vectors and use affine maps (matrix multiplication plus offset), creating a well‑founded order where a term's "size" can increase non‑polynomially with the argument while still ensuring the rewrite step strictly decreases the combined vector. By choosing matrices that amplify the weight of the recursive argument just enough, the right‑hand side becomes smaller than the left, even though both sides contain a copy of `f4` applied to a smaller term. This technique is standard for proving termination of systems that embed primitive recursive functions like addition, multiplication, and factorial, and it works without extending the system with extra rules or types.
