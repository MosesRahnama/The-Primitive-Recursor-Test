The method follows directly from the problem constraints: the only data are the raw equations (no external lemmas, no well-founded orders assumed a priori, no guards or extra defining equations). The most economical way to decide whether those equations by themselves already guarantee that every ground term reaches a constructor normal form is therefore to read them as a first-order term-rewrite system and ask whether that system is terminating on the free term algebra.

That reading immediately isolates the sole critical rule
\[
\mathsf{fac}(S\,x)\;\to\;\mathsf{times}(\mathsf{fac}(\mathsf{p}(S\,x)),S\,x).
\]
All other rules decrease a constructor argument in the obvious way; this one does not. Establishing a decrease therefore reduces to proving, from the two equations for \(\mathsf{p}\) alone, that \(\mathsf{p}(S\,n)\) is strictly smaller than \(S\,n\) for every numeral \(n\). The only proofs of that statement are inductive. Because induction is an extra-logical schema (not an equational consequence of the given rules), it lies outside the permitted boundary. Hence the equations do not establish termination.

The same diagnosis can be reached by model-theoretic means (the equations admit non-standard models containing infinite \(\mathsf{p}\)-descending chains) or by attempting a syntactic recursive-path order; both routes again require an external well-foundedness argument for \(\mathsf{p}\). The rewrite-system view is simply the most direct of these equivalent approaches.
