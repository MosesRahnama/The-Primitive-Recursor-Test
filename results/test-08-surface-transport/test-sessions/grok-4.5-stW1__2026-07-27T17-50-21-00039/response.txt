The given equations orient naturally into a first-order term rewrite system over the signature with constructors \(Z,S\) and defined symbols \(\mathsf{plus},\mathsf{times},\mathsf{p},\mathsf{fac}\). Termination on all inputs would mean that every ground term built from these symbols possesses only finite rewrite sequences (in fact, that every application of a defined symbol to closed constructor terms reaches a unique constructor normal form).

No such argument can be obtained from the equations and the bare syntax of terms. The first five equations (those defining \(\mathsf{plus}\) and \(\mathsf{times}\)) are the usual primitive-recursive schemes; each recursive call is on an immediate constructor predecessor. The two equations defining \(\mathsf{p}\) are likewise decreasing on their constructor argument. The final equation, however,
\[
\mathsf{fac}(S\,x)\;\to\;\mathsf{times}(\mathsf{fac}(\mathsf{p}(S\,x)),S\,x)
\]
performs a recursive call on the *defined* term \(\mathsf{p}(S\,x)\). Establishing that this call is smaller requires a separate proof that
\[
\mathsf{p}(S\,n)\;=\;n
\]
(or at least that the constructor measure of the right-hand side is strictly smaller) for every numeral \(n\). The only derivations of that fact from the two equations for \(\mathsf{p}\) proceed by induction on \(n\). Induction is a structural axiom schema; it is not a consequence of equational logic applied to the given rewrite rules, nor is it implied by the mere inductive syntax of finite ground terms once one moves to the object level.

Without that axiom one cannot rule out non-standard models of the equations in which infinite descending \(\mathsf{p}\)-chains exist. In any such model the final equation fails to be well-founded, so termination does not hold for every inhabitant of the domain. Consequently the equations by themselves do not establish termination of all five functions on all inputs.

(The same obstruction already appears for the single function \(\mathsf{fac}\); the remaining equations cannot compensate for it.)
