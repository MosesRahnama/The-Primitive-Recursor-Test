### Verdict
**No**.

Termination of all the given functions on all inputs *cannot* be established from the presented equations alone.

### Termination argument (addressing every equation)
The equations are treated as a pure constructor-based rewrite system over the free syntax of unary naturals (terms built from the constructors \(\mathsf{Z}\) and \(\mathsf{S}\), with the four function symbols as the only defined operations). A standard termination argument is admissible only when it relies solely on that syntax together with equational consequences of the given rules (congruence closure under substitution and contexts). No external well-founded orders on a semantic copy of \(\mathbb{N}\), no induction schema, no added base cases/guards/side-conditions, and no freeness/Peano axioms beyond the raw term algebra are permitted.

- \(\mathsf{plus}\,x\,\mathsf{Z}\to x\)  
  Non-recursive. The right-hand side is a syntactic subterm of the left-hand side. Immediate termination.

- \(\mathsf{plus}\,x\,(\mathsf{S}\,y)\to\mathsf{S}\,(\mathsf{plus}\,x\,y)\)  
  The single recursive call is on the immediate subterm \(y\) of the second argument. The call is therefore strictly smaller in the subterm order generated by the syntax of \(\mathsf{S}\). Termination follows by structural recursion on that argument.

- \(\mathsf{times}\,\mathsf{Z}\,y\to\mathsf{Z}\)  
  Non-recursive. Immediate termination.

- \(\mathsf{times}\,x\,\mathsf{Z}\to\mathsf{Z}\)  
  Non-recursive. Immediate termination.

- \(\mathsf{times}\,(\mathsf{S}\,x)\,y\to\mathsf{plus}\,(\mathsf{times}\,x\,y)\,y\)  
  The recursive call is on the immediate subterm \(x\) of the first argument (again a decrease in the subterm order of the syntax). The auxiliary function \(\mathsf{plus}\) has already been shown terminating. Termination follows by structural recursion on the first argument.

- \(\mathsf{p}\,(\mathsf{S}\,(\mathsf{S}\,x))\to\mathsf{S}\,(\mathsf{p}\,(\mathsf{S}\,x))\)  
  The recursive call is on the proper subterm \(\mathsf{S}\,x\) of the argument \(\mathsf{S}\,(\mathsf{S}\,x)\). Decrease in the subterm order of the syntax is immediate, so the rule is structurally recursive.

- \(\mathsf{p}\,(\mathsf{S}\,\mathsf{Z})\to\mathsf{Z}\)  
  Non-recursive. Immediate termination.  
  (Note that the two rules for \(\mathsf{p}\) together cover every ground argument headed by \(\mathsf{S}\), but that observation is not required for the purely syntactic termination check above.)

- \(\mathsf{fac}\,(\mathsf{S}\,x)\to\mathsf{times}\,(\mathsf{fac}\,(\mathsf{p}\,(\mathsf{S}\,x)))\,(\mathsf{S}\,x)\)  
  This is the fatal equation. The recursive call is on the term \(\mathsf{p}\,(\mathsf{S}\,x)\).  
  - \(\mathsf{p}\,(\mathsf{S}\,x)\) is *not* a syntactic subterm of \(\mathsf{S}\,x\).  
  - The equational theory generated by the seven preceding rules does not prove \(\mathsf{p}\,(\mathsf{S}\,x)=x\) (or any other equation that would make the argument identical to a proper subterm). The term \(\mathsf{p}\,(\mathsf{S}\,x)\) is already in normal form for a variable \(x\), and is distinct from \(x\).  
  - Consequently there is no decrease in the only well-founded order that can be read off the raw syntax (the subterm order generated by \(\mathsf{Z}\) and \(\mathsf{S}\)).  

Any attempt to recover a decrease must leave the permitted boundary:
- one must either impose an external measure (e.g., “the numeric value of the argument”) whose well-foundedness is the structural induction principle on the constructors, or  
- one must perform a case analysis/induction on \(x\) in order to exploit the two rules for \(\mathsf{p}\) and conclude that the call is smaller; that induction schema is precisely a structural axiom not derivable from the equations.  

Either route imports object-level assumptions beyond the given syntax and equations. Moreover the system supplies no equation at all for \(\mathsf{fac}\,\mathsf{Z}\). Hence already on the input \(\mathsf{Z}\) (and, by one unfolding, on every input \(\mathsf{S}\,n\)) evaluation reaches an irreducible term still headed by \(\mathsf{fac}\). The equations therefore do not establish that computation terminates yielding a result for every input.

Because the single non-structural, non-equationally-justified recursive call cannot be discharged inside the stated boundary, termination of the whole collection of functions on all inputs cannot be established from the presented equations alone.
