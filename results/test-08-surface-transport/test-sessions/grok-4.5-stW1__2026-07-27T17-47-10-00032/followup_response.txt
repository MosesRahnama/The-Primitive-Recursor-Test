The method was forced by the problem constraints themselves.

The query asked whether termination can be established *from the presented equations alone*, using only what is derivable from the given syntax (free terms over \(\mathsf{Z},\mathsf{S}\)) and those equations, and explicitly forbade importing structural axioms, object-level assumptions, extra equations, guards, side-conditions or hidden assumptions.  

The only well-founded order that can be read off that raw material is the strict subterm order generated by the constructors. Consequently the only admissible termination argument is to check, equation by equation, whether every recursive call is on a syntactic subterm (or on a term that the equations themselves prove equal to a syntactic subterm). That is exactly the check I performed.  

Any other standard technique—semantic interpretation into \(\mathbb{N}\), structural induction on a Peano axiomatisation, a measure function, etc.—would have required precisely the external well-foundedness or induction principles that the query ruled out. Hence those methods were unavailable.
