## Verdict

**Yes. The rewrite system is terminating**, and this can be established solely from the displayed syntax and rules. A standard dependency-pair proof suffices; no additional rewrite rules, guards, structural axioms, or assumptions about the represented objects are needed.

Write \(s=f1\) and \(0=c0\).

## Dependency pairs

Mark defined symbols by \(F_i\). The dependency pairs are:

1. From  
   \(f0(x,s(y)) \to s(f0(x,y))\):
   \[
   F0(x,s(y)) \to F0(x,y).
   \]

2. From  
   \(f2(s(x),y) \to f0(f2(x,y),y)\):
   \[
   F2(s(x),y) \to F2(x,y),
   \]
   \[
   F2(s(x),y) \to F0(f2(x,y),y).
   \]

3. From  
   \(f3(s(s(x))) \to s(f3(s(x)))\):
   \[
   F3(s(s(x))) \to F3(s(x)).
   \]

4. From  
   \(f4(s(x)) \to f2(f4(f3(s(x))),s(x))\):
   \[
   F4(s(x)) \to F4(f3(s(x))),
   \]
   \[
   F4(s(x)) \to F3(s(x)),
   \]
   \[
   F4(s(x)) \to F2(f4(f3(s(x))),s(x)).
   \]

The other rules have no defined-root subterm on their right-hand sides and therefore generate no dependency pairs:

- \(f0(x,0)\to x\),
- \(f2(0,x)\to0\),
- \(f2(x,0)\to0\),
- \(f3(s(0))\to0\).

## Dependency graph

The only cyclic components are the self-components for \(F0,F2,F3,F4\):

- \(F0\to F0\),
- \(F2\to F2\),
- \(F3\to F3\),
- \(F4\to F4\).

The other pairs only give acyclic transitions:

\[
F4\to F2,\qquad F4\to F3,\qquad F2\to F0.
\]

Thus it remains to prove that each self-component is finite.

### \(F0\)-component

Project \(F0\) to its second argument:

\[
s(y) \mathrel{\triangleright} y.
\]

This is a strict proper-subterm decrease, so the subterm criterion proves this component finite.

### \(F2\)-component

Project \(F2\) to its first argument:

\[
s(x) \mathrel{\triangleright} x.
\]

Again this is a strict proper-subterm decrease.

### \(F3\)-component

Project \(F3\) to its only argument:

\[
s(s(x)) \mathrel{\triangleright} s(x).
\]

This is also a strict proper-subterm decrease.

### \(F4\)-component

The remaining cyclic pair is

\[
F4(s(x)) \to F4(f3(s(x))).
\]

Its usable rules are precisely the two \(f3\)-rules:

\[
f3(s(s(x)))\to s(f3(s(x))),
\qquad
f3(s(0))\to0.
\]

Use the monotone interpretation over \(\mathbb N\):

\[
[0]=0,\qquad [s(x)]=[x]+1,
\]
\[
[f3(x)]=\max([x]-1,0),
\qquad
[F4(x)]=[x].
\]

Both usable \(f3\)-rules are weakly oriented—in fact, as equalities:

\[
[f3(s(s(x)))]
 = \max(x+2-1,0)
 = x+1,
\]

while

\[
[s(f3(s(x)))]
 = \max(x+1-1,0)+1
 = x+1,
\]

and

\[
[f3(s(0))]=\max(1-1,0)=0=[0].
\]

The cyclic \(F4\) dependency pair is strictly decreasing:

\[
[F4(s(x))]=x+1
>
x
=[F4(f3(s(x)))].
\]

Hence the \(F4\)-component is finite by the standard usable-rules/reduction-pair dependency-pair processor.

## Conclusion

Every cyclic dependency-pair component is finite, and all remaining dependency-graph edges are acyclic. Therefore no infinite dependency-pair chain exists. By the dependency-pair termination theorem, the original rewrite system is terminating.

This argument covers every rule and uses only a meta-level termination ordering on the terms already present; it does not strengthen or modify the rewrite system.
