I chose the **dependency-pair method** because the system combines several distinct recursive patterns that do not fit one obvious global syntactic measure.

In particular:

- `f0(v0,f1(v1))` recurses on a proper subterm of its second argument.
- `f2(f1(v0),v1)` recurses on a proper subterm of its first argument, but its right-hand side also introduces `f0` and duplicates `v1`.
- `f3(f1(f1(v0)))` recurses by removing one `f1`.
- The `f4` rule is expansive:
  \[
  f4(f1(x))\to f2(f4(f3(f1(x))),f1(x)).
  \]
  Ordinary term size increases, and the recursive `f4` call is not visibly on a syntactic subterm: its argument changes from \(f1(x)\) to \(f3(f1(x))\).

That last rule makes a simple proof by term size or the plain subterm ordering unsuitable. A single direct polynomial interpretation or recursive path ordering might be sought, but it would have to orient the entire expanding right-hand side at once, including the nested recursive call and duplication. Dependency pairs avoid that unnecessary burden: they isolate only the calls that could participate in an infinite computation.

The resulting dependency graph also matches the structure of the system especially well:

\[
F4 \longrightarrow F2 \longrightarrow F0,
\qquad
F4 \longrightarrow F3,
\]

with only the self-recursions \(F0,F2,F3,F4\) cyclic. Consequently:

- the `F0`, `F2`, and `F3` cycles are discharged immediately by proper-subterm descent;
- only the `F4` cycle needs semantic treatment;
- for that cycle, the actual `f3` rules justify viewing `f3` as decreasing an `f1`-height by one.

The interpretation
\[
[f1(x)]=x+1,\qquad [f3(x)]=\max(x-1,0)
\]
is not an imported assertion that terms “are natural numbers.” It is only a meta-level termination interpretation, and its compatibility with every relevant `f3` rewrite rule is checked explicitly. Thus the decrease of the `F4` call is derived from the presented rules rather than assumed.

So I picked dependency pairs because they:

1. handle the expanding `f4` rule cleanly;
2. separate the different recursive measures by strongly connected component;
3. avoid requiring one global size measure to decrease on every full rule;
4. stay within the given rewrite system—the interpretation and dependency graph are proof machinery, not added object-level axioms or rules.
