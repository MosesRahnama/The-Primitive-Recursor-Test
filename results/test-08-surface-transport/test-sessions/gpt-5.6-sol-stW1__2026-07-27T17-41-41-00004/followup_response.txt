I chose a **direct well-founded descent on the syntax of unary numerals** because it uses the least beyond what was presented.

Each unary input is a finite term \(S^n Z\), so its number of `S` constructors gives a metatheoretic measure:

- `plus` decreases the second argument’s size.
- `times` decreases the first argument’s size and delegates to already-terminating `plus`.
- `p` decreases its argument until `S Z`.
- `fac` is less syntactically direct because its recursive argument is `p (S x)`. First deriving
  \[
  p(S^n Z)\to^*S^{n-1}Z
  \]
  shows that each recursive `fac` call has a strictly smaller numeral argument.

I preferred this over, for example, trying to orient every equation with a simple term-size ordering. The `fac` equation makes the right-hand side larger and wraps the recursive argument in `p`, so ordinary syntactic-size descent is not immediately visible. The staged argument—first prove termination and behavior of `p`, then use that result for `fac`—matches the actual dependency structure.

It also exposes an important issue that a coarse “recursive argument gets smaller” claim could hide: reduction eventually reaches `fac Z`, but there is no equation for it. Thus the computation cannot continue indefinitely, yet it also does not produce a unary numeral. Similarly, `p Z` is stuck.

The induction involved is **metatheoretic induction over finite syntax**, not an imported object-level induction axiom about naturals. Since inputs are terms generated by `Z` and `S`, reasoning by their finite constructor depth is available from the syntax itself. This method therefore stays within the requested boundary while distinguishing:

1. **termination/no infinite reduction**, which the descent establishes, from
2. **total evaluation to a numeral**, which the missing base equations prevent.
