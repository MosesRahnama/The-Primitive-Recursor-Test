### Why this method

The equations are already in the exact syntactic shape of primitive recursion on a natural-number argument (base case at `0`, single recursive call on the immediate predecessor `i` inside the `Suc i` case).  

Well-founded induction on that argument under the predecessor order is therefore the most direct proof principle that applies: it matches the recursion pattern one-for-one, discharges both equations without remainder, and needs nothing beyond the inductive structure already present in the numerals.  

Other common techniques (e.g. an explicit measure function, a lexicographic order, or a separate accessibility predicate) would work but would be strictly heavier; they introduce extra machinery that the given equations do not require.
