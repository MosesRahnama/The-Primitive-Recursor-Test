I chose the **Lexicographic Path Ordering (LPO)** because it is the simplest, most natural, and most elegant fit for the specific structural characteristics of your rewrite system. 

Here is a breakdown of why LPO was the optimal choice compared to other standard termination methods:

**1. It perfectly handles structural recursion on specific argument positions**
Look at the recursive call in your second rule:
`extract_prefix(w, graph, succ(i)) -> ... extract_prefix(w, graph, i)`
This is classic structural induction. The first two arguments (`w`, `graph`) remain unchanged, while the third argument strictly decreases in structural size (`succ(i)` becomes `i`). LPO is explicitly designed to handle this: it compares arguments left-to-right (or right-to-left). Because the first two arguments are identical, LPO falls back to the third argument, effortlessly recognizing that `succ(i)` is structurally larger than `i`. 

**2. It ignores term size (unlike Knuth-Bendix or Polynomials)**
Look at your third rule:
`extract_prefix_k(w, graph, i, memo) -> append(memo, cons(best_eclass_term(w, memo, nth(graph, i)), nil))`
The left-hand side is a single function symbol with four arguments. The right-hand side explodes into a complex term introducing five new function symbols (`append`, `cons`, `best_eclass_term`, `nth`, `nil`). 
* If I used a weight-based method like the **Knuth-Bendix Ordering (KBO)**, I would have to carefully assign numerical weights to every single symbol to ensure the weight of the LHS is heavier than the combined weight of all those new symbols on the RHS. 
* If I used **Polynomial Interpretations**, I would have to invent algebraic functions for every symbol that satisfy strict inequalities. 
Because LPO is a *purely syntactic* path ordering, it completely ignores the "size" or "weight" of the RHS. By simply declaring that `extract_prefix_k` has a higher precedence than the new symbols, LPO instantly satisfies the inequality, bypassing the need for tedious mathematical bookkeeping.

**3. It requires no extra framework (unlike Dependency Pairs)**
The **Dependency Pairs** framework is highly powerful, but it is typically reserved for systems where simple path orderings fail (e.g., systems with complex mutually recursive loops, argument swapping, or non-structural recursion). Your system is essentially a definition of a primitive recursive function. Using Dependency Pairs here would be like using a sledgehammer to crack a nut. LPO provides a direct, self-contained proof in just a few lines.

**Summary:**
I chose LPO because it operates purely on syntax and precedence. It recognized the "decrease" in Rule 2 via lexicographic comparison, and it handled the "symbol explosion" in Rule 3 via simple precedence, allowing for a rigorous proof without needing to invent arbitrary numerical weights or polynomials.
