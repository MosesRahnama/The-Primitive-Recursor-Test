**Termination argument using Recursive Path Ordering (RPO)**

Choose the precedence on function symbols (all are assumed to have lexicographic argument status):

\[
f5 \succ f6 \succ f2 \succ f1 \succ f4 \succ f3 \succ f0 \succ c1 \succ c0
\]

Now verify that each rule is strictly oriented by RPO.

**Rule 1:** \( f5(v_0, v_1, c_0) \to c_1 \)
- The left‑hand side is rooted by \(f5\) and \(f5 \succ c_1\). A non‑variable term rooted by a symbol greater than a constant is RPO‑greater than that constant. Hence the rule is strictly decreasing.

**Rule 2:** \( f5(v_0, v_1, f_0(v_2)) \to f6(v_0, v_1, v_2, f5(v_0, v_1, v_2)) \)
- Left‑hand side root \(f5\), right‑hand side root \(f6\). Since \(f5 \succ f6\), it suffices to show that the left‑hand side is greater than every argument of the right‑hand side.
  - \(v_0\), \(v_1\), \(v_2\) are proper subterms of the left‑hand side (\(v_2\) occurs inside \(f_0(v_2)\)), so the left side is greater than each.
  - For the fourth argument \(f5(v_0, v_1, v_2)\): both sides have root \(f5\). Compare arguments lexicographically:
    - 1st argument: \(v_0 = v_0\)
    - 2nd argument: \(v_1 = v_1\)  
    - 3rd argument: \(f_0(v_2)\) versus \(v_2\). Since \(v_2\) is a proper subterm of \(f_0(v_2)\), we have \(f_0(v_2) \succ v_2\). Thus the left side is strictly greater than the recursive call.
- All conditions hold; the rule is strictly decreasing.

**Rule 3:** \( f6(v_0, v_1, v_2, v_3) \to f2(v_3,\; f1(f4(v_0, v_3, f3(v_1, v_2)), c_1)) \)
- Left‑hand side root \(f6\), right‑hand side root \(f2\). With \(f6 \succ f2\) we need the left side greater than the two arguments of \(f2\).
  - First argument \(v_3\): proper subterm of the left side, so left side \(\succ v_3\).
  - Second argument \(f1(\dots)\): since \(f6 \succ f1\), we need the left side greater than its arguments:
    - \(f4(v_0, v_3, f3(v_1, v_2))\): \(f6 \succ f4\), so require left side greater than \(f4\)'s arguments:
      - \(v_0\): subterm.
      - \(v_3\): subterm.
      - \(f3(v_1, v_2)\): \(f6 \succ f3\), need left side greater than \(v_1, v_2\); both are subterms.
    - \(c_1\): constant; \(f6 \succ c_1\) and left side is non‑variable, so left side \(\succ c_1\).
- Every requirement is met; the rule is strictly decreasing.

All three rules are strictly decreasing under the chosen RPO. RPO is a well‑founded rewrite order closed under substitutions and contexts, hence the system is terminating.
