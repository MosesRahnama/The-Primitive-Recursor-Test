### Why this method

The given rules are a pure first-order encoding of a tiny fragment of arithmetic (zero/successor via \(c_0/f_1\), addition via \(f_0\), multiplication via \(f_2\), a “half-predecessor” via \(f_3\), and factorial via \(f_4\)).  Consequently the most direct, self-contained termination argument is a pair of inductive interpretations
\[
V,U:\text{Terms}\to\mathbb{N}
\]
in which

* \(V\) is a homomorphism that realises the intended arithmetic semantics and is therefore invariant under every rule, and
* \(U\) is a strict ranking function whose recursive clauses are exactly large enough to absorb the growth produced by each rule (the only non-trivial clause being the recurrence that defines the auxiliary \(\phi\) for the factorial rule).

This choice has three decisive advantages inside the constraints you imposed:

1. It never enlarges the rewrite system, never adds guards or side-conditions, and never appeals to any equation that is not an immediate arithmetic consequence of the eight rules themselves.
2. It is completely finitary: both maps are defined by structural recursion on the term, using only the ring operations of \(\mathbb{N}\) and the total function \((-)!\).  No ordinals, no AC-matching, no external well-founded order beyond \(>\) on the naturals is required.
3. Every other standard “off-the-shelf” technique either fails outright or silently imports extra structure:
   - A linear/polynomial interpretation cannot dominate the factorial rule.
   - RPO/LPO (even with status or argument filtering) fails on the duplicating multiplication rule together with the nested \(f_4\)-redex.
   - Dependency-pair / size-change methods need an external call graph or a constraint solver, i.e., machinery that goes beyond “the presented rules alone.”

Hence the interpretation method is forced upon us: it is the weakest technique that still succeeds while staying strictly inside the given syntax and the eight rewrite rules.  The concrete definitions of \(V\) and \(U\) are simply the minimal ones that make the eight inequalities \(U(\ell)>U(r)\) hold by elementary arithmetic.
