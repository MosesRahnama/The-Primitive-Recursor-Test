### Verdict
Yes, termination can be established from the given rules alone.

### Termination Argument
We work with arbitrary terms over the signature (including those containing the variables \(v_0, v_1\)). Define two auxiliary maps \(V\) and \(U\) from terms to natural numbers by structural recursion, as follows.

- \(V(c_0) = 0\),
- \(V(v) = 0\) for any variable \(v\),
- \(V(f_1(t)) = V(t) + 1\),
- \(V(f_0(t, s)) = V(t) + V(s)\),
- \(V(f_2(t, s)) = V(t) \cdot V(s)\),
- \(V(f_3(t)) = V(t) - 1\) if \(V(t) > 0\), and \(0\) otherwise,
- \(V(f_4(t)) = V(t)!\) (where \(0! = 1\)).

- \(U(c_0) = 0\),
- \(U(v) = 0\) for any variable \(v\),
- \(U(f_1(t)) = U(t)\),
- \(U(f_0(t, s)) = U(t) + U(s) + V(s) + 1\),
- \(U(f_2(t, s)) = (V(t) + 1) \cdot (U(s) + V(s) + 2) + U(t)\),
- \(U(f_3(t)) = U(t) + V(t)\),
- \(U(f_4(t)) = \phi(V(t)) \cdot (U(t) + 1)\),

where the auxiliary function \(\phi : \mathbb{N} \to \mathbb{N}\) is itself defined by recursion:
- \(\phi(0) = 1\),
- \(\phi(n + 1) = (n! + 1) \cdot (n + 3) + \phi(n) \cdot (n + 2) + 1\).

(Note that both maps are well-defined on every finite term, as every recursive call is on an immediate subterm and the arithmetic operations involved—including factorial—are total on \(\mathbb{N}\).)

The map \(V\) is invariant under the rewrite relation: \(V(\ell) = V(r)\) for each of the eight rules (verified by direct expansion using the arithmetic definitions of \(+\), \(\cdot\), predecessor and factorial). Consequently every rewrite step (root or non-root) preserves the \(V\)-value of every subterm.

The map \(U\) is a strict ranking function, i.e., \(t \to s\) implies \(U(t) > U(s)\). This is established in two steps.

**Root steps.** For an arbitrary instance of each rule one has \(U(\ell) \ge U(r) + 1\) (hence \(U(\ell) > U(r)\)):

- Rule \(f_0(v_0, c_0) \to v_0\):  
  \(U(\ell) = U(v_0) + 0 + 0 + 1 = U(v_0) + 1 \ge U(v_0) + 1\).

- Rule \(f_0(v_0, f_1(v_1)) \to f_1(f_0(v_0, v_1))\):  
  \(U(\ell) = U(v_0) + U(v_1) + (V(v_1) + 1) + 1\),  
  \(U(r) + 1 = U(v_0) + U(v_1) + V(v_1) + 2\),  
  and the two sides are equal.

- Rule \(f_2(c_0, v_0) \to c_0\):  
  \(U(\ell) = (0 + 1) \cdot (U(v_0) + V(v_0) + 2) + 0 \ge 2 \ge 1 = U(r) + 1\).

- Rule \(f_2(v_0, c_0) \to c_0\):  
  \(U(\ell) = (V(v_0) + 1) \cdot 2 + U(v_0) \ge 2 \ge 1 = U(r) + 1\).

- Rule \(f_2(f_1(v_0), v_1) \to f_0(f_2(v_0, v_1), v_1)\):  
  Let \(u = U(v_0)\) and \(n = V(v_0)\). Then  
  \(U(\ell) = (n + 2) \cdot (U(v_1) + V(v_1) + 2) + u\),  
  \(U(r) + 1 = \bigl[(n + 1) \cdot (U(v_1) + V(v_1) + 2) + u\bigr] + U(v_1) + V(v_1) + 2\),  
  and the two sides are equal.

- Rule \(f_3(f_1(f_1(v_0))) \to f_1(f_3(f_1(v_0)))\):  
  \(U(\ell) = U(v_0) + (V(v_0) + 2)\),  
  \(U(r) + 1 = U(v_0) + (V(v_0) + 1) + 1\),  
  and the two sides are equal.

- Rule \(f_3(f_1(c_0)) \to c_0\):  
  \(U(\ell) = 0 + 1 = 1 \ge 1 = U(r) + 1\).

- Rule \(f_4(f_1(v_0)) \to f_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(v_0))), f_1(v_0))\):  
  Let \(n = V(v_0)\) and \(u = U(v_0)\). The definition of \(\phi\) is exactly the recurrence needed to guarantee  
  \(U(\ell) = \phi(n + 1) \cdot (u + 1)\)  
  is at least  
  \(1 + U(f_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(v_0))), f_1(v_0)))\),  
  because the latter expands (using the already-established clauses for \(U(f_2)\), \(U(f_3)\) and \(U(f_1)\), together with \(V(f_3(f_1(v_0))) = n\) and \(V(f_4(\ldots)) = n!\)) to a quantity whose coefficient of \(u\) is \(n! + 1 + \phi(n)\) and whose constant term is \((n! + 1)(n + 3) + \phi(n)(n + 2) + 1\). Both are bounded above by \(\phi(n + 1)\).

**Non-root steps.** Each of the operations defining \(U\) is strictly monotonic in the \(U\)-value of every immediate subterm (the corresponding \(V\)-value being held fixed). Indeed:

- \(U(f_1(\_\,))\) copies its argument;
- \(U(f_0)\) and \(U(f_3)\) contain the relevant \(U\)-argument with coefficient \(+1\);
- \(U(f_2)\) contains the first \(U\)-argument with coefficient \(+1\) and the second with coefficient \(V(t) + 1 \ge 1\);
- \(U(f_4)\) multiplies the factor \((U(\_\,) + 1)\) by \(\phi(V(\_\,)) \ge 1\).

Consequently, if a rewrite occurs in a proper subterm, the strict increase of that subterm’s \(U\)-value is inherited by the whole term, and \(U\) therefore drops by at least 1.

Every single rewrite step (regardless of position or which of the eight rules is used) therefore produces a strict decrease of the natural number \(U(t)\). An infinite rewrite sequence is impossible.

(Note that the argument never enlarges the original rewrite system, never adds side-conditions or guards, and never relies on any equation that is not an immediate consequence of the eight given rules together with the inductive definitions of \(V\) and \(U\).)
