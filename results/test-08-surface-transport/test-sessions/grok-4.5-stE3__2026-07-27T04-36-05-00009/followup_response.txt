### Why RPO
I chose recursive path order (RPO) because the system is a tiny first-order TRS whose rules already exhibit a clear syntactic decrease once a suitable precedence is fixed: the only recursion is the self-call of \(f_5\) on a proper subterm of its third argument, while every other right-hand side is built from strictly smaller function symbols. RPO turns exactly that observation into a well-founded order without any extra machinery.

- It is a pure simplification order, so termination follows at once from the three orientation checks; nothing beyond the given signature and rules is required.
- The precedence is read off directly from the call graph (\(f_5\) calls \(f_6\), \(f_6\) builds an \(f_2\)-term, etc.), making the proof essentially mechanical.
- More heavyweight techniques (polynomial interpretations, dependency pairs, semantic labelling, \ldots) also succeed, but they introduce auxiliary functions or constraints that are unnecessary for a three-rule system whose decrease is already visible at the root and argument positions.

Thus RPO is the lightest standard method that stays strictly inside the boundary you set.
