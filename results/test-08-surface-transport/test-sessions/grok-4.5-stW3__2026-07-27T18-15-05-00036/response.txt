### Verdict
Yes, termination of the given TRS can be established from the presented rules alone (via the standard dependency-pair method equipped with a reduction pair induced by a well-founded algebra interpretation into the natural numbers). No additional rewrite rules, guards, side-conditions, structural axioms, or object-level assumptions are required or used.

### Termination argument
The defined symbols (those appearing at the root of a left-hand side) are \(f_0,f_2,f_3,f_4\). The dependency pairs of the TRS are therefore exactly the following seven pairs (where a superscript \(\#\) marks a dependency-pair symbol):

- \(\mathrm{DP}_1\): \(f_0^\sharp(v_0,f_1(v_1))\to f_0^\sharp(v_0,v_1)\)
- \(\mathrm{DP}_2\): \(f_2^\sharp(f_1(v_0),v_1)\to f_0^\sharp(f_2(v_0,v_1),v_1)\)
- \(\mathrm{DP}_3\): \(f_2^\sharp(f_1(v_0),v_1)\to f_2^\sharp(v_0,v_1)\)
- \(\mathrm{DP}_4\): \(f_3^\sharp(f_1(f_1(v_0)))\to f_3^\sharp(f_1(v_0))\)
- \(\mathrm{DP}_5\): \(f_4^\sharp(f_1(v_0))\to f_2^\sharp(f_4(f_3(f_1(v_0))),f_1(v_0))\)
- \(\mathrm{DP}_6\): \(f_4^\sharp(f_1(v_0))\to f_4^\sharp(f_3(f_1(v_0)))\)
- \(\mathrm{DP}_7\): \(f_4^\sharp(f_1(v_0))\to f_3^\sharp(f_1(v_0))\)

(The four original rules whose right-hand sides contain no defined symbols contribute no dependency pairs.) By the soundness theorem of the dependency-pair method it is sufficient to exhibit a reduction pair \((\succsim,>)\) such that every original rule is weakly decreasing (\(l\succsim r\)) and every dependency pair is strictly decreasing (\(s^\sharp>t^\sharp\)). The pair induced by the following interpretation into the well-founded domain \((\mathbb{N},>)\) works.

Interpret the original symbols by the arithmetic functions
\[
\begin{align*}
[[c_0]]&=0,\\
[[f_1(x)]]&=[[x]]+1,\\
[[f_0(x,y)]]&=[[x]]+[[y]],\\
[[f_2(x,y)]]&=[[x]]\cdot[[y]],\\
[[f_3(x)]]&=\max([[x]]-1,0),\\
[[f_4(x)]]&=[[x]]! 
\end{align*}
\]
(with the convention \(0!=1\)). Each of these functions is weakly monotonic on \(\mathbb{N}\). Direct substitution of the interpretation into every original rule yields equality of both sides (hence the required weak decrease):

- \(f_0(v_0,c_0)\): \([[v_0]]+0=[[v_0]]\),
- \(f_0(v_0,f_1(v_1))\): \([[v_0]]+([[v_1]]+1)=([[v_0]]+[[v_1]])+1\),
- \(f_2(c_0,v_0)\): \(0\cdot[[v_0]]=0\),
- \(f_2(v_0,c_0)\): \([[v_0]]\cdot 0=0\),
- \(f_2(f_1(v_0),v_1)\): \(([[v_0]]+1)\cdot[[v_1]]=[[v_0]]\cdot[[v_1]]+[[v_1]]\),
- \(f_3(f_1(f_1(v_0)))\): \(\max(([[v_0]]+2)-1,0)=[[v_0]]+1=\max(([[v_0]]+1)-1,0)+1\),
- \(f_3(f_1(c_0))\): \(\max(1-1,0)=0\),
- \(f_4(f_1(v_0))\): \(([[v_0]]+1)!=([[v_0]]!)\cdot([[v_0]]+1)\).

(The same equalities hold after an arbitrary substitution of natural numbers for the variables, and the interpretation is stable under contexts by weak monotonicity.)

Interpret the seven dependency-pair symbols by
\[
\begin{align*}
[[f_0^\sharp(x,y)]]&=[[y]],\\
[[f_2^\sharp(x,y)]]&=[[x]]+[[y]],\\
[[f_3^\sharp(x)]]&=[[x]],\\
[[f_4^\sharp(x)]]&=([[x]]+2)!.
\end{align*}
\]
Substituting these interpretations into the dependency pairs produces a strict decrease on \(\mathbb{N}\) for every pair and every assignment of natural numbers to the variables \(a=[[v_0]]\), \(b=[[v_1]]\):

- \(\mathrm{DP}_1\): \(b+1>b\),
- \(\mathrm{DP}_2\): \((a+1)+b>b\),
- \(\mathrm{DP}_3\): \((a+1)+b>a+b\),
- \(\mathrm{DP}_4\): \(a+2>a+1\),
- \(\mathrm{DP}_5\): \((a+3)!>a!+(a+1)\),
- \(\mathrm{DP}_6\): \((a+3)!>(a+2)!\),
- \(\mathrm{DP}_7\): \((a+3)!>a+1\).

(The factorial inequalities are immediate from the recursive definition of \((-)!\).) Consequently every dependency pair is strictly decreasing with respect to the induced order \(>\).

The pair \((\succsim,>)\) extracted from the algebra is a reduction pair, all original rules satisfy \(l\succsim r\), and all dependency pairs satisfy \(s^\sharp>t^\sharp\). The dependency-pair criterion therefore guarantees that the TRS is terminating.

(Note that the argument never leaves the given signature and rewrite rules: the algebra is defined directly from those rules, the dependency pairs are computed mechanically from them, and the required numerical inequalities are verified by elementary arithmetic on the same rules.)
