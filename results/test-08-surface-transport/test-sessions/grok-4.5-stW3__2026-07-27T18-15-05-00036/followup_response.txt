### Why this method?
The TRS is a pure first-order term rewriting system (no binders, conditions, or AC symbols), so the classical dependency-pair (DP) framework applies directly and is complete for termination in the sense that a finite TRS terminates iff it has no infinite DP chains (relative to the rewrite relation). I therefore started from the DPs, which are obtained mechanically by inspecting the seven rules that contain a defined symbol in a right-hand side; this immediately isolates the recursive “calls” that must be shown to decrease.

A plain reduction order on the original signature is awkward: several rules (the recursive equations for \(f_0\), \(f_2\) and \(f_4\)) are size-preserving under any reasonable interpretation that mirrors their arithmetic meaning, so a single strict order cannot orient them all. The DP method solves the problem cleanly by demanding only *weak* decrease on the original rules and *strict* decrease on the DPs.  

An algebra interpretation into \((\mathbb{N},>)\) supplies a reduction pair with exactly those properties:
- the interpretations of the original symbols are the obvious arithmetic operations suggested by the rules themselves (addition, multiplication, predecessor, factorial);
- the interpretations of the DP symbols can be chosen independently and with faster growth, guaranteeing the seven strict inequalities needed for the DPs;
- weak monotonicity is free, so the pair is stable under contexts and substitutions.

No other standard technique is simpler for this particular system.  Recursive path orders fail because of the nested factorial-like growth hidden in the last rule; a direct polynomial interpretation on the whole signature cannot make every rule strictly decrease; Knuth-Bendix orders run into the same orientation obstacles as path orders.  The DP+algebra proof therefore stays inside the given syntax, uses only elementary arithmetic, and discharges every rule exactly once.
