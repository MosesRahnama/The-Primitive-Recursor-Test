I chose **structural induction over the free `Z`/`S` term algebra** (i.e., the standard subterm well‑founded order) for a few specific reasons tied to the constraints of the task:

1. **It's the ordering the syntax itself provides.** The naturals here are built only from `Z` and `S`, and in three of the four functions (`plus`, `times`, `p`) the recursive call's argument is *literally* a syntactic subterm of the corresponding argument in the head (`y` vs `S y`, `x` vs `S x`, `S x` vs `S (S x)`). That means termination follows immediately from the constructor discipline of the datatype, with no need to invent an external measure, ordinal assignment, or complexity metric. Using the subterm order is the minimal, most directly justified choice — it doesn't add any information not already implicit in "this is data freely generated by `Z`/`S`."

2. **It avoids importing axioms.** The task explicitly forbids appealing to structural axioms or hidden assumptions beyond what's derivable from the given equations. Structural induction on an inductively-defined datatype is not an "imported" axiom in the problematic sense — it's the basic reasoning principle that *defines* what it means for `Z`/`S` terms to be a well-founded inductive type at all. Any other method (e.g. well-founded recursion via an externally supplied ranking function, or ordinal induction) would require *stipulating* a measure not already present in the problem, which the prompt disallows.

3. **It composes modularly without circularity.** Since `times` calls `plus` and `fac` calls both `p` and `times`, I needed an argument that lets me discharge each function's termination using only *previously established* facts about "smaller" functions, never assuming what I was trying to prove. Structural induction naturally supports this layered/modular treatment: prove `plus` terminates using only its own equations, then use that fact (as a black box) plus structural induction on `times`'s first argument, etc. This matches the acyclic dependency structure of the equations exactly.

4. **`fac` needed a bridge, not a new method.** The call `fac (p (S x))` isn't a *syntactic* subterm of `S x` — `p (S x)` is a redex, not literally `x`. So I couldn't just wave at "obviously smaller." Rather than reaching for a different termination technique (like a general measure/norm function), I instead proved, by ordinary structural induction using only `p`'s two equations, the equational lemma `p (S x) = x`. This stays entirely inside the equations given — it's a derived fact, not an added axiom — and it lets the *same* structural-subterm argument apply to `fac` after all, reducing the seemingly non-structural call to a genuinely structural one.

In short: structural induction on the subterm order was the only method that (a) is licensed purely by the shape of the equations and the freely-generated `Z`/`S` datatype, (b) requires no additional postulated ranking or axiom, and (c) can be applied compositionally to handle the dependencies between `plus`, `times`, `p`, and `fac` without circularity.
