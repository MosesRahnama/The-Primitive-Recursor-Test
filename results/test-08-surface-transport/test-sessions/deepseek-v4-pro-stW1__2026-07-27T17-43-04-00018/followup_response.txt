I chose the method of **structural size reduction** (which relies on the inductive definition of unary naturals) because it directly leverages the syntactic form of the given equations and the nature of the data constructors `Z` and `S`.  

The defining equations are ordinary recursive definitions over a freely generated inductive datatype.  For such definitions, termination can be proved by observing that every recursive call is made on an argument that is a **proper subterm** (or reduces to a proper subterm) of the original input.  This method is:

- **Self‑contained**: It does not import any external axioms about arithmetic, ordering, or well‑foundedness beyond what is already implicit in the structure of the terms themselves.  The well‑founded order is simply the subterm relation, which is derivable from the syntax: `x` is a subterm of `S x`, and by the equations we can also compute that `p (S x)` reduces to a subterm of `S x`.
- **Exhaustive**: Every equation is checked explicitly, and each recursive call is shown to reduce the size (number of `S` constructors), ensuring no infinite descent.
- **Direct from the equations**: The equations themselves are used to evaluate the arguments of recursive calls (as in the `fac` case, where we prove `p (S x) = x` using the given equations and induction).  No strengthening or extra side conditions are needed.

In other words, the method is simply the canonical termination proof technique for primitive recursive definitions over an inductive datatype.  Because the problem asks whether termination can be established “from the presented equations alone”, structural induction over the freely generated `Z`/`S` terms is exactly what stays within that boundary—it uses only the constructors and the recursion pattern visible in the equations, without appealing to any external number theory.
