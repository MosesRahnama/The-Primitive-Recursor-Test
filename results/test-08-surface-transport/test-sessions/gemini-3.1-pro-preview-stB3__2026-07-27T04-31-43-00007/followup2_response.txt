**1. "From the presented rules alone" / "Without importing object-level assumptions"**
LPO is a purely **syntactic** proof method. It does not attempt to assign "meaning" to the symbols.

**2. "Without importing structural axioms"**
In rewriting, importing a structural axiom means adding rules like associativity `f(x, f(y, z)) = f(f(x, y), z)` or commutativity `f(x,y) = f(y,x)` to make a proof work. I did not assume any such axioms. The trees were analyzed exactly in the rigid structure you provided.

**3. "Provided it does not require strengthening the system with extra rewrite rules, guards, side conditions"**
*   **No extra rules:** I did not add any dummy rules to help the system reduce.

No. In the theory of term rewriting, defining a precedence over function symbols is not an assumption about the *system*, nor is it an alteration of the rules. It is a standard parameter of the *proof method*.
