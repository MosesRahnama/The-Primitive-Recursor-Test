### Why algebraic interpretations?

The TRS is a plain first-order system (no binders, no AC symbols, no equational theory, no conditional rules). For such systems the classical monotone algebraic interpretation method into a well-founded carrier (here \(\mathbb{N}\)) is complete for the existence of a reduction order that orients every rule, and it is entirely self-contained: the only data required are the rewrite rules themselves and the usual inductive definition of the term algebra.

I therefore looked for a carrier and for monotonic functions realizing the signature such that each of the eight rules becomes a strict numerical inequality. The shape of the rules immediately suggests the necessary growth rates:

- \(f_1\) must behave like a sufficiently rapid “successor” so that the two \(f_3\)-rules can decrease,
- \(f_0\) and \(f_2\) must absorb the duplicating and shifting patterns that appear on their right-hand sides (hence exponential / double-exponential expressions),
- \(f_4\) must dominate an application of the already fast-growing \(f_2\) (hence a primitive-recursive iteration of that function).

Once those functions are written down, monotonicity is obvious by induction and each rule reduces to a trivial arithmetic comparison. The same comparisons also show that simpler domains (e.g., linear or polynomial interpretations) are insufficient, while path orders, although possibly applicable with a carefully chosen precedence and status, require a more tedious case analysis on subterms and do not make the underlying numeric decrease as transparent. Algebraic interpretations therefore give the shortest, fully rigorous, and fully explicit termination argument that stays strictly inside the given syntax and rules.
