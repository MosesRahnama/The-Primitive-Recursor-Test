### Verdict
Yes, termination of the given TRS can be established from the rules alone (via the standard method of monotone algebraic interpretations into the well-founded domain \(\mathbb{N}\)).

### Termination Argument
Consider the following interpretation of the function symbols as functions on \(\mathbb{N} = \{0, 1, 2, \dots\}\) (with the standard order):
- \([[c_0]] = 0\),
- \([[f_1]](x) = 2x + 2\),
- \([[f_3]](x) = 0\) if \(x = 0\), and \(x - 1\) otherwise,
- \([[f_0]](x, y) = (x + 1) \cdot 2^{y + 1}\),
- \([[f_2]](x, y) = 2^{(x + 1) \cdot 2^y}\),
- \([[f_4]](0) = 0\), and \([[f_4]](n + 1) = 2^{([[f_4]](n) + 1) \cdot 2^{n + 1}} + 1\) for \(n \geq 0\).

Each of these functions is monotonic (non-decreasing) in every argument. (This is immediate by inspection for \([[f_1]]\), \([[f_3]]\), \([[f_0]]\), and \([[f_2]]\); for \([[f_4]]\), it holds by a trivial inductive argument on the recursive definition, as the right-hand side is always strictly larger than \([[f_4]](n)\).) Consequently, the interpretation of any term is stable under substitutions and compatible with contexts: if a term \(s\) rewrites to \(t\), then the interpretation of any context filled by \(s\) is \(\geq\) the interpretation of the same context filled by \(t\). It remains only to check that each rule is *strictly* decreasing under the interpretation (for all values of the variables in \(\mathbb{N}\)). We write \(x, y\) for the values of \(v_0, v_1\).

- Rule \(f_0(v_0, c_0) \to v_0\):  
  \([[f_0]](x, 0) = (x + 1) \cdot 2^{1} = 2x + 2 > x\).

- Rule \(f_0(v_0, f_1(v_1)) \to f_1(f_0(v_0, v_1))\):  
  Left: \((x + 1) \cdot 2^{(2y + 2) + 1} = (x + 1) \cdot 2^{2y + 3}\).  
  Right: \(2 \cdot ((x + 1) \cdot 2^{y + 1}) + 2 = (x + 1) \cdot 2^{y + 2} + 2\).  
  The inequality \((x + 1) \cdot 2^{2y + 3} > (x + 1) \cdot 2^{y + 2} + 2\) holds for all \(x, y \geq 0\) (for \(y = 0\) it simplifies to \(8(x + 1) > 4(x + 1) + 2\), i.e., \(4x + 2 > 0\); for \(y \geq 1\) the left-hand side grows strictly faster).

- Rule \(f_2(c_0, v_0) \to c_0\):  
  \([[f_2]](0, x) = 2^{1 \cdot 2^x} = 2^{2^x} \geq 2 > 0\).

- Rule \(f_2(v_0, c_0) \to c_0\):  
  \([[f_2]](x, 0) = 2^{(x + 1) \cdot 1} = 2^{x + 1} \geq 2 > 0\).

- Rule \(f_2(f_1(v_0), v_1) \to f_0(f_2(v_0, v_1), v_1)\):  
  Left: \(2^{(2x + 3) \cdot 2^y}\).  
  Right: \(\bigl(2^{(x + 1) \cdot 2^y} + 1\bigr) \cdot 2^{y + 1}\).  
  The right-hand side is strictly bounded above by \(2^{(x + 1) \cdot 2^y + y + 2}\) (using \(u + 1 < 2u\) for \(u = [[f_2]](x, y) \geq 2\)). The exponent on the left exceeds this upper-bound exponent by \((x + 2) \cdot 2^y - y - 2 \geq 0\), with equality to zero *only* at \(x = y = 0\). In the equality case the original (non-loosened) right-hand side evaluates to 6 while the left-hand side evaluates to 8; in all other cases the left-hand side is at least twice the upper bound on the right-hand side. Thus the inequality holds for all \(x, y \geq 0\).

- Rule \(f_3(f_1(f_1(v_0))) \to f_1(f_3(f_1(v_0)))\):  
  Left: \([[f_3]](2 \cdot (2x + 2) + 2) = [[f_3]](4x + 6) = 4x + 5\).  
  Right: \([[f_1]]([[f_3]](2x + 2)) = [[f_1]](2x + 1) = 4x + 4\).  
  Clearly \(4x + 5 > 4x + 4\).

- Rule \(f_3(f_1(c_0)) \to c_0\):  
  \([[f_3]](2) = 1 > 0\).

- Rule \(f_4(f_1(v_0)) \to f_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(v_0))), f_1(v_0))\):  
  Let \(m = 2x + 2\) (so \(m \geq 2\) and \(m - 1 = 2x + 1\)).  
  Left: \([[f_4]](m)\).  
  Right: \(2^{([[f_4]](m - 1) + 1) \cdot 2^m}\).  
  By the recursive definition of \([[f_4]]\), we have exactly \([[f_4]](m) = 2^{([[f_4]](m - 1) + 1) \cdot 2^m} + 1\), which is strictly larger than the right-hand side.

Every rule is therefore strictly decreasing in the interpretation. Combined with monotonicity, this yields a reduction order on terms. Hence there are no infinite rewrite sequences.

(Note that the precise growth rates chosen above are not unique; many other interpretations into \(\mathbb{N}\) also work. The only essential requirements are that \([[f_1]]\) grows quickly enough for \([[f_3]]\) to be able to drop on its image while still satisfying the two \(f_3\)-rules, that \([[f_0]]\) and \([[f_2]]\) grow quickly enough to absorb the duplications and the “successor” shifts appearing in their rules, and that \([[f_4]]\) grows quickly enough to absorb an application of \([[f_2]]\).)
