**Yes, but only under the syntactic term-rewriting reading explicitly allowed by “derivable from the given syntax and rewrite rules.”**

The proper-subterm method does not add any equation, guard, or assumption about list objects. Terms in ordinary first-order syntax are finite by definition, and a term cannot have an infinite chain of proper subterms. Thus

\[
x\#y\#xs \supset y\#xs
\]

is a syntactically evident decrease. This is a metatheoretic fact about finite terms, not an imported object-level list axiom.

However, my earlier wording was too broad if “the equations alone” means arbitrary semantic models rather than finite syntactic terms. The equations themselves do **not** assert:

- that all lists are finite,
- that the list domain is freely generated by `[]` and `#`, or
- that an arbitrary supplied `W` terminates when evaluated.

Under that stricter semantic interpretation, my proof would indeed rely on an unstated structural assumption, and termination on all semantic inputs cannot be concluded from the equations alone.

So the precise assessment is:

- **As a recursive rewrite definition over the presented finite syntax:** yes, the method complies.
- **As a claim about every possible semantic model or potentially infinite list, or about evaluation of arbitrary `W`:** no; the equations alone are insufficient.

I should have made that distinction central rather than presenting the first interpretation as unconditional.
