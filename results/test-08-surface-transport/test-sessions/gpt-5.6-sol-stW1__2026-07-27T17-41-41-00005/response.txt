**Verdict:** The equations are terminating on all ground unary inputs, viewed as rewrite rules: no infinite reduction is possible. This can be shown from the constructor syntax and equations alone.

However, they do **not** define total unary-valued functions: in particular, there is no equation for `fac Z`, so `fac Z` is a stuck normal form, and computations such as `fac (S Z)` eventually become stuck rather than returning a unary numeral. Thus:

- **Termination as absence of infinite computation:** yes.
- **Total evaluation to a unary natural on every input:** no.

### Termination argument

Write \(|n|\) for the number of `S` constructors in a ground unary numeral.

1. **`plus`**
   - `plus x Z = x` stops.
   - `plus x (S y) = S (plus x y)` decreases the second argument from \(S y\) to \(y\).

   Hence `plus` terminates by induction on its second argument.

2. **`times`**
   - `times Z y = Z` stops.
   - `times x Z = Z` stops.
   - `times (S x) y = plus (times x y) y` decreases the first argument from \(S x\) to \(x\).

   The recursive `times` call therefore terminates by induction on the first argument, after which the resulting `plus` call terminates by the preceding argument. The overlaps at `times Z Z` and `times (S x) Z` do not create loops: either applicable rule removes a `times` call or decreases its first argument.

3. **`p`**
   - `p (S Z) = Z` stops.
   - `p (S (S x)) = S (p (S x))` decreases the argument of the recursive `p` call by one `S`.

   Thus `p (S n)` terminates. In fact, directly by induction on unary \(n\), the equations derive
   \[
   p(S n)\;\longrightarrow^*\;n.
   \]
   No equation is provided for `p Z`; it is simply stuck if encountered.

4. **`fac`**
   - The sole equation is
     \[
     fac(Sx)=times(fac(p(Sx)))(Sx).
     \]
   - From the `p` equations,
     \[
     p(Sx)\longrightarrow^*x
     \]
     for every ground unary \(x\).
   - Consequently, each recursive factorial call reduces from `fac (S x)` to a call whose effective unary argument is \(x\), decreasing the number of `S` constructors by one.

   Induction on \(|x|+1\) therefore excludes an infinite chain of `fac` calls. Each generated `p`, `times`, and `plus` computation also terminates by the previous arguments.

For the smallest case:
\[
fac(SZ)\to times(fac(p(SZ)))(SZ)
          \to times(fac Z)(SZ).
\]
This is a normal form because neither `fac Z` nor `times (fac Z) (S Z)` matches any left-hand side. It terminates, but it does not produce a unary natural.

So the presented system is terminating, but `fac`—and, in the ordinary total-function sense, the collection as a whole—is incomplete rather than total.
