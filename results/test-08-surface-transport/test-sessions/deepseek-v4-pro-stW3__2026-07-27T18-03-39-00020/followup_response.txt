I chose the **dependency pair method combined with a polynomial interpretation to the natural numbers** for a few reasons:

1. **Non‑trivial recursion patterns**  
   Some rules, like  
   `f2(f1(v0), v1) → f0(f2(v0, v1), v1)`  
   and  
   `f4(f1(v0)) → f2(f4(f3(f1(v0))), f1(v0))`,  
   perform recursive calls that are not immediate structural subterms. In the first one `f2` is called inside `f0`, so a simple structural or lexicographic path order would not directly accept it because the recursive `f2` is not the top‑level symbol. In the second, `f4` recurses on `f3(f1(v0))`, which requires showing that `f1(v0) > f3(f1(v0))` in some measure – not just by removing a constructor.

2. **Need for a flexible well‑founded measure**  
   The dependency pair method isolates the *recursive calls* (the dependency pairs) from the rewrite rules, allowing us to focus on just those pairs. Then we can assign a measure (a polynomial over ℕ) that makes every dependency pair strictly decrease, while the rules themselves are at least non‑increasing. This separation is ideal when rules seem to “bury” the recursive call inside other functions.

3. **The polynomial interpretation fits the data flow**  
   - `f4` reduces its argument from `f1(v0)` to `v0` (via `f3(f1(v0))`), so a linear weight like `[f1](x)=x+1` and `[f3](x)=max(x-1,0)` captures the size drop.  
   - For `f2`, the first argument size drops from `v0+1` to `v0`, which again suits a linear interpretation on the dependency pair `f2#`.  
   - The interpretation `[f4#](x) = 2x` over‑approximates the “effort” and gives a strict decrease from `2(v0+1)` to `2 v0`.  
   All weights are simple polynomials (mostly linear) over ℕ, which satisfies the necessary monotonicity and well‑foundedness conditions.

4. **Fully self‑contained**  
   This method does not require any imported axioms, structural properties beyond the rewrite rules, or external termination arguments. It works purely within the given syntactic presentation, exactly as requested.

In short, the **dependency pair technique with a polynomial interpretation** is a standard, powerful tool that handles systems where recursion is hidden inside other symbols or involves non‑structural descent, while still being formalizable entirely from the rewrite rules themselves.
