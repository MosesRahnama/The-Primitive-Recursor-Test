The system is structurally simple and non‑erasing: every recursive call occurs on a syntactically smaller argument (`succ(i)` → `i`), which immediately suggests a termination proof based on “basic syntactic descent”. Recursive Path Ordering is the standard, lightweight proof method that captures exactly that kind of syntactic decrease without any need for interpretations, polynomial arithmetic, or auxiliary well‑founded orderings.  

With only three unconditional rules, RPO allows us to assign a precedence that mirrors the call graph—`extract_prefix` calls `extract_prefix_k` and `extract_prefix` itself on a subterm, and `extract_prefix_k` calls `append`, `cons`, `best_eclass_term`, and `nth`. A single well‑founded order then covers all rules at once, and verifying each rule reduces to the straightforward subterm check or precedence‑based comparison. No additional reasoning about guards, memory, or implicit assumptions is needed.  

I did not choose polynomial interpretations because the simple subterm decrease is immediate and does not require constructing a monotone polynomial. I did not use dependency pairs because the system has no mutual recursion that would benefit from that analysis, and RPO already handles the straightforward nesting here. The method was chosen because it is the simplest tool that suffices.
