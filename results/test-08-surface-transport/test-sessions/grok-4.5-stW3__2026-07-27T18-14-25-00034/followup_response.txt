### Why this method?

The system is a pure first-order term rewriting system (no binders, no AC symbols, no built-in arithmetic, no conditional rules). For such systems the two most common fully syntactic/semantic termination techniques that stay strictly inside the given signature and rules are:

- simplification orders (RPO/LPO/KBO and their variants), and  
- the dependency-pair (DP) framework combined with a reduction pair (usually a polynomial or monotone-algebra interpretation).

A direct simplification order is unattractive here for two concrete reasons visible from the rules alone:

- The rule  
  `f4(f1(v0)) → f2(f4(f3(f1(v0))), f1(v0))`  
  duplicates the recursive call inside a context that itself contains the original argument.  Most path orders therefore require a status or a precedence that makes `f2` “smaller” than `f4` while simultaneously making the second argument of `f2` decrease; this quickly forces an inconsistent precedence or an extra argument-filtering that is no longer a pure simplification order.
- The two `f3`-rules implement a predecessor on a unary numeral representation.  Path orders treat the predecessor essentially as a projection and therefore cannot easily justify the decrease hidden inside the `f4`-rule.

The DP method sidesteps both problems.  It separates the “structural” recursive calls (the DPs) from the original rules.  Once the DPs are extracted, a single monotone algebra interpretation over \(\mathbb{N}\) can be read off the intended meaning of the symbols

- \(c_0\) ~ 0, \(f_1\) ~ successor,  
- \(f_0\) ~ addition, \(f_2\) ~ multiplication,  
- \(f_3\) ~ predecessor, \(f_4\) ~ a fast-growing function (essentially \(n\mapsto 3^n\cdot n!\))

that makes every original rule weakly decreasing and every DP strictly decreasing.  Because the interpretation is defined only on the symbols that actually appear, and because weak monotonicity is immediate from the defining clauses, the reduction-pair constraints stay entirely within the given syntax; no extra rewrite rules, guards or object-level lemmas are required.

In short, DPs + a suitably chosen monotone algebra are the lightest standard technique that (a) respects the “no extra axioms” boundary you imposed and (b) succeeds on every rule without further elaboration.  That is why the method was selected.
