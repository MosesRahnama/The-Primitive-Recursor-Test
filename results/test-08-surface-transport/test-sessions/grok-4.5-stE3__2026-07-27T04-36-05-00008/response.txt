### Verdict
**Yes**, termination of this TRS can be established from the given rules alone (no extra rules, guards, side-conditions, structural axioms, or object-level assumptions are required).

### Termination argument
The system is a pure first-order TRS over the signature with constants \(c_0,c_1\) and function symbols \(f_0\) (unary), \(f_1,f_2,f_3\) (binary), \(f_4\) (ternary), \(f_5\) (ternary) and \(f_6\) (quaternary). The only possible redexes are rooted at \(f_5\) or \(f_6\). We exhibit a single simplification order that orients every rule; the existence of such an order is a standard, self-contained sufficient condition for termination.

Consider the lexicographic path order (LPO) induced by the precedence
\[
f_5 > f_6 > f_2 > f_1 > f_4 > f_3 > f_0 > c_1 > c_0
\]
(with every symbol given lexicographic status, arguments compared left-to-right). LPO is a well-founded simplification order on ground terms; it extends immediately to open terms by treating variables as smallest. It remains only to check that each of the three rules is oriented from left to right.

- Rule \(f_5(v_0,v_1,c_0)\to c_1\).  
  The root symbol of the left-hand side is strictly larger than the constant on the right-hand side, so the inequality holds.

- Rule \(f_5(v_0,v_1,f_0(v_2))\to f_6(v_0,v_1,v_2,f_5(v_0,v_1,v_2))\).  
  The root-precedence \(f_5>f_6\) reduces the claim to showing that the left-hand side is strictly larger (w.r.t. the same LPO) than every immediate argument of the right-hand side:
  - \(f_5(v_0,v_1,f_0(v_2))>v_0\), \(>v_1\) and \(>v_2\) hold by the subterm property;
  - \(f_5(v_0,v_1,f_0(v_2))>f_5(v_0,v_1,v_2)\) holds because the two sides have identical root and identical first two arguments, while the third arguments satisfy \(f_0(v_2)>v_2\) (again by the subterm property).

- Rule \(f_6(v_0,v_1,v_2,v_3)\to f_2(v_3,f_1(f_4(v_0,v_3,f_3(v_1,v_2)),c_1))\).  
  The root-precedence \(f_6>f_2\) reduces the claim to showing that the left-hand side is strictly larger than both arguments of the right-hand side:
  - \(f_6(\dots)>v_3\) holds by the subterm property;
  - \(f_6(\dots)>f_1(f_4(v_0,v_3,f_3(v_1,v_2)),c_1)\) holds by root-precedence \(f_6>f_1\), which in turn requires
    \(f_6(\dots)>f_4(v_0,v_3,f_3(v_1,v_2))\) and \(f_6(\dots)>c_1\).
    The second inequality is immediate from the precedence. The first follows from root-precedence \(f_6>f_4\), which reduces to three further comparisons, all of which hold by the subterm property (or by one extra application of \(f_6>f_3\) for the third argument).

Every rule is therefore strictly decreasing in a well-founded order. Consequently every rewrite sequence is finite.

(The same orientation can be obtained by a multiset path order or by a simple recursive polynomial interpretation that maps each \(f_5\)-term to a value strictly larger than the value of the term obtained after one unfolding of rules 2+3; the LPO argument above is merely the most compact.)
