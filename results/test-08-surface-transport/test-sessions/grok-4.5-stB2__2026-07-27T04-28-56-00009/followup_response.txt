### Why RPO (recursive path order)

I chose RPO because it is a purely syntactic, off-the-shelf simplification order that works directly from the three rules and the ambient first-order term signature—no numeric domains, no external lemmas about addition or lists, and no need to add guards or extra rules.  

The system has a single recursive function whose recursion is structural on the second argument (a list built from `nil`/`cons`). RPO is designed exactly for this situation: a precedence that makes the defined symbol larger than the constructors and operations appearing on right-hand sides, plus the built-in subterm property, orients every rule in one uniform pass. Lexicographic status takes care of the fact that the recursive call keeps the first argument unchanged while strictly decreasing the second.  

That is enough to obtain a well-founded reduction order, so termination follows at once. (A polynomial or exponential interpretation also works, but it requires choosing a concrete carrier and verifying monotonicity arithmetic; RPO avoids that extra layer while staying inside the same formal boundary.)
