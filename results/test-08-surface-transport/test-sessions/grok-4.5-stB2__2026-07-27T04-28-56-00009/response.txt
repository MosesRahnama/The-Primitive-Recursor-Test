### Verdict
Yes, termination of the given TRS can be established from the rules (and the ambient first-order term syntax) alone.

### Termination argument
We exhibit an explicit reduction order: the recursive path order (RPO) induced by the precedence
\[
\texttt{exec_walk_weight} > \texttt{add},\qquad
\texttt{exec_walk_weight} > \texttt{app},\qquad
\texttt{exec_walk_weight} > \texttt{zero}
\]
(all other function symbols being pairwise incomparable) together with lexicographic status (left-to-right) for every symbol.  RPO is a simplification order, hence a well-founded reduction order on first-order terms; it is therefore sufficient to verify that each of the three rules is oriented by \(>_{\mathrm{RPO}}\).

- Rule \(\texttt{exec_walk_weight}(w,\texttt{nil})\to\texttt{zero}\).  
  The root precedence \(\texttt{exec_walk_weight}>\texttt{zero}\) applies and the right-hand side has no immediate subterms, so the inequality holds.

- Rule \(\texttt{exec_walk_weight}(w,\texttt{cons}(x,\texttt{nil}))\to\texttt{zero}\).  
  Identical to the previous case.

- Rule  
  \[
  \texttt{exec_walk_weight}(w,\texttt{cons}(x,\texttt{cons}(y,xs)))
  \;\to\;
  \texttt{add}\bigl(
    \texttt{app}(\texttt{app}(w,x),y),\;
    \texttt{exec_walk_weight}(w,\texttt{cons}(y,xs))
  \bigr).
  \]
  The root precedence \(\texttt{exec_walk_weight}>\texttt{add}\) reduces the claim to the two strict inequalities
  \[
  \begin{align*}
  L &>_{\mathrm{RPO}} \texttt{app}(\texttt{app}(w,x),y),\\
  L &>_{\mathrm{RPO}} \texttt{exec_walk_weight}(w,\texttt{cons}(y,xs)),
  \end{align*}
  \]
  where \(L\) denotes the left-hand side.

  - For the recursive call the two sides have identical root symbols, so RPO compares argument tuples lexicographically.  The first arguments are identical; the second arguments satisfy
    \[
    \texttt{cons}(x,\texttt{cons}(y,xs)) \;>_{\mathrm{RPO}}\; \texttt{cons}(y,xs)
    \]
    because the right-hand term is a proper subterm of the left-hand term (and every simplification order contains the proper-subterm relation).  Hence \(L\) is larger than the recursive call.
  - For the \(\texttt{app}\)-term the root precedence \(\texttt{exec_walk_weight}>\texttt{app}\) reduces the claim to
    \[
    L \;>_{\mathrm{RPO}}\; \texttt{app}(w,x)
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    L \;>_{\mathrm{RPO}}\; y.
    \]
    The variable \(y\) is a proper subterm of \(L\).  For the remaining atom the same precedence reduces it once more to \(L>_{\mathrm{RPO}}w\) and \(L>_{\mathrm{RPO}}x\), both of which hold by the proper-subterm relation.  Consequently \(L\) is larger than the \(\texttt{app}\)-term.

All three rules are therefore strictly decreasing in a well-founded reduction order, so every rewrite sequence is finite.

(The same orientation can be obtained from a simple exponential interpretation into the positive integers, e.g. \(\llbracket\texttt{exec_walk_weight}(w,l)\rrbracket=\llbracket w\rrbracket\cdot 2^{\llbracket l\rrbracket}\) with the obvious strictly monotone interpretations of the remaining constructors; the RPO argument above is sufficient and does not require any numeric coding.)
