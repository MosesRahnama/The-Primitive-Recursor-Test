**Verdict:** Yes—if “termination” means that the defining equations, oriented from left to right, admit no infinite reduction starting from unary-natural inputs. This can be proved from the syntax and equations alone.

However, termination must not be confused with **total evaluation to a numeral**. In particular, `p Z` and `fac Z` have no defining equations, so they are stuck normal forms. Consequently, `fac` does not compute numeral factorials; for example,
\[
fac(SZ)\to times(fac(p(SZ)),SZ)\to times(fac Z,SZ),
\]
which is stuck.

### Termination argument

Unary-natural inputs are precisely the finite constructor terms \(S^nZ\). Induction on \(n\) here is metatheoretic induction on finite syntax, not an imported object-level axiom.

Orient every displayed equation left-to-right.

#### `plus`

- `plus x Z → x` terminates immediately.
- `plus x (S y) → S (plus x y)` removes one leading `S` from the second argument of the recursive call.

Thus every `plus` reduction on numeral arguments makes finitely many recursive steps, bounded by the size of its second argument.

#### `times`

- `times Z y → Z` terminates immediately.
- `times x Z → Z` terminates immediately.
- `times (S x) y → plus (times x y) y` decreases the first numeral argument from `S x` to `x`.

By induction on the first argument, all recursively generated `times` calls terminate; the generated `plus` calls terminate by the preceding argument. At `times Z Z`, the first two equations overlap, but both yield `Z`, so the overlap cannot cause an infinite reduction.

#### `p`

- `p (S Z) → Z` terminates immediately.
- `p (S (S x)) → S (p (S x))` removes one `S` from the argument of the recursive `p` call.

Hence `p(S^nZ)` terminates for every \(n>0\), producing \(S^{n-1}Z\). `p Z` has no applicable equation and is therefore already a normal form.

#### `fac`

The apparent recursive call is
\[
fac(Sx)\to times(fac(p(Sx)),Sx).
\]

Normalize with respect to the non-`fac` rules. Whenever \(Sx\) denotes the numeral \(S^nZ\), the argument of the new `fac` call,
\[
p(Sx),
\]
normalizes to \(S^{n-1}Z\). Therefore each `fac` contraction decreases the numeral rank of its recursive argument by one.

This also establishes strong termination under arbitrary interleaving, not merely under an eager strategy:

1. The subsystem consisting of `plus`, `times`, and `p` terminates by the decreasing arguments above.
2. Every reachable `fac` call has an argument whose normalization under that subsystem is a numeral.
3. A contraction of `fac(Sx)` introduces exactly one descendant `fac` call, and its normalized numeral rank is one smaller.
4. Thus only finitely many `fac` contractions are possible.
5. Between those contractions, only finitely many `plus`/`times`/`p` reductions are possible.

Eventually the recursion reaches `fac Z`, where no rule applies. Therefore there is no infinite reduction from any unary-natural input.

### Important limitation

The equations prove **termination by reaching a normal form**, but not **totality as numeral-valued functions**:

- `p Z` is undefined/stuck.
- `fac Z` is undefined/stuck.
- Positive `fac` inputs consequently also generally terminate in stuck non-numeral terms.

Finally, this verdict assumes the displayed equations are computational rules oriented left-to-right. If equations may be used freely in both directions, termination is impossible—for example, reversing `plus x Z = x` permits the endless expansion \(x\to plus(x,Z)\to\cdots\).
