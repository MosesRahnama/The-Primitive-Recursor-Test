I chose a **modular well-founded descent argument on constructor syntax** because it fits the unusual features of these equations without adding assumptions.

In particular:

1. **The recursion in `plus`, `times`, and `p` is visibly structural.**  
   Their recursive calls remove an `S` from a designated argument. Measuring a finite unary term \(S^nZ\) by its number of `S` constructors gives an immediate metatheoretic termination measure.

2. **`fac` is not directly structurally recursive.**  
   Its recursive argument is `p (S x)`, not syntactically the variable `x`:
   \[
   fac(Sx)\to times(fac(p(Sx)),Sx).
   \]
   Therefore, simply claiming that the recursive call is on `x` would silently assume that `p` is predecessor. Instead, I first derived from the displayed `p` equations that
   \[
   p(S^nZ)\to^*S^{n-1}Z\qquad(n>0).
   \]
   Only then did I use numeral height as the decreasing measure for `fac`.

3. **It handles the missing base equations correctly.**  
   There is no `fac Z` rule and no `p Z` rule. A proof by intended mathematical meaning—“`fac` is factorial and `p` is predecessor”—could accidentally assume those functions are total. The reduction-based method instead treats `fac Z` and `p Z` as stuck normal forms. Stuckness still means termination, although not successful numeral evaluation.

4. **It can establish strong termination, not merely exhibit one terminating strategy.**  
   Proving that eager evaluation terminates would not by itself exclude another infinite reduction order. The modular argument separates:
   - the terminating `plus`/`times`/`p` subsystem, and
   - the finite number of possible `fac` contractions, measured by normalized unary height.

   Thus arbitrary interleavings cannot create an infinite reduction.

5. **It requires no object-level induction axiom.**  
   The induction is over the finite syntax trees \(Z,SZ,S(SZ),\ldots\), at the metalevel. It does not add equations such as `p Z = Z` or `fac Z = S Z`, nor assume an external arithmetic model.

I did not use a simpler “every right-hand side is structurally smaller” argument because that is not literally true for the whole right-hand side of `times` or `fac`, and `fac`’s decrease is mediated by evaluating `p`. The modular rank argument makes that mediated decrease explicit and also exposes the distinction between **termination** and **total numeral-valued computation**.
