## Why structural/subterm induction was the method of choice

**1. It matches the syntactic shape of the definitions exactly.**
Every equation here is a standard *primitive-recursion-style* pattern match on `Z`/`S`: each right-hand side either makes no recursive call (base cases like `plus x Z = x`, `times Z y = Z`, `p (S Z) = Z`) or makes a recursive call whose argument is written syntactically as a component of the left-hand side's pattern (`y` inside `S y`, `x` inside `S x`, `S x` inside `S (S x)`). This is precisely the situation the subterm ordering is designed for — no arithmetic reasoning, no auxiliary measure function needs to be invented; the decrease is visible directly in the term structure of the equations as given.

**2. It requires no imported axioms.**
The problem explicitly forbids importing external object-level axioms or hidden assumptions. The subterm relation on terms freely generated by `Z` and `S` is not an "extra axiom" about the objects (naturals) — it's a property of the term algebra itself (well-foundedness of the immediate-subterm relation on finite terms), which is available for free once we fix that `Z, S` are the only constructors. So this stays within the boundary: I'm reasoning about the shape of terms produced by the given grammar, not asserting new facts about `plus`, `times`, `p`, `fac` beyond their defining equations.

**3. It composes cleanly across mutually dependent functions.**
`times` calls `plus`, and `fac` calls `p` (and `times`, `fac`). Rather than needing a single global measure spanning all four functions, structural/subterm decrease lets me establish termination *function by function*, using already-proved termination of the callee as a black box (`plus` terminates → `times`'s call to `plus` is fine as a subroutine; `p` terminates and satisfies `p(S x) = x` → `fac`'s call is on a genuine subterm). This modularity mirrors how the definitions are laid out (each function only calls itself and previously defined functions), so the proof method should respect that same layering — a single well-founded order (subterm) applied locally at each definition, rather than a bespoke ordinal or numeric measure for the whole system.

**4. Alternatives were unnecessary/overkill.**
- A numeric measure (e.g., "size of second argument") would work too, but it's just an encoding of the same subterm fact (`|S y| = |y|+1`), so it adds no extra strength — I used size only as bookkeeping notation, with the real justification being the subterm relation.
- Lexicographic or multiset orderings (common in more complex term-rewriting termination proofs) aren't needed since every recursive call here decreases in *one single argument position* already dictated by the match — there's no interleaving of multiple decreasing arguments requiring a combined order.
- Well-founded induction over a custom-built ordinal was unnecessary since termination on `Z`/`S`-generated terms already gives a well-founded order.

**5. It handles the one delicate case (`fac`) honestly.**
`fac`'s recursive argument is `p (S x)`, not literally a subterm written on the surface. Structural induction alone doesn't immediately show `p (S x) ⊏ S x`; it only shows it *after* separately proving, from `p`'s own two equations (again by structural induction on `x`), that `p (S x)` evaluates to `x`. So the same method (subterm induction) was reapplied recursively — first to prove `p` terminates, then to derive its closed behavior, then to feed that fact into `fac`'s termination argument. This layering is exactly why I picked one uniform method: it scales down to prove auxiliary lemmas about `p` and back up to discharge `fac`, without switching proof technology mid-argument.

**In short:** subterm/structural induction was chosen because (a) it's the minimal, most directly licensed well-founded order given only `Z`/`S`-generated terms, (b) it matches the pattern-matching style of every equation with no gap needing an invented measure, (c) it composes across the call-graph (`plus → times`, `p → fac`) without extra machinery, and (d) it stays strictly within the stated constraint of not importing any object-level axioms beyond the syntax and equations themselves.
