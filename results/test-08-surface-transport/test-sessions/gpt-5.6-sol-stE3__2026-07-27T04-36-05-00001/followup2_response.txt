Yes—under the standard definition of a first-order TRS over **finite terms**, the LPO proof complies with those requirements.

- **No extra rewrite rules** were introduced.
- **No guards or side conditions** were imposed on any rule.
- **No interpretation or object-level equations** for \(f0,\ldots,f6,c0,c1\) were assumed.
- The chosen **precedence and lexicographic status are proof parameters**, not additions to the rewrite system. They define a meta-level well-founded ordering used to show that each given rewrite step decreases.
- LPO’s subterm property, substitution stability, context closure, and well-foundedness are standard facts about the syntax of finite first-order terms. Using them is permitted by the prompt’s allowance of “any standard proof method”; they are not imported object-level axioms.

In particular, the proof does not assume semantically that \(f0(v2)\) is “larger” than \(v2\). It uses the syntactic fact that \(v2\) is a proper subterm of \(f0(v2)\), which is already determined by the displayed term syntax.

The only ambient convention is that “first-order term rewriting system” means rewriting over finite first-order terms. If infinitary terms or infinitary rewriting were intended, that would be a different framework and would need to be stated explicitly. Under ordinary finite-term rewriting, the method stays within the requested boundary.
