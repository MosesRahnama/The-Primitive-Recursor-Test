Choose an LPO with lexicographic status for all symbols and precedence

\[
f5 \succ f6 \succ f2 \succ f1 \succ f4 \succ f3 \succ f0 \succ c1 \succ c0.

### Rule 1

\[
f5(v0,v1,c0) \to c1.
\]

Because \(f5\succ c1\), the left side is strictly greater than \(c1\).

### Rule 2

Let

\[
L=f5(v0,v1,f0(v2)),\qquad
Q=f5(v0,v1,v2).

By the subterm property,

\[
f0(v2)>v2,

so, using the lexicographic status of \(f5\),

\[
L>Q.

Also \(L>v0,v1,v2\) by the subterm property. Since \(f5\succ f6\), \(L\) dominates every argument of the right-hand side, and therefore

\[
f5(v0,v1,f0(v2))

### Rule 3

Let

\[
S=f6(v0,v1,v2,v3).
\]

By the subterm property,

\[
S>v0,v1,v2,v3.
\]

Because \(f6\succ f3\),

\[
S>f3(v1,v2).

Because \(f6\succ f4\), and \(S\) dominates \(v0,v3,f3(v1,v2)\),

\[
S>f4(v0,v3,f3(v1,v2)).

Also \(S>c1\). Since \(f6\succ f1\),

\[
S>f1(f4(v0,v3,f3(v1,v2)),c1).

Finally, since \(f6\succ f2\) and \(S\) dominates both arguments of the outer \(f2\),

\[

f2(v3,f1(f4(v0,v3,f3(v1,v2)),c1)).
