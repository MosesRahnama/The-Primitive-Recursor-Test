### Verdict
**No.**

Termination of the full set of functions cannot be established from the given equations and syntax alone.

### Argument
The syntax generates the free algebra of unary naturals under constructors \(Z\) and \(S\). The subterm relation on this algebra is well-founded by construction of the terms themselves; this is the only well-founded order available without importing additional object-level structure. We examine each equation against that boundary.

- The two equations for `plus` are structurally recursive on the second argument: the sole recursive call is `plus x y` where \(y\) is an immediate subterm of the matched pattern \(S\, y\). Every reduction therefore descends in the subterm order. Termination follows at once from well-foundedness of that order.
- The three equations for `times` are likewise structurally recursive on the first argument (the overlapping base cases `times Z y` and `times x Z` introduce no calls). The sole recursive call is `times x y` where \(x\) is an immediate subterm of \(S\, x\). Termination again follows from the subterm order. (The auxiliary calls to the already-terminated function `plus` do not alter the argument.)
- The two equations for `p` are structurally recursive on the argument: the sole recursive call is `p (S x)` where \(S\, x\) is an immediate subterm of the matched pattern \(S\,(S\, x)\). The base case `p (S Z)` introduces no call. Termination follows from the subterm order. (Note that there is simply no equation for \(p\, Z\); the function is already partial.)

- The single equation for `fac` is
  \[
  \mathrm{fac}\,(S\, x)=\mathrm{times}\,(\mathrm{fac}\,(p\,(S\, x)))\,(S\, x).
  \]
  The recursive call is on the term \(p\,(S\, x)\). This term is not a subterm of the matched pattern \(S\, x\). Consequently the subterm order supplies no descent.  

  The only way to recover a decreasing measure is to appeal to a semantic property of the already-defined function \(p\), namely that
  \[
  p\,(S\, n)\;=\;n
  \]
  (hence \(|p\,(S\, n)|<|S\, n|\)) for every numeral \(n\). Proving the displayed identity requires a case analysis on \(n\) followed by induction over the constructors \(Z\) and \(S\), using precisely the two equations given for \(p\). That inductive step is a structural induction axiom on the naturals. It is not derivable by equational reasoning from the presented equations, nor is it implied by the bare syntax of the constructors.  

  Without that axiom (or an equivalent external well-founded relation, size function, or guardedness condition) there is no justification that the call `fac (p (S x))` terminates. Strengthening the definition of `fac` by an extra base case, an explicit measure, a side condition, or a different set of equations would leave the given system, which is forbidden.  

Because the termination argument for `fac` cannot be closed inside the permitted boundary, termination of the whole collection of functions on all inputs cannot be established from the equations alone. (Partiality of `p` and `fac` on \(Z\) is a separate defect of coverage, but the failure already occurs on the covered fragment \(S\,\_\).)
