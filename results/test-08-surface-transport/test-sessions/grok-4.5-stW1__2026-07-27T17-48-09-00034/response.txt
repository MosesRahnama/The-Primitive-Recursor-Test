### Verdict
**No**, termination of these functions on all inputs (i.e., totality: every ground input built from \(Z\) and \(S\) reduces to a unique constructor normal form) **cannot** be established from the given equations alone.

The rewrite relation induced by the equations is finitely branching and locally decreasing on the equations that do exist, but the system is not total: there are ground inputs (in particular every input to \(\mathsf{fac}\)) for which reduction reaches an irreducible term that is not a numeral. No equational consequence of the given rules eliminates those stuck terms, and the missing cases cannot be derived. Strengthening by extra equations, default values, or domain restrictions is disallowed.

### Equation-by-equation examination

**plus**  
- \(\mathsf{plus}\,x\,Z=x\) covers the base constructor.  
- \(\mathsf{plus}\,x\,(S\,y)=S\,(\mathsf{plus}\,x\,y)\) covers the step constructor; the recursive call is on the strictly smaller second argument.  
For every ground numeral \(n\) the second argument decreases under the usual ordering on unary naturals, so every ground term \(\mathsf{plus}\,m\,n\) reaches a numeral after finitely many steps. Termination of \(\mathsf{plus}\) on all inputs follows directly from the two equations.

**times**  
- \(\mathsf{times}\,Z\,y=Z\) covers a zero first argument.  
- \(\mathsf{times}\,x\,Z=Z\) covers a zero second argument (overlap on \(\mathsf{times}\,Z\,Z\) is harmless).  
- \(\mathsf{times}\,(S\,x)\,y=\mathsf{plus}\,(\mathsf{times}\,x\,y)\,y\) covers a successor first argument; the recursive call is on the strictly smaller first argument and the subsequent \(\mathsf{plus}\) has already been shown to terminate.  
Hence every ground term \(\mathsf{times}\,m\,n\) reduces to a numeral. Termination of \(\mathsf{times}\) follows from the three equations.

**p**  
- \(\mathsf{p}\,(S\,Z)=Z\) covers the numeral 1.  
- \(\mathsf{p}\,(S\,(S\,x))=S\,(\mathsf{p}\,(S\,x))\) covers every larger successor; the recursive call is on the strictly smaller argument \(S\,x\).  
Consequently every ground term \(\mathsf{p}\,(S\,n)\) (\(n\ge0\)) reduces to the numeral \(n\).  
However, the equations supply **no reduct for \(\mathsf{p}\,Z\)**. The term \(\mathsf{p}\,Z\) is therefore irreducible and is not a numeral. Already \(\mathsf{p}\) fails to be total, and this fact is visible from the syntax of the two equations alone.

**fac**  
- The single equation \(\mathsf{fac}\,(S\,x)=\mathsf{times}\,(\mathsf{fac}\,(\mathsf{p}\,(S\,x)))\,(S\,x)\) is the only rule.  
  From the equations of \(\mathsf{p}\) one can derive (by a trivial induction on ground \(x\)) the identity \(\mathsf{p}\,(S\,x)=x\). Substituting yields the simpler recursive equation  
  \[
  \mathsf{fac}\,(S\,x)=\mathsf{times}\,(\mathsf{fac}\,x)\,(S\,x).
  \]
  Each recursive call is therefore on a structurally smaller numeral, and the surrounding \(\mathsf{times}\) terminates. The call stack is consequently finite.  

- Nevertheless there is **no equation for \(\mathsf{fac}\,Z\)**. Unrolling the recursion on any concrete input \(S\,n\) therefore ends at the stuck term  
  \[
  \mathsf{times}\,(\mathsf{times}\,(\dots\mathsf{times}\,(\mathsf{fac}\,Z,S\,Z)\dots),S\,n)
  \]
  in which the inner redex \(\mathsf{fac}\,Z\) matches none of the given rules, nor does the outer \(\mathsf{times}\) (its first argument is neither \(Z\) nor a successor, and its second argument is not \(Z\)). The resulting term is irreducible and is not a numeral.

Because every ground application of \(\mathsf{fac}\) reduces to a non-numeral normal form, \(\mathsf{fac}\) is not total. The failure is a direct consequence of the missing base-case equation; nothing derivable from the eight presented equations supplies the absent reduct.

### Conclusion
plus and times terminate on all ground inputs. p and fac do not. The given equations alone already exhibit the stuck terms \(\mathsf{p}\,Z\) and \(\mathsf{fac}\,Z\), so totality cannot be proved without extending the definition.
