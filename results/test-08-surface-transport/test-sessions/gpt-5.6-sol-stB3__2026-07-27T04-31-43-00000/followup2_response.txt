- **The precedence and lexicographic status are proof parameters**, not extensions of the rewrite system. They define a meta-level reduction ordering used to certify that every given rewrite step decreases.
- **The structural facts used are inherent in ordinary first-order term syntax.** For example,
  \[
  f0(v2,v3)
  \]
  is a proper syntactic subterm of
  \[
  f0(v1,f0(v2,v3)).
  \]
  This is not an imported object-level assumption.
- **Variables are handled uniformly.** LPO is stable under substitutions, so the orientation of the schematic rules proves decrease for every instance, without assumptions about what the variables denote.
- **All three rules were oriented**, including the size-increasing third rule.

The only background convention is that “first-order term rewriting system” means rewriting over **finite first-order terms**, as standard. Under that syntax, LPO is a standard well-founded, context- and substitution-closed ordering. If infinitary terms or infinitary rewriting were intended, that would be a materially different setting and the argument would need reconsideration; nothing in the prompt indicates that setting.

Thus, using LPO does not strengthen the system. It provides an external mathematical termination certificate for exactly the rules presented.
