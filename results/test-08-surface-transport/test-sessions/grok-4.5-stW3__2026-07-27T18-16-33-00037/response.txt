### Verdict
Yes, termination of the given TRS can be established from the rules alone (via the dependency-pair method together with a suitable reduction pair induced by a weakly monotone interpretation into the naturals).

### Termination argument
Let \(\mathcal{R}\) be the given TRS. The defined symbols of \(\mathcal{R}\) (those appearing at the root of a left-hand side) are \(\{f_0,f_2,f_3,f_4\}\); the remaining symbols \(\{c_0,f_1\}\) are constructors. The dependency pairs of \(\mathcal{R}\) are therefore precisely the following (writing \(t^\sharp\) for the root-marked variant of a term \(t\) used in the dependency-pair framework, though the marking may be ignored for the subsequent numeric interpretation):

- From the second rule: \(f_0(v_0,f_1(v_1))^\sharp\to f_0(v_0,v_1)^\sharp\)
- From the fifth rule: \(f_2(f_1(v_0),v_1)^\sharp\to f_0(f_2(v_0,v_1),v_1)^\sharp\) and \(f_2(f_1(v_0),v_1)^\sharp\to f_2(v_0,v_1)^\sharp\)
- From the sixth rule: \(f_3(f_1(f_1(v_0)))^\sharp\to f_3(f_1(v_0))^\sharp\)
- From the eighth rule: \(f_4(f_1(v_0))^\sharp\to f_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(v_0))),f_1(v_0))^\sharp\), \(f_4(f_1(v_0))^\sharp\to f_4(f_3(f_1(v_0)))^\sharp\) and \(f_4(f_1(v_0))^\sharp\to f_3(f_1(v_0))^\sharp\).

(The first, third, fourth and seventh rules contribute no dependency pairs, as their right-hand sides contain no defined symbols.)

Consider the following interpretation of function symbols as weakly monotone maps on the naturals \(\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}\):

- \([[c_0]]=0\),
- \([[f_1]](n)=n+1\),
- \([[f_0]](n,m)=n+m\),
- \([[f_2]](n,m)=n\cdot(m+1)\),
- \([[f_3]](n)=\max(n-1,0)\),
- \([[f_4]](n)=(n+2)!\)

(this is well-defined and weakly monotone in every argument). Extending the interpretation to open terms in the usual homomorphic way, one obtains a reduction pair \((\ge,>)\) on terms by
\[
s\ge t\quad\text{iff}\quad[[s]]\ge[[t]]\qquad\text{and}\qquad s>t\quad\text{iff}\quad[[s]]>[[t]]
\]
(the pair is compatible, stable under substitutions, and closed under contexts because every \([[f]]\) is weakly monotone; the relation \(>\) is well-founded because it is the standard order of \(\mathbb{N}\)).

Direct inspection of each rule of \(\mathcal{R}\) shows \(l\ge r\):

- \([[f_0(v_0,c_0)]]=[[v_0]]+0=[[v_0]]\),
- \([[f_0(v_0,f_1(v_1))]]=[[v_0]]+([[v_1]]+1)=[[f_1(f_0(v_0,v_1))]]\),
- \([[f_2(c_0,v_0)]]=0\cdot([[v_0]]+1)=0=[[c_0]]\),
- \([[f_2(v_0,c_0)]]=[[v_0]]\cdot 1\ge 0=[[c_0]]\),
- \([[f_2(f_1(v_0),v_1)]]=([[v_0]]+1)\cdot([[v_1]]+1)\ge[[f_2(v_0,v_1)]]+[[v_1]]=[[f_0(f_2(v_0,v_1),v_1)]]\),
- \([[f_3(f_1(f_1(v_0)))]]=[[v_0]]+1=[[f_1(f_3(f_1(v_0)))]]\),
- \([[f_3(f_1(c_0))]]=0=[[c_0]]\),
- \([[f_4(f_1(v_0))]]=([[v_0]]+3)!\ge([[v_0]]+2)\cdot([[v_0]]+2)!=[[f_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(v_0))),f_1(v_0))]]\).

Likewise, direct inspection of each dependency pair shows a *strict* decrease:

- \([[f_0(v_0,f_1(v_1))]]=[[v_0]]+[[v_1]]+1>[[v_0]]+[[v_1]]=[[f_0(v_0,v_1)]]\),
- \([[f_2(f_1(v_0),v_1)]]=([[v_0]]+1)\cdot([[v_1]]+1)>[[v_0]]\cdot([[v_1]]+1)+[[v_1]]=[[f_0(f_2(v_0,v_1),v_1)]]\),
- \([[f_2(f_1(v_0),v_1)]]=([[v_0]]+1)\cdot([[v_1]]+1)>[[v_0]]\cdot([[v_1]]+1)=[[f_2(v_0,v_1)]]\),
- \([[f_3(f_1(f_1(v_0)))]]=[[v_0]]+1>[[v_0]]=[[f_3(f_1(v_0))]]\),
- \([[f_4(f_1(v_0))]]=([[v_0]]+3)!>([[v_0]]+2)\cdot([[v_0]]+2)!=[[f_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(v_0))),f_1(v_0))]]\),
- \([[f_4(f_1(v_0))]]=([[v_0]]+3)!>([[v_0]]+2)!=[[f_4(f_3(f_1(v_0)))]]\),
- \([[f_4(f_1(v_0))]]=([[v_0]]+3)!>[[v_0]]=[[f_3(f_1(v_0))]]\).

By the soundness theorem of the dependency-pair method it follows that \(\mathcal{R}\) is terminating.

(Note that the argument uses nothing beyond the syntax of the eight rules, the standard definition of dependency pairs for a first-order TRS, and elementary arithmetic properties of the interpretation; no equational theories, no guards, and no external lemmas about the intended arithmetic meaning of the symbols are required.)
