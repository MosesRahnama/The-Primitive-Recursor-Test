### Why this method?

The TRS has a mutually recursive structure with nested defined symbols on right-hand sides (especially the rule for \(f_4\), which feeds an \(f_3\)-image back into \(f_4\) and then into \(f_2\), and the rule for \(f_2\), which feeds into \(f_0\)). A direct well-founded order on all terms that orients every rule strictly is therefore awkward to write down by hand: several rules are “size-preserving” under any simple polynomial measure (they act like the recursive clauses of addition/multiplication), so a pure polynomial interpretation on the original signature typically only yields a weak decrease, which is not enough by itself for termination.

The dependency-pair (DP) framework isolates exactly the recursive calls. Once those calls are extracted, it becomes sufficient to find a reduction pair \((\ge,>)\) such that
- every original rule satisfies \(l\ge r\) (weak decrease), and
- every dependency pair satisfies \(s^\sharp>t^\sharp\) (strict decrease).

That pair can be realized by a weakly monotone interpretation into \(\mathbb{N}\), which is easy to read off from the shape of the rules:
- \(f_1\) behaves like a successor, so interpret it as \(+1\);
- \(f_0\) and \(f_2\) behave like addition and multiplication, so interpret them by the corresponding arithmetic operations;
- \(f_3\) peels a successor, so interpret it by a truncated predecessor;
- \(f_4\) produces a rapidly growing value (factorial-like), which supplies the strict decrease needed for its own dependency pairs.

The resulting arithmetic inequalities are elementary and can be checked by hand for every rule and every DP; no extra rewrite rules, no equational theory, and no semantic side-conditions beyond the interpretation itself are required. The soundness theorem of the DP method then immediately yields termination.

Other standard techniques are possible in principle (a carefully chosen recursive path order with status, or a Knuth–Bendix order with admissible weights), but they demand more trial-and-error with precedences and statuses, and they obscure the transparent arithmetic content already present in the rules. The DP+interpretation argument is therefore the most direct, fully self-contained proof that stays inside the given syntax.
