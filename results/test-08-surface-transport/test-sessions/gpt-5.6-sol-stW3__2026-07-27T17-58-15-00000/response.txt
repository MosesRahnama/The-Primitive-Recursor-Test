**Verdict: yes.** The TRS is terminating on all finite first-order terms. No structural axioms, intended interpretations, guards, or additional rewrite rules are needed.

A standard dependency-pair proof suffices.

### 1. Dependency pairs

Mark the defined symbols by \(F_0,F_2,F_3,F_4\). The dependency pairs are:

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_0(x,f_1(y))&\to F_0(x,y),\\
F_2(f_1(x),y)&\to F_0(f_2(x,y),y),\\
F_2(f_1(x),y)&\to F_2(x,y),\\
F_3(f_1(f_1(x)))&\to F_3(f_1(x)),\\
F_4(f_1(x))&\to F_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(x))),f_1(x)),\\
F_4(f_1(x))&\to F_4(f_3(f_1(x))),\\
F_4(f_1(x))&\to F_3(f_1(x)).
\end{aligned}
\]

The rules

\[
f_0(x,c_0)\to x,\quad
f_2(c_0,x)\to c_0,\quad
f_2(x,c_0)\to c_0,\quad
f_3(f_1(c_0))\to c_0
\]

produce no dependency pairs because their right-hand sides contain no defined-root subterm.

The dependency graph has only the following cyclic components:

- the \(F_0\)-self pair;
- the \(F_2\)-self pair;
- the \(F_3\)-self pair;
- the \(F_4\)-self pair.

All other pairs go strictly downstream:

\[
F_4\longrightarrow F_2,\ F_3,\qquad F_2\longrightarrow F_0,
\]

with no path back.

### 2. The \(F_0,F_2,F_3\) components

Each is discharged by the subterm criterion:

- For
  \[
  F_0(x,f_1(y))\to F_0(x,y),
  \]
  project to the second argument:
  \[
  f_1(y)\mathrel{\triangleright}y.
  \]

- For
  \[
  F_2(f_1(x),y)\to F_2(x,y),
  \]
  project to the first argument:
  \[
  f_1(x)\mathrel{\triangleright}x.
  \]

- For
  \[
  F_3(f_1(f_1(x)))\to F_3(f_1(x)),
  \]
  project to the sole argument:
  \[
  f_1(f_1(x))\mathrel{\triangleright}f_1(x).
  \]

Thus these three cyclic components cannot support infinite dependency chains.

### 3. The \(F_4\) component

The remaining cyclic pair is

\[
F_4(f_1(x))\to F_4(f_3(f_1(x))).
\]

Its usable rules are precisely the two \(f_3\)-rules:

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_3(f_1(f_1(x)))&\to f_1(f_3(f_1(x))),\\
f_3(f_1(c_0))&\to c_0.
\end{aligned}
\]

Use the following interpretation over \(\mathbb N\):

\[
[c_0]=0,\qquad
[f_1](n)=n+1,\qquad
[f_3](n)=\max(n-1,0),\qquad
[F_4](n)=n.
\]

Both usable rules are weakly decreasing—in fact, equal under this interpretation:

\[
\begin{aligned}
[f_3(f_1(f_1(x)))]
 &= \max((x+2)-1,0)=x+1,\\
[f_1(f_3(f_1(x)))]
 &=1+\max((x+1)-1,0)=x+1,
\end{aligned}
\]

and

\[
[f_3(f_1(c_0))]=\max(1-1,0)=0=[c_0].
\]

The \(F_4\) dependency pair is strictly decreasing:

\[
[F_4(f_1(x))]=x+1
>
\max((x+1)-1,0)
=[F_4(f_3(f_1(x)))].
\]

Hence the \(F_4\) component also admits no infinite dependency chain.

### Conclusion

Every cyclic dependency-pair component terminates, while all remaining dependency-pair edges are acyclic. By the dependency-pair theorem, the original TRS is strongly terminating.
