**Yes, the dependency-pair method itself complies.** Dependency pairs, dependency graphs, subterm criteria, and well-founded interpretations are meta-level proof techniques; they do not add object-level axioms or change the rewrite relation.

That said, my original presentation left a technical detail implicit: the \(F_4\) argument invoked the usable-rules processor and interpreted only the symbols relevant to that processor. Although standard, it is cleaner under your strict boundary to avoid that shortcut entirely.

We can instead weakly orient **all eight original rules** with one algebra over \(\mathbb N\):

\[
\begin{aligned}
[c_0]&=0,\\
[f_1](n)&=n+1,\\
[f_0](a,b)&=a+b,\\
[f_2](a,b)&=ab,\\
[f_3](n)&=\max(n-1,0),\\
[f_4](n)&=n!.
\end{aligned}
\]

Every original rule is interpreted equally on both sides:

\[
\begin{aligned}
f_0(x,c_0):&\quad x+0=x,\\
f_0(x,f_1(y)):&\quad x+(y+1)=(x+y)+1,\\
f_2(c_0,x):&\quad 0x=0,\\
f_2(x,c_0):&\quad x0=0,\\
f_2(f_1(x),y):&\quad (x+1)y=xy+y,\\
f_3(f_1(f_1(x))):&\quad \max(x+1,0)=1+\max(x,0)=x+1,\\
f_3(f_1(c_0)):&\quad \max(1-1,0)=0,\\
f_4(f_1(x)):&\quad (x+1)!=(x+1)x!.
\end{aligned}
\]

For the last rule, its right side evaluates as

\[
f_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(x))),f_1(x))
=x!\,(x+1).
\]

Thus every rewrite rule is weakly decreasing under the induced reduction pair.

For the marked dependency symbols, take projections

\[
[F_0](a,b)=b,\qquad
[F_2](a,b)=a,\qquad
[F_3](a)=a,\qquad
[F_4](a)=a.
\]

The sole cyclic dependency pair in each strongly connected component is then strictly decreasing:

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_0(x,f_1(y))&\to F_0(x,y):
&y+1&>y,\\
F_2(f_1(x),y)&\to F_2(x,y):
&x+1&>x,\\
F_3(f_1(f_1(x)))&\to F_3(f_1(x)):
&x+2&>x+1,\\
F_4(f_1(x))&\to F_4(f_3(f_1(x))):
&x+1&>x.
\end{aligned}
\]

The other dependency pairs only connect different components in an acyclic direction.

Therefore the proof can be made without usable-rule assumptions at all: all original rules are weakly oriented, and every cyclic dependency-pair component is strictly decreasing. No rewrite rules, guards, side conditions, structural axioms, or intended meanings have been added. The natural-number interpretation is only an external well-founded proof algebra, which is permitted by the prompt’s allowance of standard termination methods.
