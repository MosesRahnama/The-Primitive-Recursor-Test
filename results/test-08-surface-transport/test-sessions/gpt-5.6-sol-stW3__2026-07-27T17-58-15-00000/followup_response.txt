I chose the **dependency-pair method** because the system combines several features that make a single global simplification order awkward:

1. **Some rules increase ordinary term size.**  
   For example,
   \[
   f_4(f_1(x))\to f_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(x))),f_1(x))
   \]
   substantially enlarges the term, so a basic size measure cannot prove termination.

2. **There is duplication.**  
   In
   \[
   f_2(f_1(x),y)\to f_0(f_2(x,y),y),
   \]
   the variable \(y\) occurs once on the left and twice on the right. This obstructs many straightforward polynomial or symbol-counting arguments.

3. **The \(f_3\) and \(f_4\) rules create conflicting-looking global requirements.**  
   The \(f_3\)-rule moves \(f_1\) outward:
   \[
   f_3(f_1(f_1(x)))\to f_1(f_3(f_1(x))),
   \]
   while the \(f_4\)-rule places an \(f_3\)-term inside the recursive \(f_4\)-call. A simple recursive path ordering would tend to want incompatible precedence choices—roughly \(f_3>f_1\) for the former rule but \(f_1>f_3\) to make the recursive \(f_4\)-argument smaller.

Dependency pairs are suited to exactly this situation because they separate:

- **recursive calls**, which are relevant to possible infinite reductions, from
- **surrounding term construction**, which may increase size without itself causing recursion.

After that separation, the recursive structure becomes simple:

- \(f_0\) recurses on the proper subterm \(y\) of \(f_1(y)\);
- \(f_2\) recurses on the proper subterm \(x\) of \(f_1(x)\);
- \(f_3\) removes one outer \(f_1\) from its recursive argument;
- only the \(f_4\) recursion needs a more tailored measure.

The dependency graph also isolates these into small strongly connected components. That means I did not need to find one global measure orienting every rule strictly. The first three components are handled by the subterm criterion, and the remaining \(f_4\) component is handled by an interpretation under which its usable \(f_3\)-rules are weakly decreasing while the recursive \(f_4\)-call is strictly decreasing.

So the method was chosen because it exposes the genuine recursive descent while tolerating the system’s size growth, duplication, and rearrangement of symbols. It also stays entirely within the given TRS: the interpretation is a proof device, not an added object-level axiom or rewrite rule.
