I chose a decreasing-measure/primitive-recursion argument because the equations expose an immediate well-founded parameter: the third argument.

In the successor case, the function moves exactly from `Suc i` to `i`, independently of `w`, `G`, `memo`, or the value returned by `best_eclass_term`. Thus the measure “third argument” decreases on every recursive call and reaches `0`, whose equation has no recursive call.

This method is preferable here because it:

- follows the defining equations directly;
- covers both equations exactly;
- requires no induction invariant about the generated list;
- requires no assumptions about `best_eclass_term` beyond totality;
- requires no relationship between `i` and `G`;
- avoids strengthening the definition with a bounds guard or side condition.

In other words, structural induction on the natural-number argument and well-founded descent on that argument are essentially the same proof here; I used the measure presentation because it makes the termination obligation—`i < Suc i`—explicit.
