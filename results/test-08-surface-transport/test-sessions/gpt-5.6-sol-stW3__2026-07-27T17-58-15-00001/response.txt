**Verdict: yes.** Termination follows from the presented rules alone. A dependency-pair proof combined with a well-founded interpretation over \(\mathbb N\) establishes termination without adding rules, guards, equations, or object-level assumptions.

Let variables have arbitrary values \(x,y\in\mathbb N\), and interpret the unmarked symbols by

\[
\begin{aligned}
[c_0]&=0,\\
[f_1(t)]&=[t]+1,\\
[f_0(s,t)]&=[s]+[t],\\
[f_2(s,t)]&=([s]+1)[t],\\
[f_3(t)]&=\max([t]-1,0),\\
[f_4(t)]&=F([t]),
\end{aligned}
\]

where \(F:\mathbb N\to\mathbb N\) is defined by

\[
F(0)=0,\qquad F(n+1)=(F(n)+1)(n+1).
\]

All these functions are weakly monotone. In particular, \(F\) is increasing.

### Every rewrite rule is weakly decreasing

Under this interpretation:

1. \(\;f_0(x,c_0)\to x\):
   \[
   x+0=x.
   \]

2. \(\;f_0(x,f_1(y))\to f_1(f_0(x,y))\):
   \[
   x+(y+1)=x+y+1.
   \]

3. \(\;f_2(c_0,x)\to c_0\):
   \[
   (0+1)x=x\ge 0.
   \]

4. \(\;f_2(x,c_0)\to c_0\):
   \[
   (x+1)0=0.
   \]

5. \(\;f_2(f_1(x),y)\to f_0(f_2(x,y),y)\):
   \[
   (x+2)y=(x+1)y+y.
   \]

6. \(\;f_3(f_1(f_1(x)))\to f_1(f_3(f_1(x)))\):
   \[
   \max(x+2-1,0)=x+1
   \]
   and
   \[
   1+\max(x+1-1,0)=x+1.
   \]

7. \(\;f_3(f_1(c_0))\to c_0\):
   \[
   \max(1-1,0)=0.
   \]

8. \(\;f_4(f_1(x))\to f_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(x))),f_1(x))\):
   since \([f_3(f_1(x))]=x\),
   \[
   F(x+1)=(F(x)+1)(x+1),
   \]
   exactly the interpretation of the right-hand side.

Thus rewriting never increases the interpretation.

## Dependency pairs

Write \(F_i\) for the marked version of the defined symbol \(f_i\). The dependency pairs are:

\[
\begin{aligned}
F_0(x,f_1(y))&\to F_0(x,y),\\
F_2(f_1(x),y)&\to F_0(f_2(x,y),y),\\
F_2(f_1(x),y)&\to F_2(x,y),\\
F_3(f_1(f_1(x)))&\to F_3(f_1(x)),\\
F_4(f_1(x))&\to F_2(f_4(f_3(f_1(x))),f_1(x)),\\
F_4(f_1(x))&\to F_4(f_3(f_1(x))),\\
F_4(f_1(x))&\to F_3(f_1(x)).
\end{aligned}
\]

Their dependency graph has four possible cyclic components, one for each marked root \(F_0,F_2,F_3,F_4\). All cross-root pairs move only downward:

\[
F_4\to F_2,F_3,\qquad F_2\to F_0,
\]

with no path back. Therefore only the four self-pairs need strict orientation.

For each component, interpret the relevant marked symbol by projection:

- \(F_0(s,t)\mapsto [t]\):
  \[
  [f_1(y)]=y+1>y.
  \]

- \(F_2(s,t)\mapsto [s]\):
  \[
  [f_1(x)]=x+1>x.
  \]

- \(F_3(t)\mapsto[t]\):
  \[
  [f_1(f_1(x))]=x+2>x+1=[f_1(x)].
  \]

- \(F_4(t)\mapsto[t]\):
  \[
  [f_1(x)]=x+1>x=[f_3(f_1(x))].
  \]

Hence every cyclic dependency pair strictly decreases a natural number, while all intervening ordinary rewriting is weakly decreasing. No infinite dependency-pair chain exists. By the dependency-pair termination theorem, the original TRS is terminating.

This proof uses the natural-number interpretation only as a metatheoretic well-founded measure; it adds no axioms or behavior to the rewrite system itself.
