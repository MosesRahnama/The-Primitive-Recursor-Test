I chose a **syntax-directed well-founded descent argument**, organized by function dependency, because the equations already reveal the relevant decreasing quantities:

- `plus` decreases the number of `S` constructors in its second argument.
- `times` decreases the number in its first argument and calls the already-handled `plus`.
- `p` decreases the number in its argument.
- `fac` uses `p` to replace a positive numeral by its predecessor, then calls already-handled `times`.

This method stays within the requested boundary: it uses only the finite constructor syntax and the displayed rewrite equations. Metalevel induction on the number of `S`s is justified by the syntax of unary numerals; it does not add an object-level induction axiom or a missing defining equation.

I did **not** use raw term size as a measure because several rules increase term size or duplicate material. For example,

```text
times (S x) y → plus (times x y) y
```

is larger than its left-hand side, and

```text
fac (S x) → times (fac (p (S x))) (S x)
```

also expands substantially. So a simplistic “every rewrite makes the term smaller” proof would fail.

The dependency-ordered argument is also useful because `fac` is not visibly primitive recursive as written: its recursive argument is `p (S x)`, not syntactically just `x`. Proving from the `p` equations that

```text
p (S (S^n Z)) →* S^n Z
```

supplies the required strict descent without assuming externally that `p` means predecessor.

Finally, I explicitly considered every equation and the overlaps in `times`, because non-disjoint rules can undermine a strategy-independent termination claim if one only follows a preferred equation. Here, either choice at an overlap still terminates.

One qualification to my earlier wording: “left-to-right” was unnecessary and potentially too restrictive. For numeral inputs, the descent argument is intended to establish absence of infinite rewrite sequences regardless of redex choice, not merely termination of one evaluation strategy. The result remains termination, not numeral-valued totality: `fac Z` is a normal form only because no rule applies to it.
