Yes, the `Step` system is strongly normalizing.  

All rewrite rules are top-level patterns only; there are no congruence (compatibility) clauses, so reduction never descends into subterms. Every reduction either  

* strictly decreases the total number of constructors (rules `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`, `R_eq_refl`), or  
* produces a term that cannot reduce further (`R_rec_succ` yields `app s (recΔ b s n)` with no rule for `app`, and `R_eq_diff` yields `integrate (merge a b)` where `merge a b` is never a `delta`, so `integrate` has no redex).  

Because no reduction creates a new redex that could lead to unbounded growth and because every rule eventually stops after finitely many steps, any reduction sequence is finite. A trivial well-founded measure (e.g., lexicographic order on the “redices available” or simply the number of constructors for the shrinking rules and a step counter for the others) suffices. Thus strong normalization is provable directly from the given syntax and rules without external assumptions.
