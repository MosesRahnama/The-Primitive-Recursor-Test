## Assessment: **Yes**, strong normalization is establishable from the given calculus alone.

### Reasoning

The relation `Step` is defined by eight root-level rules and nothing else (no congruence/contextual closure appears in the inductive definition). It is therefore enough to show that this binary relation on the inductively defined type `Trace` admits no infinite chains. The argument uses only the syntax of `Trace` and the shape of the given rules.

Partition the rules into two classes:

- **Size-decreasing rules**  
  `R_int_delta`, `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, `R_rec_zero`, `R_eq_refl`.  
  In each case the right-hand side is a proper subterm of the left-hand side (or `void`), so the ordinary inductive size of the term strictly decreases.

- **Irreducible-producing rules**  
  `R_rec_succ` and `R_eq_diff`.  
  Their right-hand sides are headed by `app` and by `integrate (merge _ _)` respectively. No left-hand side of any rule is headed by `app`, and the only `integrate`-rule demands a `delta` argument, so neither form is a root redex. Consequently a step of this class can occur at most once and must be the final step of any chain.

Any `Step`-sequence therefore consists of a finite number (bounded by the size of the initial term) of decreasing steps, possibly followed by a single irreducible-producing step, and then ends. Hence every reduction sequence is finite.

(The same conclusion holds if one silently closes the rules under contexts, by a slightly richer lexicographic measure that first counts the non-reproducible constructors `eqW`, `recΔ` and `delta`—none of which ever appear on a right-hand side except for a strictly smaller recursive occurrence—but that extension is unnecessary for the relation as literally defined.)

No extra rewrite rules, side-conditions, guards or external axioms are required.
