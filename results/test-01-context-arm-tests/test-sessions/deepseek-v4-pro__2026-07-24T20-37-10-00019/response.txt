**Assessment:** Yes, strong normalization of the `Step` system **can** be established from the presented calculus alone. No extra axioms, side conditions, or hidden assumptions are required.

**Reasoning:**
The rewrite system is purely “top-level” — there are no congruence rules allowing reduction inside subterms. Every `Step` replaces the entire term by a new one that matches one of the eight given patterns. All reduction sequences therefore consist of root rewrites only.

One can prove SN directly by defining a suitable well-founded measure on `Trace` that strictly decreases with each step. For example:

* Assign a weight to each term that bounds the maximum possible number of remaining steps.  
* Every rule either  
  – strictly decreases the total number of constructors (e.g., `merge` rules, `integrate (delta t) → void`, `recΔ … void → …`, `eqW a a → void`), or  
  – increases the size but moves to a “dead” term that cannot reduce further (rule 6 yields an `app` at the root, which is irreducible; rule 8 increases size by 1 but can be followed by at most one more step via rule 1).  

Because the reduction graph is finitely branching and contains no cycles or infinite expansions, a lexicographic measure (e.g., the number of “active” top-level constructors that can still fire, combined with term size) suffices to prove that every term is strongly normalizing under `Step`.
