Yes. The `Step` relation contains only top-level pattern‑match rules; there are no congruence rules that allow rewriting under constructors. Consequently, every term that can take a step must exactly match one of the eight left‑hand sides, and the reduction applies only at the root.

A very direct induction on term structure, or a simple well‑founded lexicographic measure, suffices:

* The reductions `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel` and `R_rec_zero` replace a term by a direct subterm, so the number of constructors strictly decreases.
* `R_int_delta` and `R_eq_refl` reduce to the constant `void`, which is also smaller.
* The two rules that can increase the measure, `R_rec_succ` and `R_eq_diff`, produce terms whose topmost constructor is `app` or `integrate`. No subsequent rule applies to `app` or to `integrate` applied to a `merge` term, so these terms are irreducible normal forms. Hence any sequence that uses one of these rules can take at most one more step (if the term before the step was reducible) and then terminates.
* No cycle is possible because every “shrinking” step consumes a constructor or a nesting layer, and the purely syntactic nature of the inductive `Trace` type guarantees that such decreases cannot continue indefinitely.

Therefore strong normalization holds without any extra axioms, side conditions, or hidden assumptions beyond the given syntax and rewrite rules.
