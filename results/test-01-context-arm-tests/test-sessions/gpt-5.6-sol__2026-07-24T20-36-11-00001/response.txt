Yes. For the `Step` relation exactly as defined, strong normalization follows purely from the syntax and rules.

Any rule whose right-hand side can increase term size produces a root-normal term:

- `R_rec_succ` produces an `app`, for which no root `Step` rule exists.
- `R_eq_diff` produces `integrate (merge a b)`, which cannot match `integrate (delta t)`.
- The remaining such rules produce `void`, also normal.

Thus, any nonterminal reduction chain can only continue through `merge` projection/cancellation rules or `R_rec_zero`. Each of those replaces the term by a strict syntactic subterm, strictly decreasing constructor count. An infinite chain would therefore require an infinite descending sequence of natural-number term sizes, which is impossible.

Rule overlaps, such as `eqW a a` matching both equality rules, do not change this: both outcomes are root-normal. No additional axioms, guards, or object-level assumptions are needed.
