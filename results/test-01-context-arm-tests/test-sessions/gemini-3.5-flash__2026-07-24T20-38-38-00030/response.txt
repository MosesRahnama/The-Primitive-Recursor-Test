### Reasoning

1. **Absence of Congruence Rules:** 
   The `Step` relation is defined strictly as a root-reduction relation. There are no congruence rules (rules of the form `Step t t' → Step (delta t) (delta t')`, etc.) that allow reduction inside subterms. Reductions can only occur at the top level (the root) of a `Trace` term.

2. **Analysis of Redex Targets:**
   Since there are no congruence rules, we can analyze the contractum (the right-hand side) of each of the 8 constructors of `Step` to determine if they can be reduced further:
   * **Immediate Termination (Normal Forms):**
     * `R_int_delta` and `R_eq_refl` reduce to `void`. `void` matches no left-hand sides (LHS) of the `Step` relation, so it is a normal form.
     * `R_rec_succ` reduces to `app s (recΔ b s n)`. There are no rules in `Step` with `app` as the top-level constructor. Thus, this term is a normal form.
     * `R_eq_diff` reduces to `integrate (merge a b)`. The only rule matching `integrate` is `R_int_delta`, which requires its argument to be `delta t`. Since the argument here is `merge a b` (which is not `delta`), this term cannot step and is a normal form.
   * **Size Reduction (Strict Subterms):**
     * `R_merge_void_left`, `R_merge_void_right`, `R_merge_cancel`, and `R_rec_zero` all reduce a term $t$ to a **strict subterm** of $t$ (either $t$ or $b$).

3. **Well-Foundedness:**
   For any term $t$, a transition $t \xrightarrow{\text{Step}} t'$ either:
   1. Results in a normal form $t'$ (where no further steps are possible).
   2. Results in a strict subterm $t'$ of $t$. 

   By structural induction on `Trace` (or by using the size of the term as a termination measure), any chain of steps must eventually hit a normal form in a finite number of transitions (bounded by the depth of the initial term). Thus, the relation `Step` is well-founded (strongly normalizing).
