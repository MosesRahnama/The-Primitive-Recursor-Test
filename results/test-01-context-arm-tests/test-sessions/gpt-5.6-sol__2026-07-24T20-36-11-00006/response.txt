**Yes.** Strong normalization follows from the presented syntax and rules alone.

A standard recursive/lexicographic path ordering can orient every rule strictly downward. For example, choose constructor precedence satisfying:

- `eqW > integrate` and `eqW > merge`,
- `recΔ > app`,
- `integrate > void`.

Then:

- merge elimination, `recΔ ... void`, and similar rules reduce to proper subterms;
- `recΔ b s (delta n) → app s (recΔ b s n)` decreases the recursive argument from `delta n` to `n`, despite duplicating `s`;
- `eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)` decreases by the chosen head-symbol precedence;
- the remaining rules are immediate.

Thus no infinite reduction sequence exists. This argument also works for the contextual closure of these rules; for `Step` exactly as defined, which only rewrites at the root, normalization is even more direct. The overlap between `R_eq_refl` and `R_eq_diff` when both arguments coincide introduces nondeterminism but no cycle. No external structural or object-level assumptions are needed.
