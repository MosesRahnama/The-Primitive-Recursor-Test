**Yes**, strong normalization of the `Step` system can be established from the presented calculus alone. 

### Reasoning

1. **Absence of Congruence (Context) Rules:** 
   The inductive definition of `Step` consists entirely of top-level reduction axioms. It lacks any congruence rules (such as `Step a b → Step (delta a) (delta b)`). This means reductions can only occur at the root of a term.

2. **Finiteness of Root Reductions:**
   Every reduction step $t \to t'$ defined by the rules falls into one of two cases:
   - **Immediate Termination:** The right-hand side $t'$ is a normal form (it cannot undergo any root reductions). 
     - `R_int_delta` reduces to `void`.
     - `R_rec_succ` reduces to `app s (recΔ b s n)`. Since there are no rewrite rules starting with the `app` constructor on the left-hand side, this term is in normal form.
     - `R_eq_refl` reduces to `void`.
     - `R_eq_diff` reduces to `integrate (merge a b)`. Since the only rule for `integrate` requires a `delta` argument (`R_int_delta`), this is a normal form.
   - **Subterm Reduction:** The right-hand side $t'$ is a strict subterm of $t$.
     - `R_merge_void_left` / `R_merge_void_right` / `R_merge_cancel` reduce to a subterm $t$.
     - `R_rec_zero` reduces to the subterm $b$.

Because any sequence of steps must either terminate immediately (length 1) or transition to a strictly smaller subterm, the maximum reduction depth of any term $t$ is bounded by its structural size. Thus, every reduction sequence is finite, and strong normalization holds.
