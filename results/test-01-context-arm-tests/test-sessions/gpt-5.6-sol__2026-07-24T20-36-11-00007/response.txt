Yes. The presented `Step` relation is strongly normalizing without additional axioms or side conditions.

A direct ranking can be defined because `Step` only rewrites at the root:

- `merge` rules return a proper argument.
- `recΔ b s void` returns the subterm `b`.
- `recΔ b s (delta n)` produces an `app`, for which no rule exists.
- `eqW` produces either `void` or `integrate (merge a b)`; neither result has an applicable root rule.
- `integrate (delta t)` reduces to `void`.

Thus one can assign each term a natural-number “remaining root-reduction height” that strictly decreases with every `Step`. The syntax-increasing right-hand sides of `R_rec_succ` and `R_eq_diff` do not threaten termination because their outer constructors are irreducible under the displayed relation. Rule overlap, such as applying `R_eq_diff` when both arguments are equal, also creates no cycle.

Equivalently, the rules can be oriented by a standard well-founded recursive path ordering. No structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond the given syntax and rules are needed.
