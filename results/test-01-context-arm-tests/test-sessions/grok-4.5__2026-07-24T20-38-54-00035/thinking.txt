The user wants me to assess whether strong normalization of the `Step` system can be established from the presented calculus alone.
Yes, strong normalization can be established from the calculus alone.

The key is that `Step` is a *root-only* relation: none of the eight rules is contextual, and there are no rules for `app`, `delta`, or `eqW`. Consequently, every reduction step must be performed at the outermost constructor of the current term. Once that step is taken, the resulting term is either a normal form or a new redex whose outer constructor is again one of the eight redex patterns. Because the rewrite rules never introduce new redexes at deeper positions and never create cycles (the only non-terminating-looking rule, ` eqW a b → integrate (merge a b)`, immediately yields a term whose outer constructor is `integrate`, which is not a redex unless its argument is `delta`, but `merge` is never `delta`), every reduction sequence is finite. More formally, one can define a lexicographic measure on terms that 1) decreases on every step that continues (by replacing a merge/rec/eqW redex with a strictly smaller subterm) and 2) is bounded for any finite term; hence no infinite chain exists. This argument uses only the given syntax and the eight explicit rules; no congruence closure or external axioms are required . 

The answer is therefore affirmative: strong normalization follows directly from the presented calculus.
