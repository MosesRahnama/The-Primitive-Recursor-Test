All five methods prove termination and remain within the benchmark boundary.

### (A) LPO with \(F>G>S>Z\)

**Succeeds:** Yes.

- \(F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} x\) by the subterm property.
- For the recursive rule, since \(F>G\), it is enough to show
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n))>y
  \quad\text{and}\quad
  F(x,y,S(n))>F(x,y,n).
  \]
  The first follows by the subterm property. The second follows lexicographically because \(S(n)>n\).

**Within boundary:** Yes. This is a purely syntactic ordering argument using only the displayed terms and rules.

---

### (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation

**Succeeds:** Yes.

For the base rule:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)]=x+(y+1)=x+y+1>x.
\]

For the recursive rule:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=x+(y+1)(n+2),
\]
while
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
=y+x+(y+1)(n+1).
\]
The left side exceeds the right side by exactly \(1\).

The interpretations are strictly monotone in every argument over \(\mathbb N\), so they induce a valid reduction order.

**Within boundary:** Yes. This is a standard semantic interpretation of the given signature, not an assumption that the object-level terms themselves satisfy additional axioms.

---

### (C) MPO with \(F>G>S>Z\)

**Succeeds:** Yes.

- The first rule is oriented by the subterm property.
- For the second rule, \(F>G\), and the left side dominates both arguments of \(G\).
- In particular,
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n))>_{\mathrm{MPO}}F(x,y,n)
  \]
  because the argument multiset
  \[
  \{x,y,S(n)\}
  \]
  exceeds
  \[
  \{x,y,n\},
  \]
  using \(S(n)>n\).

**Within boundary:** Yes. It is entirely syntactic.

---

### (D) Dependency pairs with third-argument projection

**Succeeds:** Yes.

The only recursive dependency pair is
\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n))\to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]
Projecting \(F^\#\) to its third argument gives
\[
S(n)\mathrel{\triangleright}n,
\]
a strict subterm decrease. Thus the dependency-pair problem has no infinite chain.

**Within boundary:** Yes. The argument uses only the dependency pair generated from the rule and the syntactic subterm relation; it does not assume an external induction principle for object-level naturals.

---

### (E) Exponential interpretation

**Succeeds:** Yes.

Let \(b=x+y+2\), so \(b\ge 2\).

For the base rule:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)]=b>x.
\]

For the recursive rule:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=b^{n+2},
\]
whereas
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+b^{n+1}.
\]
Their difference is
\[
b^{n+2}-\bigl(y+b^{n+1}\bigr)
=b^{n+1}(b-1)-y>0,
\]
because \(b-1=x+y+1>y\).

The interpretation is strictly monotone in all arguments since the base is always at least \(2\).

**Within boundary:** Yes. As with (B), this is a valid interpretation of the given symbols over a well-founded domain, not an imported object-level axiom.
