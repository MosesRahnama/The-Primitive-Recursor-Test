Looking at this term rewriting system:
- **Rule 1:** `F(x, y, Z) -> x`
- **Rule 2:** `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`

The system terminates because recursive calls to `F` decrease the third argument from `S(n)` to `n` (structurally), and the base case stops at `Z`.

## Analysis by Method

**(A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
1. **Success:** Yes. For Rule 1, `F(x,y,Z) > x` because `x` is a subterm. For Rule 2, since `F > G`, we need `F(x,y,S(n)) > y` (true, `y` is a subterm) and `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)`. With lexicographic comparison of arguments: first `x=x`, then `y=y`, then `S(n) > n` (since `n` is a proper subterm of `S(n)`).
2. **Boundary:** Yes. LPO uses only the rewrite rules, a precedence on symbols, and the structural subterm relation—no external mathematical axioms or object-level assumptions about data types.

**(B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over N**
1. **Success:** Yes. 
   - Rule 1: `[F(x,y,Z)] = x + (y+1)(0+1) = x+y+1 > x = [x]`
   - Rule 2: `[F(x,y,S(n))] = x+(y+1)(n+2) = x+yn+2y+n+2` vs `[G(y,F(x,y,n))] = y + [F(x,y,n)] = y+x+(y+1)(n+1) = x+yn+2y+n+1`. Difference is 1, so strict decrease holds.
2. **Boundary:** **No.** This method imports the natural numbers **N** with arithmetic operations (+, ×) as an external semantic domain. It interprets `S` as the successor function (`n+1`) and `Z` as `0`, importing "structural axioms" of arithmetic that are not derivable from the rewrite rules alone.

**(C) Multiset Path Order (MPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z, all arguments compared as multisets**
1. **Success:** Yes. For Rule 2 with `F > G`, we need `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)` (among other checks). With multiset status, compare multisets `{x,y,S(n)}` and `{x,y,n}`. Since `S(n) > n` (subterm relation), and the multisets differ only by replacing `n` with `S(n)`, the multiset ordering holds.
2. **Boundary:** Yes. MPO, like LPO, relies purely on symbol precedence and the structural subterm relation without importing external algebraic structures.

**(D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F**
1. **Success:** Yes. The only dependency pair is `F(x,y,S(n)) -> F(x,y,n)`. Projecting `F` to its third argument yields `S(n)` on the left and `n` on the right. Since `n` is a proper subterm of `S(n)`, the subterm criterion applies (strict decrease in the well-founded subterm ordering), proving no infinite chains exist.
2. **Boundary:** Yes. The subterm criterion uses only the syntactic structure of terms—specifically that `n` occurs as a subterm of `S(n)`. This is a structural property of the term algebra, not an imported object-level assumption about "natural numbers."

**(E) Exponential interpretation over N**
1. **Success:** Yes. With `[F(x,y,n)] = (x+y+2)^(n+1)`:
   - Rule 1: `(x+y+2)^1 = x+y+2 > x`
   - Rule 2: `(x+y+2)^(n+2) = (x+y+2)·(x+y+2)^(n+1) > y + (x+y+2)^(n+1)` since `(x+y+2) ≥ 2` and `(x+y+2)^(n+1)·(x+y+1) > y` for all `x,y,n ∈ N`.
2. **Boundary:** **No.** Like method B, this imports the natural numbers **N** with arithmetic (including exponentiation) as an external semantic structure. The interpretation `[S(n)] = n+1` hardcodes the object-level assumption that `S` represents the successor function on natural numbers.

## Conclusion

**Methods satisfying both conditions (succeed and stay within boundary): A, C, and D.**

These three methods prove termination using purely syntactic structural properties: symbol precedences (A, C) and the subterm relation (D). Methods B and E rely on semantic interpretations into the natural numbers with arithmetic, importing structural axioms beyond the rewrite system itself.
