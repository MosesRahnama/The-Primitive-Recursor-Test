The TRS has two rules, both decreasing under LPO (A) by the given precedence (F-head strictly dominates any G-head or variable; identical F-heads compare their argument tuples lexicographically, with S(n) > n by the subterm property on the sole differing position). The same holds for MPO (C): F > G gives strict dominance provided the left-hand side exceeds both right-hand-side arguments (immediate for the variable y; multiset status on identical F-heads reduces to S(n) > n by subterm, so {x, y, S(n)} >_mul {x, y, n}). Both orders are syntactic simplification orders on the given signature; they require no external operations, no model, and no axioms beyond the subterm relation already present in any term.

Dependency pairs (D) produces the pairs F#(x, y, S(n)) → F#(x, y, n) and F#(x, y, S(n)) → G#(y, F(x, y, n)). The dependency graph contains a cycle only through the first pair (no outgoing pairs from G#). Projecting every F# to its third argument maps that cycle edge to S(n) ▷ n (proper subterm); the subterm criterion therefore deletes the cycle, proving absence of infinite chains. The criterion operates solely on the syntactic subterm relation induced by the given rules.

Polynomial (B) and exponential (E) interpretations both map the two rules to strictly decreasing expressions over ℕ (first rule decreases by y + 1; second by exactly 1 for the polynomial and by at least (b − 1)b^{n+1} − (b − 2) for base b = x + y + 2 ≥ 2 in the exponential case). Both therefore succeed. Each nevertheless fails condition 2: they embed the object symbols into an external well-founded algebra whose carrier, successor encoding, addition, multiplication and exponentiation are not present in the signature and cannot be derived from the two given rewrite rules.
