All five methods prove termination and remain within the benchmark boundary.

| Method | Proves termination? | Within boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **A. LPO** | Yes | Yes | \(F(x,y,Z) > x\) by the subterm property. For the recursive rule, \(F>G\), the left side dominates \(y\), and \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) lexicographically because \(S(n)>n\). |
| **B. Polynomial interpretation** | Yes | Yes | The first rule decreases by \(y+1\). For the second rule, the interpreted left and right sides are \(x+(y+1)(n+2)\) and \(y+x+(y+1)(n+1)\), respectively, so the decrease is exactly \(1\). The interpretations are strictly monotone over \(\mathbb N\). |
| **C. MPO** | Yes | Yes | For equal \(F\)-roots, the argument multiset \(\{x,y,S(n)\}\) exceeds \(\{x,y,n\}\), since \(S(n)>n\). With \(F>G\), the left side also dominates both arguments of the outer \(G\). |
| **D. Dependency pairs** | Yes | Yes | The only dependency pair is \(F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n)\). Projecting to the third argument gives \(S(n)\mathrel{\triangleright}n\), a strict proper-subterm decrease. |
| **E. Exponential interpretation** | Yes | Yes | Let \(b=x+y+2\ge2\). The recursive rule requires \(b^{n+2}>y+b^{n+1}\), and the difference is \(b^{n+1}(b-1)-y>0\). The first rule also decreases, and all interpretations are strictly monotone. |

The precedences, projections, and interpretations are meta-level termination tools; they do not assume that object-level terms satisfy datatype exhaustiveness, induction axioms, or any additional equations.

**Methods satisfying both conditions: A, B, C, D, and E.**
