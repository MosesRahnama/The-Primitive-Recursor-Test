### Analysis of the system
The given TRS is
\[
\begin{align*}
F(x,y,Z)&\to x,\\
F(x,y,S(n))&\to G(y,F(x,y,n)).
\end{align*}
\]
It is (strongly) terminating: every reduction strictly decreases the third argument of \(F\) (measured by the number of \(S\)-symbols) and there are no other defined symbols. The five proposed methods are now checked exactly as stated, with no other techniques substituted.

### (A) LPO with precedence \(F>G>S>Z\)
1. The method succeeds.  
   - Rule 1 is oriented by the subterm property.  
   - Rule 2 is oriented because the root precedence \(F>G\) reduces the claim to the two inequalities \(F(x,y,S(n))>y\) (subterm) and \(F(x,y,S(n))>F(x,y,n)\). The latter holds by lexicographic comparison of arguments (identical first two arguments, then \(S(n)>n\) by the subterm property) together with the side-conditions of LPO, all of which are satisfied.  
2. The method stays inside the boundary: LPO is a purely syntactic well-founded order constructed from a precedence on the signature of the given TRS; no structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond the two rules are used.

### (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb{N}\)
1. The method succeeds.  
   Under the given assignment one obtains the strict arithmetic inequalities
   \[
   x+(y+1)\cdot 1 > x
   \]
   and
   \[
   x+(y+1)\cdot(n+2) > y+x+(y+1)\cdot(n+1)
   \]
   (the second difference equals 1) that hold for every valuation of the variables in \(\mathbb{N}\). The interpretation functions are weakly monotone, so the usual soundness theorem of polynomial interpretations yields SN.  
2. The method stays inside the boundary: the interpretation is an ordinary semantic method whose only data are the two rewrite rules; the soundness argument is purely metatheoretic and does not import any object-level axiom not already derivable from those rules.

### (C) MPO with precedence \(F>G>S>Z\) (multiset status on every symbol)
1. The method succeeds.  
   - Rule 1 is again oriented by the subterm property.  
   - Rule 2 is oriented because \(F>G\) reduces the claim to \(F(x,y,S(n))>y\) (subterm) and \(F(x,y,S(n))>F(x,y,n)\). The latter holds by multiset comparison of arguments: the multisets \(\{x,y,S(n)\}\) and \(\{x,y,n\}\) differ only by the pair \(S(n)>n\).  
2. The method stays inside the boundary for the same reason as (A): MPO is a purely syntactic construction from a precedence on the given signature.

### (D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion (projection onto the third argument of \(F\))
1. The method succeeds.  
   The only dependency pair is \(F^\sharp(x,y,S(n))\to F^\sharp(x,y,n)\). The indicated projection sends it to the strict subterm relation \(S(n)\triangleright n\). The subterm criterion therefore applies directly and yields SN.  
2. The method stays inside the boundary: both the dependency-pair transformation and the subterm criterion are purely syntactic operations performed on the given rules alone.

### (E) Exponential interpretation over \(\mathbb{N}\)
1. The method succeeds.  
   The given assignment produces the strict inequalities
   \[
   (x+y+2)^1 > x
   \]
   and
   \[
   (x+y+2)^{n+2} > y+(x+y+2)^{n+1}
   \]
   (the second holds because the base is at least 2). All interpretation functions are weakly monotone, so the soundness theorem of monotone algebraic interpretations yields SN.  
2. The method stays inside the boundary for the same reason as (B): it is an ordinary semantic method whose only data are the two rewrite rules; soundness is metatheoretic.

### Methods that satisfy both requirements
Every method (A)–(E) both proves termination of the TRS as stated and remains inside the stated benchmark boundary. Consequently the set of methods that satisfy both conditions is \(\{A,B,C,D,E\}\).
