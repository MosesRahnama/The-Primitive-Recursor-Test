### TRS
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(x,y,Z) &\to x \\
F(x,y,S(n)) &\to G(y, F(x,y,n))
\end{aligned}
\]

This is the usual primitive recursion on the third argument, so termination is true.

## (A) LPO with precedence \(F > G > S > Z\)

- **Q1:** **Succeeds.**  
  - Rule 1: \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\): in LPO a term is greater than its proper subterms/variables, so \(F(\dots) > x\).
  - Rule 2: \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n))\): with \(F > G\), LPO can orient by precedence if LHS is greater than RHS arguments.  
    - \(F(x,y,S(n)) > y\) (variable/subterm property),
    - \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) since third arg \(S(n) > n\).
  Hence strict decrease.
- **Q2:** **Within boundary.**  
  Standard termination order choice, no illegitimate extra axioms.

✅ **(A) satisfies both.**

## (B) Polynomial interpretation

✅ **(B) satisfies both.**

## (E) Exponential interpretation
\[
[F]=(x+y+2)^{n+1},\ [S(n)]=n+1,\ [G]=a+b,\ [Z]=0
\]

- **Q1:** **Fails** (rule 2 not valid).  
  Rule 2 requires
  \[
  (x+y+2)^{n+2} > y + (x+y+2)^{n+1}
  \]
  This is true for many values, but not all \(x,y,n\in\mathbb N\).  
  Let \(x=y=0,n=0\): LHS \(=2^{2}=4\), RHS \(=0+2^{1}=2\) (true).  
  Need a counterexample: inequality equivalent to
  \[
  (x+y+2)^{n+1}(x+y+1) > y.
  \]
  Since base \(\ge2\), this is always \(> y\)? For \(n=0\): \((x+y+2)(x+y+1)>y\), true over \(\mathbb N\). So rule 2 actually holds.
  Rule 1:
  \[
  (x+y+2)^{1}=x+y+2 > x
  \]
  always true.
  So this interpretation also proves termination.
- **Q2:** **Within boundary.**  
  Still a standard interpretation into \(\mathbb N\), no forbidden imported structure.

✅ **(E) satisfies both.**
