## Method A: LPO with precedence F > G > S > Z

**Rule 1:** F(x, y, Z) → x. The RHS x is a proper subterm of the LHS. LPO satisfies subterm property. ✓

**Rule 2:** F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n)). Since F > G, we need F(x,y,S(n)) to dominate each argument of G:
- y: it's a subterm of LHS (argument). ✓
- F(x, y, n): compare F(x,y,S(n)) vs F(x,y,n). Same head F, lexicographic on arguments: first two equal, third S(n) vs n — S(n) > n by subterm. ✓ (Also need LHS > subterms of F(x,y,n): x, y, n all subterms. ✓)

**Succeeds:** Yes.
**Boundary:** LPO is a standard syntactic method derived entirely from the rules and a precedence on the given signature. No imported structural axioms. Within boundary.

## Method B: Polynomial interpretation
[F(x,y,n)] = x + (y+1)(n+1), [S(n)] = n+1, [G(a,b)] = a+b, [Z]=0

**Rule 1:** [F(x,y,Z)] = x + (y+1)(0+1) = x + y + 1 > x. ✓

**Rule 2:**

- Difference: LHS − RHS = (y+1)(n+2) − y − (y+1)(n+1) = (y+1)(n+2 − n−1) − y = (y+1) − y = 1 > 0. ✓

**Succeeds:** Yes.
**Boundary:** Standard polynomial interpretation over N derived from the rules. Within boundary.

## Method C: MPO with precedence F > G > S > Z, multiset comparison

**Rule 1:** F(x, y, Z) → x, subterm. ✓

**Rule 2:** F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n)). F > G, so need LHS to dominate arguments {y, F(x,y,n)}.
- y ✓ (subterm)
- F(x, y, n): same head as LHS is not relevant here since we compare F(x,y,S(n)) with the argument F(x,y,n). Under MPO, comparing two terms with equal head F uses **multiset** comparison of arguments: {x, y, S(n)} vs {x, y, n}. Remove equal x, y: {S(n)} vs {n}, and S(n) > n. ✓

**Succeeds:** Yes.
**Boundary:** MPO is syntactic, derived from rules plus precedence. Within boundary.

## Method D: Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to third arg of F

## Method E: Exponential interpretation
[F(x,y,n)] = (x+y+2)^(n+1), [S(n)]=n+1, [G(a,b)]=a+b, [Z]=0

**Rule 1:** [F(x,y,Z)] = (x+y+2)^1 = x+y+2 > x. ✓

**Rule 2:**

- Let b = x+y+2 ≥ 2. LHS − RHS = b^(n+2) − y − b^(n+1) = b^(n+1)(b−1) − y.
- Since b ≥ 2, b^(n+1)(b−1) ≥ b^(n+1) ≥ b = x+y+2 > y. So difference > 0. ✓

**Succeeds:** Yes.
