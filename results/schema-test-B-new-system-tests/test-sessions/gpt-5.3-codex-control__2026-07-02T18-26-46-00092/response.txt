Here’s the assessment for each method, sticking strictly to what’s listed.

### TRS
- \(F(x,y,Z) \to x\)
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y, F(x,y,n))\)

This is the usual primitive-recursive pattern on the 3rd argument, so termination is true.

---

### (A) LPO with precedence \(F > G > S > Z\)

1. **Succeeds?** **No.**  
   The critical rule is
   \[
   F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)).
   \]
   Under LPO, to show \(F(\dots) > G(\dots)\), we need \(F(\dots)\) to be \(>\) each argument of \(G\), in particular \(>\ F(x,y,n)\).  
   But \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) fails lexicographically because first args are equal (\(x\) vs \(x\)), and LPO’s strict variable/subterm conditions do not give a strict decrease there.

2. **Within boundary?** **Yes.**  
   It is a standard syntactic termination method applied directly to the given TRS.

---

### (B) Polynomial interpretation
\[
[F(x,y,n)] = x + (y+1)(n+1),\ [S(n)]=n+1,\ [G(a,b)] = a+b,\ [Z]=0
\]

1. **Succeeds?** **No.**  
   Check second rule:
   - LHS: \(x + (y+1)((n+1)+1)=x+(y+1)(n+2)\)
   - RHS: \(y + \bigl(x+(y+1)(n+1)\bigr)=x+y+(y+1)(n+1)\)

   Difference:
   \[
   \text{LHS} - \text{RHS} = 1.
   \]
   So it is strictly decreasing for all valuations.  
   But first rule:
   - \(F(x,y,Z)\): \(x+(y+1)(0+1)=x+y+1\)
   - RHS \(x\)

   Also strictly decreasing.  
   So actually this interpretation **does** orient both rules strictly.

   Therefore **Succeeds: Yes.**

2. **Within boundary?** **Yes.**  
   Standard interpretation over \(\mathbb N\), no extra object-level axioms.

---

### (C) MPO with precedence \(F > G > S > Z\), multiset status

1. **Succeeds?** **No.**  
   Same obstruction as LPO-style simplification orders: for
   \[
   F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)),
   \]
   since \(F>G\), need \(F(x,y,S(n))\) to dominate arguments \(y\) and \(F(x,y,n)\). The \(F(x,y,n)\) part cannot be made strictly smaller under MPO here (no strict decrease in head-symbol/argument multiset sufficient to prove \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) in this setup).

2. **Within boundary?** **Yes.**  
   Standard reduction order method on the TRS itself.

---

### (D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion (project 3rd argument of \(F\))

1. **Succeeds?** **Yes.**  
   Dependency pair from second rule:
   \[
   F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n).
   \]
   Projecting to 3rd arg gives \(S(n) \triangleright n\), a strict subterm decrease, so DP problem is finite/terminating.

2. **Within boundary?** **Yes.**  
   This is a standard termination technique; no imported structural axioms beyond ordinary meta-theory.

---

### (E) Exponential interpretation
\[
[F(x,y,n)] = (x+y+2)^{n+1},\ [S(n)]=n+1,\ [G(a,b)]=a+b,\ [Z]=0
\]

1. **Succeeds?** **Yes.**  
   - First rule: \((x+y+2)^{0+1}=x+y+2 > x\).
   - Second rule:
     \[
     (x+y+2)^{(n+1)+1}=(x+y+2)^{n+2}
     \]
     vs
     \[
     y + (x+y+2)^{n+1}.
     \]
     Since base \(x+y+2 \ge 2\), we have
     \[
     (x+y+2)^{n+2} = (x+y+2)\cdot (x+y+2)^{n+1}
     > (x+y+2)^{n+1} + y
     \]
     for all \(x,y,n\in\mathbb N\). So strict decrease holds.

2. **Within boundary?** **Yes.**  
   Still a direct interpretation-based proof over \(\mathbb N\); no extra structural imports.
