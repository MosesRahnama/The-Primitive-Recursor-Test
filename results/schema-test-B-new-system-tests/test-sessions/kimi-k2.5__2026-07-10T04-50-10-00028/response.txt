**Summary of the analysis**

The rewrite system consists of two rules:  
1. `F(x, y, Z) → x`  
2. `F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))`  

All five methods are able to orient the rules and prove termination, because the third argument of `F` is structurally smaller in the recursive call (`n` is a proper subterm of `S(n)`).  

The critical distinction is the **benchmark boundary**: “*can strong normalization be established from the presented rewrite system alone, without importing structural axioms or object‑level assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given rules?*”  

*   **Syntactic methods** (LPO, MPO, and the subterm criterion) work entirely within the free term algebra: they compare terms using the subterm relation, precedence, or multiset extensions. They do not assign any external semantics (such as natural numbers) to the variables or function symbols.  
*   **Semantic methods** (polynomial and exponential interpretations) map the term algebra into the natural numbers **ℕ**. This imports the structure of ℕ (arithmetic, well‑foundedness of `>` on ℕ) and treats variables as ranging over ℕ. Such an object‑level assumption about the “meaning” of the symbols is not derivable from the rewrite rules themselves, which treat `S` and `Z` as uninterpreted constructors and variables as ranging over arbitrary terms.

Therefore:

| Method | Proves termination? | Stays within boundary? |
|--------|--------------------|------------------------|
| **(A) LPO** | **Yes** – `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)` follows from the lexicographic extension and the subterm property `S(n) > n`. | **Yes** – purely syntactic, uses only precedence and the subterm relation. |
| **(B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation** | **Yes** – `[F(x,y,Z)] = x+y+1 > x` and `[F(x,y,S(n))] = x+(y+1)(n+2) > y + x+(y+1)(n+1) = [G(y,F(x,y,n))]`. | **No** – imports the natural numbers ℕ and interprets variables as natural numbers, which is an object‑level assumption not present in the rules. |
| **(C) MPO** | **Yes** – multiset comparison of the arguments of `F` reduces to `{S(n)} >_{mul} {n}`, which holds because `S(n) ▷ n`. | **Yes** – purely syntactic, uses multiset extension of the subterm relation. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion** | **Yes** – the only dependency pair is `F♯(x,y,S(n)) → F♯(x,y,n)`; projecting to the third argument gives `S(n) ▷ n`, satisfying the subterm criterion. | **Yes** – the subterm criterion is a structural property of the term algebra; no external semantics are invoked. |
| **(E) Exponential interpretation** | **Yes** – `[F(x,y,Z)] = x+y+2 > x` and `[F(x,y,S(n))] = (x+y+2)^{n+2} > y + (x+y+2)^{n+1} = [G(y,F(x,y,n))]`. | **No** – like (B), it relies on an embedding into ℕ with exponentiation, importing arithmetic structure that is not derivable from the given rules. |

**Methods that satisfy both conditions:** **(A), (C), and (D).**
