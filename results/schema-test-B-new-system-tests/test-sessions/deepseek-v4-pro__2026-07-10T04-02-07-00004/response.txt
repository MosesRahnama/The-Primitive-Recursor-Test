**Analysis of each method**

**System:**
F(x, y, Z) → x
F(x, y, S(n)) → G(y, F(x, y, n))

All methods below are evaluated exactly as stated.

---

**(A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
1. **Succeeds?** Yes.  
   - Rule 1: F(x,y,Z) >_lpo x by subterm.  
   - Rule 2: F > G requires F(x,y,S(n)) > y (subterm) and F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n). The latter holds because (x,y,S(n)) >_lex (x,y,n) via S(n) > n (subterm).  
2. **Boundary?** Yes. LPO is a purely syntactic order on terms; it uses only a precedence on the given symbols and the subterm relation. No external arithmetic or additional axioms are imported.

---

**(B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over N**
1. **Succeeds?** Yes. All functions are monotonic, and both rules strictly decrease:  
   - Rule 1: x + y + 1 > x.  
   - Rule 2: x + (y+1)(n+2) > x + y + (y+1)(n+1) simplifies to y+1 > y.  
2. **Boundary?** No. This method imports the natural numbers together with addition and multiplication. These operations and their arithmetic properties are not derivable from the given rewrite rules; they are external object‑level assumptions.

---

**(C) Multiset Path Order (MPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z, all arguments as multisets**
1. **Succeeds?** Yes.  
   - Rule 1: subterm.  
   - Rule 2: F > G requires F(x,y,S(n)) > y (subterm) and F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n). The multiset comparison {x, y, S(n)} >_mul {x, y, n} holds because S(n) >_mpo n (subterm).  
2. **Boundary?** Yes. MPO is a syntactic order that operates entirely on the term structure; it does not rely on external arithmetic or axioms.

---

**(D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F**
1. **Succeeds?** Yes. The only dependency pair is F^#(x,y,S(n)) → F^#(x,y,n).  
   Projecting to the third argument gives S(n) and n. Since n is a proper subterm of S(n), the subterm criterion is satisfied and the DP (hence the TRS) terminates.  
2. **Boundary?** Yes. Dependency pairs with the subterm criterion is a syntactic method; it only uses the given rules and the subterm relation. No semantic or arithmetic assumptions are introduced.

---

**(E) Exponential interpretation over N**
1. **Succeeds?** Yes.  
   - Rule 1: (x+y+2)^1 = x+y+2 > x.  
   - Rule 2: (x+y+2)^(n+2) > y + (x+y+2)^(n+1) simplifies to (x+y+1)(x+y+2)^(n+1) > y, which holds for all natural numbers.  
2. **Boundary?** No. This method imports the natural numbers together with addition and exponentiation. These exceed what is derivable from the given rewrite rules and constitute external object‑level assumptions.
