All five methods succeed as ordinary termination proofs. Under the stated narrow benchmark-boundary condition, only (D) stays within the boundary.

| Method | Proves termination? | Within stated boundary? |
|---|---:|---:|
| (A) LPO with supplied precedence | Yes | No |
| (B) Polynomial interpretation | Yes | No |
| (C) MPO with supplied precedence | Yes | No |

## (A) LPO

**Termination proof:** succeeds.

For the first rule,
\[
F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} x
\]
by the subterm property.

For the second rule, since \(F>G\), it is enough to show
\[
F(x,y,S(n)) > y
\quad\text{and}\quad
F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n).
\]
The first holds by subtermhood. The second holds by lexicographic comparison of the two \(F\)-terms: the first two arguments agree and
\[
S(n) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} n.
\]
Thus both rules decrease.

**Boundary:** outside.

The proof depends on an imported global symbol precedence and on the lexicographic status of the path order. Those are not structural facts fixed by the rewrite rules themselves.

---

## (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation

**Termination proof:** succeeds.

For the first rule:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = x+(y+1)(0+1)=x+y+1>x=[x].
\]

For the second rule:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]
 =x+(y+1)(n+2),
\]
while
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
 =y+x+(y+1)(n+1).
\]
Their difference is
\[
x+(y+1)(n+2)-\bigl(y+x+(y+1)(n+1)\bigr)=1.
\]
Hence the left-hand side is strictly greater for every valuation in \(\mathbb N\). The interpretations are monotone, so the interpretation is compatible with rewriting in contexts.

**Boundary:** outside.

The specific numerical meanings assigned to \(F,G,S,Z\), including the nonlinear formula for \(F\), are an externally selected global semantic structure. They are not determined by the rewrite rules.

---

## (C) MPO

**Termination proof:** succeeds.

The first rule decreases by the subterm property:
\[
F(x,y,Z)>_{\mathrm{MPO}}x.
\]

For the second rule, \(F>G\), and the left-hand side dominates both arguments of the right-hand side:
\[
F(x,y,S(n))>y
\]
by subtermhood, and
\[
F(x,y,S(n))>F(x,y,n)
\]
because, under multiset comparison of arguments,
\[
\{x,y,S(n)\}>_{\mathrm{mul}}\{x,y,n\},
\]
using \(S(n)>n\).

Thus the MPO orients both rules strictly.

**Boundary:** outside.

As with (A), the method relies on an imported global precedence and on the chosen multiset argument status. Those structural choices are not supplied by the rules themselves.

---

## (D) Dependency pairs with projection to the third argument of \(F\)

**Termination proof:** succeeds.

The only recursive dependency pair is
\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n))\to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]

Projecting to the third argument gives
\[
S(n)\to n
\]
at the level of the subterm criterion. The projected left side is a proper superterm of the projected right side:
\[
S(n)\triangleright n.
\]
Therefore no infinite dependency-pair chain is possible.

The first rule yields no recursive dependency pair, since its right-hand side is merely the variable \(x\).

**Boundary:** within.

The selected third argument is justified directly by the presented recursive rule: it is the argument in which the recursive call changes from \(S(n)\) to \(n\). The proof uses only the syntactic subterm relation of the given terms and the ordinary soundness of the dependency-pair/subterm-criterion framework. It does not require a separately imposed global precedence, global interpretation, or object-level assumption.

---

## (E) Exponential interpretation

**Termination proof:** succeeds.

Let
\[
a=x+y+2.
\]
Since \(x,y\in\mathbb N\), \(a\ge 2\).

For the first rule:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)]=(x+y+2)^1=x+y+2>x.
\]

For the second rule:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=a^{n+2},
\]
while
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+a^{n+1}.
\]
It suffices to show
\[
a^{n+2}>y+a^{n+1},
\]
equivalently,
\[
a^{n+1}(a-1)>y.
\]
Since \(a\ge 2\) and \(n\ge0\),
\[
a^{n+1}(a-1)\ge a(a-1).
\]
Moreover,
\[
a(a-1)=(x+y+2)(x+y+1)>y.
\]
Hence the second rule strictly decreases as well.

**Boundary:** outside.

This proof relies on an externally chosen global exponential interpretation of the symbols. That interpretation is not fixed by, or derivable as a structural feature of, the rewrite rules.

---

## Result

The method satisfying both requirements is:

\[
\boxed{\text{(D) Dependency pairs with the third-argument subterm criterion.}}
\]

Methods (A), (B), (C), and (E) are valid termination proofs, but each depends on an imported global precedence/status or an externally selected global semantic interpretation, so each falls outside the stated benchmark boundary.
