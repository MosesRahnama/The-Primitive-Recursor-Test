### Brief checks

- **A succeeds**:
  - \(F(x,y,Z) > x\) by subterm.
  - \(F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))\) since \(F>G\), and the lhs is bigger than both arguments \(y\) and \(F(x,y,n)\); the latter because \(S(n)>n\).

- **B succeeds**:
  - Rule 1: \(x+(y+1)(0+1)=x+y+1 > x\).
  - Rule 2:
    \[
    [F(x,y,S(n))]=x+(y+1)(n+2),
    \]
    \[
    [G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+x+(y+1)(n+1),
    \]
    difference \(=1\).

- **C succeeds**:
  - Same essential descent as A, but with multiset comparison:
    \(\{x,y,S(n)\} >_{\mathrm{mul}} \{x,y,n\}\) since \(S(n)>n\).

- **D succeeds**:
  - Only recursive call is \(F(x,y,n)\), so only DP is
    \[
    F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n).
    \]
  - Projection to argument 3 yields \(S(n)\) vs. \(n\), and \(n\) is a proper subterm of \(S(n)\).

- **E succeeds**:
  - Let \(b=x+y+2\ge 2\).
  - Rule 1: \(b>x\).
  - Rule 2:
    \[
    [F(x,y,S(n))]=b^{n+2},\qquad [G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+b^{n+1}.
    \]
    Since \(b^{n+2}-b^{n+1}=b^{n+1}(b-1)\) and \(b-1=x+y+1\ge y+1\), the lhs is strictly larger.
