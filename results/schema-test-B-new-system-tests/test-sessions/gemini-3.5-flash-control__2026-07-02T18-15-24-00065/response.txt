### **(A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.** 
   * For Rule 1: $F(x, y, Z) \to x$ holds by the subterm property since $x$ is a subterm of the left-hand side (LHS).
   * For Rule 2: $F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n))$, since $F > G$ in the precedence, we must show:
     1. $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{lpo} y$ (holds by the subterm property).
     2. $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{lpo} F(x, y, n)$. Comparing the arguments lexicographically (left-to-right), the first two arguments are identical ($x = x, y = y$), and for the third argument, $S(n) >_{lpo} n$ holds by the subterm property.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes.** LPO is a standard syntax-directed termination proof method requiring no external structural axioms or object-level assumptions.

2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes.** Standard polynomial interpretations over $\mathbb{N}$ fall within ordinary metatheoretic soundness reasoning.

### **(C) Multiset Path Order (MPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.**
   * For Rule 1: $F(x, y, Z) >_{mpo} x$ holds because $x$ is a subterm of the LHS.
   * For Rule 2: $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{mpo} G(y, F(x, y, n))$ holds since $F > G$, and:
     1. $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{mpo} y$ (by subterm).
     2. $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{mpo} F(x, y, n)$. Comparing the arguments as multisets: $\{x, y, S(n)\} >_{mul} \{x, y, n\}$ holds because removing the common elements $\{x, y\}$ leaves $\{S(n)\} >_{mul} \{n\}$, which is true since $S(n) >_{mpo} n$.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes.** MPO is a standard syntax-directed termination proof method.

### **(D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion**
1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.**

     $$\pi(F^\sharp(x, y, S(n))) = S(n) \;\rhd\; n = \pi(F^\sharp(x, y, n))$$

2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes.** The dependency pair framework combined with the subterm criterion is a standard metatheoretic method.
