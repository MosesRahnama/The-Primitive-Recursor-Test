### Results

| Method | Proves termination? | Within benchmark boundary? |
|---|---:|---:|
| **(A) LPO** | Yes | No |
| **(B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation** | Yes | No |
| **(C) MPO** | Yes | No |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion** | Yes | Yes |
| **(E) Exponential interpretation** | Yes | No |

#### (A) LPO with \(F>G>S>Z\)

Both rules orient strictly:

- \(F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} x\) by the subterm property.
- For the recursive rule, \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\), since \(S(n)>n\), and the left side also dominates \(y\). With \(F>G\), this gives
  \[
  F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n)).
  \]

Thus LPO proves termination. However, the global precedence is an imported structural choice not fixed by the rules, so this method is outside the stated boundary.

#### (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation

For the first rule:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = x+y+1>x.
\]

For the recursive rule:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=x+(y+1)(n+2),
\]
whereas
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
 =y+x+(y+1)(n+1).
\]
The difference is exactly \(1\), so the rule decreases strictly. The interpretations are also strictly monotone over \(\mathbb N\).

Hence this is a valid termination proof. Nevertheless, it depends on an imported global interpretation of every symbol over \(\mathbb N\), not one fixed by the rewrite rules themselves, so it is outside the boundary under the supplied narrow criterion.

#### (C) MPO with \(F>G>S>Z\)

The first rule follows from the subterm property. For the second, the multiset of arguments
\[
\{x,y,S(n)\}
\]
strictly dominates
\[
\{x,y,n\},
\]
because \(S(n)>n\). Therefore
\[
F(x,y,S(n))>F(x,y,n),
\]
and \(F>G\) then orients the complete right-hand side.

Thus MPO succeeds, but the precedence and global multiset status are imported structural choices not fixed by the rules. It is outside the boundary.

#### (D) Dependency pairs with third-argument projection

The only dependency pair is
\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n))\to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]

Projecting to the third argument gives
\[
S(n)\mathrel{\triangleright}n,
\]
a strict syntactic subterm decrease. Therefore the subterm criterion eliminates the sole dependency-pair component and proves termination.

This stays within the boundary: the dependency pair and its decreasing third argument are obtained directly from the displayed recursive rule, using only the intrinsic syntax/subterm relation. No arbitrary global symbol precedence or semantic interpretation is imposed.

#### (E) Exponential interpretation

Let \(a=x+y+2\), so \(a\ge 2\). For the recursive rule:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=a^{n+2},
\]
while
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+a^{n+1}.
\]
Their difference is
\[
a^{n+1}(a-1)-y>0.
\]
The first rule also decreases:
\[
(x+y+2)^1>x.
\]
The interpretation is strictly monotone in all arguments, so it proves termination.

As with (B), however, it imports a global semantic interpretation over \(\mathbb N\) not fixed by the rules, placing it outside the stated boundary.

### Conclusion

All five methods successfully prove termination as stated, but **only method (D)** both proves termination and remains within the original benchmark boundary.
