# Analysis of the Five Proof Methods

**The system:**
```
F(x, y, Z)    -> x
F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))
```

---

## (A) LPO, precedence F > G > S > Z

**Success?** Yes.
- Rule 1: `x` is a proper subterm of `F(x,y,Z)` — immediate by the subterm property.
- Rule 2: Since F > G, we need `F(x,y,S(n)) >_lpo y` (subterm, ✓) and `F(x,y,S(n)) >_lpo F(x,y,n)` (same head symbol, compare argument tuples lexicographically: first two components equal, third component `S(n) >_lpo n` by subterm — ✓).

Both rules are strictly oriented. LPO succeeds.

**Boundary?** Yes. The order is built entirely from a precedence on the symbols already occurring in the rules; no external domain or extra axioms are introduced.

**Verdict: Succeeds, stays in boundary.**

---

## (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over ℕ

**Success?** Yes, arithmetically.
- Rule 1: `x+y+1 > x` since `y ≥ 0`.
- Rule 2: `x+(y+1)(n+2) > y+x+(y+1)(n+1)` reduces to `y+1 > y`, always true.
- The interpretation is strictly monotone in every argument, so the ordering lifts to all contexts.

**Boundary?** No. This method requires embedding the term algebra into ℕ and invoking the well-founded ordering of the natural numbers together with the algebraic laws of `+`, `*` (associativity, monotonicity, etc.). None of this arithmetic structure is part of the given signature {F,G,S,Z}; it is an external semantic domain and axiom set imported on top of the rules, not something derivable from the rules themselves.

**Verdict: Succeeds as a proof, but exceeds the stated boundary.**

---

## (C) MPO, precedence F > G > S > Z, multiset status

**Success?** Yes.
- Rule 1: subterm case, trivial.
- Rule 2: F > G forces comparison of `F(x,y,S(n))` against `y` (subterm ✓) and against `F(x,y,n)` (same head, multiset comparison `{x,y,S(n)}` vs `{x,y,n}`; cancel the two common elements `x`, `y`, leaving `S(n)` vs `n`, and `S(n) ▷ n` by the subterm property).

Both rules orient correctly.

**Boundary?** Yes. Like LPO, MPO is defined purely from a precedence over the rule's own function symbols and the subterm structure of the terms; no outside domain is used.

**Verdict: Succeeds, stays in boundary.**

---

## (D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion, projection to 3rd argument of F

**Success?** Yes.
- Rule 1 contributes no dependency pair (its right side is just the variable `x`).
- Rule 2 gives the single DP: `F#(x,y,S(n)) -> F#(x,y,n)`.
- With the simple projection `π(F#(_,_,n)) = n`, we get `π(lhs) = S(n)`, `π(rhs) = n`, and `S(n) ⊳ n` (proper subterm), which is a strict decrease. The single SCC is thus removed entirely, leaving no infinite chains.

**Boundary?** Yes. The subterm criterion works directly on the syntactic subterm relation of the terms occurring in the rules; it needs no external well-founded domain or arithmetic — only the term structure already present.

**Verdict: Succeeds, stays in boundary.**

---

## (E) Exponential interpretation over ℕ

**Success?** Yes, arithmetically.
- Rule 1: `(x+y+2) > x` trivially.
- Rule 2: writing `a = x+y+2 ≥ 2`, the needed inequality `(a-1)a^{n+1} > y` holds since `(a-1) ≥ y+1` and `a^{n+1} ≥ 1`.
- Monotonicity in each argument holds since the base and exponent are increasing quantities with base ≥ 2.

**Boundary?** No, for the same reason as (B) — and more so. This method not only imports the arithmetic of ℕ (addition, multiplication) but also exponentiation and its monotonicity/well-foundedness properties, none of which are derivable from the rewrite rules; it relies on an external semantic algebra far beyond the given signature.

**Verdict: Succeeds as a proof, but exceeds the stated boundary.**

## Conclusion

All five methods are mathematically valid termination arguments for this system. However, only **A, C, and D** satisfy *both* conditions: they establish strong normalization using orderings/criteria defined purely on the syntactic structure (precedences and subterm relations) of the given rules, without invoking an external numerical domain or its arithmetic axioms. Methods **B** and **E**, while correct proofs, step outside the benchmark boundary by importing the algebra of ℕ (and, in E's case, exponentiation) as an external structural assumption.

**Methods satisfying both conditions: A, C, D.**
