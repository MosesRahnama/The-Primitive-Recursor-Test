| Method | Proves termination? | Within benchmark boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **A. LPO** | Yes | Yes | The first rule decreases by the subterm property. For the second, \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) lexicographically because \(S(n)>n\); it also exceeds \(y\). Since \(F>G\), it exceeds \(G(y,F(x,y,n))\). |
| **B. Nonlinear polynomial interpretation** | Yes | Yes | Rule 1 decreases by \(y+1\). For rule 2, the interpreted left side exceeds the right side by exactly \(1\). The interpretations are strictly monotone over \(\mathbb N\). |
| **C. MPO** | Yes | Yes | For the recursive call, \(\{x,y,S(n)\}\) exceeds \(\{x,y,n\}\) in the multiset extension because \(S(n)>n\). Together with \(F>G\) and domination of the arguments of \(G\), the second rule is oriented. |
| **D. Dependency pairs with subterm criterion** | Yes | Yes | The only dependency pair is \(F^\#(x,y,S(n))\to F^\#(x,y,n)\). Projection to the third argument gives \(S(n)\triangleright n\), so the subterm criterion removes the pair. |
| **E. Exponential interpretation** | Yes | Yes | Let \(A=x+y+2\ge 2\). Rule 1 gives \(A>x\). Rule 2 requires \(A^{n+2}>y+A^{n+1}\), which holds since \(A^{n+1}(A-1)>y\). The interpretation is strictly monotone over \(\mathbb N\). |

For **B** and **E**, using \(\mathbb N\) as a well-founded interpretation domain is standard meta-level termination machinery; it does not assume that object-level terms are Peano naturals or that every third argument has the form \(S^k(Z)\). Thus it does not cross the stated boundary.

**Conclusion: all five methods, A–E, both prove termination and remain within the original benchmark boundary.**
