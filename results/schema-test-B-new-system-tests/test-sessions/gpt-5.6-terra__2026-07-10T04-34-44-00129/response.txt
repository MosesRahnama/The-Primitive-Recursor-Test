All five methods succeed, and all five remain within the stated benchmark boundary.

The key point is that none of them assumes that the third argument is a natural-number-shaped term built only from `Z` and `S`. Their arguments apply to arbitrary terms substituted for variables.

| Method | Proves termination? | Stays within boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| (A) LPO | Yes | Yes | Both rules are oriented by the stated precedence and lexicographic comparison. |
| (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation | Yes | Yes | Both rules strictly decrease in the given interpretation for every valuation in \(\mathbb N\). |
| (C) MPO | Yes | Yes | The second rule decreases the multiset of arguments of `F`, replacing `S(n)` by `n`. |
| (D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion | Yes | Yes | The only recursive dependency pair strictly decreases from `S(n)` to `n` in the third `F` argument. |
| (E) Exponential interpretation | Yes | Yes | Both rules strictly decrease under the supplied exponential interpretation. |

## (A) LPO with \(F > G > S > Z\)

Let

\[
s = F(x,y,S(n)).
\]

For the first rule,

\[
F(x,y,Z) \to x,
\]

the left-hand side is greater than `x` by the subterm property of LPO.

For the second rule,

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)),
\]

we have:

- \(s > y\), since \(y\) is a subterm of \(s\);
- \(s > F(x,y,n)\), because the roots are both `F` and, lexicographically,
  \[
  S(n) > n.
  \]
- Since \(F > G\), and \(s\) is greater than both arguments of `G`, it follows that
  \[
  s > G(y,F(x,y,n)).
  \]

Therefore (A) proves termination.

It uses only a syntactic reduction order on the presented rules, so it remains within the boundary.

---

## (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation

Given:

\[
[F(x,y,n)] = x+(y+1)(n+1),\quad [S(n)] = n+1,
\]
\[
[G(a,b)] = a+b,\quad [Z]=0.
\]

For the first rule:

\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = x+(y+1)(0+1)=x+y+1>x=[x].
\]

For the second rule, the left side evaluates to

\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]
= x+(y+1)(n+2).
\]

The right side evaluates to

\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
= y+x+(y+1)(n+1).
\]

Their difference is

\[
\bigl(x+(y+1)(n+2)\bigr)
-\bigl(y+x+(y+1)(n+1)\bigr)
=1.
\]

Hence

\[
[F(x,y,S(n))] > [G(y,F(x,y,n))]
\]

for every valuation over \(\mathbb N\).

Thus (B) proves termination. It is a standard semantic termination argument, not an added axiom about the object terms, so it stays within the boundary.

---

## (C) MPO with \(F > G > S > Z\)

The first rule is oriented by the subterm property:

\[
F(x,y,Z) > x.
\]

For the second rule, compare the two `F` terms:

\[
F(x,y,S(n))
\quad\text{and}\quad
F(x,y,n).
\]

Under multiset status, the argument multiset

\[
\{x,y,S(n)\}
\]

is greater than

\[
\{x,y,n\},
\]

because the common arguments \(x,y\) cancel and

\[
S(n)>n
\]

by the subterm property.

Therefore,

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n).
\]

Also the left-hand side is greater than \(y\), and \(F>G\). Hence

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n)).
\]

So (C) proves termination and stays within the stated boundary.

---

## (D) Dependency pairs with projection to the third argument of `F`

The only defined symbol is `F`, since it is the only symbol occurring at the root of a left-hand side.

The recursive call in the second rule gives the dependency pair

\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]

The first rule yields no dependency pair, since its right side is merely the variable `x`.

Projecting the third argument gives

\[
S(n) \triangleright n,
\]

where \(\triangleright\) is the strict subterm relation. Thus the subterm criterion strictly decreases on the only dependency pair cycle.

Consequently there can be no infinite dependency-pair chain, so the dependency-pair method proves termination.

This does not require assuming that arbitrary terms are generated only by `Z` and `S`; it merely uses the syntactic fact that \(n\) is a proper subterm of \(S(n)\). Therefore (D) also stays within the benchmark boundary.

---

## (E) Exponential interpretation

Let

\[
b=x+y+2.
\]

Then \(b\ge 2\) over \(\mathbb N\).

For the first rule:

\[
[F(x,y,Z)]
= (x+y+2)^{0+1}
= x+y+2
> x.
\]

For the second rule:

\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]
= b^{n+2}.
\]

The right-hand side evaluates to

\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
= y+b^{n+1}.
\]

We need

\[
b^{n+2} > y+b^{n+1}.
\]

Equivalently,

\[
b^{n+1}(b-1)>y.
\]

Since \(b=x+y+2\), we have

\[
b-1=x+y+1>y,
\]

and \(b^{n+1}\ge 1\). Thus the inequality holds for every natural-number valuation.

Therefore (E) proves termination. Like (B), it is a semantic interpretation proof and does not add object-level axioms or structural assumptions about the terms. It stays within the boundary.

## Conclusion

The methods satisfying both requirements are:

\[
\boxed{\text{(A), (B), (C), (D), and (E).}}
\]

Each proves strong normalization of the rewrite system as given, without requiring extra structural assumptions such as “every third argument is a numeral” or an external induction principle about `Z` and `S`.
