**Analysis of each method**

| Method | 1. Termination proof succeeds? | 2. Stays within the benchmark boundary? | Remarks |
|--------|------------------------------|------------------------------------------|---------|
| **(A) LPO** (precedence F > G > S > Z) | **Yes** | **Yes** | The order orients both rules purely syntactically:  • F(x,y,Z) > x because the left‑hand side contains the variable x.  • F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n)) because F > G and the left‑hand side is greater than each argument of G (notably F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n) by the lexicographic comparison of the third argument). No external theory is used. |
| **(B) Non‑linear polynomial interpretation** | **Yes** | **No** | The interpretation orients both rules:  • [F(x,y,Z)] = x + y + 1 > x.  • [F(x,y,S(n))] = x + (y+1)(n+2) = [G(y,F(x,y,n))] + 1.  However, the proof imports the natural numbers ℕ and arithmetic (successor, addition, multiplication) to interpret the symbols S, Z and G. These are object‑level assumptions about the data‑type that are **not** derivable from the rewrite rules alone. |
| **(C) MPO** (precedence F > G > S > Z, multiset status for all symbols) | **Yes** | **Yes** | The multiset path order orients the system:  • F(x,y,Z) > x by the subterm property.  • F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n)) because F > G and the left‑hand side is greater than each argument of G; in particular F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n) since the multiset {x,y,S(n)} is larger than {x,y,n} (S(n) is a proper super‑term of n). The proof uses only the term structure and the given precedence. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion** (project to the third argument of F) | **Yes** | **Yes** | The only dependency pair is **F**^#(x,y,S(n)) → **F**^#(x,y,n). Projecting to the third argument yields the comparison S(n) ⊳ n (strict super‑term). The subterm criterion is a purely syntactic check on the term structure; it does not rely on any external algebraic theory. |
| **(E) Exponential interpretation** | **Yes** | **No** | The interpretation orients the rules:  • [F(x,y,Z)] = x+y+2 > x.  • [F(x,y,S(n))] = (x+y+2)^{n+2} > y + (x+y+2)^{n+1} = [G(y,F(x,y,n))].  Like (B), this method imports the natural numbers with exponentiation, assuming that S behaves as the successor function. Such arithmetic assumptions are object‑level structural axioms not present in the rewrite system. |
