**Analysis of the five methods**

| Method | 1. Does it prove termination? | 2. Does it stay within the benchmark boundary? |
|--------|------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| **(A) LPO** with precedence **F > G > S > Z** | **Yes.** Rule 1 is oriented because a term is greater than its proper subterms (so **F(x,y,Z) > x**). Rule 2 is oriented because **F > G** and the left‑hand side is greater than both arguments of **G**; in particular **F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)** follows lexicographically from **S(n) > n** (subterm property). | **Yes.** LPO is a purely syntactic simplification order defined solely from the signature and the chosen precedence; it imports no external arithmetic or structural axioms. |
| **(B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over N** | **Yes.** The interpretation is monotone and strictly orients both rules: **[[F(x,y,Z)]] = x+y+1 > x** and **[[F(x,y,S(n))]] = x+(y+1)(n+2) = [[G(y,F(x,y,n))]]+1**. | **No.** The method imports the natural numbers together with addition and multiplication, i.e. an external arithmetic model whose axioms are not derivable from the two rewrite rules alone. |
| **(C) MPO** with precedence **F > G > S > Z** (multiset status for all) | **Yes.** Rule 1 follows from the subterm property. For rule 2, **F > G** and the recursive call is handled by multiset comparison of the arguments: **{x, y, S(n)} >> {x, y, n}** because **S(n) > n** by the subterm property. | **Yes.** Like LPO, MPO is a purely syntactic order built only from term structure and the precedence. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion** (project to the 3rd argument of **F**) | **Yes.** The only dependency pair is **F(x,y,S(n)) → F(x,y,n)**. Projecting both sides to the third argument yields **S(n)** and **n**; since **n** is a strict subterm of **S(n)**, the subterm criterion eliminates the DP, leaving no infinite chain. | **Yes.** The subterm criterion is a syntactic processor that inspects only the subterm relation on the terms appearing in the extracted dependency pairs; it adds no external axioms. |
| **(E) Exponential interpretation over N** | **Yes.** The interpretation is monotone and orients both rules: **[[F(x,y,Z)]] = x+y+2 > x**, and **[[F(x,y,S(n))]] = (x+y+2)^{n+2} > y + (x+y+2)^{n+1} = [[G(y,F(x,y,n))]]** because the base **x+y+2 ≥ 2**. | **No.** It imports the natural numbers and exponentiation, an external mathematical structure not present in the rewrite system. |

**Methods that satisfy both conditions**

The methods that successfully prove termination **and** remain within the benchmark boundary (working from the rewrite system alone without importing external arithmetic or structural axioms) are:

**(A), (C), and (D).**
