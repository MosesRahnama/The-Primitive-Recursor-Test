**Method (A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO)**
1. **Success:** Yes. LPO evaluates the rules recursively. For the first rule `F(x, y, Z) -> x`, LPO easily orients it because `x` is an immediate subterm of the left-hand side (LHS). For the second rule `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`, since the precedence states `F > G`, we must check that the LHS is strictly greater than both arguments of `G`. The LHS is trivially greater than `y` (by subterm property). To show `F(x, y, S(n)) > F(x, y, n)`, LPO lexicographically compares the arguments left-to-right. The first two arguments (`x` and `y`) are equal, and for the third argument, `S(n) > n` holds by the subterm property. 
2. **Boundary:** Yes. LPO is a standard, purely syntactic metatheoretic method that relies entirely on the presented rewrite rules without importing external object-level structural assumptions.

**Method (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over N**
1. **Success:** Yes. This interpretation maps terms into natural numbers. We verify strict monotonic decrease for both rules:

2. **Boundary:** Yes. Polynomial interpretations are standard termination proofs acting as metatheoretic soundness certificates. They do not inject object-level structural axioms into the rewrite system itself.

Because MPO compares arguments as multisets, it compares `{x, y, S(n)}` against `{x, y, n}`. Removing the common elements `x` and `y` leaves us comparing `{S(n)}` strictly greater than `{n}`, which successfully holds by the subterm property. 
2. **Boundary:** Yes. MPO operates identically within the syntactic constraints of standard metatheory as LPO.
