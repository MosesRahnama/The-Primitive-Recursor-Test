All five methods can prove termination, but methods (A) and (C) rely on an externally chosen global symbol precedence and therefore fall outside the stated boundary under the narrow interpretation given.

| Method | Proves termination? | Within boundary? |
|---|---:|---:|
| (A) LPO | Yes | No |
| (B) Polynomial interpretation | Yes | Yes |
| (C) MPO | Yes | No |
| (D) Dependency pairs | Yes | Yes |
| (E) Exponential interpretation | Yes | Yes |

### (A) LPO with \(F>G>S>Z\)

- \(F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} x\) by the subterm property.
- For the second rule, \(F>G\), and the left side dominates both arguments of \(G\):
  - it dominates \(y\) by the subterm property;
  - \(F(x,y,S(n))>F(x,y,n)\), since the first two arguments agree and \(S(n)>n\).

Thus LPO orients both rules strictly.

**Boundary:** No. The proof depends on a global precedence among all function symbols that is not itself fixed by the rewrite rules.

### (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation

For the first rule:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)]=x+(y+1)(0+1)=x+y+1>x.
\]

For the second rule:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=x+(y+1)(n+2),
\]
while
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
 =y+x+(y+1)(n+1).
\]
The difference is
\[
x+(y+1)(n+2)-\bigl(y+x+(y+1)(n+1)\bigr)=1.
\]

All interpretation functions are strictly monotone in their arguments over \(\mathbb N\), so this is a valid termination interpretation.

**Boundary:** Yes. The interpretation is a metatheoretic ranking certificate whose required inequalities are verified directly from the rules; it does not impose an object-level arithmetic meaning on the terms.

### (C) MPO with multiset status

- The first rule follows from the subterm property.
- For the second rule, \(F>G\), and
  \[
  \{x,y,S(n)\}>_{\mathrm{mul}}\{x,y,n\}
  \]
  because \(S(n)>n\). The left side also dominates \(y\).

Hence both rules are strictly oriented.

**Boundary:** No. Like (A), it depends on an imported global symbol precedence; it also globally fixes multiset argument status.

### (D) Dependency pairs with third-argument projection

The only dependency pair is
\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n))\to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]

Projecting \(F^\#\) to its third argument gives
\[
S(n)\mathrel{\triangleright}n,
\]
a strict subterm decrease. Thus the dependency-pair problem is finite, proving termination.

**Boundary:** Yes. The projection directly exposes the recursive decrease present in the second rule and does not require an unrelated global structural ordering.

### (E) Exponential interpretation

Let \(b=x+y+2\), so \(b\ge 2\).

For the first rule:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)]=b>x.
\]

For the second:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=b^{n+2},
\]
whereas
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+b^{n+1}.
\]
Their difference is
\[
b^{n+2}-\bigl(y+b^{n+1}\bigr)
=b^{n+1}(b-1)-y>0,
\]
because \(b-1=x+y+1>y\). The interpretation is strictly monotone over \(\mathbb N\).

**Boundary:** Yes. As in (B), this is a directly checked metatheoretic ranking interpretation, not an object-level assumption about the terms.

## Conclusion

The methods satisfying both conditions are:

\[
\boxed{(B),\ (D),\ \text{and }(E).}
\]
