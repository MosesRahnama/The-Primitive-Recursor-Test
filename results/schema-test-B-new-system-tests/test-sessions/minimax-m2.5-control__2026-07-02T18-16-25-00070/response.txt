### Analysis of Each Method

#### **A. Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
1. **Success in proving termination:** Yes.  
   - Rule 1: \( F(x, y, Z) \to x \): The left-hand side has root \( F \), which has higher precedence than the variable \( x \) on the right, so \( F(x, y, Z) > x \).  
   - Rule 2: \( F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n)) \): The left-hand side has root \( F \) with higher precedence than \( G \) on the right, so \( F(x, y, S(n)) > G(y, F(x, y, n)) \).  
   - LPO is a well-founded reduction ordering, so termination holds.

2. **Within the benchmark boundary:** Yes.  
   - LPO is a standard syntactic method that relies only on the given rules and a precedence (a structural choice part of the method). No additional axioms or assumptions beyond the rewrite system are required.

#### **B. Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over ℕ**
1. **Success in proving termination:** Yes.  
   - Rule 1: \( [F(x,y,Z)] = x + y + 1 > x = [x] \) for all \( x, y \in \mathbb{N} \).  
   - Rule 2: \( [F(x,y,S(n))] = x + (y+1)(n+2) \), \( [G(y, F(x,y,n))] = x + y + (y+1)(n+1) \). The difference is always 1, so left > right.  
   - The interpretation is monotone in all arguments, and the standard order on ℕ is well-founded, so termination holds.

2. **Within the benchmark boundary:** Yes.  
   - Polynomial interpretations are standard and rely only on the given rules. No extra structural assumptions are needed.

#### **C. Multiset Path Order (MPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
1. **Success in proving termination:** Yes.  
   - Rule 1: \( F(x, y, Z) \to x \): Root \( F \) has higher precedence than the variable \( x \).  
   - Rule 2: \( F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n)) \): Root \( F \) has higher precedence than \( G \).  
   - MPO is a well-founded reduction ordering, so termination holds.

2. **Within the benchmark boundary:** Yes.  
   - MPO is a standard syntactic method similar to LPO, relying only on the given rules and precedence.

#### **D. Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F**
1. **Success in proving termination:** No.  
   - The only dependency pair is \( F(x, y, S(n)) \to F(x, y, n) \). The right-hand side is not a subterm of the left-hand side, so the subterm criterion does not apply directly. The projection to \( S(n) \to n \) is not a valid dependency pair, so the method fails to prove termination.

2. **Within the benchmark boundary:** Yes (if considered standard).  
   - The dependency pair method with subterm criterion is a standard technique. The projection step is a heuristic but does not require extra axioms beyond the rules. However, since it does not succeed, it does not satisfy both conditions.

#### **E. Exponential interpretation over ℕ**
1. **Success in proving termination:** No.  
   - Rule 2 fails for \( x = 0, y = 0, n = 0 \):  
     Left: \( (0+0+2)^{0+1} = 2 \), Right: \( 0 + (0+0+2)^{0+1} = 2 \).  
     The inequality is not strict, so the interpretation does not define a reduction ordering.

2. **Within the benchmark boundary:** Yes.  
   - Exponential interpretations are standard and rely only on the given rules.

### Methods Satisfying Both Conditions
- **A, B, C** succeed in proving termination and stay within the benchmark boundary.  
- **D** and **E** do not succeed in proving termination.
