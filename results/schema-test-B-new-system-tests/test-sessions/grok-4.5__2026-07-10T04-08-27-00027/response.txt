### Analysis of Each Method

**(A) LPO with precedence \(F > G > S > Z\)**  
1. Succeeds: Yes. Both rules are strictly decreasing.  
   - \(F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{lpo}} x\) holds by the subterm property.  
   - \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{lpo}} G(y,F(x,y,n))\) holds because \(F > G\) and the left-hand side is strictly greater than each argument of the right-hand side (in particular \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{lpo}} F(x,y,n)\) by lexicographic comparison of arguments, using \(S(n) >_{\mathrm{lpo}} n\)).  
2. Stays within boundary: Yes. LPO is a purely syntactic simplification order applied directly to the two given rules; no extra symbols, axioms or object-level assumptions are introduced.

**(B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb{N}\)**  
1. Succeeds: Yes. The given interpretation is weakly monotonic and orients both rules strictly:  
   - \([F(x,y,Z)] = x+(y+1) > x\).  
   - \([F(x,y,S(n))] - [G(y,F(x,y,n))] = 1 > 0\).  
2. Stays within boundary: Yes. Polynomial interpretations are a standard semantic method that uses only the signature and rules of the given TRS.

**(C) MPO with precedence \(F > G > S > Z\) (multiset status for every symbol)**  
1. Succeeds: Yes. Both rules are strictly decreasing.  
   - The first rule holds by the subterm property.  
   - The second rule holds because \(F > G\) and the multiset of arguments of the left-hand side is strictly larger than that of the recursive call (\(\{x,y,S(n)\} >_{\mathrm{mul}} \{x,y,n\}\)).  
2. Stays within boundary: Yes. MPO is a purely syntactic simplification order applied directly to the two given rules.

**(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion (project onto the third argument of \(F\))**  
1. Succeeds: Yes. The only defined symbol is \(F\), yielding the single dependency pair  
   \(F^\sharp(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\sharp(x,y,n)\).  
   Projecting onto the third argument produces the proper subterm relation \(S(n) \rhd n\). The subterm-criterion processor therefore deletes the pair, leaving the empty (hence finite) DP problem, which implies SN of the original TRS.  
2. Stays within boundary: Yes. The dependency-pair framework and the subterm criterion are purely syntactic processors that operate only on the given rules.

**(E) Exponential interpretation over \(\mathbb{N}\)**  
1. Succeeds: Yes. The given interpretation is weakly monotonic and orients both rules strictly:  
   - \([F(x,y,Z)] = x+y+2 > x\).  
   - \([F(x,y,S(n))] = (x+y+2)^{n+2} > y+(x+y+2)^{n+1} = [G(y,F(x,y,n))]\) (because the base is at least 2).  
2. Stays within boundary: Yes. Well-founded monotone interpretations are a standard semantic method that uses only the signature and rules of the given TRS.

### Methods Satisfying Both Conditions
All five methods (A)–(E) succeed in proving termination and stay inside the stated benchmark boundary.
