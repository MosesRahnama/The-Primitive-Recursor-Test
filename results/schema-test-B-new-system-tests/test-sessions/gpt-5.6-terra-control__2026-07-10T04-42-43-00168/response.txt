## (A) LPO with \(F > G > S > Z\)

For

\[
F(x,y,Z) \to x,
\]

the right-hand side is a proper subterm/argument of the left-hand side, so the rule decreases.

For

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)),
\]

the left-hand side has root \(F\), while the right-hand side has root \(G\), and \(F>G\). It remains to show that the left-hand side is greater than both arguments of \(G\):

- \(F(x,y,S(n)) > y\), by the subterm property;
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\), since the first two arguments agree and \(S(n)>n\).

Thus both rules orient strictly under LPO.

## (B) Polynomial interpretation

Let

\[
[F(x,y,n)] = x+(y+1)(n+1),\quad [S(n)]=n+1,
\]
\[
[G(a,b)]=a+b,\quad [Z]=0.
\]

For the first rule:

\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = x+(y+1)(0+1)=x+y+1>x=[x].
\]

For the second rule:

\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]
 =x+(y+1)(n+2),
\]

whereas

\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
 =y+x+(y+1)(n+1).
\]

Their difference is exactly

\[
\bigl(x+(y+1)(n+2)\bigr)
-\bigl(y+x+(y+1)(n+1)\bigr)
=1.
\]

Hence the left-hand side is strictly larger for every valuation in \(\mathbb N\). The interpretation is monotone, so it establishes termination.

## (C) MPO with multiset status

The first rule again decreases by the subterm property.

For the second rule, the root symbol decreases from \(F\) to \(G\), since \(F>G\). To use the root-precedence clause, the left-hand side must dominate the arguments \(y\) and \(F(x,y,n)\) of the right-hand side:

- it dominates \(y\) as a subterm;
- it dominates \(F(x,y,n)\) because, under multiset comparison,

\[
\{x,y,S(n)\} >_{\mathrm{mul}} \{x,y,n\},
\]

using \(S(n)>n\).

Thus the second rule is strictly oriented, and MPO proves termination.

## (D) Dependency pairs with projection to F’s third argument

The only recursive defined symbol is \(F\), giving the dependency pair

\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]

Projecting to the third argument gives

\[
S(n) \to n.
\]

The projected left-hand side properly contains the projected right-hand side as a subterm. Hence the subterm criterion eliminates the only dependency-pair cycle. Therefore there can be no infinite dependency-pair chain, and the TRS terminates.

## (E) Exponential interpretation

Write

\[
b=x+y+2.
\]

Then \(b\ge 2\), and

\[
[F(x,y,n)] = b^{n+1}.
\]

For the first rule,

\[
[F(x,y,Z)]=b=x+y+2>x.
\]

For the second rule,

\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=b^{n+2},
\]

while

\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+b^{n+1}.
\]

Since

\[
b^{n+2}=b^{n+1}\cdot b
\]

and \(b-1=x+y+1>y\), one has

\[
b^{n+2}
=
b^{n+1}+b^{n+1}(b-1)
>
b^{n+1}+y.
\]

Thus the second rule strictly decreases as well. The interpretation is monotone over \(\mathbb N\), so it proves termination.
