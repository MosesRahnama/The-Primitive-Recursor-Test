**(A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
1. **Succeeds:** Yes.

   - For `F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))`, because `F > G`, we must check that the LHS is strictly greater than both `y` and `F(x, y, n)`. The LHS contains `y` as a subterm. When comparing the LHS to `F(x, y, n)`, since the root symbols are the same, LPO compares the arguments lexicographically (left-to-right). The first two arguments are identical (`x=x`, `y=y`), and the third argument strictly decreases because `n` is a proper subterm of `S(n)`.
2. **Within boundary:** Yes. LPO relies purely on the syntactic structure of the rewrite system.
