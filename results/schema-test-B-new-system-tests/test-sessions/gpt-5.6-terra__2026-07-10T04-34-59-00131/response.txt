All five methods succeed, and all remain within the stated benchmark boundary.

### (A) LPO with \(F > G > S > Z\)

**Succeeds:** Yes.

For the first rule,
\[
F(x,y,Z) \to x,
\]
the left-hand side is greater than \(x\) by the subterm property.

For the second rule,
\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)),
\]
the root symbol decreases from \(F\) to \(G\). To use the precedence comparison, the left-hand side must exceed both arguments of the right-hand side:

- \(F(x,y,S(n)) > y\), by subterm;
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\), because the roots are both \(F\), the first two arguments agree, and \(S(n) > n\).

Thus both rules are strictly oriented.

**Boundary:** Yes. LPO is a syntactic termination order; it does not assume that \(S\) and \(Z\) satisfy Peano axioms or impose restrictions on substitutions.

---

### (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation

\[
[F(x,y,n)] = x+(y+1)(n+1),\quad [S(n)]=n+1,\quad [G(a,b)]=a+b,\quad [Z]=0.
\]

**Succeeds:** Yes.

For the first rule:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = x+(y+1)(0+1)=x+y+1 > x.
\]

For the second rule:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]
= x+(y+1)(n+2),
\]
while
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
= y+x+(y+1)(n+1).
\]
Their difference is
\[
\bigl(x+(y+1)(n+2)\bigr)
-\bigl(y+x+(y+1)(n+1)\bigr)
=1.
\]
Hence the left-hand side is strictly larger for every valuation in \(\mathbb N\).

The interpretation is strictly monotone in every argument: the relevant coefficients are positive over \(\mathbb N\).

**Boundary:** Yes. The natural-number interpretation is a meta-level termination certificate. It does not add equations, axioms, or intended-object assumptions to the rewrite system.

---

### (C) MPO with \(F > G > S > Z\), multiset status

**Succeeds:** Yes.

Again, the first rule decreases by the subterm property.

For the second rule, the root decreases from \(F\) to \(G\). The left-hand side exceeds \(y\) by subterm. It also exceeds \(F(x,y,n)\): under multiset comparison,
\[
\{x,y,S(n)\} >_{\mathrm{mul}} \{x,y,n\},
\]
because \(S(n)>n\), while the other two arguments are unchanged.

Thus
\[
F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{MPO}} G(y,F(x,y,n)).
\]

**Boundary:** Yes. MPO is a syntactic ordering argument and does not rely on an external structural interpretation of \(S\) or \(Z\).

---

### (D) Dependency pairs with projection to the third argument of \(F\)

**Succeeds:** Yes.

The only defined symbol is \(F\), since it is the only root symbol occurring on a left-hand side. The relevant dependency pair from the second rule is
\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]

Projecting to the third argument gives
\[
S(n) \triangleright n,
\]
a strict proper-subterm decrease.

The first rule creates no recursive dependency pair, and \(G\) has no defining rules. Therefore the dependency-pair problem is eliminated by the subterm criterion.

**Boundary:** Yes. The subterm criterion uses only syntactic term structure. It does not assume that terms headed by \(S\) represent natural numbers, nor that \(Z\) is a numerical zero.

---

### (E) Exponential interpretation

\[
[F(x,y,n)] = (x+y+2)^{n+1},\quad [S(n)]=n+1,\quad [G(a,b)]=a+b,\quad [Z]=0.
\]

**Succeeds:** Yes.

For the first rule:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = (x+y+2)^1=x+y+2>x.
\]

For the second rule, let \(B=x+y+2\), so \(B\geq 2\). Then:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=B^{n+2},
\]
and
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+B^{n+1}.
\]
It suffices to show
\[
B^{n+2}>y+B^{n+1}.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
B^{n+1}(B-1)>y.
\]
Since \(B-1=x+y+1\geq y+1\) and \(B^{n+1}\geq 2\), the left-hand side is strictly greater than \(y\). Thus the second rule is strictly decreasing.

The interpretation is strictly monotone in every argument over \(\mathbb N\), since the base is always at least \(2\).

**Boundary:** Yes. As with method (B), this is a semantic termination interpretation, not an added object-level axiom about the symbols in the rewrite system.

## Methods satisfying both conditions

All of them:

\[
\boxed{\text{(A), (B), (C), (D), and (E)}.
\]

Each proves strong normalization of the presented first-order rewrite system without requiring assumptions such as “\(S\) and \(Z\) are the natural numbers,” induction principles for those symbols, constructor freeness assumptions, or any additional rewrite rules.
