### **Method (A): Lexicographic Path Order (LPO)**
* **Precedence:** $F > G > S > Z$

1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.** 
   * **Rule 1:** $F(x, y, Z) \succ_{\text{lpo}} x$ holds trivially by the subterm property since $x$ is a proper subterm of the Left-Hand Side (LHS).
   * **Rule 2:** $F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{\text{lpo}} G(y, F(x, y, n))$ holds because $F > G$. We verify:
     1. $F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{\text{lpo}} y$ (holds by the subterm property).
     2. $F(x, y, S(n)) \succ_{\text{lpo}} F(x, y, n)$. Since the root symbols are identical ($F$), we compare their arguments lexicographically (left-to-right):
        * First argument: $x$ vs $x$ (equal)
        * Second argument: $y$ vs $y$ (equal)
        * Third argument: $S(n) \succ_{\text{lpo}} n$ (holds by the subterm property).
        
        We must also check that the LHS is strictly greater than each of the RHS's subterms $x, y, n$, which holds by the subterm property.

2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes.** LPO is a standard syntactic termination method that relies entirely on structural ordering constraints derived from the given rewrite rules without importing external axioms.

### **Method (C): Multiset Path Order (MPO)**

2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes.** MPO is a standard syntactic termination method that stays strictly within the rewrite system's boundary.
