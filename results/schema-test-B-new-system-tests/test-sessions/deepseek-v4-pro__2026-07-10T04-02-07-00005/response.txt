**Method A (LPO with precedence F > G > S > Z)**
- **Succeeds?** Yes. Rule 1: F(x,y,Z) > x because x is a proper subterm. Rule 2: F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n)) because F > G in precedence and both y and F(x,y,n) are smaller (y is a proper subterm; F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n) by lexicographic comparison since S(n) > n). 
- **Boundary?** Yes. LPO uses only the given rules and precedence; no extra axioms are imported.
- **Verdict:** satisfies both.

**Method B (Polynomial interpretation)**
- **Succeeds?** Yes. Rule 1: x + y + 1 > x. Rule 2: x + (y+1)(n+2) > y + x + (y+1)(n+1) simplifies to (y+1) > 0, always true.
- **Boundary?** Yes. Polynomial orders over N are a standard first-order termination method.
- **Verdict:** satisfies both.

**Method C (MPO with precedence F > G > S > Z, multiset arguments)**
- **Succeeds?** Yes. Rule 1 holds as x is a subterm. Rule 2: F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n)) by precedence F > G and both y and F(x,y,n) are smaller; F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n) by multiset comparison {x, y, S(n)} >mul {x, y, n} because S(n) > n.
- **Boundary?** Yes. MPO is a standard rewrite order.
- **Verdict:** satisfies both.

**Method D (Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F)**
- **Succeeds?** No. The dependency pair is F#(x,y,S(n)) → F#(x,y,n). Projecting F# to its third argument yields S(n) → n, a strict subterm decrease. However, the subterm criterion also requires orienting the original rules under the same projection. Whether we project F to its third argument (which makes rule F(x,y,Z)→x fail) or leave F unprojected (which makes rule F(x,y,S(n)) → G(y,F(x,y,n)) fail), we cannot satisfy the weak decrease condition for the rules. Hence the criterion does not prove termination as stated.
- **Boundary?** Not applicable (it fails condition 1).
- **Verdict:** does **not** satisfy condition 1.

**Method E (Exponential interpretation)**
- **Succeeds?** Yes. Rule 1: (x+y+2)¹ > x because x+y+2 ≥ x+2 > x. Rule 2: (x+y+2)^(n+2) > y + (x+y+2)^(n+1) because (x+y+2)^(n+2) – (x+y+2)^(n+1) = (x+y+1)(x+y+2)^(n+1) > y for all x,y,n ∈ N.
- **Boundary?** Yes. Exponential interpretations over N are a standard first-order termination method.
- **Verdict:** satisfies both.

**Methods satisfying both conditions:** A, B, C, E.
