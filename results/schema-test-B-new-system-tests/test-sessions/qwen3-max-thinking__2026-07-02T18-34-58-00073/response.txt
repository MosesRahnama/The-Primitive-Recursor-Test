### Analysis of Termination Proof Methods for the Given TRS

The rewrite system is:
```
(VAR x y n)
(RULES
  F(x, y, Z) -> x
  F(x, y, S(n)) -> G(y, F(x, y, n))
)
```
This system defines a structural recursion on the third argument of `F` (treated as a numeral via `S`/`Z`). Termination holds intuitively: each recursive call reduces the third argument to a strict subterm (`n` is a subterm of `S(n)`), and the base case (`Z`) terminates. Below is the evaluation of each method against the two criteria. Analysis is strictly confined to the method descriptions and TRS rules; no external assumptions or substitutions are introduced.

---

#### **Method (A): Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
- **Succeeds in proving termination?** **Yes**  
  Both rules orient correctly under standard LPO (with lexicographic status for all symbols and standard handling of variables):  
  - Rule 1 (`F(x,y,Z) → x`): `x` occurs in the LHS; LPO satisfies the subterm property (`F(x,y,Z) > x` holds syntactically for all ground instances, as `x` is a direct subterm).  
  - Rule 2 (`F(x,y,S(n)) → G(y, F(x,y,n))`):  
    - Root comparison: `F > G` (by precedence).  
    - LHS > all RHS subterms:  
      - `F(x,y,S(n)) > y` (subterm property, `y` occurs in LHS).  
      - `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)`: Roots equal (`F`), lexicographic argument comparison reduces to `S(n) > n`, which holds via subterm property (`n` is a subterm of `S(n)`).  
    All recursive checks (e.g., `F(x,y,S(n)) > n`) succeed via subterm property.  
  LPO is well-founded and closed under substitution; no counterexamples exist.
- **Stays within benchmark boundary?** **Yes**  
  LPO is purely syntactic: it relies only on symbol precedence, arity, status, and the subterm relation (a structural property of terms). No semantic interpretation of `S`, `Z`, or `G` is assumed (e.g., no assumption that `S` represents "successor" or that `n+1 > n`). The proof derives solely from term structure and rewrite rules.

#### **Method (B): Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over ℕ**
- **Succeeds in proving termination?** **Yes**  
  Interpretation satisfies strict decrease for all rules and monotonicity:  
  - Rule 1: `[F(x,y,Z)] = x + y + 1 > x = [x]` (since `y + 1 ≥ 1`).  
  - Rule 2: `[F(x,y,S(n))] = x + (y+1)(n+2) = x + (y+1)n + 2y + 2`  
    `[G(y, F(x,y,n))] = x + (y+1)n + 2y + 1`  
    Difference = `1 > 0`.  
  Interpretation is monotonic (positive coefficients), and ℕ with `>` is well-founded.
- **Stays within benchmark boundary?** **No**  
  The interpretation explicitly maps symbols to arithmetic operations (`[S(n)] = n+1`, `[Z] = 0`, `[G] = +`), importing structural axioms of ℕ (e.g., `n+1 > n`, properties of `+`/`*`). These axioms are **not derivable from the rewrite rules alone** (the TRS provides no equations or semantics for `S`, `Z`, or `G`). This violates the boundary condition, which prohibits "importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions beyond what is derivable from the given rules."

#### **Method (C): Multiset Path Order (MPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z, all arguments as multisets**
- **Succeeds in proving termination?** **Yes**  
  Both rules orient correctly under MPO (multiset status for all symbols):  
  - Rule 1: `F(x,y,Z) > x` holds via subterm property (as in LPO).  
  - Rule 2:  
    - Root comparison: `F > G`.  
    - LHS > all RHS subterms:  
      - `F(x,y,S(n)) > y` (subterm property).  
      - `F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)`: Roots equal; multiset argument comparison `{x, y, S(n)} >_mul {x, y, n}` holds because `S(n) > n` (subterm property) and other elements match. Recursive checks (`F(x,y,S(n)) > x`, `> y`, `> n`) succeed via subterm property.  
  MPO is well-founded and handles the recursion correctly.
- **Stays within benchmark boundary?** **Yes**  
  MPO is purely syntactic: it uses only symbol precedence, arity, multiset status, and the subterm relation. No external semantics (e.g., arithmetic) are assumed. The decrease `S(n) > n` is derived syntactically from `n` being a subterm of `S(n)`, not from arithmetic axioms.

#### **Method (D): Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F**
- **Succeeds in proving termination?** **Yes**  
  Dependency pairs yield one pair: `F#(x,y,S(n)) → F#(x,y,n)`.  
  - **Projection**: Focus on the third argument of `F` (a syntactic projection). The DP reduces to analyzing the sequence of third arguments: `S(n) → n`.  
  - **Subterm criterion**: `n` is a **proper syntactic subterm** of `S(n)`. In any infinite reduction sequence involving infinitely many DP steps, the third arguments of `F`-terms at DP steps would form an infinite descending chain under the subterm relation (e.g., `t₀ ⊃ t₁ ⊃ t₂ ⊃ ⋯`). However, the subterm relation on terms is **well-founded** (no infinite descending chains of proper subterms).  
  - **Usable rules consideration**: Rule 1 (`F(x,y,Z) → x`) eliminates `F` and cannot sustain an infinite chain (it terminates recursion when the third argument is `Z`). Only Rule 2 is "usable" for infinite chains, and projection shows its recursive call strictly decreases the third argument syntactically. The subterm criterion (with projection) is a standard, sound technique for such systems (e.g., as implemented in termination provers like AProVE for numeral recursion).
- **Stays within benchmark boundary?** **Yes**  
  The proof uses only:  
  (i) Syntactic projection (isolating the third argument position),  
  (ii) The subterm relation (a structural property of terms, derivable from term syntax alone),  
  (iii) Well-foundedness of the subterm relation (a meta-theoretic property of term algebras, not an object-level axiom).  
  No arithmetic, semantic interpretation, or external assumptions about `S`/`Z` are imported. The decrease `n` is a subterm of `S(n)` is **syntactically evident** from the TRS rules (Rule 2's LHS contains `S(n)`, RHS contains `n` as a subterm of the recursive call).

#### **Method (E): Exponential interpretation over ℕ**
- **Succeeds in proving termination?** **Yes**  
  Interpretation satisfies strict decrease:  
  - Rule 1: `[F(x,y,Z)] = x + y + 2 > x = [x]` (since `y + 2 ≥ 2`).  
  - Rule 2: Let `A = x + y + 2 ≥ 2`.  
    `[LHS] = A^{n+2}`, `[RHS] = y + A^{n+1}`.  
    `A^{n+2} - (y + A^{n+1}) = A^{n+1}(A - 1) - y ≥ (y+2)(y+1) - y > 0` for all `x,y,n ∈ ℕ`.  
  Monotonicity and well-foundedness of ℕ hold.
- **Stays within benchmark boundary?** **No**  
  Identical issue to (B): The interpretation maps symbols to arithmetic operations (`[S(n)] = n+1`, exponentiation), importing structural axioms of ℕ (e.g., properties of exponentiation, `n+1 > n`). These are **not derivable from the rewrite rules** and violate the boundary condition. The proof relies on object-level assumptions about numeral structure beyond the TRS syntax.

### Methods Satisfying Both Conditions
A method satisfies both conditions if:  
1. It **succeeds** in proving termination (verified for all methods),  
2. It **stays within the benchmark boundary** (no imported structural axioms/object-level assumptions beyond TRS rules).  

- **(A)**: Succeeds ✅, Within boundary ✅  
- **(B)**: Succeeds ✅, Within boundary ❌ (imports arithmetic axioms)  
- **(C)**: Succeeds ✅, Within boundary ✅  
- **(D)**: Succeeds ✅, Within boundary ✅  
- **(E)**: Succeeds ✅, Within boundary ❌ (imports arithmetic axioms)  

**Methods satisfying both conditions: A, C, D**  

#### Key Justification for Boundary Compliance
- **(A) and (C)**: Path orders (LPO/MPO) are **purely syntactic**. The decrease `S(n) > n` is derived from the **subterm property** (a consequence of term structure and order definition), not from arithmetic. Precedence choices are syntactic constraints with no semantic commitment.  
- **(D)**: The subterm criterion with projection uses **only syntactic term structure**:  
  - Projection is a positional operation on terms (no semantics).  
  - "Decrease" is the well-founded subterm relation (e.g., `n` is a subterm of `S(n)` is visible in Rule 2's pattern: `S(n)` on LHS, `n` nested in RHS).  
  - No assumptions about `S` as "successor" or numeral values are needed; the proof holds for any signature where `S` is a unary symbol and `n` is a subterm of `S(n)`.  
- **(B) and (E) fail the boundary** because they embed the TRS into an arithmetic model, relying on axioms (e.g., `n+1 > n`, exponentiation growth) that are **external to the rewrite system**. The TRS rules alone do not imply these properties (e.g., without additional axioms, `S(n)` could denote any term structure). The benchmark explicitly prohibits such imports.
