To determine which of the proposed proof methods satisfy both conditions, we evaluate them against the two given criteria:

2. **Boundary:** Does it rely only on the presented rewrite system without importing structural axioms or object-level assumptions? (All standard term rewriting termination orders—like path orders, interpretations over $\mathbb{N}$, and dependency pair frameworks—inherently satisfy this since they analyze the syntactic or algebraic properties of the rules themselves).

**(A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
1. **Succeeds:** Yes.

2. **Boundary:** Yes, LPO is a standard syntactic termination order.

**(B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over $\mathbb{N}$**
1. **Succeeds:** Yes. The interpretation is well-defined and strictly monotonic over the natural numbers.

2. **Boundary:** Yes, polynomial interpretations over non-negative integers are standard algebraic proofs of termination and do not rely on imported external assumptions.

**(C) Multiset Path Order (MPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z, all arguments compared as multisets**
1. **Succeeds:** Yes.

We verify the recursive conditions: the left side has $y$ as a subterm, and for the inner $F$, we compare multisets $\{x, y, S(n)\}$ and $\{x, y, n\}$. Dropping the common $x$ and $y$, we are left with $S(n) > n$, which holds by the subterm property.

**(D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F**
1. **Succeeds:** Yes.
