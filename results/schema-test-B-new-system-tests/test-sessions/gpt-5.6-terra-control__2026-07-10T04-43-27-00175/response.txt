All five methods succeed, and all five remain within the stated benchmark boundary.

The key point for the boundary question is that these methods use their orders/interpretations as metatheoretic termination certificates. They do not add rewrite rules, assume that every term is a Peano numeral, or assume object-level equations or structural axioms not present in the TRS. In particular, interpreting `S` and `Z` over `N` does not assert that the rewrite-system terms themselves are natural numbers; it supplies a well-founded measure for all substitutions.

| Method | Proves termination? | Within boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| (A) LPO | Yes | Yes | Both rules orient strictly under the stated precedence. |
| (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation | Yes | Yes | Both rules strictly decrease numerically for every valuation in `N`. |
| (C) MPO | Yes | Yes | The recursive `F` call is smaller by multiset comparison in the third argument. |
| (D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion | Yes | Yes | The only recursive dependency pair decreases from `S(n)` to `n` under projection 3 of `F`. |
| (E) Exponential interpretation | Yes | Yes | Both rules strictly decrease for all natural-number valuations. |

## (A) LPO with \(F > G > S > Z\)

First rule:

\[
F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} x
\]

by the subterm property.

Second rule:

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)).
\]

Since \(F > G\), it suffices to show that the left-hand side is greater than both arguments of `G`:

- \(F(x,y,S(n)) > y\), by subterm;
- \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\), since the first two arguments agree and
  \[
  S(n) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} n
  \]
  by subterm.

Thus (A) proves termination.

The precedence is a termination-order certificate, not an added axiom about the rewrite system, so it stays within the boundary.

## (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation

Let

\[
[F(x,y,n)] = x+(y+1)(n+1),\quad [S(n)]=n+1,\quad [G(a,b)]=a+b,\quad [Z]=0.
\]

For the first rule:

\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = x+(y+1)(0+1)=x+y+1>x=[x].
\]

For the second rule:

\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]
= x+(y+1)(n+2),
\]

while

\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
= y+x+(y+1)(n+1).
\]

Their difference is

\[
\bigl(x+(y+1)(n+2)\bigr)
-\bigl(y+x+(y+1)(n+1)\bigr)
=1.
\]

Hence the left-hand side is strictly greater for every valuation over \(\mathbb N\).

So (B) proves termination and remains within the boundary. It does not require an object-level assumption that `S` and `Z` denote all naturals or that every term is a numeral.

## (C) MPO with the stated precedence and multiset status

The first rule decreases by the subterm property:

\[
F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{MPO}} x.
\]

For the second rule, \(F>G\), so it is enough that

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{MPO}} y
\]

and

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{MPO}} F(x,y,n).
\]

The first follows by subterm. For the second, the argument multisets compare as

\[
\{x,y,S(n)\} >_{\mathrm{mul}} \{x,y,n\},
\]

because \(S(n)>n\) by subterm, with `x` and `y` unchanged.

Thus (C) proves termination and is within the boundary.

## (D) Dependency pairs with projection to the third argument of `F`

The recursive occurrence in the second rule gives the dependency pair

\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]

Projecting to the third argument yields

\[
S(n) \triangleright n,
\]

a strict subterm decrease. Therefore the subterm criterion eliminates the only recursive dependency pair.

The first rule produces no recursive dependency pair.

Thus (D) proves termination. The use of the dependency-pair theorem and the syntactic subterm relation is ordinary metatheory, not an imported object-level structural axiom, so it remains within the boundary.

## (E) Exponential interpretation

Let

\[
[F(x,y,n)]=(x+y+2)^{n+1},\quad [S(n)]=n+1,\quad [G(a,b)]=a+b,\quad [Z]=0.
\]

For the first rule:

\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = (x+y+2)^1=x+y+2>x.
\]

For the second rule, write \(b=x+y+2\), so \(b\ge 2\). Then

\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=b^{n+2},
\]

while

\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+b^{n+1}.
\]

It suffices to show

\[
b^{n+2}>y+b^{n+1},
\]

equivalently,

\[
b^{n+1}(b-1)>y.
\]

Since \(b=x+y+2\ge y+2\) and \(n\ge0\),

\[
b^{n+1}(b-1)\ge b(b-1)\ge (y+2)(y+1)>y.
\]

Hence the second rule decreases strictly.

Therefore (E) proves termination and is within the boundary for the same reason as (B): it is a metatheoretic well-founded interpretation, not an assumption about the intended object-level meaning of the symbols.
