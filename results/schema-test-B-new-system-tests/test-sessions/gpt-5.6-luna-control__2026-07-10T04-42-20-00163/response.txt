| Method | Proves termination? | Within benchmark boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **(A) LPO** | **Yes** | **No** | For the second rule, \(F(x,y,S(n))\) dominates \(G(y,F(x,y,n))\): the root precedence gives \(F>G\), and the recursive call is smaller because \(S(n)>n\). However, the proof depends on the externally chosen precedence \(F>G>S>Z\), which is an imported global structural choice. |
| **(B) Polynomial interpretation** | **Yes** | **Yes** | Rule 1 decreases by \(y+1\). For rule 2, writing the interpretations explicitly, the difference is \[x+(y+1)(n+2)-\bigl(y+x+(y+1)(n+1)\bigr)=1.\] The interpretation is monotone over the well-founded order on \(\mathbb N\). This is an ordinary metatheoretic ranking argument, not an added object-level assumption. |
| **(C) MPO** | **Yes** | **No** | The recursive \(F\)-call is smaller because the multiset argument comparison replaces \(S(n)\) by its proper subterm \(n\), and the root comparison uses \(F>G\). As with LPO, the proof essentially relies on the externally supplied precedence \(F>G>S>Z\). |
| **(D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion** | **Yes** | **Yes** | The only relevant dependency pair is essentially \[F^\#(x,y,S(n))\to F^\#(x,y,n).\] Projecting to the third argument gives \(S(n)\) on the left and \(n\) on the right; \(n\) is a proper subterm of \(S(n)\). This uses the syntactic recursion already present in the rules and requires no imported global precedence or object-level axiom. |
| **(E) Exponential interpretation** | **Yes** | **Yes** | Let \(a=x+y+2\). Rule 1 gives \(a>x\). For rule 2, \[a^{n+2}-(y+a^{n+1})=a^{n+1}(a-1)-y>0,\] since \(a-1=x+y+1>y\). The interpretation is monotone over \(\mathbb N\), so it supplies a standard metatheoretic termination proof. |

Thus, **all five methods prove termination**, but the methods satisfying **both** requirements are:

\[
\boxed{\text{(B), (D), and (E)}}
\]

Methods **(A)** and **(C)** succeed as termination proofs but fall outside the stated boundary because they depend on the externally chosen symbol precedence.
