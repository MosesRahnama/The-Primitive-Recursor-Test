**Analysis of each method:**

**(A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
- **Termination:** Yes. 
  - Rule 1: F(x,y,Z) > x holds because x is a subterm (argument).
  - Rule 2: F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n)) holds because F > G in precedence, and both F(x,y,S(n)) > y (subterm) and F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n) (lexicographic decrease via S(n) > n, since n is a subterm of S(n)) are satisfied.
- **Boundary:** Yes. LPO uses only the syntax of the given rewrite system (term algebra) and a precedence on its signature. No external arithmetic or structural axioms are imported.

**(B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over N**
- **Termination:** Yes.
  - Rule 1: [F(x,y,Z)] = x + y + 1 > x.
  - Rule 2: [F(x,y,S(n))] = x + (y+1)(n+2) > y + x + (y+1)(n+1) = [G(y,F(x,y,n))].
- **Boundary:** No. The proof relies on interpreting symbols into the natural numbers and using algebraic properties of addition and multiplication (distributivity, associativity, etc.). These are structural axioms of arithmetic not derivable from the given rewrite rules.

**(C) Multiset Path Order (MPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
- **Termination:** Yes.
  - Rule 1: F(x,y,Z) > x by the subterm property.
  - Rule 2: F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n)) because F > G, F(x,y,S(n)) > y by subterm, and F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n) by multiset extension of the argument multisets (S(n) > n via the subterm property).
- **Boundary:** Yes. MPO is a purely syntactic method that relies only on the term structure and the given precedence.

**(D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F**
- **Termination:** Yes.
  - The only dependency pair is F#(x,y,S(n)) → F#(x,y,n). Projecting to the third argument yields S(n) and n. Since S(n) is a proper superterm of n and F# is a fresh symbol not reduced by the rules, any infinite chain would produce an infinite descending proper-subterm chain, which is impossible.
- **Boundary:** Yes. Dependency pairs and the subterm criterion are constructed entirely from the given system; they use no external axioms.

**(E) Exponential interpretation over N**
- **Termination:** Yes.
  - Rule 1: (x+y+2)¹ = x+y+2 > x.
  - Rule 2: (x+y+2)^(n+2) = (x+y+2)·(x+y+2)^(n+1) ≥ 2·(x+y+2)^(n+1) > y + (x+y+2)^(n+1).
- **Boundary:** No. Like (B), this method imports the structural axioms of exponentiation and arithmetic on natural numbers, which are not derivable from the rewrite rules.

**Methods satisfying both conditions:**  
**A, C, D**
