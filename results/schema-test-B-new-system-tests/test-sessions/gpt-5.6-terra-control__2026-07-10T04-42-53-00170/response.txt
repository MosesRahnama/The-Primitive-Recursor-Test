All five methods succeed as termination proofs, and all remain within the stated boundary when understood as metatheoretic proof certificates rather than added rewrite axioms.

| Method | Proves termination? | Within boundary? |
|---|---:|---:|
| (A) LPO | Yes | Yes |
| (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation | Yes | Yes |
| (C) MPO | Yes | Yes |
| (D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion | Yes | Yes |
| (E) Exponential interpretation | Yes | Yes |

## (A) LPO with \(F > G > S > Z\)

**Succeeds:** Yes.

For the first rule,

\[
F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} x
\]

by the subterm property.

For the second rule, since \(F>G\), it suffices to show

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} y
\quad\text{and}\quad
F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} F(x,y,n).
\]

The first is again by subterm. For the second, the roots are both \(F\), and lexicographically the first two arguments agree while

\[
S(n) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} n
\]

by subterm. Hence the second rule is oriented strictly.

**Boundary:** Yes. The precedence is a metatheoretic witness used to define a well-founded reduction order; it is not an object-level axiom imposed on the rewrite system or its terms. No additional rewrite rule, equational theory, typing assumption, or structural property of the object language is assumed.

---

## (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation

\[
[F(x,y,n)] = x+(y+1)(n+1),\qquad [S(n)]=n+1,
\]
\[
[G(a,b)]=a+b,\qquad [Z]=0.
\]

**Succeeds:** Yes.

For the first rule:

\[
[F(x,y,Z)]
= x+(y+1)(0+1)
= x+y+1
> x=[x].
\]

For the second rule:

\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]
= x+(y+1)(n+2),
\]

whereas

\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
= y+x+(y+1)(n+1).
\]

Their difference is

\[
x+(y+1)(n+2)-\bigl(y+x+(y+1)(n+1)\bigr)=1.
\]

Thus the left-hand side is strictly greater for every valuation over \(\mathbb N\).

The interpretation is monotone in all arguments, and \(>\) on \(\mathbb N\) is well-founded, so this establishes termination.

**Boundary:** Yes. This is an ordinary semantic termination argument. The interpretation is a proof certificate; it does not add an object-level arithmetic theory or assume that rewrite terms themselves satisfy arithmetic equations.

---

## (C) MPO with \(F > G > S > Z\)

**Succeeds:** Yes.

Again, the first rule is handled by the subterm property:

\[
F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{MPO}} x.
\]

For the second rule, \(F>G\), so MPO can orient

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{MPO}} G(y,F(x,y,n))
\]

provided the left-hand side is greater than both arguments of the right-hand side.

It is greater than \(y\) by subterm. Also,

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{MPO}} F(x,y,n)
\]

because the multiset of arguments

\[
\{x,y,S(n)\}
\]

strictly dominates

\[
\{x,y,n\},
\]

using \(S(n)>_{\mathrm{MPO}}n\).

**Boundary:** Yes. As in (A), the precedence and MPO are metatheoretic devices used to exhibit a well-founded order. They do not alter the rewrite system or introduce an object-level structural axiom.

---

## (D) Dependency pairs with projection to the third argument of \(F\)

**Succeeds:** Yes.

The only defined symbol is \(F\), since it is the only root symbol of a left-hand side. The relevant dependency pair from the recursive occurrence is

\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]

Projecting \(F^\#\) to its third argument gives

\[
S(n) \triangleright n,
\]

where \(\triangleright\) is the proper-subterm relation.

Thus every dependency-pair step strictly decreases the projected third argument. The subterm relation is well-founded on finite first-order terms, so there can be no infinite dependency-pair chain. Therefore the TRS terminates.

**Boundary:** Yes. The projection is read directly from the recursive call structure of the given rule. The subterm criterion is a standard sound metatheorem about dependency pairs and finite terms; it adds no object-level assumption or rewrite axiom.

## (E) Exponential interpretation

\[
[F(x,y,n)] = (x+y+2)^{n+1},\qquad [S(n)]=n+1,
\]
\[
[G(a,b)]=a+b,\qquad [Z]=0.
\]

**Succeeds:** Yes.

For the first rule:

\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = (x+y+2)^1=x+y+2>x.
\]

For the second rule, put \(b=x+y+2\), so \(b\ge 2\). Then

\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=b^{n+2},
\]

while

\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+b^{n+1}.
\]

The difference is

\[
b^{n+2}-(y+b^{n+1})
=(b-1)b^{n+1}-y.
\]

Since \(b-1=x+y+1>y\) and \(b^{n+1}\ge 1\), this is strictly positive. Hence

\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]>[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
\]

for every valuation over \(\mathbb N\).

The interpretation is monotone, and strict descent in \(\mathbb N\) is well-founded.

**Boundary:** Yes. As with (B), exponentiation is used only in the metatheoretic interpretation certifying decrease. It is not an added equation, axiom, or assumption about the rewrite terms.

---

## Methods satisfying both conditions

All five methods satisfy both conditions:

\[
\boxed{\text{(A), (B), (C), (D), and (E).}}
\]

Each proves termination of the stated TRS, and none requires an object-level structural axiom or assumption beyond the rules. The precedences, interpretations, and projection are proof witnesses in the metatheory, not additional structure imposed on the rewrite system.
