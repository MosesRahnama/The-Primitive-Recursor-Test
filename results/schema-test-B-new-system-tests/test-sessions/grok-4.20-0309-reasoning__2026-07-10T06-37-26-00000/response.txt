Methods satisfying both: A, C, D  


The TRS has two rules whose orientations must be checked in each method. All five methods orient both rules in a strictly decreasing way under the stated parameters (LPO/MPO via head precedence plus subterm/multiset/lex status on arguments or variables; polynomial/exponential interpretations via direct substitution and arithmetic comparison over \(\mathbb{N}\) showing a strict drop of at least 1 in every case; DP via the obvious DP \(F^\sharp(x,y,S(n))\to F^\sharp(x,y,n)\) together with the projection onto the third argument, which descends by the proper-subterm relation). Thus every method succeeds on termination.

The boundary requires that the proof use only symbols and relations derivable from the two given rules (no external well-founded domains, no operations such as \(+\), \(*\), or exponentiation, and no axioms about them). LPO (A), MPO (C), and DP+subterm (D) are purely syntactic on the given signature and rules; they compare terms by precedence, embedding, multiset status, or argument projection and require no external structure. The two interpretations (B, E) map into \(\mathbb{N}\) and appeal to concrete arithmetic operations and their properties to justify decrease; those operations and their ordering axioms are not present in the TRS and are therefore outside the boundary. Hence only A, C, and D meet both criteria.
