**Analysis of the rewrite system**

The system consists of two rules over the signature  
\(\Sigma=\{F/3,G/2,S/1,Z/0\}\):

1. \(F(x,y,Z)\to x\)  
2. \(F(x,y,S(n))\to G(y,F(x,y,n))\)

Termination is driven by the third argument of \(F\): in the recursive call the
pattern \(S(n)\) is replaced by the proper subterm \(n\).  All five methods
exploit this structural decrease, but they differ in whether they stay purely
inside the syntax of the system or import an external semantic structure (the
natural numbers with arithmetic).

---

### Method (A) – Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence \(F>G>S>Z\)

1. **Success?**  
   - Rule 1: \(F(x,y,Z) >_{\sf lpo} x\) because \(x\) is a variable occurring in
     the left‑hand side.  
   - Rule 2: \(F>G\), so we must have \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\sf lpo} y\) (true,
     \(y\) is a variable) and \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\sf lpo} F(x,y,n)\).  
     For the latter, the heads are equal; lexicographic comparison of the
     arguments succeeds because the first two are identical and the third
     satisfies \(S(n) >_{\sf lpo} n\) (subterm case).  Moreover the
     left‑hand side is larger than every argument of the right‑hand side.
   Hence LPO orients both rules.

2. **Inside the boundary?**  
   LPO is a purely syntactic reduction order.  The precedence is a finite
   ordering on the signature that is supplied as part of the method; it does
   not import any object‑level axioms about the data constructors.  It relies
   only on the subterm relation and the given precedence, both of which are
   fixed by the rewrite system’s syntax.  
   **→ Stays inside.**

---

### Method (B) – Non‑linear polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\)

Interpretation:  
\([Z]=0,\;[S(n)]=n+1,\;[G(a,b)]=a+b,\;[F(x,y,n)]=x+(y+1)(n+1)\).

1. **Success?**  
   - Rule 1: \([F(x,y,Z)] = x+y+1 > x = [x]\).  
   - Rule 2: \([F(x,y,S(n))] = x+(y+1)(n+2)\) and
     \([G(y,F(x,y,n))] = y + x+(y+1)(n+1)\).  
     The difference is \((y+1)(n+2)-y-(y+1)(n+1)=1>0\).  
   The interpretation is strictly monotone in every argument, so it proves
   termination.

2. **Inside the boundary?**  
   The method interprets the symbols \(S\) and \(Z\) as the successor and zero
   of the natural numbers and uses addition and multiplication on \(\mathbb N\).
   This imports the global algebraic structure of the naturals, which is **not**
   derivable from the rewrite rules alone (the rules never mention \(+\) or
   \(\times\)).  Although well‑foundedness of \(\mathbb N\) is ordinary
   metatheoretic knowledge, the *choice* of \(\mathbb N\) with its specific
   operations is an arbitrary imported structural model.  
   **→ Outside the boundary.**

---

### Method (C) – Multiset Path Order (MPO) with precedence \(F>G>S>Z\)

1. **Success?**  
   - Rule 1: \(F(x,y,Z) >_{\sf mpo} x\) (variable case).  
   - Rule 2: \(F>G\), so we need \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\sf mpo} y\) (true) and
     \(F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\sf mpo} F(x,y,n)\).  
     With equal heads, MPO compares the multisets of arguments:
     \(\{x,y,S(n)\} >_{\sf mpo}^{\rm mul} \{x,y,n\}\) holds exactly when
     \(S(n) >_{\sf mpo} n\), which follows from the subterm relation.
   Both rules are oriented.

2. **Inside the boundary?**  
   Like LPO, MPO is a syntactic path order defined solely from the signature
   and a precedence.  It uses no external semantic domain.  
   **→ Stays inside.**

---

### Method (D) – Dependency pairs with subterm criterion (projection to the
third argument of \(F\))

1. **Success?**  
   The only defined symbol is \(F\).  The only dependency pair generated from
   rule 2 is  
   \[
   F^\sharp(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\sharp(x,y,n).
   \]
   Projecting \(F\) to its third argument yields the pair of terms
   \(S(n)\) and \(n\).  Since \(n\) is a proper subterm of \(S(n)\), the
   subterm criterion applies and the dependency‑pair problem is finite.
   Hence termination is proved.

2. **Inside the boundary?**  
   The subterm criterion uses only the (syntactic) subterm ordering, which is
   fixed by the term algebra of the given signature.  No external structures
   are invoked.  
   **→ Stays inside.**

---

### Method (E) – Exponential interpretation over \(\mathbb N\)

Interpretation:  
\([Z]=0,\;[S(n)]=n+1,\;[G(a,b)]=a+b,\;[F(x,y,n)]=(x+y+2)^{\,n+1}\).

1. **Success?**  
   - Rule 1: \([F(x,y,Z)]=(x+y+2)^1 = x+y+2 > x\).  
   - Rule 2: \([F(x,y,S(n))]=(x+y+2)^{\,n+2}\) and
     \([G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+(x+y+2)^{\,n+1}\).  
     Because \(x+y+2\ge 2\),
     \((x+y+2)^{\,n+2}= (x+y+2)\cdot(x+y+2)^{\,n+1}
     \ge 2\cdot(x+y+2)^{\,n+1} > y+(x+y+2)^{\,n+1}\).
   The interpretation is strictly monotone (base \(\ge 2\) guarantees monotonicity
   in the exponent), so termination follows.

2. **Inside the boundary?**  
   As with method B, this imports the natural numbers together with the
   exponential function.  The choice of \(\mathbb N\) and the exponential
   operation is a global structural assumption not present in the rewrite
   system.  
   **→ Outside the boundary.**

---

### Summary

| Method | Proves termination? | Stays inside the boundary? |
|--------|---------------------|----------------------------|
| (A) LPO | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| (B) Non‑linear polynomial | **Yes** | **No** (imports \(\mathbb N\) with arithmetic) |
| (C) MPO | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| (D) DP + subterm criterion | **Yes** | **Yes** |
| (E) Exponential interpretation | **Yes** | **No** (imports \(\mathbb N\) with exponentiation) |

**The methods that satisfy both conditions are (A), (C), and (D).**
