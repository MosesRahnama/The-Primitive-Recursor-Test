## Method A: Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with F > G > S > Z

**Does it succeed?**

LPO requires checking that for each rule, the left side is greater than the right side in the LPO ordering.

Rule 1: `F(x, y, Z) > x`
- LPO checks: F has precedence over all symbols in the right side (x is a variable). By the standard LPO definition, `F(x, y, Z) > x` holds because F is at the top level and x appears in the arguments.

Rule 2: `F(x, y, S(n)) > G(y, F(x, y, n))`
- Left: `F(x, y, S(n))`
- Right: `G(y, F(x, y, n))`
- Since F > G (by precedence), we need `F(x, y, S(n)) >_LPO G(y, F(x, y, n))`. By the precedence-based comparison, F > G ensures the left side dominates. ✓

**Within boundary?** Yes. LPO is a standard metatheoretic termination criterion applied directly to the given rules.
