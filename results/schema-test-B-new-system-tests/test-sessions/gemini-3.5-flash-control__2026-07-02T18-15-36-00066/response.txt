### Analysis of the Methods

#### **(A) Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence $F > G > S > Z$**
1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.** 
   * **Rule 1 ($F(x, y, Z) \to x$):** Since $x$ is a strict subterm of the left-hand side (LHS), $F(x, y, Z) >_{\text{lpo}} x$ holds.
   * **Rule 2 ($F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n))$):** Since $F > G$, we must show:
     1. $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{\text{lpo}} y$ (holds because $y$ is a strict subterm of the LHS).
     2. $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{\text{lpo}} F(x, y, n)$. Comparing the arguments lexicographically (left-to-right), the first difference is at the third argument: $S(n) >_{\text{lpo}} n$ (holds because $n$ is a strict subterm of $S(n)$). All variables on the right-hand side (RHS) are also bounded by the LHS.
   Thus, both rules are strictly decreasing under LPO.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes.** LPO is a purely syntactic ordering operating directly on the term algebra of the TRS. It does not import any external algebraic structures or object-level assumptions.

#### **(B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over $\mathbb{N}$**
1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.**
   * **Monotonicity:** $[F]$, $[S]$, and $[G]$ are strictly monotonic in all arguments over $\mathbb{N}$.
   * **Rule 1:** $[F(x, y, Z)] = x + y + 1 > x = [x]$ (strictly decreasing).
   * **Rule 2:** $[F(x, y, S(n))] = x + (y+1)(n+2) = x + y(n+1) + 2y + n + 2$. 
     $[G(y, F(x, y, n))] = y + x + (y+1)(n+1) = x + y(n+1) + 2y + n + 1$.
     Comparing LHS and RHS: $[LHS] - [RHS] = 1 > 0$ (strictly decreasing).
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **No.** It maps terms to the domain of natural numbers $\mathbb{N}$ and relies on the algebraic structural axioms of $(\mathbb{N}, +, \times)$ (such as associativity, commutativity, and distributivity), which are external to the given rewrite system and not derivable from its rules.

#### **(C) Multiset Path Order (MPO) with precedence $F > G > S > Z$**
1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.**
   * **Rule 1 ($F(x, y, Z) \to x$):** Holds by the subterm property.
   * **Rule 2 ($F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n))$):** Since $F > G$, we need:
     1. $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{\text{mpo}} y$ (holds by subterm).
     2. $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{\text{mpo}} F(x, y, n)$. Comparing the arguments as multisets: $\{x, y, S(n)\} >_{\text{mul}} \{x, y, n\}$. Removing common elements $x$ and $y$ leaves $\{S(n)\} >_{\text{mul}} \{n\}$, which holds because $S(n) >_{\text{mpo}} n$.
   Thus, both rules are strictly decreasing under MPO.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes.** Like LPO, MPO is a purely syntactic ordering on the term algebra of the TRS and does not import external structural axioms.

#### **(D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion**
1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.** 
   * The only defined symbol is $F$.
   * The only dependency pair is: $F^\#(x, y, S(n)) \to F^\#(x, y, n)$.
   * Projecting $F^\#$ to its third argument ($\pi(F^\#) = 3$) yields:
     $\pi(F^\#(x, y, S(n))) = S(n) \;\rhd\; n = \pi(F^\#(x, y, n))$.
   * Since $S(n) \rhd n$ is a strict subterm relation, the dependency pair is eliminated, proving termination.
2. **Does it stay within the boundary?** **Yes.** The dependency pair framework with the subterm criterion is a syntactic metatheoretic method operating solely on the term structures of the TRS without importing external algebraic structures.

#### **(E) Exponential interpretation over $\mathbb{N}$**
1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.**
   * **Monotonicity:** All interpretations are strictly monotonic over $\mathbb{N}$ (since the base of the exponentiation in $[F]$ is always $\ge 2$).
