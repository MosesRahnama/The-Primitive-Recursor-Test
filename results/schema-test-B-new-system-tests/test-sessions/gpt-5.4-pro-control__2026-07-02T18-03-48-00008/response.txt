### Quick checks

- **(A)**  
  - \(F(x,y,Z) > x\) by subterm property.  
  - \(F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)\) since \(S(n) > n\), and then \(F>G\) gives  
    \(F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))\).

- **(B)**  
  \[
  [F(x,y,Z)] = x+(y+1)\cdot 1 = x+y+1 > x
  \]
  \[
  [F(x,y,S(n))] = x+(y+1)(n+2)
  \]
  \[
  [G(y,F(x,y,n))] = y + x + (y+1)(n+1)
  \]
  Difference \(=1\), so strict decrease.

- **(C)**  
  Same idea as (A), but with multiset comparison: \(\{x,y,S(n)\} > \{x,y,n\}\).

- **(D)**  
  Only recursive call yields the DP
  \[
  F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n).
  \]
  Projecting to the 3rd argument gives \(S(n) \triangleright n\), a strict subterm decrease.

- **(E)**  
  Let \(a=x+y+2\ge 2\). Then
  \[
  [F(x,y,S(n))]=a^{n+2},\qquad [G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+a^{n+1}.
  \]
  Since
  \[
  a^{n+2}-\bigl(y+a^{n+1}\bigr)=a^{n+1}(a-1)-y \ge (y+1)-y > 0,
  \]
  rule 2 decreases strictly; rule 1 is also immediate:
  \[
  (x+y+2)^1 = x+y+2 > x.
  \]
