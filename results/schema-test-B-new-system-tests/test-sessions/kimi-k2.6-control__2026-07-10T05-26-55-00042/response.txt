**Method (A) – LPO with precedence F > G > S > Z**
1. **Success:** Yes. Both rules are oriented:
   - Rule 1: \(F(x,y,Z) > x\) because \(x\) is a variable occurring in the left-hand side.
   - Rule 2: Since \(F > G\), we need \(F(x,y,S(n))\) greater than each argument of \(G\). It is greater than \(y\) (variable occurrence) and greater than \(F(x,y,n)\) by the lexicographic comparison \((x,y,S(n)) >_{lex} (x,y,n)\), because \(S(n) > n\).
2. **Boundary:** Yes. LPO is a purely syntactic ordering built from the signature and precedence; it imports no object‑level structural assumptions.

**Method (B) – Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over \(\mathbb N\)**
1. **Success:** Yes. For all valuations in \(\mathbb N\):
   - Rule 1: \([F(x,y,Z)] = x+(y+1)\cdot1 = x+y+1 > x = [x]\).
   - Rule 2: \([F(x,y,S(n))] = x+(y+1)(n+2) = x+(y+1)(n+1)+y+1 > y + x+(y+1)(n+1) = [G(y,F(x,y,n))]\).
   The interpretation is strictly monotone in every argument.
2. **Boundary:** Yes. Monotone algebra interpretations over the natural numbers are standard metatheoretic soundness reasoning; they do not import object‑level axioms about the rewrite signature beyond the given rules.

**Method (C) – MPO with precedence F > G > S > Z (multiset status)**
1. **Success:** Yes.
   - Rule 1: \(F(x,y,Z) > x\) by the variable condition.
   - Rule 2: \(F > G\) requires the lhs to exceed each argument of \(G\). It exceeds \(y\) trivially, and it exceeds \(F(x,y,n)\) because the multisets \(\{x,y,S(n)\}\) and \(\{x,y,n\}\) satisfy \(\{x,y,S(n)\} >_{mul} \{x,y,n\}\) via \(S(n) > n\).
2. **Boundary:** Yes. MPO, like LPO, is a syntactic path order requiring no external structural axioms.

**Method (D) – Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F**
1. **Success:** Yes. The only dependency pair is \(F(x,y,S(n)) \to F(x,y,n)\). Projecting to the third argument yields \(S(n)\) on the left and \(n\) on the right; \(S(n)\) is a strict superterm of \(n\). The subterm criterion therefore deletes this DP, leaving no cycles, so termination follows.
2. **Boundary:** Yes. The subterm criterion relies solely on the (syntactic) subterm relation on the fixed term algebra; it assumes no imported data‑type structure.

**Method (E) – Exponential interpretation over \(\mathbb N\)**
1. **Success:** Yes. Let \(A=x+y+2\;( \ge 2)\). Then
   - Rule 1: \([F(x,y,Z)] = A^{1} = x+y+2 > x = [x]\).
   - Rule 2: \([F(x,y,S(n))] = A^{n+2} = A\cdot A^{n+1} \ge 2A^{n+1} > y + A^{n+1} = [G(y,F(x,y,n))]\).
   The function \((x,y,n)\mapsto (x+y+2)^{\,n+1}\) is strictly monotone in each argument over \(\mathbb N\).
2. **Boundary:** Yes. As with (B), this is a monotone algebra interpretation, i.e. ordinary metatheoretic soundness reasoning.

### Methods satisfying both conditions
All five proposed methods meet both requirements: **A, B, C, D, and E**.
