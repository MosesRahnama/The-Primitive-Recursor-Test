## (A) LPO with \(F > G > S > Z\)

**Succeeds:** Yes.

For the first rule,
\[
F(x,y,Z) \mathrel{>} x
\]
holds by the subterm property.

For the second rule, since \(F>G\), it suffices that
\[
F(x,y,S(n)) > y
\quad\text{and}\quad
F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n).
\]
The first is by the subterm property. For the second, the roots agree and lexicographic comparison reaches the third arguments:
\[
S(n) > n
\]
by the subterm property. Hence the second rule is strictly decreasing.

**Boundary:** Yes. The precedence is an explicitly stated syntactic proof parameter, not an additional rewrite axiom or an assumption about objects denoted by terms.

## (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation

**Succeeds:** Yes.

For rule 1:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = x+(y+1)(0+1)=x+y+1>x=[x].
\]

For rule 2:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]
 =x+(y+1)(n+2),
\]
whereas
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
 =y+x+(y+1)(n+1).
\]
Their difference is
\[
\bigl(x+(y+1)(n+2)\bigr)
-\bigl(y+x+(y+1)(n+1)\bigr)
=1.
\]
Thus every instance of the second rule strictly decreases.

The interpretation functions are monotone over \(\mathbb N\), so this is a valid natural-number interpretation proof.

**Boundary:** Yes. Interpreting terms in \(\mathbb N\) is ordinary metatheoretic termination reasoning. It does not assume that variables range only over special syntactic forms or that the rewrite system has extra algebraic axioms.

## (C) MPO with the stated precedence

**Succeeds:** Yes.

Rule 1 decreases by the subterm property:
\[
F(x,y,Z)>x.
\]

For rule 2, \(F>G\), so it is enough that the left-hand side dominate both arguments of the right-hand-side \(G\):
\[
F(x,y,S(n))>y
\]
by subterm, and
\[
F(x,y,S(n))>F(x,y,n).
\]

For the latter comparison, the multisets of arguments are
\[
\{x,y,S(n)\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\{x,y,n\}.
\]
Since \(S(n)>n\), the first multiset strictly dominates the second. Hence the rule decreases in MPO.

**Boundary:** Yes. As with LPO, the precedence and multiset status are explicit syntactic proof data, not imported object-level assumptions.

## (D) Dependency pairs with projection to the third argument of \(F\)

**Succeeds:** Yes.

The only defined symbol is \(F\), because it is the only root symbol occurring on a left-hand side. In particular, \(G\) has no defining rules and is not a defined symbol for ordinary dependency-pair generation.

The relevant dependency pair is therefore
\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]

Projecting the third argument gives
\[
S(n) \to n.
\]
This is a strict subterm decrease. Thus the dependency pair cannot be repeated infinitely, and the subterm criterion removes the only recursive dependency-pair cycle.

**Boundary:** Yes. The projection is a finite syntactic certificate for the dependency-pair problem; it does not impose an additional property on the original terms or rewrite relation.

## (E) Exponential interpretation

**Succeeds:** Yes.

Let
\[
b=x+y+2.
\]
Then \(b\ge 2\).

For rule 1:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = b^{0+1}=b=x+y+2>x.
\]

For rule 2:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=b^{n+2},
\]
while
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+b^{n+1}.
\]
It suffices to show
\[
b^{n+2}>y+b^{n+1},
\]
equivalently,
\[
b^{n+1}(b-1)>y.
\]
Since \(n\ge 0\) and \(b\ge 2\),
\[
b^{n+1}(b-1)\ge b(b-1).
\]
Also \(b=x+y+2\ge y+2\), so
\[
b(b-1)>y.
\]
Therefore the second rule strictly decreases.

The interpretation is monotone on \(\mathbb N\), including monotonicity in the exponent argument because the base is always at least \(2\).

**Boundary:** Yes. This is again a semantic ranking argument checked uniformly for all substitutions, not an extra assumption about the rewrite system.
