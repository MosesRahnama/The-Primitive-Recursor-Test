**Method-by-method analysis**

**A – Lexicographic Path Order (LPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z**
1. **Does it succeed?** Yes. Rule 1 decreases by the subterm property. Rule 2 is decreasing because F > G and the lexicographic comparison on the third argument yields S(n) > n, while the other side conditions hold.
2. **Within the boundary?** Yes. LPO is a purely syntactic method; the precedence is a parameter of the order, not an external structural axiom or an object-level assumption (e.g., arithmetic). No external theory is imported.

**B – Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over N**
1. **Does it succeed?** Yes. Both rules strictly decrease under the given polynomial assignment, and all functions are monotonic over N.
2. **Within the boundary?** No. The interpretation maps symbols to functions on the natural numbers, thereby importing the theory of arithmetic (addition, multiplication, ordering on N). These are object-level assumptions not derivable from the rewrite rules themselves.

**C – Multiset Path Order (MPO) with precedence F > G > S > Z, all arguments compared as multisets**
1. **Does it succeed?** Yes. Rule 1 by subterm; rule 2 by F > G and the multiset comparison {S(n)} >₁ {n} after removing shared elements.
2. **Within the boundary?** Yes. Like LPO, MPO is a syntactic method. The precedence and multiset status do not constitute imported structural axioms or external object-level assumptions.

**D – Dependency pairs with subterm criterion, projecting to the third argument of F**
1. **Does it succeed?** Yes. The only dependency pair is F♯(x,y,S(n)) → F♯(x,y,n). Projecting to the third argument gives S(n) ▷ n, a strict subterm decrease. The criterion removes the pair, proving termination.
2. **Within the boundary?** Yes. Dependency pairs with the subterm criterion is a syntactic technique; the projection is a structural choice that does not import external axioms or semantic domains.

**E – Exponential interpretation over N**
1. **Does it succeed?** Yes. Both rules strictly decrease (e.g., (x+y+2)^(n+2) > y + (x+y+2)^(n+1) for all naturals).
2. **Within the boundary?** No. Like B, it interprets symbols as arithmetic functions over N, importing the object-level theory of natural numbers beyond what is derivable from the rules.

**Methods satisfying both conditions:** A, C, D.
