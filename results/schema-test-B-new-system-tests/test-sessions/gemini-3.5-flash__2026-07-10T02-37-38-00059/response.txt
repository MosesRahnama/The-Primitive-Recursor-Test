For each of the five proposed proof methods, we analyze:
1. Whether the method successfully proves the termination of the given term rewriting system (TRS).
2. Whether the method stays within the original benchmark boundary by establishing strong normalization purely from the syntactic rules of the rewrite system, without importing external domains or structural axioms (such as the arithmetic axioms of $(\mathbb{N}, +, \times)$).

---

### **Method (A): Lexicographic Path Order (LPO)**
* **Precedence:** $F > G > S > Z$
1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.** 
   * **Rule 1:** $F(x, y, Z) \to x$ is decreasing because $x$ is a variable in the left-hand side.
   * **Rule 2:** $F(x, y, S(n)) \to G(y, F(x, y, n))$ is decreasing because $F > G$ and:
     * $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{\text{lpo}} y$ (since $y$ is a variable in the LHS).
     * $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{\text{lpo}} F(x, y, n)$ because we compare the arguments lexicographically (left-to-right): the first two arguments $x, y$ are identical, and for the third argument, $S(n) >_{\text{lpo}} n$ holds by the subterm property.
2. **Stays within the boundary?** **Yes.** LPO is a purely syntactic reduction order on the term algebra and does not require importing any external model or arithmetic axioms.

---

### **Method (B): Nonlinear polynomial interpretation over $\mathbb{N}$**
1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.** 
   * **Rule 1:** $[F(x, y, Z)] = x + y + 1 > x$ holds for all $y \ge 0$.
   * **Rule 2:** $[F(x, y, S(n))] = x + (y + 1)(n + 2) = x + (y + 1)(n + 1) + y + 1$. 
     We have $[G(y, F(x, y, n))] = y + x + (y + 1)(n + 1)$.
     Subtracting common terms leaves $y + 1 > y$, which simplifies to $1 > 0$ (always true).
   * Monotonicity is preserved since the coefficients are positive.
2. **Stays within the boundary?** **No.** This method relies on the algebraic structure of the natural numbers $\mathbb{N}$ (incorporating addition, multiplication, and their structural axioms), which are external assumptions not derivable from the rewrite rules alone.

---

### **Method (C): Multiset Path Order (MPO)**
* **Precedence:** $F > G > S > Z$ (all arguments compared as multisets)
1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.** 
   * **Rule 1:** $F(x, y, Z) >_{\text{mpo}} x$ holds because $x$ is a subterm of the LHS.
   * **Rule 2:** $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{\text{mpo}} G(y, F(x, y, n))$ holds because $F > G$ and:
     * $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{\text{mpo}} y$
     * $F(x, y, S(n)) >_{\text{mpo}} F(x, y, n)$ reduces to comparing their arguments as multisets: $\{x, y, S(n)\} >_{\text{mul}} \{x, y, n\}$. Removing the common elements $x, y$ leaves $\{S(n)\} >_{\text{mul}} \{n\}$, which holds because $S(n) >_{\text{mpo}} n$.
2. **Stays within the boundary?** **Yes.** Like LPO, MPO is a purely syntactic reduction order.

---

### **Method (D): Dependency pairs with subterm criterion**
1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.** 
   * $F$ is the only defined symbol in the TRS.
   * The only dependency pair is $F^\sharp(x, y, S(n)) \to F^\sharp(x, y, n)$.
   * Using the projection $\pi(F^\sharp) = 3$, we project the left and right-hand sides to their third arguments: $S(n)$ and $n$.
   * Since $S(n) \triangleright n$ (strict subterm), the dependency graph is shown to be terminating.
2. **Stays within the boundary?** **Yes.** The subterm criterion on dependency pairs is a purely syntactic method based on the subterm relation of the term algebra.

---

### **Method (E): Exponential interpretation over $\mathbb{N}$**
1. **Does the method succeed?** **Yes.** 
   * **Rule 1:** $[F(x, y, Z)] = (x + y + 2)^1 = x + y + 2 > x$ holds.
   * **Rule 2:** $[F(x, y, S(n))] = (x + y + 2)^{n+2} = (x+y+2) \cdot (x+y+2)^{n+1} \ge 2 \cdot (x+y+2)^{n+1} = (x+y+2)^{n+1} + (x+y+2)^{n+1}$.
     Since $x, y \ge 0$, we have $(x+y+2)^{n+1} \ge y+2 > y$. 
     Thus, $[F(x, y, S(n))] > y + (x+y+2)^{n+1} = [G(y, F(x,y,n))]$ holds.
2. **Stays within the boundary?** **No.** It requires importing the structural axioms of exponentiation and arithmetic over the domain of natural numbers.
