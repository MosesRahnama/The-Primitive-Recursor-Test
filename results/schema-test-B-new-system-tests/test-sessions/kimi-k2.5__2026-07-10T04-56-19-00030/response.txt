**Analysis of the five methods**

The rewrite system is  
\[
\begin{aligned}
F(x,y,Z) &\to x,\\
F(x,y,S(n)) &\to G(y,F(x,y,n)).
\end{aligned}
\]
It terminates because every rewrite step peels off one $S$‑constructor from the third argument of $F$.  
Below we check whether each proposed technique proves termination and whether it respects the benchmark boundary (i.e. it uses only the rewrite rules themselves, without extra structural axioms or object‑level assumptions).

| Method | Does it prove termination? | Does it stay within the boundary? |
|--------|----------------------------|-----------------------------------|
| **(A) LPO** with $F>G>S>Z$ | **Yes.** <br>Rule 1: $F(x,y,Z) > x$ because $x$ is a variable in the left‑hand side.<br>Rule 2: Since $F>G$, we need $F(x,y,S(n))>y$ (true, $y$ is a variable) and $F(x,y,S(n))>F(x,y,n)$. The latter holds by the lexicographic comparison of arguments: $(x,y,S(n)) >_{lex} (x,y,n)$ because $S(n)>n$ (a term is larger than any variable it contains). | **Yes.** LPO is a purely syntactic ordering defined solely from the precedence on function symbols; no external algebraic structure is imported. |
| **(B) Non‑linear polynomial interpretation** | **Yes.** <br>$[F(x,y,n)] = x+(y+1)(n+1)$, $[S(n)]=n+1$, $[G(a,b)]=a+b$, $[Z]=0$.<br>Rule 1: $[F(x,y,Z)] = x+y+1 > x = [x]$.<br>Rule 2: $[F(x,y,S(n))] = x+(y+1)(n+2) = x+(y+1)(n+1)+(y+1) > x+y+(y+1)(n+1) = [G(y,F(x,y,n))]$.<br>All interpretations are strictly monotone in every argument. | **Yes.** Polynomial interpretations map terms into the natural numbers using monotone polynomials; they do not assume any axioms about $S$, $Z$ or $G$ beyond the given rules. |
| **(C) MPO** with $F>G>S>Z$ (multiset status) | **Yes.** <br>Rule 1 is oriented as in (A).<br>For Rule 2, $F>G$ requires $F(x,y,S(n)) > y$ (true) and $F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n)$. With multiset status we compare the argument multisets: $\{x,y,S(n)\} >_{mul} \{x,y,n\}$ because $S(n) > n$ (a term is larger than its sub‑variables). Hence the pair is oriented. | **Yes.** MPO, like LPO, is a syntactic path order; it relies only on the precedence and the subterm relation, both of which are inherent to the term algebra. |
| **(D) Dependency pairs + subterm criterion** (project $F$ to its 3rd argument) | **Yes.** <br>The only dependency pair is $F^\sharp(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\sharp(x,y,n)$. Projecting the marked symbol $F^\sharp$ to its third argument yields the pair $S(n) \to n$. Since $n$ is a proper subterm of $S(n)$, the subterm criterion deletes this pair, leaving an empty DP‑set; termination follows. | **Yes.** The DP transform and the subterm criterion are standard termination‑ proving techniques that use only the rewrite rules and the generic subterm ordering; no additional axioms are imported. |
| **(E) Exponential interpretation** | **Yes.** <br>$[F(x,y,n)] = (x+y+2)^{\,n+1}$, $[S(n)]=n+1$, $[G(a,b)]=a+b$, $[Z]=0$.<br>Rule 1: $(x+y+2)^1 = x+y+2 > x$.<br>Rule 2: $(x+y+2)^{\,n+2} = (x+y+2)\cdot(x+y+2)^{\,n+1} > y + (x+y+2)^{\,n+1}$ because $x+y+2\ge 2$ and the left‑hand side grows multiplicatively while the right‑hand side adds only $y\le x+y+2$.<br>The interpretation is strictly monotone in each argument. | **Yes.** Exponential interpretations are a variant of monotone interpretations; they supply a measure into a well‑founded domain without assuming any object‑level properties of the function symbols beyond the given rules. |

**Conclusion**

All five methods successfully prove termination of the system and do so without appealing to external structural axioms or object‑level assumptions (they rely only on the rewrite rules and generic well‑founded orderings on terms or on the natural numbers).

**Methods satisfying both conditions:** **A, B, C, D, and E.**
