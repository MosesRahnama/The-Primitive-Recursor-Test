All five methods succeed, and—under the stated narrow boundary—all five stay within the benchmark boundary.

## (A) LPO with \(F > G > S > Z\)

First rule:
\[
F(x,y,Z) \to x.
\]
The left-hand side is greater than \(x\) by the subterm property.

Second rule:
\[
F(x,y,S(n)) \to G(y,F(x,y,n)).
\]
Since \(F>G\), it suffices that
\[
F(x,y,S(n)) > y
\quad\text{and}\quad
F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n).
\]
The first is by the subterm property. For the second, the two \(F\)-terms are compared lexicographically: the first two arguments agree, and
\[
S(n)>n
\]
by the subterm property. Thus the second rule is strictly decreasing.

The precedence is a standard reduction-order witness, not an added axiom about the rewrite system. Moreover, the relevant precedence relation \(F>G\) is exactly what is needed by the rule with root symbols \(F\) and \(G\); the additional listed comparisons are not substantively relied upon. Thus this remains within the stated boundary.

## (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation

Let
\[
[F(x,y,n)] = x+(y+1)(n+1),\qquad [S(n)]=n+1,
\]
\[
[G(a,b)]=a+b,\qquad [Z]=0.
\]

For the first rule:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = x+(y+1)(0+1)=x+y+1>x=[x].
\]

For the second rule, the left-hand side has value
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]
= x+(y+1)(n+2).
\]
The right-hand side has value
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
= y+x+(y+1)(n+1).
\]
Their difference is exactly
\[
\bigl(x+(y+1)(n+2)\bigr)
-\bigl(y+x+(y+1)(n+1)\bigr)
=1.
\]
Hence the left-hand side is strictly larger for every natural-number valuation.

This is an ordinary semantic termination proof; it does not posit any extra object-level property of terms.

## (C) MPO with \(F>G>S>Z\)

The first rule is decreasing by the subterm property.

For the second rule, \(F>G\), and the left-hand side exceeds both arguments of the right-hand side:

\[
F(x,y,S(n)) > y
\]
by the subterm property, and
\[
F(x,y,S(n)) >_{\mathrm{mul}} F(x,y,n)
\]
because the argument multisets are
\[
\{x,y,S(n)\}
\quad\text{and}\quad
\{x,y,n\},
\]
with \(S(n)>n\).

Thus the rule is strictly decreasing in MPO. As in (A), the precedence is a proof-order parameter rather than an imported structural axiom about rewriting, so this is within the stated boundary.

## (D) Dependency pairs with projection to the third argument of \(F\)

The only recursive call is the occurrence of \(F(x,y,n)\) in the second rule. Hence the relevant dependency pair is
\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]

Projecting to the third argument gives
\[
S(n) \to n.
\]
The right side is a proper subterm of the left side:
\[
S(n)\triangleright n.
\]
Therefore the subterm criterion eliminates the only dependency-pair cycle, establishing absence of infinite dependency-pair chains and hence termination.

The chosen projection is justified directly by the syntactic recursive descent already present in the rule; it adds no structural axiom or object-level assumption.

## (E) Exponential interpretation

Let
\[
[F(x,y,n)] = (x+y+2)^{n+1},\qquad [S(n)]=n+1,
\]
\[
[G(a,b)]=a+b,\qquad [Z]=0.
\]

Write
\[
b=x+y+2.
\]
Then \(b\ge y+2\).

For the first rule:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)] = b>x.
\]

For the second rule:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=b^{n+2},
\]
while
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+b^{n+1}.
\]
It remains to show
\[
b^{n+2}>y+b^{n+1}.
\]
Indeed,
\[
b^{n+2}-b^{n+1}=b^{n+1}(b-1).
\]
Since \(b\ge y+2\) and \(n\ge0\), this quantity is greater than \(y\). Therefore
\[
b^{n+2}>b^{n+1}+y.
\]

So this interpretation strictly orients both rules. It uses only standard well-foundedness of \(\mathbb N\) and ordinary interpretation soundness, not an extra assumption about the rewrite system.
