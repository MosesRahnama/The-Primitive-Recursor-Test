All five methods succeed, and all remain within the stated benchmark boundary. They use only syntactic ordering machinery or semantic interpretations as termination-proof devices; none requires adding object-level axioms such as induction, injectivity of `S`, distinctness of `Z` and `S(_)`, or an assumption that the third argument is a natural-number constructor term.

| Method | Proves termination? | Within benchmark boundary? | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| (A) LPO, `F > G > S > Z` | Yes | Yes | `F(x,y,Z) > x` by the subterm property. For rule 2, `F(x,y,S(n)) > G(y,F(x,y,n))`: since `F > G`, it suffices that the left side exceeds both `y` and `F(x,y,n)`. It exceeds `y` by subterm, and exceeds `F(x,y,n)` lexicographically because the first two arguments agree and `S(n) > n`. |
| (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation | Yes | Yes | Rule 1 gives `x+y+1 > x`. For rule 2, the left interpretation is `x+(y+1)(n+2)`, while the right is `y+x+(y+1)(n+1)`; their difference is exactly `1`. The interpretation is monotone over `N`. |
| (C) MPO, same precedence | Yes | Yes | Rule 1 follows by subterm. In rule 2, `F > G`, and the left side dominates `y` and `F(x,y,n)`. The latter follows from the multiset comparison `{x,y,S(n)} >mul {x,y,n}`, using `S(n) > n`. |
| (D) Dependency pairs plus subterm criterion | Yes | Yes | The only recursive dependency pair is `F#(x,y,S(n)) -> F#(x,y,n)`. Projection to argument 3 gives `S(n) -> n`, a strict subterm decrease. Thus the dependency-pair problem is eliminated. No assumption that `n` is a numeral is needed. |
| (E) Exponential interpretation | Yes | Yes | Let `B=x+y+2`, so `B≥2`. Rule 1 gives `B>x`. For rule 2, the left side is `B^(n+2)` and the right side is `y+B^(n+1)`. Since `B^(n+2)-B^(n+1)=B^(n+1)(B-1)>y`, the rule decreases strictly. |

For method (E), more explicitly, `B-1=x+y+1>y`, and `B^(n+1)≥1`, so:
\[
B^{n+1}(B-1) > y.
\]

The use of `N` in (B) and (E) is a meta-level interpretation for proving termination; it does not add an object-level claim that `Z` and `S` obey Peano axioms or that terms must denote natural numbers.
