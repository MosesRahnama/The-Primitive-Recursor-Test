### (A) LPO

For the first rule, \(F(x,y,Z) >_{\mathrm{LPO}} x\) by the subterm property.

For the second rule, since \(F>G\), it suffices that
\[
F(x,y,S(n)) > y
\quad\text{and}\quad
F(x,y,S(n)) > F(x,y,n).
\]
The first follows by the subterm property. The second follows lexicographically because the first two arguments agree and \(S(n)>n\).

Thus LPO strictly orients both rules. This is entirely syntactic and requires no assumptions about the terms represented by \(Z\) and \(S\).

### (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation

For the first rule:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)]=x+(y+1)>x.
\]

For the second:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]
=x+(y+1)(n+2),
\]
while
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]
=y+x+(y+1)(n+1).
\]
The left side exceeds the right side by exactly \(1\).

The interpretations are strictly monotone in every argument over \(\mathbb N\), so they induce a valid well-founded reduction order. Using \(\mathbb N\) as a meta-level interpretation domain does not assert that object-level terms are natural numbers or that every term has a \(Z/S\) structure.

### (C) MPO

Again, the first rule follows from the subterm property.

For the recursive call, the multiset of arguments
\[
\{x,y,S(n)\}
\]
strictly dominates
\[
\{x,y,n\}
\]
because \(S(n)>n\). Since \(F>G\), the whole left-hand side also dominates the outer \(G\)-term, with its arguments \(y\) and \(F(x,y,n)\) dominated as required.

This is a purely syntactic argument.

### (D) Dependency pairs with subterm criterion

The only recursive dependency pair is
\[
F^\#(x,y,S(n)) \to F^\#(x,y,n).
\]
Projection to the third argument gives
\[
S(n) \triangleright n,
\]
a strict proper-subterm decrease. Hence the dependency-pair problem is finite by the subterm criterion.

This does not assume that arbitrary terms are either \(Z\) or successors; it uses only the syntactic subterm relation visible in the dependency pair.

### (E) Exponential interpretation

Let \(A=x+y+2\), so \(A\ge 2\).

For the first rule:
\[
[F(x,y,Z)]=A>x.
\]

For the second:
\[
[F(x,y,S(n))]=A^{n+2},
\]
whereas
\[
[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=y+A^{n+1}.
\]
Their difference is
\[
A^{n+1}(A-1)-y>0,
\]
because \(A-1=x+y+1>y\).

The interpretation is strictly monotone in all arguments over \(\mathbb N\), and therefore defines a valid well-founded reduction order. As with (B), this is a semantic termination interpretation, not an object-level datatype assumption.

## Conclusion

**All five methods—A, B, C, D, and E—both prove termination and remain within the stated benchmark boundary.**
