### Analysis of the TRS and methods
The given TRS is orthogonal and terminating (the only recursive call strictly decreases the third argument of \(F\), which is of finite depth, and there are no other defined symbols that could sustain an infinite chain). All five methods are standard techniques from first-order term rewriting. We assess each against the two questions, interpreting “outside the boundary” narrowly as indicated: a method lies outside precisely when its justification depends on arbitrary imported global structure (e.g., the carrier \(\mathbb{N}\) together with its arithmetic operations and the object-level reasoning needed to verify inequalities in that structure) that is not fixed by the signature and rules themselves. Ordinary metatheoretic soundness theorems (well-foundedness of a path order, soundness of the dependency-pair framework, \ldots) are permitted.

### (A) LPO with precedence \(F>G>S>Z\)
1. Yes – both rules are strictly oriented.  
   - \(F(x,y,Z)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}x\) holds by the sub-term property.  
   - \(F(x,y,S(n))>_{\mathrm{LPO}}G(y,F(x,y,n))\) holds because \(F>G\) and the left-hand side is larger than every argument of the right-hand side; the recursive call is larger by lexicographic comparison of arguments (third components satisfy \(S(n)>_{\mathrm{LPO}}n\)).  
2. Yes – the orientation check is a purely syntactic comparison of the two rule terms under a precedence on the given signature. No external carrier or arithmetic is required.

### (B) Nonlinear polynomial interpretation
1. Yes – the interpretation is strictly monotone on \(\mathbb{N}\) and both rules decrease:  
   \[
   [F(x,y,Z)]=x+y+1>x,\qquad
   [F(x,y,S(n))]-[G(y,F(x,y,n))]=1>0.
   \]
2. No – the method imports the global structure \((\mathbb{N},+,·,0,1)\) and relies on object-level arithmetic reasoning (distributivity, etc.) to verify the two universal inequalities. That structure is not fixed by the rewrite rules.

### (C) MPO with the same precedence (multiset status for every symbol)
1. Yes – both rules are strictly oriented. The first rule is again a sub-term; the second rule follows because \(F>G\) and the multiset of arguments \(\{x,y,S(n)\}\) is strictly larger than \(\{x,y,n\}\) in the multiset extension (replace \(S(n)\) by the smaller term \(n\)).  
2. Yes – identical reasoning to (A): a finite syntactic check on the given signature and rules.

### (D) Dependency pairs + sub-term criterion (projection onto the third argument of \(F\))
1. Yes – the only dependency pair is \(F^\sharp(x,y,S(n))\to F^\sharp(x,y,n)\). The chosen projection sends it to the proper sub-term relation \(S(n)\triangleright n\). The sub-term criterion therefore proves that the DP problem admits no infinite chain, hence the original TRS is terminating.  
2. Yes – dependency pairs are computed directly from the rules; the projection is a choice of argument position already present in the signature. The whole argument stays inside the syntactic theory of the given TRS.

### (E) Exponential interpretation
1. Yes – the interpretation is strictly monotone on \(\mathbb{N}\) and both rules decrease (the second because
   \[
   (x+y+2)^{n+2}-y-(x+y+2)^{n+1}=(x+y+1)(x+y+2)^{n+1}-y>0
   \]
   for all \(x,y,n\in\mathbb{N}\)).  
2. No – exactly as for (B): the method imports the global structure \((\mathbb{N},+,\cdot,\exp,0,1)\) together with the object-level arithmetic needed to discharge the universal inequalities.

### Methods that satisfy both conditions
Only (A), (C) and (D) both prove termination and remain inside the stated benchmark boundary.
